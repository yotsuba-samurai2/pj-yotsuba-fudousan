## What exactly cannot be built on a Japanese "no-rebuild" property?

"No rebuild" means a state in which, because the property's site does not meet the frontage-road duty and the like of the Building Standards Act, the existing building cannot be demolished and built anew (new construction after demolition). The point to note is that it does not mean the building cannot be used. The building actually standing can often be used and even leased, but once it is demolished it cannot be rebuilt at the same scale, and extensions and renovations also face the wall of the building-confirmation — that is the reality of "no rebuild."

What a Chinese-speaking buyer should first grasp is that "no rebuild" is not "no rights" but a state in which "with the site as it is, the building-confirmation will not be granted," and that depending on the frontage situation there may remain room to rebuild. Why frontage is at issue is that the Building Standards Act requires the site to touch a road of a certain width for evacuation and firefighting in a disaster.

| Category | What happens with "no rebuild" |
| --- | --- |
| Use and lease of the existing building | In principle possible (the property can still yield income) |
| New construction after demolition | With the site as it is, the building-confirmation is not granted; it cannot be rebuilt |
| Extension / large-scale repair | Work requiring a building-confirmation is restricted |

## How do you confirm the frontage-road duty (2 m frontage, Art. 42 roads, Art. 43)?

The basis of the frontage-road duty is Article 43(1) of the Building Standards Act, which provides that a building's site must touch a road over 2 metres or more. "Road" here is defined by Article 42 and in principle means one 4 metres or more wide (Art. 42(1)). Even under 4 metres wide, there is a "paragraph-2 road (deemed road)" that is deemed a road under certain requirements, in which case the line set back from the centre line of the road is deemed the road boundary (Art. 42(2)).

In DD, you first check whether the passage in front qualifies as a "road" under the Building Standards Act, and whether the site touches it over 2 metres or more, by cross-checking the authority's road survey (confirmation of the road category) against the cadastral map, the land-survey drawing and on-site measurement. If what looks like a road is actually a "passage" that is not a road under the Act, or if the frontage is under 2 metres, the frontage-road duty is not met and it becomes no-rebuild. This confirmation of the road category and the measurement of the frontage is the first fork that separates buying from passing.

| Item to confirm | What to look at | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category of the frontage road | Art. 42(1) (4 m+ wide), Art. 42(2) (deemed road), etc. | Road survey / road-category map of the specified administrative agency |
| Frontage (length of contact) | Whether the site touches the road over 2 m or more | Cadastral map, land-survey drawing, on-site measurement |
| Setback | For a paragraph-2 road, the range of setback from the centre line | Specified administrative agency / on site |

## Is there room to rebuild under the recognition or permission of Article 43(2)?

Even if the frontage is insufficient, Article 43(2) of the Building Standards Act has exceptions, and if the property falls within them, room to rebuild arises. Paragraph 2 treats as a "recognition" under item 1 a building whose site touches a way 4 metres or more wide (excluding one that is a road) over 2 metres or more and that conforms to standards prescribed by Order of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT), such as having few users, which the specified administrative agency recognises as causing no hindrance from the standpoint of traffic, safety, fire prevention and sanitation; and as a "permission" under item 2 a building that conforms to MLIT-Order standards, such as having a large open space around the site, which the specified administrative agency permits with the consent of the building council. The details of the standards are prescribed by Article 10-3 of the Enforcement Regulations of the Building Standards Act.

The item-1 recognition and item-2 permission were established by Act No. 67 of 2018 (promulgated 27 June 2018 (Heisei 30); the frontage-related provisions took effect on 25 September of the same year), reorganising the former proviso permission of Article 43(1). The item-1 recognition is a simpler treatment not requiring the consent of the building council; the item-2 permission requires the consent of the building council; and specified administrative agencies set operational standards such as a comprehensive-consent standard and a batch-review standard. What matters is that whether these "can be used" is an individual judgment of the specified administrative agency and is not settled until you check the operational standards of the specified administrative agency with jurisdiction over the property. We cannot assert that "it can be rebuilt by the recognition or permission."

| Category | Image of the requirement | Building council | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Art. 43(2) item 1 (recognition) | Touches a 4 m+ way over 2 m, conforms to standards such as few users | Consent not required | Act Art. 43(2)(i); Enforcement Regulations Art. 10-3 |
| Art. 43(2) item 2 (permission) | Conforms to standards such as a large open space around the site | Consent required | Act Art. 43(2)(ii); Enforcement Regulations Art. 10-3 |

## How should you think about the loan, price and exit of a no-rebuild property?

A no-rebuild property tends to be hard for a financial institution to appraise as collateral and hard to finance with a home loan, so it is often considered as a cash purchase or a renovation-based investment. The price tends to be lower than a rebuildable property, while the exit (the range of buyers at sale) is also limited. That said, the specific loan availability, yield and extent of discount vary greatly with the financial institution, the location and the state of the building, so this article does not assert figures and leaves them as unverified.

From the buyer's DD view, what matters is to separate, before purchase, whether the returns and the exit hold on the premise of "holding it as no-rebuild," or whether you are betting on "room to make it rebuildable through the Article 43(2) recognition or permission or buying up adjacent land." The latter is swayed by the specified administrative agency's judgment, so pricing in the prospect without confirming it with an architect and an administrative scrivener is dangerous. Loan availability is for the financial institution, valuation for a real estate appraiser, and tax for a tax accountant; we organise the property-side facts (frontage, road category, frontage length, state of adjacent land).

| Point | How to think about it |
| --- | --- |
| Loan | Hard to appraise as collateral; a home loan is hard to arrange (availability judged by the financial institution) |
| Price | Tends to be lower than a rebuildable property (the margin depends on location and building) |
| Exit | The range of buyers is limited; cash and renovation-investor segments tend to be central |
| Making it rebuildable | Art. 43(2) recognition/permission, buying up adjacent land, etc.; possibility is the specified administrative agency's individual judgment |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can the building on a no-rebuild property not be used either?**
A. It is not that it cannot be used. "No rebuild" is a state in which, with the site as it is, the building-confirmation for new construction after demolition (rebuilding) is not granted; the building actually standing can often be used and even leased. However, extension work that requires a building-confirmation is restricted. The judgment on whether rebuilding is possible is the field of an architect and the specified administrative agency; we organise the state of the frontage.

**Q. If the frontage is 2 metres, can it always be rebuilt?**
A. A 2-metre frontage is the minimum of the frontage-road duty (Building Standards Act Art. 43(1)), and that alone does not mean it can be rebuilt. What it touches must be a "road" under the Building Standards Act (in principle 4 m or more wide; Art. 42), and merely touching a passage that is not a road under the Act does not meet the duty. Confirmation of the road category is done by the specified administrative agency's road survey, and the final judgment is for an architect.

**Q. If there is an Article 43(2) recognition or permission, can it certainly be rebuilt?**
A. Not certainly. The item-1 recognition (consent of the building council not required) and the item-2 permission (consent of the building council required) of Article 43(2) are exceptions where the building conforms to MLIT-Order standards and the specified administrative agency recognises no hindrance. The standards and operation differ by specified administrative agency and are not settled until you check the operational standards of the target area. Confirm the prospect with an architect and an administrative scrivener.

**Q. Can a buyer living abroad buy a no-rebuild property?**
A. A non-resident can acquire Japanese real estate, but note that a loan is hard to arrange so a cash purchase tends to be central, and that the prospect of making it rebuildable is swayed by the specified administrative agency's judgment. The loan is for the financial institution, the appraisal for a real estate appraiser, the registration for a judicial scrivener, and tax for a tax accountant; Yotsuba Real Estate Co., Ltd. handles organising the state of the frontage and the brokerage, and connects you with each specialist engaged separately as an independent entity.
