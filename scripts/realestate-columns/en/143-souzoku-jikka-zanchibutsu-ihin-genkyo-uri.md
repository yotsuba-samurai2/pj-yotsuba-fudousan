## Whose are the belongings left in the inherited home, and may they be disposed of freely?

The movables left in the home—furniture, appliances, clothing, documents—are, in principle, inherited property, and where there are several heirs they belong to all of them in co-ownership (Civil Code art. 898). So one heir may not dispose of all of it without the others' consent.

Civil Code (Act No. 89 of 1896) art. 898 (1) provides that "where there are several heirs, the inherited property belongs to them in co-ownership." Not only the building and land of the home but also the belongings left inside, if they are inherited property, are the subject of this co-ownership of the estate. How to treat the belongings—including keepsake distribution and disposal—should, in order, be decided within estate division or agreement among the heirs.

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| A single heir | That heir may dispose of it at their discretion (check whether any other right-holder exists, or leased/rented items are mixed in) |
| Several heirs | The inherited property is co-owned (Civil Code art. 898); disposal under agreement among the heirs |
| A dispute over estate division or disposal | Can become negotiation, mediation or litigation—a lawyer's field; avoid unilateral disposal until agreement is reached |
| A third party's items mixed in | Valuables, items held for others, leased goods, etc. are treated separately; confirm with the owner |

Even when clearing on the premise of selling, it is practice for the heirs to agree who clears what, by when, and who bears the cost. For how to obtain consent when selling co-owned real estate, [consent when selling co-owned inherited real estate (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-kyoyu-fudosan-uru-doui) is a useful reference. Where the heirs are in conflict over ownership or division of the belongings, it can become negotiation, mediation or litigation—a lawyer's field; we do not mediate or judge it.

## Can you sell "as-is" without clearing?

You can. Even with belongings left, it is not unusual in practice to sell without clearing, by stipulating "as-is (delivered in its current state)" in the sale contract and making clear the treatment of the belongings, the delivery condition, and the scope of non-conformity liability. Where the buyer buys on the premise of renovation or demolition, the transaction can even close in the current state.

An as-is sale organises the following.

| Point to organise | Content |
|---|---|
| Treatment of belongings | Whether to remove them by delivery or deliver with them left; if left, the arrangement for waiver of ownership / delegation of disposal |
| Delivery condition | Whether there is cleaning or demolition, and in what state it is delivered |
| Non-conformity liability | State "as-is" and the scope and period of the seller's liability in the contract |
| Heirs' agreement | Whether all heirs agree on the disposal and delivery conditions of the belongings |

"As-is" does not mean "the seller bears no liability whatever the state." By defining specifically in the contract what the current state is and what the seller does not warrant, you avoid later disputes. There may be cases where the buyer uses a mortgage, or the building's condition or psychological circumstances affect the price. For disclosure of a stigmatised property / psychological defect, see [disclosure and sale of an inherited stigmatised property / psychological defect (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-jikobukken-shinriteki-kashi-kokuchi-baikyaku); for the overall flow of selling a family home, [the flow up to selling an inherited family home (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-suuji-souzoku-jikka-baikyaku-nagare). ## How much does clearing the belongings or demolition cost, and should it be done before the sale?

The cost varies greatly with the quantity and kinds of belongings (whether there are home-appliance-recycling items), the location, the size of the building, and whether demolition is needed, so no single figure can be given. It is practice to take several estimates from estate-clearing contractors and demolition contractors and compare them. Whether to clear/demolish before selling is decided by who bears the cost and what state the buyer wants.

| Choice | Point |
|---|---|
| Sell as-is with the belongings left | Keeps clearing cost down, but that may be reflected in the price or the buyer's effort; an arrangement for disposal of what is left is needed |
| Clear first, then sell | Easier to show the interior and may widen the range of buyers; needs the cost to an estate-clearing contractor and the heirs' agreement |
| Sell with the old house / demolish and sell as cleared land | Splits by the building's condition, location and buyer profile; needs a comparison of cost and time |

A comparison of demolition versus selling with the old house is covered in detail in [is an inherited vacant house demolition or sale with the old house (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-akiya-kaitai-koyatsuki-dochira). Leaving a vacant house unattended without clearing or demolition can make it a "poorly-managed vacant house" that risks becoming a "specified vacant house" (art. 2 (2)) if left—subject to guidance/recommendation by the mayor—under the Act on Special Measures concerning the Promotion of Vacant House Measures (Act No. 127 of 2014). This category came into force on 13 December 2023 under the amending act (Act No. 50 of 2023), and on receiving a recommendation, the special exception for fixed-asset tax on residential land can be lifted. Estimating cost and selecting a contractor is the field of an estate-clearing/demolition contractor; within the sale arrangement, we help compare whether to clear/demolish before selling and refer you to contractors. ## In relation to non-conformity liability, what should be written in the sale contract?

It is precisely when selling as-is that making the scope of non-conformity liability clear in the sale contract matters. On the premise of the Civil Code's non-conformity liability (arts. 562 to 566), define specifically what the seller warrants and does not warrant, and by when the buyer must give notice after learning of a non-conformity.

Under the Civil Code, where the delivered object does not conform to the contract in kind, quality or quantity, the buyer may demand cure (art. 562), demand a price reduction (art. 563), and claim damages / rescind the contract (art. 564). And for a non-conformity in kind or quality, if the buyer does not notify the seller within one year of learning of it, these claims become unavailable (art. 566 main text; except where the seller knew, or did not know through gross negligence, of the non-conformity at delivery). In an as-is sale, how to define the scope and period of liability by special clause, on the premise of these Civil Code principles, is the practical key.

| Point to write in the contract | Content |
|---|---|
| Stating "as-is" | That it is delivered in its current state and the seller takes the current state as the premise |
| Treatment of belongings | Whether to remove them or leave them; if left, the arrangement for ownership/disposal |
| Scope of non-conformity liability | The scope and period of the seller's liability (the content of a special clause on the premise of Civil Code arts. 562–566) |
| Disclosure of known defects | Explanation of circumstances the seller knows—leaks, termites, equipment faults, etc. |

How far to limit non-conformity liability, and how far a special clause is valid, varies by whether the buyer is a consumer or a business and whether there are circumstances the seller knew, and the final legal judgment is a lawyer's field. We work the as-is state, the treatment of the belongings, and the defects the seller knows into the sale contract and the important-matters explanation, and connect you to a lawyer for the parts needing judgment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. May I dispose of the belongings in the inherited home on my own decision?**
A. If you are the sole heir, you may dispose of them at your discretion (check that no leased/rented goods or items held for others are mixed in). Where there are several heirs, the inherited property—including the belongings—belongs to all heirs in co-ownership (Civil Code art. 898), so avoid disposing of all of it without the others' consent; it is practice to agree among the heirs who clears what, by when, and who bears the cost. Where there is a dispute over ownership or division, it is a lawyer's field.

**Q. Can I sell "as-is" without clearing the belongings?**
A. You can. By stipulating "as-is (delivered in its current state)" in the sale contract and making clear the treatment of the belongings, the delivery condition, and the scope of non-conformity liability, selling without clearing is not unusual in practice. Where the buyer buys on the premise of renovation or demolition, the transaction can close in the current state. "As-is" does not mean the seller bears no liability; defining specifically in the contract what is not warranted matters.

**Q. What happens to non-conformity liability if I sell as-is?**
A. Even as-is, the Civil Code's non-conformity liability (arts. 562–566) is the premise. The buyer may demand cure, demand a price reduction, claim damages and rescind for a non-conformity, and a non-conformity in kind/quality requires notice within one year of learning of it (art. 566 main text). In an as-is sale, how to define the scope and period of the seller's liability by special clause is the key. The validity of a special clause and other legal judgments are a lawyer's field; we handle reflecting it in the contract and the important-matters explanation.
