On 20 August 2026 I attended the gathering held in Shibadaimon, Tokyo, to mark the relocation of the Startup Island TAIWAN Tokyo Hub. It was a chance to hear, one after another, where Taiwanese startups get stuck when entering the Japanese market. What follows is what stayed with me, seen from the real estate side.

## Why decide the schedule before looking at property?

What stayed with me was a remark by a founder who had entered Japan earlier. Settle in for the long haul, he said — sometimes it takes a year and a half from arrival before the business actually starts moving. No one in the room sounded surprised. Several people there had lived through the same stretch of time.

Most of that year and a half is not spent looking at property. You incorporate, obtain a residence status, hire people, file the required notifications, and depending on the industry, obtain licences and permits. Each step has its own application and review period, and the lease falls somewhere in the middle of that queue.

The Business Manager residence status, for one, had its requirements raised by an amendment to the landing permission criteria that took effect on 16 October 2025. What the amendment contains, and which applications it applies to, belongs to the residence status side of the question. What matters on the real estate side is a single point: the residence status review and the lease can each become a precondition for the other. See [Housing for foreign residents — multilingual support](https://luck428.com/global).

Seen from Taiwan, where administrative procedures move quickly and digitisation is well advanced, Japan may look like a country with many steps and unpredictable timing. That time cannot be cut, but it can be shortened by getting the order right. Locking in a property first and only later discovering it does not match the requirements is the most expensive detour of all.

Depending on the industry, it can happen that a licence cannot be obtained for the office you have already signed for. That topic is covered in [Incorporation and choosing an office](https://luck428.com/office).

## Can a foreign corporation lease an office or a residence in its own name?

Sometimes yes, but the landlord, the management company and the guarantee company each run their own review, and the standards differ from property to property. For a corporation with no track record in Japan yet, the following tend to come under scrutiny.

| What gets checked | How it is handled in practice |
|---|---|
| Substance of the corporation | Certificate of registered matters, financial statements. A newly incorporated company has none, so the business plan or the amount of stated capital may be looked at instead |
| Residence status of the tenant and occupants | A residence card is usually requested. For some properties the review will not proceed before one is issued |
| Joint guarantor / guarantee company | Where no joint guarantor can be found in Japan, use of a guarantee company may be made a condition |
| Method of rent payment | Direct debit from a domestic corporate bank account may be required |
| Permitted use | Whether a unit leased as residential can be used as an office depends on the lease and the building rules |

None of these are certainties. They vary by property and by landlord, so the practical approach is to start with the properties whose conditions are easiest to clear.

## Which comes first, the office or the representative's residence?

It depends on the circumstances. The following are useful in deciding.

- Securing business premises is checked in the residence status review in some situations
- On the other hand, for some properties the residential review will not progress without a residence card
- Whether you use short-term accommodation in between, or go straight to a full lease, changes the total initial cost
- Deciding both at the same time means two sets of review documents running in parallel, which is a heavier load

Rather than the order itself, knowing in advance when each review will start is what makes the difference. The answer changes with the circumstances, so please bring us your schedule.

## How should company housing be handled when bringing staff to Japan?

When the company provides housing for its staff, the lease and the company housing rules move at the same time. Whether the property allows a corporate lease, whether consent to sublet can be obtained, whether the employee bears part of the rent. If only the property is decided first, parts of it can no longer be adjusted on the rules side. That topic is covered in [Introducing leased company housing](https://luck428.com/shataku).

## What is similar and what is different between the Taiwanese and Japanese real estate trades?

At the venue, my friend Mr. Cheng, president of Mola Holdings, Ltd., came with his staff. He introduced me to the Taiwanese guests around us, and the conversation opened up at once. I was grateful for it.

Talking with counterparts from Taiwan, I find our instincts sit in much the same place. What to be suspicious of when viewing a property, what to verify before signing, how much distance to keep with a client. The language differs, but those things come across.

The institutions, however, are a different matter. In Japan a licence is required to engage in the real estate brokerage business (Real Estate Brokerage Act, Article 3), and before a contract is concluded, a real estate broker must have a registered real estate transaction specialist explain the important matters in writing (Article 35). The explanation is given not by the company but by a qualified individual. That structure is not obvious at first to someone arriving from Taiwan.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can we sign an office lease before the company is incorporated?**
A. Because a lease cannot be signed in the name of a company that does not yet exist, one approach is to sign in the personal name of an incorporator or the person who will become the representative, and change the name after incorporation. Whether the landlord's consent is required for that change depends on the lease. We recommend confirming, before signing, whether a change of name is possible for that property.

**Q. What documents will be requested at signing?**
A. For a corporation, typically a certificate of registered matters and financial statements; for the representative and occupants, a residence card, passport, and documents evidencing employment or income. What is required differs by landlord and guarantee company. Documents issued overseas may need a Japanese translation.

**Q. Can viewings and the contract explanation be conducted in Chinese?**
A. We can guide you in Chinese. The explanation of important matters is given on the basis of Japanese-language documents, and we supplement the content in Chinese as we go so that it is understood.

**Q. Is it better to start from short-term accommodation?**
A. While waiting for a residence card to be issued, or where the place of work is not yet fixed, short-term accommodation is one option. On the other hand, it means paying initial costs twice. Comparing the expected length of stay against when each review will start is a useful basis for the decision.
