## In which zoning districts can an indoor columbarium be built?

Art. 48 and Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950) set which building uses are allowed in each zoning district. However, Appended Table 2 does not expressly list a "columbarium" use. So whether a zoning district allows it depends on how the specified administrative agency evaluates the building's actual use.

In practice, if it is treated as a facility attached to a temple (a religious facility), the zoning use restriction is said not to apply; on the other hand, for a stand-alone locker-style or automated-conveyor facility whose sole purpose is storage, some litigation has pointed out room to evaluate it as closer to a "warehouse" (a case over a locker-style columbarium in Osaka City, disputing its suitability of use in a Category I residential district). Because the judgment is on actual use rather than the name, confirm zoning feasibility with the specified administrative agency per property in advance. We do not assert this (unverified) and leave it to the specified administrative agency and an architect.

| What to check | Basis / approach | Whom to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Use category in Appended Table 2 | No express "columbarium"; evaluated on actual use | Specified administrative agency |
| Temple-attached or stand-alone | Feasibility may differ by temple/warehouse treatment (unverified) | Specified administrative agency / architect |
| Whether it is an urbanization-control area | If so, City Planning Act restrictions apply separately | Specified administrative agency |

## What is asked about the columbarium's structure and compartments in the building confirmation?

For new construction, extension, reconstruction, major repair or major remodelling, the building confirmation of Art. 6 of the Building Standards Act is required. What is asked is conformity with technical standards for structural strength, fire prevention and compartmentation, evacuation (stairs and exits), and lighting and ventilation. An automated-conveyor type carries large loads from conveyor machinery and storage racks, so the structural calculation and the handling of mechanical equipment tend to be at issue; structural and design judgments are an architect's field.

As to use, Art. 2(ii) of the Building Standards Act lists schools, hospitals, crematoria and the like as special buildings, closing with "buildings used for other similar purposes." Whether a columbarium is a special building (a use in Appended Table 1) is not express and is judged case by case. Because this directly affects whether the change-of-use confirmation below is needed, confirmation with the specified administrative agency is a precondition.

| Main points asked in the confirmation | Basis | In charge |
|---|---|---|
| Structural strength (conveyor machinery and rack loads) | Building Standards Act Art. 20, etc. | Architect |
| Fire prevention, compartmentation, evacuation | Technical standards of Art. 6 | Architect / specified administrative agency |
| Whether it is a special building | Art. 2(ii) "other similar purposes" | Specified administrative agency |

## What equipment does the Fire Service Act and ordinances require?

Art. 17 of the Fire Service Act (Act No. 186 of 1948) requires the persons concerned with a property under fire prevention to install and maintain fire-fighting equipment under technical standards set by cabinet order. Which category of property under fire prevention it falls in is decided by Appended Table 1 of the Enforcement Order of the Fire Service Act (Cabinet Order No. 37 of 1961), and the need for automatic fire alarms, guide lights, extinguishers and sprinklers varies with the category and the total floor area and number of storeys.

A columbarium is not expressly set out as its own item in Appended Table 1, so the fire department with jurisdiction judges the category from actual use. A temple-attached columbarium run by a religious corporation may be classified under item (11), which covers shrines, temples, churches and the like, but for a stand-alone building-type or automated-conveyor facility the judgment is case by case (we do not assert this category; unverified). Because the occupancy calculation and management during unstaffed hours are also at issue, settle the need with a prior consultation at the fire department with jurisdiction, together with the property's current fire-equipment drawings and inspection reports.

| What to check | Basis | Whom to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of the duty to install equipment | Fire Service Act Art. 17 | Fire department |
| Category of property under fire prevention | Enforcement Order Appended Table 1 (category judged on actual use; unverified) | Fire department |
| Current equipment / inspection reports | Fire Service Act Art. 17-3-3 (inspection and reporting) | Fire department / fire-equipment technician |

## When converting an existing building to a columbarium, what should you watch for?

When converting an existing office building or shop into a columbarium, Art. 87(1) of the Building Standards Act applies: where the use is changed to a special building under Art. 6(1)(i) of the Act (one where the total floor area used for that purpose exceeds 200 square metres), the confirmation provisions are applied mutatis mutandis. If a columbarium is evaluated as a special building (a use in Appended Table 1) and the converted part exceeds 200 square metres, a confirmation application is in principle required — but whether it so qualifies is, as noted above, a case-by-case judgment.

Also worth checking are whether the existing building has a certificate of inspection and the current state of its fire-fighting equipment. Without a certificate of inspection, proving legality on conversion and updating equipment tend to cause rework. If the category changes, the required fire equipment also changes, so a prior consultation with the fire department with jurisdiction is needed. These are the fields of the architect and the fire-equipment technician; we handle obtaining the property's current documents (confirmation and inspection certificates, equipment inspection reports, etc.) and organising the conditions.

| Points that tend to snag on conversion | Basis / where to confirm |
|---|---|
| Whether a change-of-use confirmation is needed | Building Standards Act Art. 87(1) / specified administrative agency |
| Whether there is a certificate of inspection | Existing building ledger / specified administrative agency |
| Excess or shortfall of fire equipment | Fire Service Act Art. 17 / fire department |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a columbarium be built in any zoning district?**
A. Not uniformly. Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act has no express "columbarium" category; evaluated as a temple-attached facility, no use restriction applies, while some litigation points out room to treat a stand-alone storage facility as closer to a warehouse. Because the judgment is on actual use rather than the name, confirm with the specified administrative agency per property in advance. This article does not assert it (unverified).

**Q. Will the real estate company also handle the operating licence?**
A. No. The columbarium operating licence (Art. 10(1) of the Act on Graveyards, Burial, etc.) is a permission of the prefectural governor and the like, and application support is the administrative scrivener's field. We handle property survey, brokerage and the important-matters explanation, and connect you to the administrative scrivener as an independent entity engaged separately. No referral fees are exchanged.

**Q. How is an automated-conveyor (apartment-type) columbarium different for construction?**
A. The conveyor machinery and storage racks carry large loads, so structural calculation and the handling of mechanical equipment tend to be at issue; structural and equipment design is an architect's field. Whether a confirmation is needed for new construction, extension or change of use depends on the post-change use category and the area, so confirm with the specified administrative agency and an architect.

**Q. When converting an existing building to a columbarium, what should I look at first?**
A. Three points: whether there is a certificate of inspection, the current state of fire equipment, and whether a change-of-use confirmation is needed. Without a certificate of inspection, proving legality and updating equipment tend to cause rework, and if the category changes the required fire equipment also changes. A prior consultation with the fire department, the specified administrative agency and an architect is the sure route.
