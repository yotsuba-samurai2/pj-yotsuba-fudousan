**The key point**: From 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7), in principle every new building must conform to the energy-saving standard. The basis is the Act on the Improvement of Energy Consumption Performance of Buildings (Building Energy Efficiency Act = Act No. 53 of 2015) and the part of its 2022 amendment (Act No. 69 of 2022) enforced on 1 April 2025. Until now the conformity obligation was limited to medium/large non-residential buildings (roughly 300 m² or more of non-residential floor area), while homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² only had the architect's "duty to explain" to the owner. From this change, in principle every new build — including rental apartments and small tenant buildings — cannot obtain building confirmation, and therefore cannot start construction, unless it conforms to the energy-saving standard. For an owner, the insulation and equipment spec is raised at the planning stage, which changes construction cost, the schedule, and how future asset value is seen. ## Why, from April 2025, is conformity with the energy-saving standard required of "every new build"?

The basis is the Building Energy Efficiency Act and its 2022 amendment. The Act (Act No. 53 of 2015) already had a mechanism requiring conformity for buildings above a certain scale. The amending law is Act No. 69 of 2022 (the Act partially amending the Act on the Improvement of Energy Consumption Performance of Buildings etc. to contribute to a decarbonised society, promulgated 17 June 2022). The part of that amendment widening the conformity obligation to, in principle, all new buildings was enforced on 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7).

Previously, the "conformity obligation" applied to medium/large non-residential buildings (roughly 300 m² or more of non-residential floor area). Homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² were only subject to the architect's "duty to explain" the energy performance to the owner, and could be built even if they did not meet the standard. From April 2025 this distinction disappears, and in principle every new build is subject to the conformity obligation.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Basis law | Building Energy Efficiency Act (Act No. 53 of 2015) |
| Amending law | Act No. 69 of 2022 (promulgated 17 June 2022) |
| Enforcement | 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7) (the widening of the conformity obligation) |
| Former conformity obligation | Medium/large non-residential (roughly 300 m² or more of non-residential) |
| From April 2025 | In principle every new building (residential/non-residential, regardless of scale) |

Behind this is the national policy of cutting energy use in the housing and building sector toward carbon neutrality by 2050. The level of the standard itself is expected to be raised in stages, and MLIT has also referred to a future rise toward ZEH/ZEB levels. The specific figures and timing are amended, so confirm the latest guidance at the planning stage.

## Are rental apartments and small tenant buildings under 300 m² also covered now?

Yes. This is the point of the April 2025 mandate that most affects owners. Homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² — that is, many rental apartments and small tenant buildings — which previously only had the "duty to explain" are now subject to the "conformity obligation." A design that does not conform to the energy-saving standard will not receive building confirmation, so construction cannot begin.

That said, the weight of the procedure differs by scale. For small buildings (roughly under 300 m² of floor area), a simplified treatment is available that omits the energy-conformity assessment by the competent authority or a registered assessment body and instead confirms conformity to the energy-saving standard within the building-confirmation review. Using the national "spec standard" (treating the standard as met if insulation, windows and equipment are set to prescribed specs), conformity can in some cases be shown without an individual energy calculation. This is designed to curb the procedural burden on small projects to a degree.

| Scale guide | Energy-conformity assessment | Where reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| Medium/large (roughly 300 m² or more) | In principle required | Undergo the assessment; submit the conformity notice at building confirmation |
| Small (roughly under 300 m²) | In principle not required (when using the spec standard etc.) | Energy conformity confirmed within the building-confirmation review |

There are also categories exempted or eased — very small buildings (new builds/extensions of 10 m² or less) and buildings with no energy-using equipment, and the like. Which treatment your plan falls under depends on scale, use and equipment, so confirming with the architect you commission the design to is the surest course.

## If the energy-conformity assessment does not pass at building confirmation, can construction not start? (the flow of review)

The effectiveness of the conformity obligation is secured by tying it to the building-confirmation procedure. For medium/large buildings, you first undergo the energy-conformity assessment by the competent authority or a registered building-energy-consumption-performance assessment body, and submit its "conformity assessment notice" within the building-confirmation procedure. If a building does not conform, the notice does not issue; without the notice, a building-confirmation certificate is not granted; without the certificate, construction cannot begin and the completion inspection cannot pass. In short, non-conformity with the energy-saving standard directly means the building cannot be built.

The rough flow is as follows. Mixing up the order and who does what can be a cause of stalled construction.

| Stage | Who mainly handles it | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Energy calculation / design | Architect | Decide the insulation and equipment spec; show conformity by calculation |
| Energy-conformity assessment | Competent authority / registered assessment body | Judge whether the design conforms and issue the conformity notice (medium/large) |
| Building confirmation | Designated confirmation body / specified administrative agency | Confirm conformity with the Building Standards Act etc. plus energy conformity (for small buildings, the energy review is here); issue the confirmation certificate |
| Construction / completion inspection | Builder / designated confirmation body / specified administrative agency | Build per the design; pass the completion inspection and receive the inspection certificate |

We do not perform the energy calculation, the conformity assessment or building confirmation themselves. Whether the assessment or confirmation issues is judged by the architect, the assessment body and the competent authority.

## Does the conformity obligation also apply to extending or substantially renovating an existing building? What about non-conforming existing buildings?

Extensions and alterations are, in principle, included in the conformity obligation from April 2025. However, to what scope and by which standard the obligation applies varies with the scale of the extension and the treatment of the existing part, and this is quite case-specific. It is not always required that the entire existing part be brought into line with the current energy-saving standard; in some cases the judgment centres on the extended part.

How far a so-called "non-conforming existing building" (legal when built, but no longer meeting the current standard after later amendment) is required to conform at the time of extension or alteration is a point that bears on the Building Standards Act as a whole, not only the energy-saving standard. Explanations here differ by material and depend in part on the municipality's operation, so this article keeps to the level of "extensions/alterations are in principle covered, but the scope of required conformity is a case-by-case judgment." We leave the specific scope of application unverified, and ask that you confirm the planned extension/alteration with an architect and the competent authority.

| Situation | Treatment of the energy-conformity obligation (general) |
|---|---|
| New build | In principle all covered |
| Extension / alteration | In principle covered. The scope of required conformity varies with scale and treatment of the existing part (case-by-case) |
| Treatment of the existing part | Non-conforming scope and whether current-standard conformity is required is a Building Standards Act-wide point; depends on the municipality's operation |

## How does the energy-saving mandate affect construction cost, rent and asset value?

Here we keep to general information. Being required to conform to the energy-saving standard raises the spec of insulation, sashes and equipment at the planning stage, so for a building of the same scale the initial cost (construction cost) tends to rise versus before. On the other hand, higher insulation performance tends to lower tenants' utility bills, and together with the energy-performance label display that began in April 2024, a property that can show "energy performance" at listing can be more likely to be chosen in a comparison within the same area and rent band.

On asset value, the more "conforming" becomes the default, the more the gap with non-conforming existing stock is noticed. However, how much higher energy performance pushes up rent or sale price varies greatly by location, supply-demand and property type, and cannot be stated uniformly. Rather than asserting a numeric effect, the realistic approach is to match the construction-cost estimate against the future income outlook early in planning, together with an architect, a builder and (as needed) a tax accountant.

| Aspect | General direction | Whom to consult |
|---|---|---|
| Construction cost (initial) | Insulation/equipment spec rises; tends to push up | Architect / builder |
| Tenants' utility bills | Tend to fall with higher insulation (a listing selling point) | Real estate (us) / architect |
| Asset value / sale | As conformity becomes the premise, the gap with non-conforming stock is noticed (the effect depends on location/supply-demand) | Real estate (us) |
| Tax (energy-saving renovation etc.) | Some schemes give preferential treatment; applicability is case-by-case | Tax accountant |

The display of the energy-performance label at listing/sale is a separate point, organised in [what the energy-performance labelling scheme from April 2024 changed for property sale/rental ads](https://luck428.com/en/column/kenchiku-shoene-ho-shoene-seino-hyoji-koukoku-2024).
