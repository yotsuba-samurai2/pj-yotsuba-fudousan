## Why, from April 2025, is conformity with the energy-saving standard required of "every new build"?

The basis is the Building Energy Efficiency Act and its 2022 amendment. The Act (Act No. 53 of 2015) already had a mechanism requiring conformity for buildings above a certain scale. The amending law is Act No. 69 of 2022 (the Act partially amending the Act on the Improvement of Energy Consumption Performance of Buildings etc. to contribute to a decarbonised society, promulgated 17 June 2022). The part of that amendment widening the conformity obligation to, in principle, all new buildings was enforced on 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7).

Previously, the "conformity obligation" applied to medium/large non-residential buildings (roughly 300 m² or more of non-residential floor area). Homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² were only subject to the architect's "duty to explain" the energy performance to the owner, and could be built even if they did not meet the standard. From April 2025 this distinction disappears, and in principle every new build is subject to the conformity obligation.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Basis law | Building Energy Efficiency Act (Act No. 53 of 2015) |
| Amending law | Act No. 69 of 2022 (promulgated 17 June 2022) |
| Enforcement | 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7) (the widening of the conformity obligation) |
| Former conformity obligation | Medium/large non-residential (roughly 300 m² or more of non-residential) |
| From April 2025 | In principle every new building (residential/non-residential, regardless of scale) |

Behind this is the national policy of cutting energy use in the housing and building sector toward carbon neutrality by 2050. The level of the standard itself is expected to be raised in stages, and MLIT has also referred to a future rise toward ZEH/ZEB levels. The specific figures and timing are amended, so confirm the latest guidance at the planning stage.

## Are rental apartments and small tenant buildings under 300 m² also covered now?

Homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² are also generally subject to the energy-performance obligation for construction started on or after 1 April 2025. **Being under 300 m² does not itself exempt a project from the separate energy assessment.** For homes assessed against the specification standards for both envelope and primary-energy performance, and other eligible cases, the separate assessment may be omitted and conformity checked in building confirmation. A simplified calculation for a non-residential building is not the same as an assessment exemption.

| Project / method | Basic procedure |
|---|---|
| Home meeting the conditions for specification-based assessment exemption | Energy conformity reviewed in building confirmation |
| Other residential or non-residential project | Check the assessment requirement; size alone is not an exemption |
| Building outside confirmation or eligible for review omission | Distinguish the substantive obligation from assessment/review procedures |

Exclusions include construction of 10 m² or less and designated uses or temporary buildings. Merely omitting electrical equipment does not establish an exemption. Ask the architect and competent authority to classify the project.

## If the energy-conformity assessment does not pass at building confirmation, can construction not start? (the flow of review)

The substantive energy-performance obligation is separate from the assessment and building-confirmation procedures. Where energy review is required in confirmation, conformity must be established before the confirmation certificate can issue. A project requiring the separate assessment submits the conformity notice; an eligible specification-based project is reviewed within confirmation instead. Exemption from building confirmation does not necessarily remove the energy-performance obligation.

| Stage | Responsible party | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Design / procedure classification | Architect | Determine requirements from use, scale and assessment method |
| Energy assessment, where required | Competent authority / registered assessment body | Issue the conformity notice |
| Confirmation / completion inspection, where required | Building official / designated inspection body | Perform the required energy review and inspection |

## Does the conformity obligation also apply to extending or substantially renovating an existing building? What about non-conforming existing buildings?

For extensions and reconstruction started on or after 1 April 2025, the energy obligation generally applies **only to the extended or reconstructed part**, for both homes and non-residential buildings, subject to exclusions such as work of 10 m² or less. It does not automatically require an energy retrofit of the entire existing building.

| Work | Basic energy obligation |
|---|---|
| New building | Generally covered; check exclusions |
| Extension / reconstruction | Generally the extended or reconstructed part only |
| Repair / remodelling, including major work | Not in itself covered by this obligation |

This is separate from structural/fire-safety rules for existing non-conforming buildings under the Building Standards Act. Ask the architect and competent authority to classify the work and confirm assessment boundaries and any separate certification requirements.

## How does the energy-saving mandate affect construction cost, rent and asset value?

Here we keep to general information. Being required to conform to the energy-saving standard raises the spec of insulation, sashes and equipment at the planning stage, so for a building of the same scale the initial cost (construction cost) tends to rise versus before. On the other hand, higher insulation performance tends to lower tenants' utility bills, and together with the energy-performance label display that began in April 2024, a property that can show "energy performance" at listing can be more likely to be chosen in a comparison within the same area and rent band.

On asset value, the more "conforming" becomes the default, the more the gap with non-conforming existing stock is noticed. However, how much higher energy performance pushes up rent or sale price varies greatly by location, supply-demand and property type, and cannot be stated uniformly. Rather than asserting a numeric effect, the realistic approach is to match the construction-cost estimate against the future income outlook early in planning, together with an architect, a builder and (as needed) a tax accountant.

| Aspect | General direction | Whom to consult |
|---|---|---|
| Construction cost (initial) | Insulation/equipment spec rises; tends to push up | Architect / builder |
| Tenants' utility bills | Tend to fall with higher insulation (a listing selling point) | Real estate (us) / architect |
| Asset value / sale | As conformity becomes the premise, the gap with non-conforming stock is noticed (the effect depends on location/supply-demand) | Real estate (us) |
| Tax (energy-saving renovation etc.) | Some schemes give preferential treatment; applicability is case-by-case | Tax accountant |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. From April 2025, can a rental apartment not be built unless it conforms to the energy-saving standard?**
A. In principle, that is so. By the Building Energy Efficiency Act amendment enforced on 1 April 2025 (Reiwa 7) (Act No. 69 of 2022), in principle every new building must conform to the energy-saving standard, and rental apartments and small tenant buildings are covered. Previously, homes and non-residential buildings under 300 m² only had the architect's "duty to explain" and could be built even if they did not meet the standard; now, without conforming, a building cannot obtain building confirmation and cannot start construction. Whether it conforms is judged by the architect, the assessment body and the competent authority.

**Q. Even for a small property, must an energy-conformity assessment always be obtained?**
A. Not on size alone. Homes assessed against specification standards for both envelope and primary-energy performance may qualify for omission, but non-residential buildings under 300 m² are not generally exempt just because of their size. Ask the architect to check confirmation/review exemptions and the applicable procedure.

**Q. Does the conformity obligation also apply when extending an existing building?**
A. For work started on or after 1 April 2025, extensions and reconstruction generally require conformity only for the affected part, subject to exclusions such as work of 10 m² or less. Repair and remodelling are not in themselves covered. Distinguish this from existing-nonconformity rules under the Building Standards Act and consult the architect/authority.

**Q. Will the energy-saving mandate raise construction cost? Can it be passed on to rent?**
A. Because insulation, sashes and equipment specs are raised at the planning stage, for the same scale the construction cost tends to rise versus before. On the other hand, higher insulation tends to lower tenants' utility bills and, together with the energy-performance label, becomes a listing selling point. However, how much higher energy performance pushes up rent or sale price varies greatly by location, supply-demand and property type and cannot be stated uniformly. Rather than asserting a numeric effect, the realistic approach is to match the construction-cost estimate against the future income outlook with an architect, a builder and a tax accountant.
