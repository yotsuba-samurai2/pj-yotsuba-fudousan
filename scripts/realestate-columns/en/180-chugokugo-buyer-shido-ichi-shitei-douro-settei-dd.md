## What is the "frontage duty," and what can you not buy if it is unmet?

The frontage duty means that a building's site must touch a "road" under the Building Standards Act for 2 m or more (Art. 43(1)). Land that does not meet this cannot in principle obtain building confirmation, so no new build or rebuild is possible (so-called non-rebuildable). Land you cannot rebuild is hard to mortgage and hard to sell on at a good price.

The "road" here means one of width 4 m or more (6 m in areas the specified administrative agency designates) that falls under the items of Art. 42(1) (roads under the Road Act, roads under the City Planning Act, a position-designated road, and so on) or the "deemed road" of Art. 42(2) (a so-called Article-42(2) road). A site touching only a "passage," "farm road," or the like that looks like a path but is not a road under the Act does not satisfy the frontage duty.

There are exceptions. A building that the specified administrative agency, after obtaining the Building Examination Commission's consent, permits as having no obstacle to traffic, safety, fire prevention or sanitation because its site has ample open space around it (Art. 43(2)(ii)), or one meeting the ministry's standards that the agency approves (item (i)), may exceptionally be allowed to build. But this approval or permission is the agency's judgment and is not guaranteed after purchase. For how to see rebuildability and the exceptions, see [does non-rebuildable change with an Article-43(2) approval or permission](https://luck428.com/en/column/saikenchiku-fuka-43jo-2ko-nintei-kyoka-dare-ga).

| What to check | Basis | If unmet |
|---|---|---|
| The site touches a road for 2 m or more | Art. 43(1) | In principle no building confirmation; non-rebuildable |
| The frontage is a "road" under the Act | Art. 42(1), (2) | A passage or farm road alone is not frontage |
| The road is 4 m or more wide | Art. 42(1) | An Article-42(2) road needs a set-back |
| An approval/permission exception is obtainable | Art. 43(2) | The agency's judgment; confirm in advance |

## For a position-designated road, what do you look at and where to confirm it?

A position-designated road is a road, built to standards set by Cabinet Order to use land as a building site, whose position the builder has had the specified administrative agency designate (Art. 42(1)(v)). Private roads running through a subdivision are often position-designated roads. If designated, it counts as an Act road usable for frontage, but the designated extent and width cannot be known from the on-site appearance alone.

The starting point is the specified administrative agency (such as a ward or city building-guidance section). The agency prepares and preserves a "designated-road map" and a "designated-road record" for designated roads, and the designated-road map shows at least the road's type and position on a plan at a scale of 1:2,500 or larger (Enforcement Regulation of the Building Standards Act, Art. 10-2). You can view these and inquire about the road type (which article's road it is) at the agency's counter. The viewing method and fees differ by municipality, so confirm the guidance for the property's agency in writing (the inquiry destination and fees differ by municipality; unverified).

An Article-42(2) road (deemed road) is a road under 4 m wide, along which buildings already stood when the Act's provisions came to apply, that the agency has designated (Art. 42(2)). In that case, a line 2 m horizontally from the road's centre line is deemed the road boundary, requiring a "set-back" of the site at rebuild. The set-back portion cannot be counted in the site area, so it is also disadvantageous for the building-coverage and floor-area ratios. For set-back practice, see [the set-back of an Article-42(2) road and site area](https://luck428.com/en/column/setback-42jo-2ko-doro-tochi-baikyaku-menseki).

| Road type | Provision | Effect on the buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Position-designated road | Art. 42(1)(v) | Confirm designated extent, width, private-road share on the map |
| Article-42(2) road (deemed road) | Art. 42(2) | A set-back is needed; usable site shrinks |
| A passage not a road under the Act | (none) | Fails the frontage duty; risk of non-rebuildable |

## Without a private-road share and digging consent, what happens to rebuilding and water/sewer connection?

When you buy a site touching a private road, if that road is co-owned and the land you buy has no share of the private road, or you lack the other co-owners' digging consent, work to connect water, sewer or gas, or to repair paving, may stall. Digging under the road in principle needs the involvement of the private road's co-owners.

The Civil Code governs co-owned things. An act involving a significant change in shape or utility needs all co-owners' consent (Art. 251(1)); management not involving a significant change in shape or utility is decided by a majority of the shares' value (Art. 252(1)); and each co-owner may perform acts of preservation alone (Art. 252(5)). The Ministry of Justice's "Guideline on responding to owner-unknown private roads" treats much work such as digging to connect lifelines as an act of management (majority of shares), and obtaining this digging consent before sale is the customary practice (the Guideline's positioning and access date are to be confirmed per property; unverified). For practice on private-road shares and digging consent, see [confirming private-road share and digging consent before sale](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-kyoyu-shidou-kussaku-shodaku-infra-hikikomi-baikyaku), and for how to sell land missing only the share, [selling land without a private-road share](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-shido-mochibun-ichitei-doro-baikyaku).

A "roadless lot" not touching a private road, or a "flag-pole lot" connected by a narrow passage, makes the frontage check heavier still; see [the flow of selling a flag-pole or roadless lot](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-hatazao-mudouro-tochi-baikyaku-nagare). Where a co-owner is of unknown whereabouts, the 2021-amended Civil Code (in force 1 April 2023) provides a court decision to make changes or management excluding the unknown co-owner (Arts. 251(2), 252(2)), but these petitions are a lawyer's area.

| Type of act | Requirement | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Paving repair, etc. (preservation) | Each co-owner alone | Art. 252(5) |
| Digging to connect lifelines, etc. (management) | Majority of the shares' value | Art. 252(1) |
| An act with a significant change | All co-owners' consent | Art. 251(1) |

## In the important-matters explanation, what should the buyer check in the "private-road burden" column?

A real estate broker, before the sale contract is concluded, has a real estate transaction specialist explain the important matters in writing (Real Estate Brokerage Act, Art. 35). As a buyer, focus on the following three points about frontage and the private road. Do not stop at hearing the explanation; cross-check it against the designated-road map and the water/sewer connection drawings.

First, the outline of restrictions under the City Planning Act, Building Standards Act and other laws (Art. 35(1)(ii)); restrictions on the frontage duty and road type appear here. Second, matters on the private-road burden (item (iii); excluding a building lease); the presence of a share, the burdened area, and the status of passage and digging consent are explained. Third, the status of facilities for water, electricity, gas supply and drainage (item (iv)); if unready, the prospect of readiness and any special burden are explained. Digging consent for a private road may relate to this "special burden" under item (iv).

| Column in the explanation | Basis | What the buyer watches |
|---|---|---|
| Outline of restrictions under laws | Art. 35(1)(ii) | Frontage duty, road type, presence of a set-back |
| Private-road burden | Art. 35(1)(iii) | Presence of a share, burdened area, passage/digging consent |
| Status of water, electricity, gas, drainage | Art. 35(1)(iv) | Whether connection is possible, digging consent, special burden |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I buy a Japanese house even if the frontage is a private road?**
A. Yes. Even a private road, if it is a road under the Building Standards Act (position-designated road, Article-42(2) road, etc.) and meets the frontage duty (touching for 2 m or more), allows building. But without a private-road share or digging consent, rebuilding and water/sewer connection need the other co-owners' involvement. Confirm the road type and private-road burden before buying.

**Q. Can I tell whether it is a position-designated road just by looking on site?**
A. No. A position-designated road is a road whose position the specified administrative agency has designated (Art. 42(1)(v)), and its extent and width are confirmed from the designated-road map the agency preserves (Enforcement Regulation, Art. 10-2). Inquiring about the road type at the ward or city building-guidance counter is the sure way.

**Q. What happens after I buy if it is an Article-42(2) road?**
A. A site touching an Article-42(2) road (a deemed road under 4 m wide) needs a set-back at rebuild, pushing the site back to the line 2 m from the road's centre line (Art. 42(2)). The set-back portion cannot be counted in the site area and is disadvantageous for the building-coverage and floor-area ratios. Estimate in advance how much usable site is lost.

**Q. Will the important-matters explanation always reveal the digging consent?**
A. It is covered under the private-road burden (Art. 35(1)(iii)) and the status of drainage and other facilities (item (iv)), but the existence and content of a consent letter are not always exhaustively covered. Cross-check the water/sewer connection drawings with the private road's co-ownership, and confirm case by case whether the digging consent letter can be carried over.
