## How does a food-manufacturing property differ from a "restaurant" property?

Even within "food," a restaurant that cooks and serves customers and a food-manufacturing business that manufactures and wholesales to other stores or retailers face different property premises. The dividing lines are the type of business under the Food Sanitation Act and the building use under the Building Standards Act.

Under the Food Sanitation Act, the licensing system was revised on 1 June 2021 and the licensed trades were reorganised into 32. Against a restaurant business that cooks and serves on site, "prepared-food (sōzai) manufacturing" that makes and wholesales prepared dishes and "confectionery manufacturing" that makes sweets and bread are classified separately as manufacturing trades. Which trade applies changes the idea of what facilities and equipment are required.

Under the Building Standards Act, a facility that manufactures food is treated as a "factory." Whereas a restaurant, as a store, is permitted in broad zones, a factory carries a heavier zoning restriction (art. 48 and Appended Table 2), with its viability decided by work-area floor size and motor output. In other words, a location that was fine as a restaurant can suddenly get caught on zoning once it becomes manufacturing.

| Item | Restaurant (on-site cooking/serving) | Food manufacturing (manufacturing/wholesale) |
|---|---|---|
| Food Sanitation Act category | Restaurant business | Prepared-food / confectionery manufacturing, etc. (by trade) |
| Building Standards Act use | Store | Factory |
| Zoning focus | Allowed in broad zones | Mainly industrial zones; limited by work-area size |
| Drainage focus | Grease trap, etc. | Scale/product can trigger a specified-facility notification |

## In which zones can you place a food-manufacturing factory?

Because food manufacturing is treated as a "factory," zoning restrictions bite first. Article 48 and Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act divide, by zone, the scale of factory that can be built, using work-area floor area and motor output. Generally, quasi-industrial, industrial and exclusive-industrial zones allow factories broadly; neighbourhood-commercial and commercial zones restrict by work-area floor area (and motor output up to a set scale); and residential zones limit factories to small ones with little danger or environmental impact.

| Zone (example) | Rough guide to siting a factory |
|---|---|
| Exclusive-industrial / industrial / quasi-industrial | Food factories broadly allowed (quasi-industrial limits more hazardous/high-impact ones) |
| Neighbourhood-commercial / commercial | Limited by work-area floor area / motor output |
| Residential | Limited to small ones with little danger/environmental impact |
| Urbanisation-control area | In principle cannot be built (individual check of development permit / siting criteria) |

Note that even the same "food manufacturing" changes viability by work-area floor area, the machinery used (motor output), and the degree of odour, drainage and noise generated. The numeric lines (up to how many m², up to what output) are set by the Appended Table 2 categories and local practice, so check with the specified administrative agency / urban-planning desk of the property's location (no unverified numbers are written here). A property in an urbanisation-control area, however industrial it may look, in principle cannot be built on and needs a development permit or an individual siting judgment. We check the property-side points — zoning, the front road, the building's condition — and organise the flow of confirming with the specified administrative agency / an architect whether it can be placed as a factory.

## What is required for drainage, grease traps and the specified-facility notification?

Drainage is easily overlooked in a food-manufacturing factory. Beyond whether a restaurant has a grease trap (oil separator), depending on scale and product the facility may fall under a "specified facility" of the Water Pollution Control Act and require a notification of installation.

Article 5 of the Water Pollution Control Act requires anyone intending to install a specified facility to notify the prefectural governor (or the mayor in designated cities) in principle 60 days before starting work. Which facilities are specified facilities is listed in Appended Table 1 of the Act's Enforcement Order, and it includes facilities related to the food-products manufacturing industry (marine-food products, noodle manufacturing, tofu and boiled-bean manufacturing, and the like). When your factory conducts such manufacturing, the procedure branches by whether wastewater goes to public water areas or to public sewers; connecting to public sewers may require a filing under the Sewerage Act or a pretreatment (de-harming) facility to keep the wastewater quality within standards (treating oil, BOD, pH, etc.).

| Drainage point | Content | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Grease trap | Presence/capacity of an oil-separating device | Building equipment drawings / management company |
| Specified-facility notification | Water Pollution Control Act art. 5; 60 days ahead in principle | Prefecture / designated city (environment division) |
| Discharge to public sewers | Sewerage Act filing, water-quality standards, pretreatment facility | Sewerage administrator (municipal sewerage bureau) |
| Discharge destination | Public water area or public sewer; combined or separated | Municipality / management company |

Drainage changes the burden of extra work greatly depending on whether the property is in a public-sewer area, combined or separated, and whether the existing piping capacity suffices. Whether it is a specified facility, which water-quality standard applies, and whether a pretreatment facility is needed differ by product and manufacturing method, so check with the environment division / sewerage administrator — not the public-health centre — noting the reference date. We do not conclude the specified-facility applicability or discharge standards; we check the property-side points of discharge destination and piping condition, and provide, as general information, the traffic-control of passing the notification and pretreatment design to an administrative scrivener / equipment designer.

## How do you check the public-health-centre facility standards (hand-washing, zoning of rooms, pest control) before a viewing?

The facility standards for a licence are set, under Article 54 of the Food Sanitation Act, by each prefecture by ordinance for each trade. In the 1 June 2021 reform, facility standards were reorganised to be set by ordinance in light of the MHLW ordinance's "standards to be referred to," and details differ by locality. Together with that, HACCP-based hygiene management (preparing and recording a hygiene-management plan) under Article 51 of the Act is required of, in principle, all food-business operators.

What you want to check in a property is the zoning of rooms (whether the flow of raw materials, manufacturing, packaging and storage crosses), the position and number of hand-washing facilities, whether the floor, walls and ceiling are easy to clean and configured with pest/rodent control in mind, whether the changing room and toilet are properly separated from the work area, and the condition of water supply/drainage and ventilation. These tie directly to the ordinance facility standards and can be roughly assessed at the viewing stage from the layout and equipment drawings.

| Check item | Content | Confirm by ordinance / practice |
|---|---|---|
| Room zoning / flow | Layout where raw materials, manufacturing, packaging, storage do not cross | Public-health centre (trade-specific facility standards) |
| Hand-washing | Position, number and tap type of hand-washing in the work area | Public-health centre |
| Floor/walls/ceiling | Easy-to-clean structure with pest/rodent control | Public-health centre / architect |
| Changing/toilet/ventilation | Separation from the work area; water supply/drainage, ventilation | Public-health centre / architect |

Facility standards differ by trade under the ordinance, and specific numbers and structural requirements are supplemented by the locality's ordinance and interpretation. The practical order is to hold a prior consultation with the public-health centre before applying and to read the facility standards against the drawings. We organise the property-side conditions bearing on the facility standards from the layout and equipment drawings; the judgment of conformity with the facility standards itself is confirmed with the public-health centre / administrative scrivener / architect as general information. We do not conclude it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I have a restaurant-business licence, can I manufacture prepared food and wholesale it?**
A. Generally, a restaurant-business licence contemplates cooking and serving customers on site, and manufacturing to wholesale to other stores or retailers may require a separate licence such as prepared-food manufacturing. In the Food Sanitation Act's 1 June 2021 reform, licences were reorganised into 32 trades, and manufacturing trades are classified separately. Which trade's licence is needed, and what happens when on-site serving and manufacturing-wholesale are done together, change by product and business form, so confirm after prior consultation with the public-health centre of the property's location. We do not conclude the need for a licence; we organise the property-side checks and the referral to an administrative scrivener.

**Q. In which zones can a food-manufacturing factory be placed?**
A. Food manufacturing is treated as a "factory" under the Building Standards Act, so the zoning restriction (art. 48 and Appended Table 2) applies. Generally it can be placed broadly in quasi-industrial, industrial and exclusive-industrial zones; it is limited by work-area floor area and motor output in neighbourhood-commercial and commercial zones; and limited to small ones in residential zones. Urbanisation-control areas in principle cannot be built on. The specific lines for floor area and output are set by the Appended Table 2 categories and local practice, so confirm with the specified administrative agency / urban-planning desk of the property's location. We check the property-side points of zoning and building condition and organise the flow of confirming viability with the specified administrative agency / an architect.

**Q. Is the drainage notification (specified facility) always required?**
A. Not necessarily. Article 5 of the Water Pollution Control Act requires a notification, in principle 60 days before starting work, when installing a specified facility listed in Appended Table 1 of its Enforcement Order. Because facilities related to the food-products manufacturing industry are among the specified facilities, applicability changes with the product and scale of manufacturing. Connecting to public sewers separately involves Sewerage Act filings and a pretreatment facility. Confirm applicability and water-quality standards with the environment division / sewerage administrator — not the public-health centre — noting the reference date. We check the property-side points of discharge destination and piping, and connect the notification to an administrative scrivener / equipment designer.
