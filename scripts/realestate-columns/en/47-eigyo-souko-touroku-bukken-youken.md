**In short:** whether a property can be a registered (commercial) warehouse first depends on whether you run a warehousing business — storing goods entrusted by others — or merely lease warehouse space as real estate. A warehousing business needs registration by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Warehousing Business Act, Art. 3), and it is a condition of registration that the facilities and equipment meet the standards for the type of warehouse (Art. 6(1)(iv), and the Enforcement Regulations). What is examined includes title to use and conformity with related laws, plus water-proofing, damp-proofing, heat insulation, fire resistance, fire compartments, security and rodent prevention. The conditions change with the type you aim for (Class 1 through refrigerated).

"I want to turn an unused building into a rental warehouse" is a question we hear more and more. But hanging up a "warehouse" sign does not make it a commercial warehouse. A registered warehouse is a scheme under the Warehousing Business Act, and it is registered only when the building meets the standards for its type. This article is for operators wanting to start a storage business and for owners who want to lease to warehouse users: what you can check before signing, set out in order from the Warehousing Business Act and its Enforcement Regulations and the guidance of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT). Final confirmation of figures and feasibility rests with the registering window — the District Transport Bureau.

## How does a "commercial warehouse" differ from an ordinary rental warehouse?

The dividing line is "whether it is a business of storing goods entrusted by others."

The Warehousing Business Act (Act No. 121 of 1956) treats the business of storing entrusted goods in a warehouse as a "warehousing business," and requires anyone who intends to run it to obtain registration by the Minister (Art. 3). What matters is "receiving goods on deposit and storing them." If you take goods from a shipper, bear responsibility for their storage and receive consideration, that is a warehousing business and needs registration.

By contrast, if you simply lease the space of a building as real estate and the tenant stores their own goods under their own management, that is a real-estate lease, not a warehousing business. Storing your own goods yourself (an in-house warehouse) is likewise not a warehousing business.

| Case | Warehouse registration | Main handler |
|---|---|---|
| Storing a shipper's goods on deposit for consideration | Required (Art. 3) | Warehousing operator |
| Leasing a warehouse building; tenant stores own goods | Not required (real-estate lease) | Licensed real estate agent (us) |
| Storing your own goods yourself (in-house warehouse) | Not required | — |

**"Renting a warehouse to use yourself" and "running a commercial warehouse that stores others' goods" are legally quite different.** Which you aim for changes both the property conditions and the procedures. Pinning down which one you are is the starting point; if in doubt, confirm with the District Transport Bureau, the registering window.

## Which parts of the building are examined for a warehouse registration?

Whether the facilities and equipment meet the MLIT-ordinance standards for the type of warehouse.

Article 6(1)(iv) of the Act requires registration to be refused where the facilities or equipment do not meet the standards fixed by MLIT ordinance according to the type of warehouse. Those standards are in the Enforcement Regulations (Ordinance No. 59 of 1956), and as standards common to all warehouses (Art. 3-3) they require title to use the warehouse and conformity with related laws such as the Building Standards Act and the City Planning Act.

The standards for a Class 1 warehouse — the most basic — (Art. 3-4) roughly comprise the following.

| Point examined | Idea of the standard |
|---|---|
| Title to use / conformity with related laws | Ownership or lease title; conformity with the Building Standards Act, City Planning Act, etc. (use-zone, use-change, inspection-certificate issues) |
| Frame, external wall, floor strength | Structure that withstands the stored goods and handling |
| Water-proofing | Prevents rain entering via roof, walls, openings |
| Damp-proofing | Prevents moisture from the floor |
| Heat insulation | Insulation of roof and external walls |
| Fire resistance / fire prevention | Fire-resistant/quasi-fire-resistant structure, fire compartments |
| Fire extinguishers, etc. | Fire extinguishers and other firefighting equipment |
| Security | Lighting, locks and other theft prevention |
| Rodent prevention | Measures against entry of rats, etc. |

**Conformity with the Building Standards Act and City Planning Act sits at the entrance of these standards.** Whether the use zone allows a warehouse, whether an existing building needs a use-change confirmation, and whether an inspection certificate exists all bite at the property-selection stage. The points when converting a used building without an inspection certificate to another use are set out at [can a used building without an inspection certificate be used for another purpose](https://luck428.com/column/kensazumisho-nashi-bukken-fukushi-youto-henko). Conformity itself is judged by an architect and the specified administrative authority.

## How do the property conditions change by type, from Class 1 to refrigerated?

The article of the facility standard changes with the type of warehouse.

Article 3 of the Enforcement Regulations divides warehouses into Class 1, 2, 3, open-yard, water-surface, storage-tank (silo), dangerous-goods, refrigerated and so on. The type is set by what you store, and each type has its own standard.

| Type | Example goods stored | Standard article | Feature of the property condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class 1 | General cargo (a wide range) | Reg. Art. 3-4 | Most comprehensive: water/damp-proof, heat, fire resistance, fire prevention, security |
| Class 2 | Narrower than Class 1 (less flammable, etc.) | Art. 3-5 | Class 1 standard minus the fire-resistance element |
| Class 3 | Goods little affected by damp/temperature | Art. 3-6 | Part of the Class 1 standard (no damp-proof/heat needed) |
| Open-yard | Goods suited to outdoor storage (logs, minerals) | Art. 3-7 | Surrounding protective facilities, security, fall prevention |
| Storage-tank | Bulk/liquid (grain, liquids) | Art. 3-9 | Sealed structure and strength of silo/tank |
| Dangerous-goods | Hazardous materials (Fire Service Act, high-pressure gas) | Art. 3-10 | Separate standards for structure-type / land-type (other laws also overlap) |
| Refrigerated | Goods stored at 10°C or below | Art. 3-11 | Temperature control: constant temperature, thermometer, insulation |

**The point is that "even in the same building, the type that passes changes with what you store."** General cargo suits Class 1; goods stored at 10°C or below, refrigerated; hazardous materials, a dangerous-goods warehouse. A dangerous-goods warehouse also overlaps with the Fire Service Act and the High Pressure Gas Safety Act, so their conformity must be seen from the property-selection stage. Deciding the type first, then matching a property to it, reduces re-work.

## May you sign the property contract before registration — what is the order?

**It is safer to confirm the prospect of registration before fixing on a property.** Locking up a property that falls short of the facility standards risks heavy renovation costs, or being unable to register and left stranded. In practice the following order is sound.

| Order | What to do | Main handler |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decide the warehouse type and goods to store | Operator |
| 2 | Pre-check the candidate's use zone, use-change, inspection certificate, structure | Licensed real estate agent (us) / architect |
| 3 | Confirm the prospect of meeting the facility standard | Architect / prior consultation with the District Transport Bureau |
| 4 | Negotiate lease/sale terms and sign (renovation-permission clauses, etc.) | Licensed real estate agent (us) |
| 5 | File the warehouse registration | Administrative scrivener |
| 6 | If there is rebuilding, building description / change registration | Land and house investigator / judicial scrivener |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do I need warehouse registration just to rent a warehouse and store my own inventory?**
A. No. An in-house warehouse where you store your own goods, or a lease of the warehouse as real estate where the tenant stores their own goods, is generally not a warehousing business (storing others' goods on deposit), so Art. 3 registration is not required. Conversely, if you take goods from a shipper and store them for consideration, registration is required. If in doubt, confirm with the District Transport Bureau.

**Q. Can I turn a used warehouse into a commercial warehouse?**
A. Sometimes yes. But it presupposes that the warehouse has title to use and conforms with related laws, and can meet the facility standard of the type you aim for (water/damp-proof, heat, fire resistance, fire compartments, security, rodent prevention, etc.). In existing buildings, the use zone, whether a use-change confirmation is needed, the inspection certificate and the structure bite first. Confirm feasibility with an architect and the District Transport Bureau.

**Q. Do property conditions differ between general cargo at ambient temperature and frozen goods?**
A. Yes. General cargo is a Class 1 warehouse (Reg. Art. 3-4); goods stored at 10°C or below are a refrigerated warehouse (Art. 3-11), which adds temperature control — constant temperature, thermometer, insulation. What you store sets the type you aim for and the performance required of the property, so deciding the type first, then matching a property, is practical.

**Q. Can I ask a real estate agent to file the warehouse registration?**
