**Key points:** Check the dialysis equipment's water supply, drainage, RO system, power and floor loads alongside medical-facility standards before signing. Inpatient beds and local planning controls require separate checks; engineers must calculate capacity from the equipment and building.

This article helps physicians and medical corporations turn dialysis specifications into property requirements. See [general clinic property checks](/en/column/clinic-bukken-youto-chiiki-kaisetsu-todokede) for opening procedures and use classifications.

## What should be checked for water, drainage and the RO system?

An RO (reverse-osmosis) system and related treatment equipment prepare water for dialysis. Required supply depends on the number of machines, simultaneous operation, pretreatment and cleaning. Physicians and clinical engineers set the clinical water-quality and treatment requirements, which inform facility design. This article adopts no particular edition or numerical limits of a clinical water-quality standard.

| Check | Documents and questions | Who checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Supply | Machine specifications, service and meter sizes, pressure, simultaneous demand | Facility engineer and water supplier |
| RO system and tanks | Layout, maintenance space, access, waterproofing, tank weight | Architect and facility engineer |
| Drainage | Volume, quality, cleaning discharge, pipe size and slope, discharge destination | Engineer and sewerage authority |
| Pretreatment measures | Whether discharge conditions require measures under local sewerage rules | Sewerage authority |

The Sewerage Act (下水道法), Article 12 and related provisions, does not mean every dialysis clinic requires a pretreatment facility. Confirm discharge conditions and the applicable local standards. A calculation needed for a particular building is different from a source document that has not been verified.

## How are power, emergency operation and floor loads assessed?

| Item | Check before contracting |
|---|---|
| Power | Combined dialysis, RO and air-conditioning load; existing supply; upgrade feasibility |
| Outages | Generator or UPS arrangements consistent with the clinical safety plan; space, exhaust and fuel |
| Structure | Full tank weights, machine and bed layout, structural drawings and reinforcement |
| Works | Pipe penetrations and common areas, landlord consent, costs and programme |

There is no universal kVA, water-volume or floor-load figure in this article. Designers use specifications, existing services and structural drawings, and a site survey. They coordinate outage procedures with the clinical team. Compare alternative premises if an upgrade is impracticable.

## How do inpatient beds affect change-of-use and opening procedures?

Clinics are an allowed use even in a Category I exclusively low-rise residential zone under the Building Standards Act (建築基準法). District plans, local rules, building and fire compliance, and medical-facility requirements still need separate assessment.

Change-of-use confirmation depends on matters including whether the clinic has facilities to accommodate inpatients. Converting to a clinic without inpatient accommodation does not require confirmation merely because the area exceeds 200 m². Dialysis treatment stations are not necessarily inpatient beds. An architect and the building authority check Articles 6 and 87, classification and similar-use exceptions. Requirements still apply where confirmation is unnecessary.

Distinguish an opening notification by a physician who has completed the required clinical training from prior permission for a medical corporation or another relevant operator under the Medical Care Act (医療法), Articles 7(1) and 8(1). The notification is generally due within 10 days after opening. Ask the local public health centre about the responsible authority and procedure. Permission for inpatient beds and its exceptions require a separate check under Article 7(3).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is zoning enough to open a dialysis clinic?**
A. No. Check additional local controls, medical-facility standards, building and fire compliance, and dialysis-specific service capacity.

**Q. Does an area over 200 m² always require change-of-use confirmation?**
A. Area alone does not decide conversion to a clinic without inpatient accommodation. An architect checks inpatient facilities, classification and exceptions. Dialysis stations and inpatient beds must be distinguished.

**Q. Is wastewater pretreatment always required?**
A. No universal rule is given here. Confirm discharge quality and volume against local sewerage standards with the authority and designer.

**Q. Are required power and floor-load figures fixed?**
A. Not in this article. Designers calculate them from the equipment, simultaneous operation, full tank weights and structure, then check consent, cost and timing of works.
