**The key point**: If inherited farmland lies within an "agricultural-use zone" (so-called blue land) of an agricultural-promotion area, in principle you cannot convert it to residential or other land as is. That is because, as a premise for a farmland-conversion permit (Farmland Act arts. 4 and 5), you first need "agri-zone removal" — a change of the agricultural-land-use plan under Article 13(2) of the Act on the Development of Agricultural Promotion Areas (the Agricultural Promotion Act). Agri-zone removal has requirements such as: it being difficult to substitute land outside the agricultural-use zone (alternative land); no risk of hindering the efficient and comprehensive use of surrounding farmland; no risk of hindering the consolidation of farmland to core farmers; no risk of hindering the functions of land-improvement facilities; and roughly eight years having passed since the completion of land-improvement works, and it is removed only when the municipality amends its development plan. Inherited farmland is first notified under Farmland Act art. 3-3 (to the agricultural committee, within roughly 10 months of learning of the inheritance); then you confirm at the municipality whether it is blue or white land; if blue, the order is agri-zone removal → farmland-conversion permit → sale. ## Where can you find out whether inherited farmland is "blue land (agricultural-use zone)"?

For farmland, how easy it is to sell or convert changes greatly with which zone it lies in. When you try to sell inherited farmland, the first thing to check is whether it is "blue land or white land."

Blue land is the common name for farmland within an "agricultural-use zone" set by a municipality under the Agricultural Promotion Act. An agricultural-use zone is land enclosed as land whose agricultural use should be secured over the long term, on the premise of intensive agricultural investment and protection. By contrast, farmland within an agricultural-promotion area but outside the agricultural-use zone is called "white land." Whether it is blue or white land is confirmed at the property's municipality (agriculture division) by inspecting the agricultural-promotion-area development plan (the agricultural-land-use plan). At the same time, confirm the city-planning zone (urbanisation-promotion area, urbanisation-control area, non-line-drawn).

| Category | Position | Rough ease of conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Blue land (farmland in an agricultural-use zone) | Farmland protected by the Agricultural Promotion Act | In principle not possible; agri-zone removal first |
| White land (within agri-promotion area, outside the use zone) | Agri-promotion area but outside the zone | Subject to a farmland-conversion permit (judged by siting criteria) |
| Farmland within an urbanisation-promotion area | Zone to be urbanised under city planning | Conversion needs only a notification, not a permit |

Note that whether it is blue land cannot be known from the registry or the property-tax notice alone. Even if the land category is "paddy" or "field," it is not necessarily blue land; conversely, even if the current state looks like residential land, it may be within an agricultural-use zone. The reliable way is confirmation by the municipality's agricultural-land-use plan. The overall flow of selling or leasing inherited farmland is organised in [What to check first when selling or leasing inherited farmland](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-nochi-baikyaku-kashidashi-nagare). This article narrows to the "blue land" case.

## Why does an agricultural-use zone require "agri-zone removal" before farmland conversion?

That blue land in principle cannot be converted as is comes from the structure of the law. The permits of Farmland Act art. 4 (self-conversion) and art. 5 (transfer of rights for conversion) allow turning farmland into non-farmland, but farmland within an agricultural-use zone is, as land whose agricultural use should be secured, in principle excluded from the subject of a conversion permit. To convert it, you first need to remove it from the agricultural-use zone — this is "agri-zone removal."

Agri-zone removal is a procedure in which the municipality amends the agricultural-land-use plan within its agricultural-promotion-area development plan, and Article 13(2) of the Agricultural Promotion Act sets its requirements. The provision states that the change may be made only when all of the following requirements are met.

| Requirement (purport of Agri-Promotion Act art. 13(2)) | Content |
|---|---|
| Alternative land | It is difficult to substitute land outside the agricultural-use zone, and it is necessary and appropriate to put the land to that use |
| Hindrance to surrounding farming | No risk of hindering the collectivisation of farmland, farm-work efficiency, or the efficient and comprehensive agricultural use of land |
| Consolidation to core farmers | No risk of hindering the consolidation of farmland to core farmers such as certified farmers (achievement of the area plan) |
| Function of land-improvement facilities | No risk of hindering the functions of land-improvement facilities such as irrigation and drainage channels |
| Years since works | Roughly eight years having passed since the completion of land-improvement works (agricultural-production-base development works) |

How the items are numbered and the operating criteria are presented differ by municipality, and some organise them as "six requirements." In particular, "eight years since completion of works" makes it hard to remove farmland right after public money was invested in base development, and applying it makes removal difficult. These requirements summarise the purport of the provision; the individual judgment of whether removal is possible rests with the municipality and the agricultural committee. We do not conclude whether removal is possible; we help, as general information, with reading the provision against the operating criteria.

## How long does it take from the agri-zone removal petition to the farmland-conversion permit?

Selling blue land takes longer than white land or farmland within an urbanisation-promotion area because it passes through two stages: agri-zone removal → farmland-conversion permit. Moreover, agri-zone removal is not necessarily accepted at any time.

Many municipalities accept petitions for agri-zone removal (amendment of the agricultural-land-use plan) periodically (it is common to set a few acceptance periods a year), and judge after consultation with the prefecture and public notice of the plan change. The acceptance period, the review flow, and the standard timeframe differ by municipality, so you need to check the latest acceptance guidance of that municipality just before you write. After agri-zone removal is publicly announced, you proceed anew to the farmland-conversion-permit (arts. 4 and 5) application.

| Stage | Procedure | Main desk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inheritance notification (Farmland Act art. 3-3) | Agricultural committee |
| 2 | Confirming blue / white land | Municipality (agriculture division) |
| 3 | Petition for agri-zone removal (amendment of the land-use plan) | Municipality (mind the acceptance period) |
| 4 | Farmland-conversion-permit (arts. 4 and 5) application | Via the agricultural committee, prefectural governor etc. |
| 5 | Sale (brokerage and settlement) | Real estate (us) |

What should be stressed here is that agri-zone removal is not something that "will always be removed if you petition" — if the requirements are not met, it will not be accepted or will not be removed. In municipalities where acceptance periods are limited to a few times a year, missing the timing means waiting months. ## Is there an option to sell "as farmland" (to a farmer or a farmland-holding corporation)?

While blue land has a high hurdle for conversion, the path of selling it "as farmland" remains. It is a method of selling to a buyer who will keep farming, for cultivation purposes.

To sell or lease farmland as farmland for cultivation purposes, a permit under Farmland Act art. 3 is required. The buyer is limited to those who meet the Farmland Act's requirements, such as individuals engaged in farming or corporations that can own farmland (farmland-holding qualified corporations). Even for blue land, if it is a transaction where use as farmland continues, agri-zone removal for conversion is unnecessary and it proceeds within the framework of an art. 3 permit. When you want to let go of inherited farmland but have no prospect of conversion, lease/sale through a nearby core farmer or the farmland intermediary-management organisation (the farmland bank) can be a realistic exit.

| Exit | Required procedure | Buyer / lessee |
|---|---|---|
| Sell / lease as farmland | Farmland Act art. 3 permit | Farmer / farmland-holding qualified corporation / via farmland bank, etc. |
| Convert to residential land, etc. and sell | Agri-zone removal → Farmland Act arts. 4 and 5 permit | Buyer matching the post-conversion use |

Selling as farmland can reach an exit faster than waiting for conversion, but buyers are limited and the price is valued as farmland. Which exit is realistic changes with the farmland's location, zone and the state of nearby core farmers. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Where can I find out whether inherited farmland is blue land?**
A. Confirm at the property's municipality (agriculture division) by inspecting the drawings of the agricultural-land-use plan in the agricultural-promotion-area development plan. You cannot tell from the registry's land category or the property-tax notice alone. Even if the land category is paddy or field, it is not necessarily blue land; even if the current state looks like residential land, it may be within an agricultural-use zone. Also confirm the city-planning zone (urbanisation-promotion / urbanisation-control / non-line-drawn). We organise how to proceed with the municipal confirmation; the final determination of blue/white land is by the municipality's and agricultural committee's confirmation.

**Q. Why can't blue land be converted to residential land and sold as is?**
A. Because farmland within an agricultural-use zone is, as land whose agricultural use should be secured, in principle excluded from the subject of a farmland-conversion permit (Farmland Act arts. 4 and 5). To convert it, you first need "agri-zone removal" (amendment of the agricultural-land-use plan under Agri-Promotion Act art. 13(2)). Agri-zone removal has requirements such as alternative land, whether there is hindrance to surrounding farming, consolidation to core farmers, the function of land-improvement facilities, and roughly eight years having passed since completion of works; if unmet, it is not removed. The decision rests with the municipality and the agricultural committee, and we do not conclude it.

**Q. How long does agri-zone removal take?**
A. It differs greatly by municipality. Many municipalities accept petitions for agri-zone removal only in a few acceptance periods a year and judge after consultation with the prefecture and public notice of the plan change. Missing the acceptance period can mean waiting months, and because you proceed anew to the farmland-conversion-permit application after removal is announced, selling blue land takes longer than white land or farmland within an urbanisation-promotion area. The acceptance period and standard timeframe differ by municipality, so confirm them on that municipality's latest acceptance guidance, noting the reference date, just before you write.
