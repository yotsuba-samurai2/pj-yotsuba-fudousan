**The key point**: A property for a care-equipped fee-charging home for the elderly (a fee-charging home that receives the designation for "daily life care for residents of a specified facility") is blocked by two walls the residential type does not face. One is the "supply cap". Daily life care for residents of a specified facility is a home-based service designated by the prefectural governor under Long-Term Care Insurance Act (Act No. 123 of 1997) art. 70, and if the required capacity for the old-age welfare zone is already reached, the governor may refuse to designate a care-only type under art. 70(4) and a mixed type under art. 70(5). Even if you secure the property, without a designation slot you cannot make it care-equipped, so confirming the slot in the prefecture/municipality's long-term-care-insurance plan comes first. The other wall is building standards. Establishing a fee-charging home requires, under Act on Social Welfare for the Elderly (Act No. 133 of 1963) art. 29(1), a prior notification to the prefectural governor; rooms are, under the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Standard operation guideline for fee-charging homes for the elderly", in principle 13 m² or more per person (new builds guided to 18 m² or more with a toilet and washbasin), with toilets, baths and a temporary-care room required. For fire safety, a facility mainly admitting care-needing persons who cannot evacuate on their own falls under the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order Appended Table 1 (6)(ro), and sprinklers are in principle required regardless of floor area. ## How do the property requirements differ between a care-equipped fee-charging home (specified facility) and a residential-type fee-charging home?

Both are fee-charging homes under Act on Social Welfare for the Elderly art. 29 and need a prior notification to the prefectural governor. The difference is whether they receive the care-insurance designation.

The care-equipped type receives the designation for "daily life care for residents of a specified facility" (Long-Term Care Insurance Act art. 8(11)), and the facility's staff (or an external service) provide bathing, toileting, meal care, etc., based on a specified-facility service plan. The residential type does not receive this designation; residents use outside home-visit care, etc., under individual contracts as needed.

| Type | Care-insurance designation | Property issues that bite first |
|---|---|---|
| Care-equipped fee-charging home (specified facility) | Designation for daily life care for residents of a specified facility required (art. 70) | The supply-cap slot / staffing and equipment standards / 13 m² rooms / fire (sprinklers) |
| Residential-type fee-charging home | Not designated | Notification and the guideline's equipment standards / fire |

This presence or absence of a designation changes the premise of property selection. ## Where are the building standards—room area, toilet, bath, corridor width—decided?

The building standards are decided largely in two streams: the MHLW "Standard operation guideline for fee-charging homes for the elderly" covering fee-charging homes generally, and the specified-facility chapter (art. 174 and after) of the "Standards for personnel, equipment and operation of designated home-based service businesses" (MHW Ordinance No. 37 of 1999) for the care-insurance designation. The guideline is national technical advice, and concrete operation is set by each prefecture/municipality in its own rules, so the final figures must be confirmed with the municipality where the property is.

| Equipment | Skeleton shown by the guideline |
|---|---|
| Room | In principle 13 m² or more per person (excluding storage, toilet, etc.). New builds guided to 18 m² or more with a toilet and washbasin |
| Toilet / bath / washbasin | In-room or shared, at a position and spec usable by care-needing persons |
| Temporary-care room | A room for concentrated temporary care (whether it can be omitted differs by scale) |
| Corridor / step / handrail | Barrier-free width, handrails and step elimination (concrete widths follow the municipality's rules) |

Because concrete corridor widths overlap the guideline, the municipality's rules and the Building Standards Act's evacuation provisions, it is surest to confirm with an architect per property. This article shows only the verifiable skeleton and leaves individual figures to the local rules. Since room area ties directly to the non-residential floor area, when converting an existing building, checking on the floor plan in advance whether "13 m² (or 18 m²) × capacity + shared/office/care-staff rooms" fits reduces backtracking.

## With a "supply cap (reference standard)", what should be confirmed before deciding on a property?

The designation for daily life care for residents of a specified facility has a "supply cap". The prefecture/municipality sets, in its long-term-care-insurance plan, the required capacity for specified facilities per old-age welfare zone. If a new designation would exceed that number, the governor may refuse to designate under art. 70(4) for a care-only type and art. 70(5) for a mixed type.

So even if the building meets the standards, you cannot make it care-equipped if the zone's slot is full. Before deciding on a property, confirm the following.

| Where to confirm | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| The prefecture/municipality (care-insurance section) | The required capacity for specified facilities in that old-age welfare zone, and the remaining developable capacity |
| The timing of public offering/consultation in the plan | Where an open-call/prior-consultation system is used, the call timing, eligibility and selection schedule |
| Mixed vs. care-only type | A care-only type limiting residents to care-needing persons, or a mixed type also admitting independent/support-needing persons |

In a zone with no slot, unless you confirm the plan's revision timing and whether a call exists before securing the property, only the acquisition/lease burden runs ahead. Whether a slot exists or is likely is the municipality's call; we help within the scope of property-suitability survey and brokerage, from arranging this confirmation.

## What are the fire-equipment (sprinkler, etc.), fire-resistance and evacuation requirements?

A care-equipped fee-charging home is generally classified under Fire Service Act Enforcement Order Appended Table 1 (6)(ro) as a facility mainly admitting care-needing persons who cannot evacuate on their own (one yardstick being that residents of care level 3 or higher make up at least half the capacity).

By the Enforcement Order amendment in force 1 April 2015, (6)(ro) facilities in principle require sprinklers regardless of floor area (previously the standard was 275 m² or more of total floor area). There is, however, an exemption for those under 275 m² with a fire-spread-suppressing structure prescribed by ministerial ordinance, and a separate relaxation for small facilities under 100 m². Whether these apply depends on structure, so prior consultation with the fire station is essential.

| Item | Point to confirm |
|---|---|
| Sprinkler | (6)(ro) in principle installed regardless of floor area; whether the structural exemption under 275 m² / 100 m² applies is confirmed with the fire station |
| Automatic fire alarm / alarm notifying the fire department | (6)(ro) is subject to installation duty per area, etc. |
| Fire-resistance / evacuation (Building Standards Act) | A fee-charging home is included in "child welfare facilities, etc."; confirm the use zone, the need for a fire-resistant building, two-direction evacuation and fire compartments |
| Change-of-use confirmation | When converting from an office, apartment, etc., a confirmation application may be needed depending on scale |

In converting an existing building, new sprinklers and the change-of-use confirmation drive the cost and schedule. The design and installation of fire equipment are the field of a fire-equipment officer and the fire station; fire-resistance, evacuation and change-of-use judgments an architect's. We separate these issues from the property's current state and hand over an arrangement of who to approach for each.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I make it a care-equipped fee-charging home if the building meets the standards?**
A. Meeting the building standards alone is not enough. The care-equipped type presupposes the designation for daily life care for residents of a specified facility (art. 70), and if the required capacity per old-age welfare zone is reached, the governor may refuse to designate a care-only type under art. 70(4) and a mixed type under art. 70(5) (the supply cap). Before deciding on a property, confirm the slot and call timing in the location's long-term-care-insurance plan. Whether a slot exists or is likely is the municipality's call.

**Q. How many m² per person does a room need?**
A. The MHLW "Standard operation guideline for fee-charging homes for the elderly" shows in principle 13 m² or more per person, and guides new builds to 18 m² or more with a toilet and washbasin. The method of calculation (treatment of storage, toilet, etc.) and corridor widths and other concrete figures overlap the location's rules and the Building Standards Act's evacuation provisions, so confirm with an architect and the municipality. The figures here are a skeleton yardstick, not an individual conformity judgment.

**Q. Can I convert an existing office or apartment?**
A. Conversion is sometimes possible, but whether 13 m² (or 18 m²) × capacity plus shared/care-staff rooms fit, whether new sprinklers are needed, and whether a change-of-use confirmation is required greatly change cost and schedule. A fee-charging home is included under "child welfare facilities, etc." in the Building Standards Act, so the use zone (it cannot be built in an exclusive industrial zone) and fire-resistance/evacuation provisions apply. Fire-resistance and change of use are confirmed with an architect, fire with the fire station.

**Q. Are sprinklers always required?**
A. A facility mainly admitting care-needing persons who cannot evacuate on their own falls under Fire Service Act Enforcement Order Appended Table 1 (6)(ro), and the amendment in force 1 April 2015 made sprinklers in principle required regardless of floor area. There are relaxations where a facility under 275 m² has a fire-spread-suppressing structure, or for small facilities under 100 m², but whether they apply depends on structure, so confirm with prior consultation at the fire station.
