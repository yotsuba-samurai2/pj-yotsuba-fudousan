**In short:** A field next to an inherited house does not necessarily change hands on the same terms as the house. Japan abolished its minimum farm-size requirement in 2023. The notice an heir files on inheriting farmland does not authorise a subsequent sale, lease or change of use.

Before listing the house, ask the buyer what they want to do with the field: grow vegetables, for example, or park a car. That answer helps you put a concrete question to the agricultural committee where the property is located. This guide focuses on individual buyers. For help with the house and its residential plot, see [inherited property consultations](https://luck428.com/en/souzoku).

## Can anyone now buy a small field because the minimum area was abolished?

No. The old test looked at the **total area the buyer would farm after the acquisition**, not just the size of the plot for sale. Article 5 of Act No. 56 of 2022 repealed that test in former Article 3(2)(v) of Japan's Cropland Act (農地法), effective 1 April 2023. A buyer starting out on a small scale is no longer disqualified solely for falling short of the old threshold.

For an ordinary sale that keeps the land in agricultural use, the local agricultural committee still decides whether to grant Article 3 permission. For an individual, it considers whether all the farmland to be cultivated can be used efficiently, whether the buyer or a qualifying household member will regularly do the necessary farm work, and whether the plan would interfere with agricultural land use in the area. A farming background is not the same as a workable farming plan.

There is a separate question if the “field” is a kitchen garden in the house grounds. The land ministry's guide distinguishes a very small garden attached to the house that cannot reasonably be traded separately from the residential plot. Such a garden is not cropland under the Act. Show the committee how it sits in relation to the house and how it is used; neither its size nor the description on the title answers the question on its own.

## Which procedure applies to inheritance, farming or conversion?

| What will happen? | General rule under the Cropland Act |
|---|---|
| An heir acquires ownership of farmland by inheritance | Notify the agricultural committee where the land is located without delay after acquisition (Art. 3-3(1)). This is not an Article 3 sale application. |
| An individual buys or rents the field to cultivate it | In an ordinary private sale or lease, the parties generally need permission from the agricultural committee (Art. 3(1)). |
| The owner converts their own field to a car park or other non-farm use | Conversion generally requires permission from the prefectural governor or the relevant designated municipal authority (Art. 4(1)). |
| The purchaser or tenant acquires rights in order to convert the field | The parties generally need conversion permission from the prefectural governor or the relevant designated municipal authority (Art. 5(1)). |

For Articles 4 and 5, the authority is generally the prefectural governor or, in a municipality designated by the Minister of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, its mayor. Ask the local agricultural committee which office handles the site. For **qualifying conversions in an urbanization promotion area** (市街化区域), prior notice to the agricultural committee replaces conversion permission (Arts. 4(1)(vii) and 5(1)(vi)). This exception does **not** turn an Article 3 sale for continued cultivation into a notification, even in the same area.

The [guide to notifying an inheritance of farmland](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/souzoku-nochi-noringyoiinkai-todoke-3jo3) explains the first step. If someone intends to change the use, see [the distinction between Articles 4 and 5](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/nochi-tenyo-4jo-5jo-kyoka-nagare).

## What should the seller check before listing the house and field together?

Match the house and each parcel to the register and cadastral map. Mark the access route, the part currently farmed and who uses it. Add photos and the buyer's proposed use. A valuation based on continued farming is not the same as one that assumes conversion has already been allowed. Do not advertise the field as a building plot or promise parking while its status is unconfirmed.

Putting both parcels in one contract does not make the rules for transferring them identical. Identify which permission or notification is needed, who will handle it, when payment and handover take place, and what happens to the price and deposit if the required steps cannot be completed. Before assuming the house can be transferred separately, check its boundaries, road access, the route to the field, and rights affecting either parcel.

## Does notifying the agricultural committee also register the inheritance?

No. The agricultural committee receives the farmland notice; the Legal Affairs Bureau handles registration of title. Since 1 April 2024, an heir generally has three years to apply for registration from the date they learn both that the succession has opened for them and that they have acquired ownership of the particular property. If both were known before 1 April 2024 and the inheritance is still unregistered, the general deadline is 31 March 2027. If the heir learned of the acquisition after the new rule took effect, the three years generally run from that later date. A judicial scrivener can check the relevant deadline and the registration needed for a sale.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I explore selling if I inherited the field but am not a farmer?**
A. The seller's occupation does not settle the question. If the buyer will farm, their ability to cultivate the land matters. If they want a different use, the relevant question is whether the proposed conversion is permissible.

**Q. Does a vacant-house listing that calls the plot a small kitchen garden mean Article 3 permission is unnecessary?**
A. The listing is not decisive. Show the location, layout and actual use to the agricultural committee. A very small garden inseparable from the house grounds is different from a separate agricultural parcel.

**Q. I notified the committee of the inheritance. Can the buyer immediately use the field for parking in an urbanization promotion area?**
A. An inheritance notice is not prior notice of conversion. If the buyer is acquiring the plot to convert it, check the Article 5 procedure before proceeding. An inheritance notice does not remove restrictions under other laws, either.

**Q. Who decides whether permission is granted, and who can help with the paperwork?**
A. The agricultural committee decides Article 3 applications. The prefectural governor or relevant designated municipal authority generally decides conversion applications under Articles 4 and 5. A licensed administrative scrivener can help prepare the application or notification if engaged; the scrivener cannot grant permission.
