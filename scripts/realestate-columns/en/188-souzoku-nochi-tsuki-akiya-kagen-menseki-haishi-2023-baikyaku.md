## Can a non-farmer actually buy a vacant house with farmland?

Yes. But the procedure differs depending on whether you buy it "as farmland" or "as residential land."

To acquire farmland and keep it as farmland requires the Art. 3 permission of the Agricultural Land Act (agricultural committee). This used to be remote for non-farmers, but the 1 April 2023 amendment abolished the minimum-area (specified-area) requirement — which had required the total area of the acquired farmland to reach a certain size — so even small plots are now eligible. This makes it easier for a buyer newly taking up farming to acquire a vacant house with farmland.

That said, for the Art. 3 permission, the requirements other than minimum area remain. These include efficiently using all of the acquired farmland (the all-efficient-use requirement), the acquirer being regularly engaged in the farm work themselves (the regular-engagement requirement), and no hindrance to the use of surrounding farmland (the regional-harmony requirement). Holding farmland "as farmland without cultivating it" is in principle not allowed. To use it as residential land or a car park, it becomes the conversion procedure (Arts. 4 and 5) described below.

| How the buyer uses it | Required procedure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Keep and cultivate it as farmland | Art. 3 permission (agricultural committee) | Agricultural Land Act Art. 3 |
| Convert to residential land, car park, etc. | Conversion permission (Arts. 4 and 5) | Agricultural Land Act Arts. 4 and 5 |
| Farmland within an urbanization-promotion area | Notification to the agricultural committee, not permission | Notification under Arts. 4 and 5 |

## What changed with the 2023 abolition of the minimum-area requirement?

What changed is that the "hurdle of the minimum area of farmland you can acquire" was removed. Previously, Art. 3(2)(v) of the Agricultural Land Act required that the farmland managed after acquisition total roughly 50 ares (2 hectares in Hokkaido), and the agricultural committee could set a different "specified area" by region. Someone acquiring only a small plot could not obtain permission under this requirement.

This minimum-area (specified-area) requirement was deleted by the amendment of the Agricultural Land Act under Art. 5 of the Act Partially Amending the Act on Promotion of Improvement of Agricultural Management Foundations and Others (Act No. 56 of 2022), and came into force on 1 April 2023 (Reiwa 5). The specified area set by agricultural committees was likewise abolished on the same date. Even a small plot of a few dozen square metres to a few ares attached to a vacant house can no longer be refused acquisition on grounds of area.

However, this only removes the area hurdle; the other requirements of the Art. 3 permission (efficient use, regular engagement, regional harmony) remain. The accurate understanding is that even a non-farmer buyer finds it easier to acquire if they have a structure to farm it themselves.

| Item | Until March 2023 | From 1 April 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum-area (specified-area) requirement | Yes (in principle 50a; 2ha in Hokkaido, etc.) | Abolished |
| Efficient-use / regular-engagement / regional-harmony requirements | Yes | Yes (continuing) |
| Basis | Former Agricultural Land Act Art. 3(2)(v) | Deletion by Act No. 56 of 2022, Art. 5 |

## How does the Art. 3 permission differ from conversion to residential land (Arts. 4 and 5)?

Art. 3 is the permission for moving a right in farmland "while keeping it as farmland." The agricultural committee grants it on the premise that the buyer continues to cultivate it as farmland. By contrast, Art. 4 is the permission to convert one's own farmland to non-farmland (residential land, car park, etc.), and Art. 5 is the permission to move a right in farmland for the purpose of conversion (e.g. the buyer buys it intending to make it residential land); for these the prefectural governor and the like is in principle the permitting authority (within an urbanization-promotion area, permission is unnecessary and a notification to the agricultural committee suffices).

Selling a vacant house with farmland "by making it residential land" takes the conversion route. Conversion has location and plan standards, and for prime farmland (such as within an agricultural-use area) conversion may be difficult. Which route is available depends on the farmland category and how the buyer will use it, so a prior consultation with the agricultural committee is the sure route. The permission application itself is the administrative scrivener's work; we refer it to [what is the flow of the Arts. 4 and 5 conversion permissions](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/nochi-tenyo-4jo-5jo-kyoka-nagare), and for the notification of inherited farmland to [what do you notify the agricultural committee for inherited farmland](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/souzoku-nochi-noringyoiinkai-todoke-3jo3).

| Procedure | Situation | Permitting authority |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 3 permission | Moving farmland while keeping it as farmland | Agricultural committee |
| Art. 4 permission | Converting one's own farmland | Prefectural governor, etc. |
| Art. 5 permission | Moving a right for the purpose of conversion | Prefectural governor, etc. |
| Notification | Farmland within an urbanization-promotion area | Agricultural committee |

## When selling the house and farmland as a set, how do you structure the price and contract?

For a vacant house with farmland, the residential-land part (the house's site) and the farmland part are governed by different laws and procedures. The house's site is an ordinary residential-land sale, but for the farmland part, ownership cannot be transferred until the Agricultural Land Act permission or notification is complete. So the practice is to structure the sale contract as subject to the condition precedent of the Agricultural Land Act permission (effective once permission is granted).

The price changes greatly depending on whether it is sold as farmland or converted to residential land. As farmland, buyers are limited to those taking up farming, and the price tends to be modest. If conversion is foreseeable, a residential-land price can be expected, but whether conversion is possible depends on the farmland category. Which way of selling is realistic is decided from the category check at the agricultural committee and the expected buyer profile. We handle the valuation, brokerage and the design of contract conditions (as a real estate brokerage), and leave the feasibility of permission to the agricultural committee and the administrative scrivener.

| Way of selling | Buyer profile | Price tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Sell as farmland | Those taking up farming / nearby farmers | Farmland price (modest) |
| Convert and sell as residential land | General home buyers | Residential-land price (depends on conversion) |
| House site first | Home buyers | Residential-land price (farmland is a separate procedure) |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Even as a non-farmer, can I sell an inherited vacant house with farmland?**
A. Yes. To sell it as farmland, the buyer needs the Art. 3 permission (agricultural committee); to sell it as residential land, it is conversion (Arts. 4 and 5). The 1 April 2023 amendment abolished the minimum-area (specified-area) requirement, making even small plots easier to acquire. We handle the steps of the sale, and the permission application goes to an administrative scrivener, engaged separately.

**Q. With the 2023 abolition of the minimum area, can anyone now buy farmland?**
A. No. What was abolished is only the "minimum line of acquisition area." To acquire farmland and keep it as farmland, the Art. 3 requirements remain — the acquirer cultivating it themselves (efficient use, regular engagement) and regional harmony. Holding it without cultivating is in principle not possible. To use it as residential land, it becomes the conversion procedure.

**Q. Can the house and the farmland be sold together in one contract?**
A. The residential-land site and the farmland differ in procedure, and ownership of the farmland part cannot be transferred until the Agricultural Land Act permission or notification is complete. In practice the contract is often made subject to the condition precedent of the Agricultural Land Act permission, and we handle the design of the conditions. The feasibility of permission is for the agricultural committee and the administrative scrivener.

**Q. In what order and to whom do I arrange the sale, registration and tax?**
A. First complete inheritance registration (judicial scrivener) to move the title to the heir, and in parallel proceed with the property survey and the design of the way of selling (us) and the Agricultural Land Act permission application (administrative scrivener). The capital-gains tax after the sale is calculated by a tax accountant. Inheritance registration has been mandatory since 1 April 2024 (within three years of learning of acquisition).
