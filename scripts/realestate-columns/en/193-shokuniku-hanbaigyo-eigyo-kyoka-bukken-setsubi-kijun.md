## Why does a meat retail business need a "licence" rather than a notification?

On 1 June 2021 the amended Food Sanitation Act came fully into force, reorganising the business-licence system and the business-notification system. For facilities of a business that has a marked effect on public health and is specified by Cabinet Order, the prefecture sets standards by ordinance (Art. 54 of the Food Sanitation Act), and a person who intends to run that business must obtain the licence of the prefectural governor (Art. 55(1)). The businesses "specified by Cabinet Order" are listed in Art. 35 of the Order for Enforcement, which currently makes 32 business types subject to a licence. A meat retail business is a licensed business expressly set out in item (iii).

That item, however, excludes "a business that purchases meat exclusively in a packaged state and sells it as it is." If you only purchase pre-packaged meat and sell it without cutting or portioning, a notification (Art. 57) suffices rather than a licence. A business such as a butcher or a wholesaler of meat for yakiniku, which cuts, slices and portions block meat at the counter or in a workroom, involves processing and therefore needs a licence. The licence carries a term of validity of not less than five years (Art. 55(3)), and a record of violating the Food Sanitation Act and the like may fall under the grounds for disqualification and prevent the licence (Art. 55(2)). Working out, at the premises-search stage, whether your own business type is a licence or a notification lets you see the outlook for facility investment.

| Example of business type | Position under the Food Sanitation Act | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting and portioning block meat for sale (butcher, yakiniku wholesaler) | Meat retail business = licence | Order for Enforcement Art. 35(iii) |
| Selling only pre-packaged meat as it is | Notification | Art. 35(iii) proviso; Act Art. 57 |
| Manufacturing ham, sausage, bacon, etc. | Meat product manufacturing = separate licence | Order for Enforcement Art. 35(xv) |

## What facility standards (zoning, refrigeration, water supply and drainage, floors and walls) are required of the premises?

The facility standards have two layers: standards common to all business types (Appended Table 19 of the Regulation for Enforcement) and standards by business type (Appended Table 20), and prefectures set standards by ordinance taking these into account. The common standards require that the premises be partitioned as necessary by partitions and the like according to the division of work, and partitioned from residences and other rooms not used for handling food (Table 19, item 2); that the floor, inner walls and ceiling be of material and structure that make cleaning, washing and disinfecting easy; and that, where water is used, the floor be impermeable and drain well and the inner walls be lined with impermeable material (dado) up to a height easily soiled from the floor (item 3, (c) and (d)). They also require a water-supply facility that supplies water of the necessary temperature and in sufficient quantity, a drainage facility that prevents backflow and discharges appropriately out of the premises, a running-water hand-washing facility that washes and disinfects the hands, and a toilet of a structure that does not affect the work area (item 3, (f), (h), (i) and (o)).

As a standard by business type (Table 20, item 3), a meat retail business must have a processing room; equipment in the processing room necessary to divide meat, offal and the like; refrigeration or freezing equipment on a scale matching the volume processed that can keep products requiring refrigerated storage at 10°C or below and products requiring frozen storage at minus 15°C or below; and containers for inedible parts and for disposal that are of impermeable material and have lids. In choosing premises, the key points are whether there is enough space, zoning, water-supply and drainage connections and electrical capacity (for refrigeration and freezing equipment) to accommodate these. Because figures and operational details vary with the ordinance and the operation of the health centre with jurisdiction, consulting the health centre in advance once you have narrowed down the property is the sure course.

| Item to check | Key point of the standard | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Zoning | Partition by division of work; partition from residences etc. | Regulation Table 19, item 2 |
| Floors / inner walls | Impermeable floor, good drainage, dado (where water is used) | Table 19, item 3(d) |
| Refrigeration / freezing | 10°C or below / minus 15°C or below; scale matching volume | Table 20, item 3(c) |
| Water supply/drainage/hand-washing | Sufficient supply, backflow-preventing drainage, running-water hand-washing | Table 19, item 3(f),(h),(i) |
| Processing room | Processing room and dividing equipment | Table 20, item 3(a),(b) |

## In which use districts and buildings can you open?

The shop of a meat retail business is, under the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950), a "store for a retail-goods business," and is subject to the restrictions on the use of buildings by use district (Art. 48 and Appended Table 2). Broadly, in a Category I exclusively low-rise residential district a store cannot in principle be built, and is limited to a store and the like whose non-residential part is of a certain scale (a certain proportion of the total floor area and 50 m² or less) combined with a residence. In a Category II exclusively low-rise residential district, a store of 150 m² or less of floor area and the like are allowed; in the medium-and-high-rise residential, residential, neighbourhood-commercial, commercial and quasi-industrial districts they are allowed depending on scale; and in an exclusively industrial district a retail-goods store is in principle not allowed. Because the specific permissibility and the upper limit of area vary with the district and scale, confirming with an architect or the specified administrative authority is the sure course.

Cutting and portioning meat falls within the scope of a "retail business," and is a separate licence and a separate treatment from manufacturing ham, sausage and the like (meat product manufacturing). Where manufacturing is involved, the premises are treated as a "factory" for use-district purposes, and the districts where it may be located may be further narrowed by the output of the motors used and the content of the work. Where you use an existing building, changing the use from the former use (office, residence, etc.) to a store may require a change-of-use confirmation application under the Building Standards Act depending on the floor area. Whether the property was already used as a restaurant or a retail store is an important clue when estimating the effort of a change of use.

| Use district | Treatment of a retail-goods store (broadly) |
|---|---|
| Category I exclusively low-rise residential | In principle not allowed (only a store etc. of a certain scale combined with a residence) |
| Category II exclusively low-rise residential | A store etc. of 150 m² or less of floor area |
| Residential / neighbourhood-commercial / commercial / quasi-industrial | Allowed depending on scale |
| Exclusively industrial | A retail-goods store is in principle not allowed |

## Who applies for the licence, and what does the real estate side check on the property?

Roles are divided by qualification. The preparation and agency of the licence application for a meat retail business lie with an administrative scrivener; the training course to obtain the qualification of food sanitation supervisor is run by the food sanitation association of each prefecture; the facility work for the kitchen, refrigeration equipment and water supply and drainage lies with a specialist contractor; and tax after opening lies with a tax accountant. Each facility must place a food sanitation supervisor (Art. 66-2(3) of the Regulation for Enforcement), a role filled by a qualified person such as a cook, confectionery sanitation specialist or dietitian, or by a person who has completed a training course run by a food sanitation association and the like. Yotsuba Real Estate Co., Ltd. organises the need for these procedures within the property survey and important-matters explanation, and connects you to each specialist as an independent entity engaged separately.

What the real estate side can check before the contract includes whether the property's area and zoning can accommodate the processing room, refrigeration equipment, hand-washing and toilet; whether the water supply, drainage and electrical capacity are enough for the equipment; whether the use district, the frontage road and the delivery line suit the business type; for an existing building, whether a change of use from the former use is needed; and for a lease, whether the lessor consents to use as a meat retail business and to facility work. Because the final judgment on conformity with the facility standards is made by the health centre with jurisdiction, consulting the health centre in advance once candidates are narrowed, and if necessary viewing the property together with an administrative scrivener and an architect, is safe. Setting contract terms that allow you to wait for the health centre's judgment (such as a cancellation clause for non-conformity with the facility standards) before rushing the contract reduces the burden of backtracking.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do I need a licence even if I only purchase pre-packaged meat and sell it?**
A. A business that purchases meat exclusively in a packaged state and sells it as it is, without cutting or portioning, is excluded from the licence of a meat retail business, and a business notification (Art. 57 of the Food Sanitation Act) suffices (the proviso to Art. 35(iii) of the Order for Enforcement). A business type that cuts, slices and portions block meat at the counter or in a workroom involves processing and needs a licence. Confirming with the health centre with jurisdiction which of these applies, before you search for premises, is the sure course.

**Q. What kind of refrigeration and freezing equipment is required?**
A. Appended Table 20, item 3 of the Regulation for Enforcement requires refrigeration or freezing equipment, on a scale matching the volume processed, that can keep products requiring refrigerated storage at 10°C or below and products requiring frozen storage at minus 15°C or below. The number and capacity required vary with the volume handled, and securing electrical capacity is a key point in choosing premises. Because the specific conformity is judged by the ordinance and the operation of the health centre with jurisdiction, consult the health centre in advance on the candidate property.

**Q. Can I open a butcher in a vacant store in a residential area?**
A. It depends on the use district. In a Category I exclusively low-rise residential district a retail-goods store cannot in principle be built, and is limited to a store and the like of a certain scale combined with a residence. In many districts from a Category II exclusively low-rise residential district upward it is possible depending on scale, but in an exclusively industrial district a retail-goods store is in principle not allowed (Art. 48 and Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act). Confirm the specific permissibility, the upper limit of area and whether a change-of-use confirmation application is needed with an architect or the specified administrative authority.
