## What is the farmland inheritance-tax deferral, and what is it deferring?

The inheritance-tax deferral for farmland etc. is a system under which a farming heir who inherits farmland etc. from a deceased person who farmed it has part of the inheritance tax deferred, on condition that they keep farming the land (or carry out certain specified leases) (Act on Special Measures Concerning Taxation art. 70-6). What is deferred is the amount over the tax that would arise if the farmland were valued at the "agricultural investment price"—that is, the difference from the inheritance tax computed on the ordinary valuation such as residential-equivalent.

This deferred amount (the farmland deferred tax) is exempted when the special-provision farming heir dies, among other cases (National Tax Agency Tax Answer No. 4147). So as long as you keep farming you need not pay, and in the end it can be exempted. In return, while under the deferral you must file a continuation report every three years from the filing deadline, and letting go of the farmland or stopping farming partway cancels the deferral.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Amount deferred | The difference between the inheritance tax on ordinary valuation and the tax if valued at the agricultural investment price |
| Condition for deferral | The farming heir keeps farming, or carries out a specified lease etc. |
| When exempted | On the farming heir's death, among other cases (No. 4147) |
| To keep it going | File a continuation report every three years from the filing deadline |
| Basis | Act on Special Measures art. 70-6 (and art. 70-6-2 / art. 70-6-3, Enforcement Order art. 40-7, Enforcement Rules art. 23-8) |

The key is that the tax was not "exempted at inheritance" but "deferred." When thinking about the property exit—sale, conversion, lease—you must first grasp how this deferral moves.

## Which of sale, conversion or lease is a cancellation trigger?

The main events that cancel the deferral (fix the deferral deadline) are disposing of / converting / leasing (apart from certain specified leases etc.) the deferred farmland, abandoning cultivation, or ceasing the farming business. The scope of the fixing is split by the proportion of farmland area moved.

| Act | Effect on the deferral |
|---|---|
| Dispose of / convert / lease / abandon cultivation of 20% or less of the deferred area | Part of the deferred tax is fixed (the tax for that part) |
| Over 20% disposed of / converted / leased / cultivation abandoned | The whole deferred tax is fixed |
| Transfer by expropriation/exchange, request to buy out a productive green space, etc. | Cited as examples amounting to full fixing |
| Ceasing the farming business | The whole deferred tax is fixed |
| Certain specified leases etc. (below) | No cancellation; the deferral can continue |

Here "sell, convert, lease" are each, as a rule, cancellation triggers. However, as with the specified leases seen below, certain leases set in law can keep the deferral going as an exception. Which act amounts to which scope of fixing, and how the 20% is judged, depends on the individual plot and is judged by a tax accountant and the tax office. We handle the exit practice (survey, valuation, brokerage) of selling, leasing or converting, and do not step into the judgment of whether the tax is fixed.

## If cancelled, what do you pay and by when?

When a fixing trigger occurs, as a rule the deferral deadline is fixed on the day two months pass after it. On top of that, besides the deferred inheritance-tax principal, you must pay interest tax for the period from the day after the inheritance-tax filing deadline to the fixing date (Act on Special Measures art. 70-6).

| What you pay | Content |
|---|---|
| Deferred inheritance tax (principal) | The fixed scope (part if partial, all if full) |
| Interest tax | Calculated for the period from the day after the filing deadline to the fixing date |
| Start of the payment deadline | The day two months pass after the triggering event |

So the longer the deferral has run, the longer the interest-tax period. You may cover the principal from the sale proceeds, but overlooking the interest-tax burden can shrink the net take below expectation. Calculating the specific tax including interest tax, which scope is fixed, and whether any special provision or amended claim applies, are a tax accountant's domain. This article stays with the framework and connects you to a tax accountant for the figures and the filing. When setting the sale schedule, it is safest to confirm with the tax accountant in advance "when the fixing trigger arises (at contract or at delivery, etc.)."

## Is there a lease (specified lease etc.) that keeps the deferral alive?

A lease is in principle a cancellation trigger, but certain leases set in law (specified leases etc.) keep the deferral going even if carried out. The National Tax Agency explains specified leases etc. as leases under the Act on Promotion of the Farmland Intermediary Management Business, the Act on Facilitating the Lease of Urban Farmland, and the Act on Special Provisions of the Farmland Act etc. for Specified Farmland Leases, among others (No. 4147, note).

| Example of a continuing lease | Cited basis law |
|---|---|
| A lease under the farmland intermediary management business | Act on Promotion of the Farmland Intermediary Management Business |
| A lease of urban farmland | Act on Facilitating the Lease of Urban Farmland |
| A specified farmland lease (community gardens etc.) | Act on Special Provisions of the Farmland Act etc. for Specified Farmland Leases |

Separately, there is also a "farming-difficulty lease" mechanism that keeps the deferral alive even when you lease out farmland because disability or illness has made it hard to keep farming yourself. Which lease you can use for your plot, whether you meet the requirements, and what notification is needed vary with the circumstances and the current system. For whether it applies and the procedure, confirm the tax side with a tax accountant and the rights-transfer/lease permit/notification side with an administrative scrivener. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I sell farmland under a deferral grant, will I always have to pay tax?**
A. Disposing of the deferred farmland etc. as a rule fixes the deferral deadline, and you pay the inheritance tax (principal) and interest tax for that part (Act on Special Measures art. 70-6). A disposal of 20% or less of the area is a partial fixing, over 20% a full fixing, as a base rule. The day two months pass after the triggering event is the start of the payment deadline. Whether it is actually fixed and how much you pay vary with the circumstances, so calculating the figure and filing are a tax accountant's domain. This article only shows the framework.

**Q. If cancelled, how much interest tax is charged?**
A. On cancellation (fixing), besides the principal you are charged interest tax for the period from the day after the inheritance-tax filing deadline to the fixing date (Act on Special Measures art. 70-6). The longer you were under the deferral, the longer the interest-tax period. The specific interest-tax rate, the calculation and any special provision need confirming with a tax accountant and the tax office. We do not step into the tax calculation; we connect you to a tax accountant.

**Q. Does leasing always cause cancellation? Can a specified lease keep it going?**
A. A lease is in principle a cancellation trigger, but where you carry out certain specified leases etc. under the Act on Promotion of the Farmland Intermediary Management Business, the Act on Facilitating the Lease of Urban Farmland, or the Act on Special Provisions of the Farmland Act etc. for Specified Farmland Leases, the deferral can continue (National Tax Agency Tax Answer No. 4147). There is also a farming-difficulty lease mechanism for when disability or illness makes farming hard. Whether you can use it for your plot, and the requirements and notifications, should be confirmed with a tax accountant for the tax side and an administrative scrivener for the rights-transfer/lease permit/notification side.

**Q. Which comes first, the sale or the Farmland Act permit?**
A. Selling farmland as farmland needs the agricultural committee's permit (Farmland Act art. 3); converting and selling needs the prefectural governor's etc. permit (art. 4, art. 5); inside an urbanization-promotion area a notification suffices. In a sale conditioned on a permit, it is common to contract with the permit as a condition precedent. The permit/notification procedure is the domain of an administrative scrivener and the agricultural committee / governor etc.; we handle the property survey, valuation and brokerage. It also helps to confirm with a tax accountant in advance whether the sale amounts to a deferral cancellation, to set the schedule.
