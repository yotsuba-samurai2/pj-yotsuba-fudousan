## Does a welfare-equipment rental/sales office have special property requirements?

It does — but unlike day care (a "facility users come to"), the requirement is an office with an attached storage area (warehouse). Art. 8(12) of the Long-Term Care Insurance Act defines "welfare-equipment rental" and Art. 8(13) defines "specified-welfare-equipment sales" as in-home services. The designation standard — the Standard on Personnel, Equipment and Operation for Designated In-Home Service Businesses (Ministry of Health and Welfare Ordinance No. 37 of 1999) — requires, for welfare-equipment rental in Art. 196 (equipment and articles), a dedicated area of a size necessary to run the business and the equipment and materials needed to store and disinfect welfare equipment. Specified-welfare-equipment sales, too, requires in its equipment provision (Art. 210) a dedicated area of the necessary size and equipment and articles.

Rental and sales weigh disinfection differently. Rental recovers used equipment and lends it out again, so disinfection equipment and materials, on top of storage, are the practical core. Specified-welfare-equipment sales targets items such as bathing and toileting aids provided in principle as new, so display and storage space is central. For personnel, welfare-equipment advisers must be assigned (rental = Art. 194, sales = Art. 208), but that is a point for the designation-application side (administrative scrivener / designating authority), not a condition of the property itself.

| Category | Basis | Equipment that matters on the property |
|---|---|---|
| Welfare-equipment rental | LTCI Act Art. 8(12); Standard Art. 196 | Dedicated area + storage + disinfection equipment/materials |
| Specified-welfare-equipment sales | LTCI Act Art. 8(13); Standard Art. 210 | Dedicated area + display/storage equipment |
| Personnel (advisers) | Standard Arts. 194, 208 | Not a property requirement (designation side) |

The final judgment on whether the designation standard is met and the designation application are handled by an administrative scrivener / designating authority; we check, on the lease side, whether the property can secure that area and equipment.

## How much space and what equipment do the disinfection room and storage need?

The standard sets no "X m² or more" floor-area figure for welfare-equipment storage or disinfection. Art. 196 requires a dedicated area of a size necessary to run the business, equipment and materials necessary to store welfare equipment, and equipment and materials necessary to disinfect it. The size is judged against the volume and kinds of equipment handled, and the concrete figure is confirmed by the designating authority's operation. Here, rather than the property's floor area itself, what is tested is whether the storage and disinfection sections can be built and kept clean.

The outsourcing exception matters. Art. 196 provides that where you have another business perform the storage or disinfection of welfare equipment, you need not hold the equipment or materials for that at the office. If you run disinfection by outsourcing it to a specialist, there is room to avoid installing large disinfection equipment at the office. Which disinfection method can "be expected to have an appropriate disinfection effect," and how to secure the hygiene level of the contractor, lie in the realm of the competent prefecture / designating authority's operation and hygiene standards, and confirming them before choosing a property reduces rework. We do not assert the individual approval of disinfection machinery or work flow here and leave it unverified, confirming with the competent authority.

| Item | What the standard requires | How the property side reads it |
|---|---|---|
| Storage equipment/materials | Hold what is needed (Art. 196) | Dedicated area matched to equipment volume; keep clean |
| Disinfection equipment/materials | Hold what is needed (Art. 196) | Can you take a section with water supply/drainage and ventilation? |
| Outsourcing exception | Need not hold if outsourced to another business | If disinfection is outsourced, equipment is lighter |
| Concrete disinfection method | No figure in the ordinance | Confirm with the authority's operation/hygiene standards (unverified) |

Take the individual disinfection/hygiene standards to the competent authority, the designation application to an administrative scrivener, and whether the property can build the disinfection/storage sections (fit-out, water supply/drainage) to us from the lease angle.

## Where do you tell, by use-district or floor area, which property cannot be used?

A welfare-equipment rental/sales office is basically judged as a combination of an "office" and a "warehouse" (storing one's own goods) that is the storage area, as a building use. Its position under the Building Standards Act differs from day care, where users spend the day (treated as a child-welfare or elderly-welfare facility). Whether an office/warehouse can be built in each use-district is decided by the use-districts of City Planning Act Arts. 8 and 9 and by Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act, and there are districts — such as Category 1 and 2 Exclusively Low-Rise Residential Districts — that restrict the scale of an office. Because storage holds one's own welfare equipment, it does not require a warehousing-business registration under the Warehousing Business Act.

When using an existing building as an office-plus-warehouse, you also look at whether a change-of-use confirmation application under Building Standards Act Arts. 6 and 87 is needed (as a rough guide, a special building after the change of use with a floor area over 200 m²). A welfare-equipment rental/sales office/warehouse often does not amount to a special building and may not need a confirmation application, but this varies with the building's scale and existing use, so confirmation with the specified administrative agency / an architect is the sure route. We do not assert the final conformity judgment on use-district or change of use here and confirm with an architect / the specified administrative agency.

| Check | Basis | What to look at |
|---|---|---|
| Can an office/warehouse be built in the use-district? | City Planning Act Arts. 8, 9; Building Standards Act App. Table 2 | Office-scale limits in low-rise residential districts |
| Need for warehousing-business registration | Warehousing Business Act | Not needed for storing one's own goods |
| Change-of-use confirmation application | Building Standards Act Arts. 6, 87 | Existing use and floor area (over 200 m² as a guide) |

The use-district / change-of-use judgment is made by an architect / the specified administrative agency; we check together, before the contract, whether a candidate property stands up on this point.

## What should you confirm in the lease and the important-matters explanation?

In the lease, first confirm that the contractual purpose of use includes "storage of welfare equipment / office" and that the fit-out of the disinfection section and the addition of water supply/drainage and ventilation fall within the scope of the lessor's consent. In the important-matters explanation (Building Lots and Buildings Transaction Business Act Art. 35), the explained matters are the registered rights, legal restrictions such as use-districts under the City Planning Act and Building Standards Act, private-road burdens, and the state of electricity/gas/water supply and drainage facilities. If your operation needs plumbing for disinfection, check the current state of water supply/drainage in both the important-matters explanation and on site.

Also settle, as contract terms, the scope of restoration, the burden of interior and equipment work to set up the storage section and disinfection equipment, and the removal obligation on move-out. Because the designation application asks for the office floor plan and documents showing the right to use (the lease, etc.), aligning the purpose of use and the drawings so they do not conflict with the designated area, together with the administrative scrivener who handles the designation application from the contract stage, avoids doing things twice. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Must a welfare-equipment rental office always build a disinfection room?**
A. A room named "disinfection room" is not mandated by the text. Designated In-Home Service Standard Art. 196 requires equipment and materials for storing and disinfecting welfare equipment while providing that, where you have another business perform storage or disinfection, you need not hold them at the office. If you outsource disinfection, the office equipment is lighter. Confirm the concrete disinfection method and hygiene standards with the competent designating authority.

**Q. For specified-welfare-equipment sales alone, is disinfection equipment unnecessary?**
A. Specified-welfare-equipment sales provides items such as bathing and toileting aids in principle as new, so storage/disinfection equipment like rental's is unlikely to be the central point; a display/storage dedicated area is the key (Standard Art. 210). But if you do both rental and sales at the same office, the rental-side storage/disinfection requirement applies. Confirm the judgment matched to your business design with the designating authority / administrative scrivener.

**Q. Can you set up a welfare-equipment rental/sales office in a property in a residential area?**
A. It depends on the use-district restriction for an office/warehouse. In Category 1 and 2 Exclusively Low-Rise Residential Districts, an office's scale is restricted and so on; approval and scale vary by district (City Planning Act Arts. 8, 9; Building Standards Act App. Table 2). A warehousing-business registration is not needed for storing one's own goods. Confirm the use-district / change-of-use conformity with the specified administrative agency / an architect; we look at a candidate property's viability with you before the contract.

**Q. Can you have one firm handle the property contract and the long-term-care-insurance designation application together?**
