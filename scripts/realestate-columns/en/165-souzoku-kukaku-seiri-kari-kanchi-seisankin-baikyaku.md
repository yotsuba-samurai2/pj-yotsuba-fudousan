## Can an inherited plot on a provisional replacement plot be sold without waiting for the disposition?

In short, yes, selling itself is possible. In a land-readjustment project, before carrying out the disposition the implementer may designate a provisional replacement plot for land in the project area when necessary for the works or for the disposition (Land Readjustment Act art. 98(1)). Ownership itself does not move to the provisional plot. Ownership in the register still attaches to the original plot, and it is that original plot's ownership that you sell.

What a provisional plot means is "an allocation of the place where you may use and profit." Art. 99 provides that, from the effective date of the provisional-plot designation until the public notice of the disposition (art. 103(4)), the person who could use and profit from the original plot by title may use and profit from the provisional plot as they would the original, and during that time may not use the original. The implementer may separately set the date on which use may begin (art. 99(2)), so there can be a period, during the works, when the provisional plot cannot yet be used.

| Term | Substance | Effect on the sale |
|---|---|---|
| Original plot | The pre-readjustment land recorded in the register | Object of ownership and registration; the thing sold |
| Provisional plot | The place usable until the disposition | What the buyer actually uses; watch the use-start date |
| Disposition | The act fixing the replacement plot in place of the original (art. 103) | From the day after the public notice, the replacement plot is treated as the original |
| Settlement money | The amount settling inequalities between plots (art. 110) | Collection or payment; set the bearer in the contract |

Even if inheritance registration is not yet done right after the death, survey and valuation can proceed in parallel. Inheritance registration (including the duty in force on 1 April 2024) and estate division belong to a judicial scrivener / administrative scrivener; we advance the survey of the property condition and of the provisional-plot and settlement-money status.

## Do you sell the buyer the original plot or the provisional plot, and how do registration and handover work?

The object of sale is the original plot's ownership, and the ownership-transfer registration is also made against the original plot. Because what the buyer actually uses is the provisional plot, the contract must not confuse the two layers: "sell the original plot (ownership), hand over the provisional plot for use."

When the disposition is carried out, from the day after its public notice the replacement plot fixed in the replacement plan is treated as the original plot (art. 104(1)). The implementer then makes the registration for the replacement plot collectively, so even if you register in the original plot's description at the time of sale, after the disposition the description moves to the replacement plot. That is exactly why, at the time of sale, you prepare registration and the important-matters explanation on the original plot's lot number and area, and confirm the provisional-plot designation (position, area, use-start date) with the implementer to explain to the buyer.

| Check item | Why it matters | Where to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Provisional-plot designation | Position, area and use-start date change what is handed over | The implementer's provisional-plot designation notice |
| Whether use has begun | During the works, the provisional plot may not yet be usable (art. 99(2)) | The implementer |
| Expected timing of the disposition | From the day after the public notice, the replacement plot is treated as the original (art. 104(1)) | The implementer's project / replacement plan |
| Reserved land / reduction (減歩) | Reserved land for project costs and reduction change the plot | The implementer's project plan |

In the important-matters explanation under Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35, you explain to the buyer the provisional-plot designation and the restrictions under the Land Readjustment Act. These must be verified by inquiry to the implementer, and that is within our survey and brokerage. The registration work is a judicial scrivener's; confirming boundaries (筆界) and lot area is a land/house surveyor's.

## Does the seller or the buyer bear the settlement money (collection / payment), and how to think about capital gains?

Settlement money evens out the inequalities that arise when fixing replacement plots (differences in value between the original plot and the replacement plot, etc.). Under Land Readjustment Act art. 104(8) it is fixed at the time of the public notice of the disposition, and the implementer must collect, or pay, the fixed settlement money (art. 110(1)). Collection is the owner's side paying; payment is receiving.

When selling at the provisional-plot stage, the issue is "to whom—seller or buyer—does the not-yet-fixed settlement money belong." Because the law does not decide the allocation between the parties, in practice the sale contract clearly sets who bears / receives the settlement money (collection / payment). The estimated amount and whether settlement money will arise are confirmed with the implementer, and the unknown parts are written into the contract as "unresolved." Not writing an unfixed amount as though it were fixed prevents later trouble.

| Issue | How it is organised | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Whether / how much settlement money | Inquire with the implementer; if unresolved, state so in the contract | Implementer / us (inquiry and reflecting it in the contract) |
| Bearer of collection / payment | The law does not set allocation; set it in the sale contract | Us (contract terms) / lawyer (if disputed) |
| Tax on the settlement money | Income classification and acquisition-cost treatment of payment / collection | Tax accountant |
| Capital gains | Acquisition cost, transfer expenses and the applicability of special provisions for the original plot | Tax accountant |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I sell on a provisional plot, without waiting for the disposition?**
A. Selling itself is possible. A provisional-plot designation is an allocation of the place where you may use and profit; ownership itself stays with the original plot, and ownership in the register also stays on the original plot (art. 98(1)). The object of sale is the original plot's ownership, and you hand the buyer the provisional plot for use. But if the implementer has separately set a use-start date, there can be a period during the works when the provisional plot cannot yet be used (art. 99(2)), so confirm the designation and the start date with the implementer and explain them to the buyer.

**Q. Do I sell the buyer the original plot or the provisional plot? What happens to registration?**
A. The object of sale is the original plot's ownership, and the ownership-transfer registration is also made against the original plot. From the day after the public notice of the disposition, the replacement plot fixed in the replacement plan is treated as the original plot (art. 104(1)), and the implementer then makes the registration for the replacement plot collectively. So you prepare registration and the important-matters explanation on the original plot's lot number and area at the time of sale, and the description moves to the replacement plot after the disposition. The registration work is a judicial scrivener's; confirming boundaries and lot area is a land/house surveyor's.

**Q. Does the seller or the buyer bear the settlement money?**
A. Settlement money evens out inequalities between plots; it is fixed at the time of the public notice of the disposition, and the implementer collects or pays it (art. 104(8), art. 110(1)). Collection is paying; payment is receiving. When selling at the provisional-plot stage, because the law does not set the seller/buyer allocation, in practice the sale contract clearly sets the bearer / receiver. The estimated amount and whether settlement money will arise are confirmed with the implementer, and the unknown parts are written in as "unresolved." The tax treatment is a tax accountant's field.

**Q. Can I prepare for a sale even if inheritance registration and estate division are not yet done?**
A. The survey and valuation can proceed in parallel. However, before the sale contract and settlement, inheritance registration (including the duty to apply in force on 1 April 2024) and, if there are several heirs, estate division, are premises. Inheritance registration is a judicial scrivener's field, drafting the estate-division agreement an administrative scrivener's, and a dispute over division a lawyer's.
