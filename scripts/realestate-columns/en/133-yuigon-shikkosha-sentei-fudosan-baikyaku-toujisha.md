**The key point**: When selling inherited real estate with an executor, who the party (seller) to the sale is changes with the form of the will. Under a "bequeath by inheritance" clause (a specific-property succession will), the heir who acquired the property is the seller, and the executor handles perfection of title such as inheritance registration. In a liquidation-type devise that sells the estate and distributes the proceeds, the executor carries out the sale. Interpretation of the will and the scope of authority go to a lawyer and registration to a judicial scrivener; the brokerage is ours.

Having acquired real estate under a notarised will, or having been designated or appointed as executor, you may wonder "do I sign the contract, or does the executor," and "who stands at the settlement." Whether there is a will, and how the will is worded ("bequeath by inheritance" or "devise"), changes both the party to the sale and the registration flow.

This article organises, from the real-estate viewpoint, who stands as a party to the brokerage, the sale contract and the settlement when an executor is involved in selling inherited real estate. Judging the will's validity or the scope of the executor's authority is the field of the lawyer; this article is general information only.

## When there is an executor, who is the seller and the signatory to the sale contract?

First, grasp the executor's authority. Article 1012(1) of the Civil Code provides that the executor has the right and duty to perform all acts necessary to administer the estate and otherwise execute the will, in order to realise its contents. Article 1012(2) provides that where there is an executor, only the executor may perform a devise (amended by Act No. 72 of 2018, in force from 1 July 2019).

Who becomes the seller depends on how the will succeeds the property.

| Form of the will | Succession of the property | Seller / signatory to the sale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Bequeath by inheritance" (specific-property succession will) | Succeeds to the heir automatically at the opening of inheritance | The heir who acquired it (the beneficiary heir) |
| Devise (specific devise) | The devisee acquires by the devise | After acquisition, the devisee; the devise is performed by the executor |
| Liquidation-type devise (to sell and distribute) | The executor liquidates and distributes the proceeds | The executor carries out the sale |

Article 1015 of the Civil Code provides that an act done by the executor within authority, indicating that they are the executor, takes effect directly against the heirs. So in a scene like a liquidation-type sale where the executor carries out the sale, the executor stands "as executor" as a party to the contract. Conversely, where a specific heir acquired the property under a "bequeath by inheritance" clause and sells it at their own discretion, the seller is that heir.

Which category applies, and the interpretation of the will's wording, is ultimately the field of the lawyer. Looking at the copy of the will and the registration status, we organise who is likely to be the party to the sale and connect you to a lawyer and judicial scrivener for points that need settling. ## How do the sale and registration flows differ between a specific-property succession will and a devise?

The registration flow that underlies the sale also changes with the form of the will. The 2021 amendment to the Real Property Registration Act (Act No. 24 of 2021) made part of the registration of transfer by devise applicable by a sole application (in force from 1 April 2023). However, the sole application is limited to a devise to an heir; a devise to a non-heir remains a joint application as before.

| Form of the will | Cause of registration | Form of application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Bequeath by inheritance" (specific-property succession will) | Inheritance | The heir applies for inheritance registration alone; the executor may also perfect title (Civil Code art. 1014(2)) |
| Devise to an heir | Devise | The devisee (heir) may apply alone (Real Property Registration Act art. 63(3), in force 1 April 2023) |
| Devise to a non-heir | Devise | Joint application by the devisee and the heir (or executor) |

Article 1014(2) of the Civil Code provides that where there is a specific-property succession will, the executor may do what is necessary to perfect title. To transfer ownership to the buyer, it is usual to put the registration in the name of the beneficiary heir or devisee as a precondition to selling. In a liquidation-type devise, the order can become complex, such as passing through the heir's name before the transfer registration by sale.

Judging the cause, order and feasibility of a sole application, and acting as agent for the application, is the work of the judicial scrivener. We do not act as agent for registration. We proceed while confirming with the judicial scrivener which registration to settle first, to fit the sale schedule.

## How do the executor and the beneficiary heir relate to the brokerage, identity verification and the settlement?

Selling real estate follows the steps of a brokerage agreement (Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 34-2), the important-matters explanation (art. 35) and delivery of the sale contract (art. 37). Where an executor is involved, it is essential to settle at the outset who the parties to these are.

| Scene | Who is the party (varies by category) |
| --- | --- |
| Brokerage agreement | The person who is the seller (the beneficiary heir, or in a liquidation type, the executor) |
| Identity verification (Act on Prevention of Transfer of Criminal Proceeds) | Both the party to the contract and the executor who acts as agent |
| Important-matters explanation | Explanation to the buyer; the seller side discloses the rights, the will and the registration status |
| Settlement and handover | The person who is the seller receives payment; in a liquidation type, the executor receives and distributes the proceeds |

Identity verification is carried out by the real-estate broker under the Act on Prevention of Transfer of Criminal Proceeds. Where the executor contracts as seller, in addition to verifying the executor personally, we confirm materials showing they were designated or appointed as executor (the will, the family court's appointment ruling, etc.). Where the beneficiary heir is the seller, we verify that heir's identity and settle the basis on which they acquired the property (the will, the registration).

At the brokerage stage, we organise from the will and registration who becomes the seller, and in the important-matters explanation we disclose the will, the executor's involvement and the registration status to the buyer. Where a dispute over the will's validity or the existence of authority is foreseeable, we ask you to consult a lawyer before we take on the brokerage.

## What happens with a sale different from the will's contents, or if an heir opposes the sale?

Where there is an executor, the heirs cannot dispose freely. Article 1013(1) of the Civil Code provides that where there is an executor, an heir may not dispose of the estate or otherwise do an act that would obstruct the execution of the will; Article 1013(2) provides that an act in violation is void (though it cannot be asserted against a good-faith third party).

| Situation | How it is organised |
| --- | --- |
| An heir tries to sell without the executor's knowledge | Obstructive acts are restricted under art. 1013; an unauthorised disposition is in principle void |
| An heir opposes the sale | Where within the executor's authority, as in a liquidation type, the executor may proceed with the sale |
| You want to sell with contents different from the will | Whether the estate division can be re-done by the agreement of all heirs is a lawyer's judgment |
| There is a dispute over the will's validity | Consult a lawyer before selling; we may be unable to take on the brokerage until validity is settled |

Whether, in such scenes—an opposing heir, a disputed will's validity, an unclear scope of the executor's authority—a sale is possible is itself an individual legal judgment. Neither we nor any unqualified person may assert "it can be sold" or "it cannot." Disputes among heirs and interpretation of the will go to a lawyer, and registration to a judicial scrivener.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. I acquired real estate under a "bequeath by inheritance" will. Is the seller me?**
A. Under a specific-property succession will ("bequeath by inheritance"), the property succeeds to that heir automatically at the opening of inheritance, so in principle the acquiring heir is the seller. The executor has authority to do what is necessary to perfect title (such as inheritance registration) (Civil Code art. 1014(2)). If the wording is unclear, confirm with a lawyer.

**Q. The will says the executor is to sell and distribute (a liquidation type). Who makes the contract?**
A. In a liquidation type that sells the estate and distributes the proceeds, the executor proceeds with the sale to realise the will's contents (Civil Code arts. 1012(1) and 1015). The executor stands "as executor" as a party to the contract. We proceed while confirming the order of registration and distribution with a judicial scrivener and tax accountant.

**Q. One of the heirs opposes the sale. Can it be sold?**
A. The treatment changes depending on whether it is a sale within the executor's authority (such as a liquidation type) or a sale by the acquiring heir themselves, and whether the will's validity is disputed. Article 1013 of the Civil Code restricts obstructive acts by heirs where there is an executor. Because feasibility is an individual legal judgment, consult a lawyer. We handle the brokerage side.

**Q. Can I apply for the registration of the devised property by myself?**
A. Under Article 63(3) of the Real Property Registration Act, in force from 1 April 2023, a devise to an heir lets the devisee apply alone for the transfer registration. A devise to a non-heir remains a joint application as before. Judging the feasibility and acting as agent for the application is the judicial scrivener's work, so we connect you there for the actual application.
