## Where can a medical mall be located under zoning?

A clinic is one of the few facilities that can be built in every zoning district under Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act, including the Category 1 low-rise exclusive residential district. A "hospital," by contrast, cannot be built in the Category 1 and 2 low-rise exclusive residential districts, the rural-residential district, the industrial district or the exclusive industrial district. A medical mall is usually a cluster of clinics without inpatient facilities (or with 19 beds or fewer), so it is treated as a group of "clinics" rather than a "hospital," and the zoning wall is relatively low—that is the practical starting point.

The dividing line is "hospital or clinic." Under the Medical Care Act, a facility with 20 or more inpatient beds is a "hospital"; one without beds or with 19 or fewer is a "clinic." If a specialty with beds (1–19) enters the mall, the building is still a "clinic," but the fire-service and barrier-free standards described below differ from those for a bedless clinic. Zoning eligibility and the facility standards required of the building are separate layers, so check them separately.

| Layer to check | Clinic (no beds) | Clinic (with beds) | Hospital (20+ beds) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zoning (Building Standards Act App. Table 2) | Buildable in all zoning districts | Buildable in all zoning districts | Not in low-rise residential, rural-residential, industrial or exclusive-industrial |
| General position in a medical mall | Standard tenant | Fire-service and barrier-free rules get heavier | Beyond the scope of a mall |

## Where in a multi-tenant building does the barrier-free law apply?

The Act on Promotion of Smooth Transportation for the Elderly, Persons with Disabilities and others (the barrier-free law) varies the weight of the obligation by use. A hospital or clinic with inpatient facilities (i.e., with beds) is a "special designated building"; when newly built, extended, altered or converted at the scale prescribed by Cabinet Order (a total floor area of 2,000 sqm or more; Order Art. 9), it must conform to the building accessibility standards (Act Art. 14(1)). This conformity is examined within the building-confirmation procedure.

A bedless clinic, on the other hand, is positioned as a "designated building," and the building owner has an endeavour obligation to conform to the standards (Art. 16). A medical mall is judged by the combined floor area of its tenants and common areas (waiting rooms, corridors, toilets, elevators), so each unit may be small while the whole building exceeds 2,000 sqm. Moreover, many local governments add stricter target uses and area thresholds by ordinance under Act Art. 14(3). Which standard applies varies with the local ordinance and building scale, so conformity design and examination are the architect's field. We organise the property-side facts of floor area and use.

| Building character | Barrier-free treatment | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Includes a clinic with beds and is 2,000 sqm or more | Obligation to conform to the accessibility standards | Act Art. 14(1); Order Art. 9 |
| Clinic without beds | Endeavour obligation to conform | Act Art. 16 |
| Subject under local ordinance | Uses and areas may be added | Act Art. 14(3) (delegation to ordinance) |

## How do fire-service notifications and divisions split between common areas and each clinic?

Article 17 of the Fire Service Act imposes on the parties to a fire-defence object prescribed by Cabinet Order (owner, manager, occupier) the duty to install and maintain fire-defence equipment in accordance with the technical standards. The use classification of fire-defence objects is set in Appended Table 1 of the Order for Enforcement; hospitals, clinics with beds and midwifery homes with beds fall under item (6)(a), while bedless clinics and bedless midwifery homes fall under item (6)(c) (the classification was reorganized by the 2016 Cabinet Order amendment). A building that holds several uses and tenants in one structure, like a medical mall, is in principle judged as a mixed-use fire-defence object (App. Table 1 item (16)) across the whole building including common areas. It is not decided by looking at each tenant unit alone and saying "we are small, so nothing is needed."

Whether a clinic with beds enters changes the standard greatly. Item (6)(a) facilities with inpatients hold users who find self-evacuation difficult, so the standards for sprinkler systems, automatic fire alarms and fire-report devices are heavier than for a bedless facility (a 2016 Order amendment extended some duties to bedless clinics too). The notifications on installation or alteration of fire-defence equipment and the appointment of a fire-prevention manager split between the common areas (the whole building—often the owner or manager) and each tenant (each occupier). Because the specific standards and filing windows are for the competent fire station to judge, a prior consultation with it at the design stage is the starting point.

| Division | Who often handles it | Typical target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-building fire-defence equipment (sprinklers, alarms) | Owner / manager | Common-area and building-wide equipment |
| Each tenant's extinguishers, guide lights, interior fire-proofing | Each tenant (occupier) | Equipment and interior restrictions within the unit |
| Appointment of a fire-prevention manager, fire plan | Both, per occupancy capacity | Whole object / unit |

## Who files each clinic's opening notification and X-ray-device installation notification, and when?

Even in a medical mall, the opening procedure is separate for each clinic. Article 8 of the Medical Care Act provides that when a physician or dentist who has completed clinical training opens a clinic, they must notify the prefectural governor of the location (the city or ward mayor where a public health centre is set up) within 10 days of opening. By contrast, Article 7 requires that a non-physician/non-dentist (including a medical corporation) who intends to open a clinic, or the establishment of beds for a hospital or a clinic with beds, obtain the prefectural governor's opening permission in advance. If a mall tenant is a branch of a medical corporation, the procedure is an opening permission, not a notification.

A clinic with a diagnostic X-ray device needs a separate notification. Under Article 15(3) of the Medical Care Act and Article 24-2 of its Enforcement Regulations, when a diagnostic X-ray device with a rated-output tube voltage of 10 kV or more and energy below 1 MeV is installed, the notification is filed within 10 days of installation. If specialties performing imaging, such as orthopaedics or internal medicine, enter as tenants, this notification arises per specialty. Preparing these opening and installation notifications and establishing tenant medical corporations are the administrative scrivener's work; we do not act for them.

| Procedure | Basis | Deadline / nature | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic opening notification (clinical-training-completed physician) | Medical Care Act Art. 8 | Notification within 10 days of opening | Each clinic (documents by administrative scrivener) |
| Clinic opening permission (medical corporation, non-physician opener, etc.) | Medical Care Act Art. 7 | Prior permission | Each clinic (documents by administrative scrivener) |
| Diagnostic X-ray device installation notification | Medical Care Act Art. 15(3); Enforcement Reg. Art. 24-2 | Within 10 days of installation | The specialty (documents by administrative scrivener) |

A medical mall gets stuck later on interiors, plumbing and electrical capacity if it proceeds with a blurred responsibility boundary between the building side (owner) and each tenant (opening doctor). Lead shielding and floor loading for an X-ray room, heavy water supply and drainage for dentistry or dialysis, electrical capacity for imaging equipment and X-ray, and flow separation for infection control are all governed by the skeleton and infrastructure conditions at contract. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a medical mall be located in a Category 1 low-rise exclusive residential district?**
A. As a clinic, yes—under Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act it can be built in all zoning districts, including the Category 1 low-rise exclusive residential district. But a "hospital" (20 or more inpatient beds) cannot be built in the low-rise residential, rural-residential, industrial or exclusive-industrial districts. If the mall is a cluster of bedless or bedded clinics, the zoning wall is low, but the building's facility standards still apply separately. Zoning eligibility is for the specified administrative agency to judge; we organise the property-side facts.

**Q. Does the barrier-free law apply even if each tenant is small?**
A. It can. The barrier-free scale is judged by the whole building's floor area including common areas, so even if each unit is small, exceeding 2,000 sqm in total (Order Art. 9) triggers the conformity obligation (Act Art. 14(1)) where a bedded clinic is included. A bedless clinic has an endeavour obligation (Art. 16), and ordinances may add more. Conformity design and examination are the architect's field.

**Q. Are fire-service notifications filed per tenant?**
A. They split. A medical mall is judged building-wide as a mixed-use fire-defence object, so whole-building equipment such as sprinklers and automatic fire alarms is generally the owner's or manager's, while each unit's extinguishers, guide lights and interior restrictions are each tenant's. Standards get heavier when a bedded clinic enters. The specific division is for the competent fire station to judge, and a prior consultation at the design stage is the starting point.

**Q. Who files each specialty's opening notification, and when?**
A. Opening is separate per clinic. When a clinical-training-completed physician opens a clinic, a notification is filed within 10 days of opening (Medical Care Act Art. 8); a medical corporation or non-physician opener needs a prior opening permission (Art. 7). If an X-ray device is installed, an installation notification is also filed within 10 days (Enforcement Reg. Art. 24-2) per specialty. Preparing these documents and establishing a medical corporation are the administrative scrivener's work.
