## What is the "decedent's residence requirement" of the inherited-vacant-house ¥30 million special deduction?

The inherited-vacant-house ¥30 million special deduction is a measure that lets an heir, when selling "a house in which the decedent lived alone just before the inheritance began," deduct up to ¥30 million (in certain cases ¥20 million) from the capital gain (Act on Special Measures Concerning Taxation Art. 35). One of the keys is this "residence requirement."

As conditions on the house side, it must have been built on or before 31 May 1981 (old earthquake standard), must not be a building registered under sectional ownership, and just before the inheritance began there must have been no resident other than the decedent (living alone) (NTA Tax Answer No. 3306). Further, it must not have been used for business, letting or residence from the time of inheritance until the time of sale. In other words, if it is rented out or someone lives there after the inheritance, it is in principle excluded. The deduction falls to a maximum of ¥20 million where, for a transfer on or after 1 January 2024, three or more heirs acquired the decedent's residential house, etc.

| Category | Example requirement |
|---|---|
| Year built | Built on or before 31 May 1981 |
| Type of building | Not registered under sectional ownership (a detached house, etc.) |
| Residence status | No resident other than the decedent just before the inheritance began |
| Use after inheritance | Not used for business, letting or residence |
| Deduction amount | Up to ¥30 million (¥20 million if three or more acquiring heirs for a transfer from 2024) |

Individual applicability is confirmed by a tax accountant against the NTA page and the statute.

## Does a family home where the decedent had entered an elderly-care facility qualify, and from which amendment did it change?

There is room for it to qualify. Originally this measure presupposed "a house in which the decedent lived just before the inheritance began," but the FY2019 (Reiwa 1) amendment extended the scope to cover the case where the decedent had entered an elderly-care facility and the house had become vacant, treating the residence requirement as met if certain conditions are satisfied. It applies to transfers on or after 1 April 2019.

The requirements organised by NTA Tax Answer No. 3307 are as follows. (1) Just before the inheritance began, the decedent — who had received long-term-care certification, support-required certification, etc. — had entered/was residing in an elderly nursing home, a special nursing home for the elderly, a fee-charging home for the elderly, a serviced housing for the elderly, a long-term-care health facility, a dwelling for dementia-type communal daily-life care, etc. (the presence of certification is judged at the point just before the house ceased to be used as a residence). (2) From the time it ceased to be a residence until just before the inheritance began, the house continued to be used for storing the decedent's belongings, etc., and was not used for business, letting or residence by anyone other than the decedent. (3) From entry until just before the inheritance began, the house the decedent is recognised to have mainly resided in is that elderly-care facility. The basis is Act on Special Measures Concerning Taxation Art. 35, Enforcement Order Arts. 20-3 and 23, Enforcement Regulation Art. 18-2, and Circular 35-9-2.

| Point | Content |
|---|---|
| Amendment | FY2019 (Reiwa 1) amendment |
| Application start | Transfers on or after 1 April 2019 |
| Timing of the certification test | Just before the house ceased to be a residence |
| Example facilities | Elderly nursing / special nursing / fee-charging homes, serviced housing for the elderly, long-term-care health facilities, etc. |

Moving in for care to a child's home, etc. — a move to somewhere other than an elderly-care facility — is in principle excluded. The final applicability judgment is made by a tax accountant.

## By what documents do you prove the long-term-care certification, the fact of entry, and the maintenance of the vacant house?

You back it up with documents. You need materials showing that long-term-care certification etc. had been received, that the decedent had entered an elderly-care facility, and that the vacant house was maintained without being rented out etc. from entry until the inheritance began. In practice, you obtain the "confirmation of the decedent's residential house, etc." issued by the municipality where the property is located and attach it to the tax return.

Long-term-care certification and support-required certification are certifications under the Long-Term Care Insurance Act (Long-Term Care Insurance Act Art. 19), confirmed by the insured-person card or the certification-result notice. The fact of entry is shown by the entry contract with the facility and materials showing the period of entry, and the maintenance of the vacant house by electricity/water usage and management records. Which document proves what, and what the confirmation application requires, are confirmed by the municipality and a tax accountant. We help with guidance on the documents needed for the sale and the arrangement of preparation to fit the sale schedule.

| Fact to prove | Main documents | Confirm / issued by |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term-care certification, etc. | Long-term-care insurance insured-person card, certification-result notice | Municipality / tax accountant |
| Fact of entry | Facility entry contract, materials on the entry period | Facility / municipality |
| Maintenance of the vacant house (unused) | Electricity/water usage, management records | Heir / municipality |
| Confirmation of the whole measure | Confirmation of the decedent's residential house, etc. | Issued by the municipality (where the property is located) |

## Does earthquake retrofitting or demolition let you use the measure, and until when is the sale deadline?

Because the house being sold is old-earthquake-standard (built on or before 31 May 1981), to sell it as is it was in principle necessary either to make it conform to the earthquake standard by the time of sale, or to demolish the house and sell as vacant land. For transfers on or after 1 January 2024, it has been relaxed so that the case may also qualify where, between the time of sale and 15 February of the year following the year of sale, it comes to conform to the earthquake standard or the whole of the house is demolished, etc. (NTA Tax Answer No. 3306). This created room for cases where the retrofit or demolition is done after handover, but because the specific application of who does it and when varies by case, a tax accountant confirms it.

The sale deadline is to sell by 31 December of the year in which the day three years from the day the inheritance began falls. In addition, as the measure's overall application period, transfers between 1 April 2016 and 31 December 2027 are covered. You must complete the sale by the earlier of these two deadlines.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Retrofit or demolition | Being old-earthquake-standard, in principle earthquake conformity or demolition (vacant-land sale) is required |
| Relaxation from 2024 | Earthquake conformity / full demolition etc. by 15 February of the following year may also qualify |
| Sale deadline | By 31 December of the year in which the day three years from the start of inheritance falls |
| Application period of the measure | Transfers from 1 April 2016 to 31 December 2027 |

Which of retrofitting or demolition is more advantageous (cost, the fixed-asset tax after clearing to vacant land, the buyer segment) is organised by us as a comparison of ways to sell; the final tax determination is made by a tax accountant.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can the ¥30 million deduction be used even for a family home that became vacant after my parent entered a special nursing home for the elderly?**
A. There is room for it. It requires that, just before the inheritance began, the decedent who had received long-term-care certification etc. had entered an elderly-care facility, that the vacant house was maintained without being rented out etc. from entry until the inheritance began, and that the decedent lived alone, among other requirements (NTA Tax Answer No. 3307; Act on Special Measures Concerning Taxation Art. 35, Enforcement Order Art. 23). The final applicability judgment is made by a tax accountant.

**Q. What if my parent left the family home for care and lived together with me, the child, at my home?**
A. A move to somewhere other than an elderly-care facility is in principle outside the scope of this residence-requirement special rule. What qualifies is the case where a decedent who had received long-term-care certification etc. had entered an elderly-care facility. Individual applicability is confirmed by a tax accountant against the NTA page and the statute.

**Q. How do I prove that long-term-care certification was received and that the vacant house was maintained?**
A. You show it with the long-term-care insurance insured-person card or the certification-result notice, the facility entry contract, electricity/water usage, etc., and obtain the "confirmation of the decedent's residential house, etc." issued by the municipality where the property is located to attach to the tax return. Confirm the required documents with the municipality and a tax accountant.

**Q. By when must I sell to be in time?**
A. The requirement is to sell by 31 December of the year in which the day three years from the day the inheritance began falls, and together with the measure's overall application period (transfers up to 31 December 2027) you must complete the sale by the earlier of them (NTA Tax Answer No. 3306). We build the sale schedule working back from the deadline, and the tax is confirmed by a tax accountant.
