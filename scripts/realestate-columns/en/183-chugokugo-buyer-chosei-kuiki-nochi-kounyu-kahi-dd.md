## Can an urbanisation-control-zone property even be rebuilt or have its use changed?

An urbanisation-control zone is a division set, for a city-planning area, as an area where urbanisation should be curbed (City Planning Act Art. 7). Here, new building, rebuilding and change of use are not freely allowed. A development act that changes the shape or character of land for the purpose of building within a control zone in principle needs the prefectural governor's development permission (Art. 29(1)). Even building without a development act (rebuilding on existing residential land, etc.) in principle needs the governor's permission outside a development area that has received development permission (Art. 43(1)).

Whether permission is granted depends on fitting one of the items of Art. 34, the locational standard. Examples are buildings for agriculture, forestry or fisheries; shops needed for the daily life of nearby residents; buildings within an area designated by ordinance (Art. 34(xi), (xii)); and those approved as causing no obstacle through the Development Examination Commission's deliberation (Art. 34(xiv)); but which item allows permission varies by the municipality's practice. Buying just "because it is cheap," if permission for rebuilding or change of use is not granted, greatly narrows the usable options.

| What to check | Basis | If unmet |
|---|---|---|
| Area division (is it a control zone) | City Planning Act Art. 7 | An area curbing urbanisation |
| Development act for building | Art. 29(1) | In principle, the governor's development permission |
| Building without development | Art. 43(1) | Outside a development area, in principle the governor's permission |
| Fitting the locational standard | Art. 34 items | If not, permission is hard to get |

## Whose permission is needed to buy farmland, and can an overseas buyer meet the requirements?

To acquire farmland (land used for cultivation) as farmland, the agricultural committee's permission is needed (Farmland Act Art. 3(1)). This permission has requirements, and it is refused if a case under the items of Art. 3(2) applies. The main ones are: being recognised as using the farmland, including the acquired land, efficiently (the full-efficient-use requirement); constantly engaging in the farm work needed for the cultivation done after acquisition (the constant-engagement requirement); and causing no obstacle to the agricultural use of farmland in the surrounding area (the area-harmony requirement). The guide for constant engagement is said to be about 150 days a year, but the treatment varies by municipality (the day count's positioning varies by agricultural committee; unverified).

This is the wall for an overseas buyer. Because Art. 3 permission in principle presupposes that the acquirer continuously engages in farming themselves, a buyer living overseas who does not constantly engage in the local farm work tends not to meet the requirement. Note that the lower-area requirement (the separately set area, in principle 50 ares, etc.) once required for permission was abolished on 1 April 2023 by the Act partially amending the Act on Promotion of Improvement of Agricultural Management Foundation and others (Act No. 56 of 2022), but other requirements such as constant engagement remain.

To use farmland by changing it to residential land, etc., a separate conversion permission is needed: Art. 4 for converting one's own farmland oneself, Art. 5 for transferring a right for conversion purposes (both in principle the prefectural governor, or the mayor in a designated municipality). However, for farmland within an urbanisation-promotion area, Arts. 4 and 5 are satisfied by notification to the agricultural committee rather than permission. Whether conversion is allowed varies greatly by the farmland's classification (locational standard): farmland in an agricultural-use zone, Class-A farmland and Class-1 farmland are in principle not permitted, while Class-3 farmland is in principle permitted (the classification is judged by the agricultural committee; unverified).

| Transaction type | Provision | Permitting authority, etc. |
|---|---|---|
| Acquiring as farmland (right transfer) | Farmland Act Art. 3 | Agricultural committee (constant-engagement, etc.) |
| Converting one's own farmland | Farmland Act Art. 4 | In principle the governor (notification in promotion areas) |
| Right transfer for conversion | Farmland Act Art. 5 | In principle the governor (notification in promotion areas) |

## How do you tell apart control-zone property you can live on (branch-family housing, ordinance-designated areas)?

Even in an urbanisation-control zone there is property usable as housing, but much of it has conditions on "who lives there." The former "existing residential land" system (building possible by notification) was abolished on 18 May 2001, so today the individual locational standard governs. Representative cases are branch-family housing and housing within an area designated by ordinance.

Branch-family housing is housing built for a member of the household of a farming family, etc. that has owned land in the control zone since before the line-drawing (area division), to live independently, based on the prefecture's Development Examination Commission standards or an ordinance. It is strongly personal — "permitted because it is that person" — so a third party who buys it cannot necessarily live in it as is. Buildings within an ordinance-designated area (Art. 34(xi), (xii)) have their area, use and scale finely set by each ordinance. In either case, "a building currently stands = I too can rebuild and live" does not necessarily hold, so confirm the basis of permission (which item, which ordinance, which commission standard) for each property. For the personal nature of branch-family housing and selling it, see [an inherited branch-family farmhouse and its personal nature](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-bunke-nouka-jutaku-zokujinsei-baikyaku).

| Basis for living | Basis / practice | Overseas buyer's caution |
|---|---|---|
| Branch-family housing | Examination Commission standards / ordinance | Strongly personal; constrains third-party succession |
| Housing in an ordinance-designated area | Art. 34(xi), (xii) | Area, use and scale limited by ordinance |
| Former "existing residential land" | System abolished in 2001 | Today judged by the individual locational standard |

## Before contracting, at which counter do you confirm the city-planning and farmland classification?

Before buying, confirm three things: (1) the city-planning area division (promotion or control zone; presence of a use zone); (2) if a control zone, the prospect of permission for building or change of use; and (3) if farmland, its classification and which of the Art. 3, 4 or 5 permissions/notifications applies. These are split across several counters.

The area division, use zone and prospect of development permission are confirmed at the municipality's city-planning section and development-permission section. Whether it is farmland (not just the registered land category but the actual state), its classification, and the possibility of Art. 3 permission or conversion permission are confirmed at the municipality's agricultural committee. Note that even if the registered category is "residential land," if the actual state is farmland, the Farmland Act applies; conversely, even if the category is "field," it may be judged non-farmland. Do not finish by word of mouth; cross-check with the important-matters explanation. Counter names and handling vary by municipality (unverified).

| What to confirm | Counter | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Area division, use zone | City-planning section | City Planning Act Art. 7, etc. |
| Prospect of development/building permission | Development-permission section | Arts. 29, 34, 43 |
| Farmland classification, right transfer/conversion | Agricultural committee | Farmland Act Arts. 3, 4, 5 |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a foreigner buy an urbanisation-control-zone property?**
A. There is no nationality restriction on acquiring ownership (buying) itself. The issue is not "can I buy" but "can I build or live on it." Building, rebuilding or change of use in a control zone in principle needs development permission (City Planning Act Art. 29) or building permission (Art. 43), and is hard to obtain if it does not fit the locational standard (Art. 34 items). Confirm the prospect of permission before buying.

**Q. I want to buy farmland and build a house; will I be permitted while living overseas?**
A. Acquiring farmland as farmland needs the agricultural committee's permission (Farmland Act Art. 3), and because constant engagement in farm work after acquisition is required, a buyer living overseas who does not engage tends not to meet the requirement. Building a house needs a separate conversion permission (Arts. 4, 5), and depending on the farmland's classification it is in principle not permitted. Confirm the classification and possibility with the agricultural committee.

**Q. I heard the lower-area requirement was abolished. Has farmland become easier to buy?**
A. The lower-area requirement (the separately set area) was abolished on 1 April 2023 by Act No. 56 of 2022. However, the constant-engagement, full-efficient-use and area-harmony requirements remain. Even as the area wall falls, it is unchanged that an overseas buyer tends not to meet the constant-engagement requirement.

**Q. If the registered land category is "residential land," is the Farmland Act irrelevant?**
A. It may be relevant. Whether land is farmland is judged by the actual state, not only the registered category. Even if the category is "residential land," if it is actually cultivated farmland, the Farmland Act applies; conversely, even if the category is "field," it may be judged non-farmland. Confirm the actual state and classification with the agricultural committee.
