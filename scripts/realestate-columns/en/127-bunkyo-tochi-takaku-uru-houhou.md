**The key point**: To seek a better price for land in Bunkyo, start by documenting its condition and the constraints on development, then choose a sales approach suited to potential buyers. Compare net proceeds after costs, the time needed to complete the sale, and the obligations the seller would take on—not just the asking price.

Owners often begin with a nearby property's advertised price per tsubo, or the thought that clearing an old house might increase the price. Yet even within the same neighbourhood, differences in road access, development potential and levels relative to adjoining land can change a buyer's assessment.

A location in Kohinata, Koishikawa or Sengoku does not, by itself, determine the right sales strategy. What evidence would someone need to decide whether this particular plot suits their plans? Preparing that evidence as the seller provides a basis for discussing price.

## Which figure tells you whether the sale achieved a good result?

First clarify your priorities. How much time can you allow to pursue your target price? Is there a deadline for moving or receiving funds? Can you pay for demolition or surveying in advance? Without these priorities, it can be difficult to choose between offers.

| What to compare | What to clarify before marketing |
| --- | --- |
| Net proceeds | How much you hope to retain after the seller's costs and taxes are deducted from the sale price |
| Timing | When payment and handover must be completed, as well as when the contract should be signed |
| Seller's obligations | Who will bear the cost and responsibility for the existing house, items left on site, surveying and other work |

If a mortgage or other borrowing remains, also check how much cash would remain after repayment. A valuation does not guarantee either the eventual sale price or your net proceeds.

For the distinction between a valuation and an actual offer, see [Valuations and purchase offers](https://luck428.com/en/column/satei-gaku-to-fuda-chigai). This article focuses on the preparation and sales terms an owner can consider once the valuation assumptions are clear.

## What evidence helps buyers understand land in Bunkyo?

Area and distance from a station are not enough for a buyer to plan how to use land. Separate what has been established from what still needs investigation. Do not fill gaps in the records with assumptions.

| Item to investigate | Evidence useful to a buyer |
| --- | --- |
| Permitted uses and building scale | Zoning, building coverage and floor-area ratios, height and fire-related designations, and an architect's assessment for the proposed plan |
| Roads and access | Road classification, width, frontage and any required setback; for private roads, documents on rights, passage and excavation |
| Area and boundaries | Registered area, survey drawings, and the availability and dates of boundary-confirmation documents |
| Changes in level and retaining walls | The site's relationship to the road and adjoining land, retaining-wall drawings, work history and investigation records |
| Living environment | The route and slopes to the station, surrounding buildings, amenities and verified school catchment information |

Bunkyo has plateaus, valleys and slopes. Walking the site can reveal conditions that a map alone does not show. This does not mean that every elevated site is safe or every low-lying site is difficult to sell. Assess the terrain and the conditions of the individual plot separately.

The designated floor-area ratio alone does not establish how much floor space can actually be built. Roads, plot shape and other restrictions also matter. Building potential requires an architect's assessment and confirmation with the relevant ward office. See [Floor-area ratios and land values](https://luck428.com/en/column/yosekiritsu-hosei-tochine).

For buyers who care about schools, provide information checked against the precise address, including its block and building numbers. Some Bunkyo catchment boundaries also depend on former neighbourhood names. A nearby school alone does not establish the designated school, and neither admission nor a higher sale price can be guaranteed.

For the technical detail, see [Boundaries and confirmed surveys](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-fudosan-kyoukai-kakutei-sokuryo-baikyaku), [Setbacks, area and price (Japanese reference)](https://luck428.com/column/setback-42jo-2ko-doro-tochi-baikyaku-menseki), and [Slopes and retaining walls in Bunkyo (Japanese reference)](https://luck428.com/column/gake-yoheki-tochi-baikyaku-bunkyo).

## How does the information to present change with the buyer?

A person planning a home and a company developing homes for sale need different information. Present the plot in relation to what the buyer wants to achieve after purchase, alongside the seller's wishes.

| Potential buyer | Main considerations | Information for the seller to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An individual planning a home | The desired lifestyle and the combined land-and-building budget | Local environment, site and road records, constraints needed for building assessment, and handover timing |
| A developer or property purchasing business | Development, construction and resale plans and costs | Building constraints, shape and changes in level, records on the existing house and retaining walls, and allocation of costs between seller and buyer |
| An adjoining owner | Uses and convenience gained by enlarging their site | Boundaries and rights, the area proposed for sale, and comparison with selling the whole plot |

These are categories to consider when planning a sale. They do not mean that a buyer is currently available or that competing offers will necessarily arise.

For a small or irregularly shaped plot, combining it with adjoining land may be one possibility. However, adjoining owners have their own budgets and plans. Do not assume they will pay more simply because they are next door; compare this route with marketing more widely. The order of approaches and the amount of information disclosed should also be agreed with the seller.

See [Options for small plots in Bunkyo (Japanese reference)](https://luck428.com/column/kyosho-tochi-15tsubo-uru-ikasu-bunkyo) and [Sales to adjoining owners and acquiring adjoining land (Japanese reference)](https://luck428.com/column/rinchi-baikyaku-kaimashi-nanari-tochi-deguchi).

Compare market evidence with attention to the property's conditions and the date. Asking prices, officially published land prices, inheritance-tax road values and actual transaction prices serve different purposes. Land transaction information in the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's Real Estate Information Library is based on sources including questionnaires; it is not a complete record of every transaction. A nearby example's unit price should not simply be applied to your own land.

## What should you compare before paying for demolition or surveying?

Spending on preparation does not necessarily increase the sale price by the amount spent. Establish why the work is needed and how it would affect the terms of sale before committing to it.

Where an old house remains, compare handing over the land with the house in place against demolition by the seller before handover. Consider the prospective price, demolition costs, work schedule, how newly discovered underground conditions would be handled, and tax implications. A buyer may wish to use the existing building, so demolition need not be a precondition to seeking advice about selling.

Demolition may affect residential-land treatment for fixed asset tax and city planning tax. Rebuilding and other circumstances can affect the treatment, so the resulting tax is not uniform. Before fixing the demolition date, consult the metropolitan tax office or a tax accountant. Capital-gains tax relief also requires different consideration depending on whether the property was a home or, for example, an inherited vacant house.

Take a similar approach to surveying. Ask a land and house investigator what further work is needed in light of the existing drawings, boundary confirmations, buyer's plan and sale terms. Having an old survey drawing does not establish that the boundary checks needed for the current transaction are complete.

When an offer arrives, compare it on the same set of terms rather than extracting only the price.

| Item | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Costs | Brokerage, surveying, demolition, removal of remaining items and other expenses; check tax-inclusive estimates, scope and exclusions |
| Conditions of the offer | Buyer financing, building-plan investigations and other conditions attached to the offer |
| Handover | The payment date, whether the house or remaining items may stay, and whether preparations can be completed on time |
| Defects and other issues | What is known about the land and building, what must be explained, and how contractual obligations will be defined |

We do not recommend concealing known defects or unresolved issues with adjoining land. Organise the facts and reflect them in the explanations and contract terms. Consult a lawyer where legal assessment of liability or other terms is needed.

## How should you review the price and marketing after launch?

Before marketing begins, agree on the basis for the asking price, the intended buyers, what information may be made public, and when progress will be reviewed. This makes it easier to assess enquiries and decide the next steps.

Wide exposure and a wish to sell discreetly allow different levels of disclosure. If you want to restrict publicity, agree on the sales approach with an understanding of how this may affect the audience reached and the time needed to sell.

Limited interest does not necessarily mean the price is the only problem. Check whether documents are missing, whether buyers have enough information to decide, and whether the handover terms suit the intended audience. Review enquiries, site visits and reasons for not proceeding before deciding whether to change the price, documents, target buyers or terms.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I market my land at a higher unit price than nearby properties?**
A. Consider the differences in shape, roads, building constraints and timing, as well as nearby advertised prices. Establish the basis for your target price and the time available for the sale, and agree how to review the approach in response to interest.

**Q. Can I consult you if I do not know where the boundaries are?**
A. Yes. We check what registration records, drawings and boundary documents are available and identify matters to discuss with a land and house investigator. You do not need to commission every possible survey on your own before consulting us.

**Q. Will demolishing the old house first achieve a higher price?**
A. The answer depends on the land and building, the buyer's intended use, demolition costs and tax treatment. Compare keeping the house with demolition in terms of net proceeds after costs and handover timing before deciding.

**Q. Can I ask for a valuation or advice before deciding to sell?**
A. Yes. Start with the address, area, presence of an old house and preferred timing, to the extent you know them. 四葉不動産 will organise the sales options and checks and explain the next steps. A consultation and a decision to appoint a broker can be considered separately.
