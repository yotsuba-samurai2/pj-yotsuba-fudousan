## How does EV charging equipment change the asset value and tenant appeal of a rental or commercial property?

EV charging equipment is becoming an element of added value and differentiation for rental housing and commercial property as electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles spread. But it is not as simple as "install charging and the value rises": on the property side, the required work, notifications and consensus-building differ entirely by the type of charging, and that must be grasped first.

Charging equipment splits broadly into normal charging (low output; often around 6 kW or less) and rapid charging (high output). Normal charging suits housing and monthly parking where cars are parked for long periods and the work is relatively light, while rapid charging, which charges in a short time, suits commercial facilities and roadside properties but brings heavier power-receiving capacity, installation standards and fire-service regulation.

| Category | Rough output guide | Suited property | What gets heavier on the property side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal charging | Mostly around 6 kW or less | Rental housing, monthly parking, long-stay | Power-receiving capacity, billing, common-area consensus |
| Rapid charging | High output (kW class) | Commercial facilities, roadside, short stay | Fire-service technical standards, notification, power-receiving capacity |

What you decide first is which charging, in which space, and how many units. This premise is the starting point for everything — confirming the power-receiving capacity, the treatment under fire-service law and the unit-ownership resolution. The impact on asset value (rent, tenant appeal, resale valuation) changes with the market and location and cannot be asserted uniformly in figures.

## What technical standards apply under the Fire Service Act and the fire-prevention ordinance when you install a rapid charger?

Rapid-charging equipment is subject to each municipality's fire-prevention ordinance under the Fire Service Act. The basis for enacting the ordinance is the national "Ministerial Order Establishing Standards for Enacting Ordinances on the Location, Structure and Management of Fire-Source Equipment, etc." (the Fire-Source Equipment Order), which contains the definition of "rapid-charging equipment" and the framework of standards on location, structure and management.

Formerly this order treated equipment with total output over 20 kW and up to 200 kW as "rapid-charging equipment," and regulated high-output equipment over 200 kW separately as "transformer equipment." The revision promulgated on 21 February 2023 (Reiwa 5) — notified by the Fire and Disaster Management Agency's Fire Prevention Division to fire departments as Shobo-yo No. 113 — abolished this 200 kW upper limit, so equipment over 200 kW is now treated as "rapid-charging equipment" under the order too. In addition, provisions for a "separated type," connected via a charging cable from a main unit with a transformer function, were put in place. The rapid-charging provisions of the order took effect on 1 October 2023. The timing of the corresponding revision and enforcement of each municipal fire-prevention ordinance differs by municipality; for example, Matsuyama City and Imabari City set enforcement on 1 October 2023.

| Item | Content | Basis / where to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory framework | Rapid-charging equipment is regulated on location, structure and management by the fire-prevention ordinance | Fire-Source Equipment Order / each municipal fire-prevention ordinance |
| Change in definition | The 200 kW upper limit on total output was abolished; over 200 kW is now rapid-charging equipment. Separated type provided for | Promulgated 21 Feb 2023 / Shobo-yo No. 113 (rapid-charging provisions took effect 1 Oct 2023) |
| Installation notification | A notification to the fire authority on installation (the output triggering notification differs by municipality) | Fire station with jurisdiction / each municipal ordinance |
| Separation distance etc. | Standards such as separation distance from buildings and fire-spread-prevention measures | Fire station with jurisdiction / each municipal ordinance |

Note that the concrete separation distance (for example, securing a certain distance from a building) and the output line for notification (for example, treating equipment above a certain total output as subject to notification) are set by each municipal fire-prevention ordinance even while following the national order, so figures and operation differ by municipality. Low-output normal charging is treated differently from this rapid-charging regulation. Before making installation concrete, be sure to confirm with the fire station with jurisdiction over the property's location the treatment under the ordinance, whether a notification is required, and the separation and equipment needed. ## When retrofitting a sectional condominium, which resolution and exclusive-use-right points need arranging?

When you retrofit EV charging equipment to the parking or common area of a sectional condominium (a sectionally-owned building), that is not a privately-owned part but a common area, so it cannot be advanced at one owner's judgment. The basis is the Act on Building Unit Ownership (Unit Ownership Act; Act No. 69 of 1962).

The point is that the required resolution splits depending on whether the installation is a "change" to the common area that involves a significant change to its shape or utility, or a minor one that does not. Where it involves a significant change to shape or utility, a resolution of three-quarters or more of both the sectional owners and the voting rights is the principle (Unit Ownership Act art. 17(1); the number of sectional owners can be reduced to a majority by the bylaws). Where it stays within management not involving a significant change, there is room for an ordinary resolution of the meeting (a majority of both the sectional owners and the voting rights) to suffice (art. 18). Which it is a case-by-case judgment based on the scale and manner of the work, and the legal assessment is a lawyer's field.

| Scene | Rough required procedure (principle) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Installation involving a significant change to the common area | Resolution of three-quarters or more of both owners and voting rights | Unit Ownership Act art. 17(1) |
| Within management not involving a significant change | Room for an ordinary resolution (majority) to suffice | Unit Ownership Act art. 18 |
| Installation in a space with an exclusive-use right | Where it has a special effect on the use of that space, the holder's consent | Unit Ownership Act art. 17(2) |

Another point is the exclusive-use right. Whether mechanical or flat, when you install charging equipment in a parking space to which a specific sectional owner has an exclusive-use right, and the change has a special effect on the use of that space, the holder's consent is separately required (Unit Ownership Act art. 17(2)). The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's "Standard Management Bylaws for Condominiums" and their commentary also show thinking on installing EV charging equipment in parking areas (the system and templates are revised, so confirm the latest MLIT version and note the reference date). The success of a resolution and disputes over exclusive-use rights are a lawyer's field, and we do not judge whether a resolution passes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How different are the property-side procedures for normal charging versus rapid charging?**
A. Very different. Rapid-charging equipment is subject to each municipality's fire-prevention ordinance under the Fire Service Act, with standards on location, structure and management (separation distance and fire-spread-prevention measures) and a notification for installation. Low-output normal charging is treated differently from this rapid-charging regulation. Which it falls under, and whether a notification or separation is required, are set by each municipal fire-prevention ordinance following the national Fire-Source Equipment Order, and figures and operation differ by municipality, so be sure to confirm with the fire station with jurisdiction over the property's location.

**Q. I hear the upper output limit for rapid-charging equipment was abolished. From when?**
A. The revision to the relevant ministerial order promulgated on 21 February 2023 (Reiwa 5) — notified as Shobo-yo No. 113 — abolished the 200 kW upper limit on total output for rapid-charging equipment, which was formerly over 20 kW and up to 200 kW, so equipment over 200 kW is now treated as rapid-charging equipment under the order too. The rapid-charging provisions of the order took effect on 1 October 2023. The timing of the corresponding revision and enforcement of each municipal fire-prevention ordinance differs by municipality, so confirm with the fire station with jurisdiction.

**Q. To retrofit the parking area of a sectional condominium, what share of owners must agree?**
A. Where the installation involves a significant change to the shape or utility of the common area, a resolution of three-quarters or more of both the sectional owners and the voting rights is the principle (Unit Ownership Act art. 17(1)). Where it stays within management not involving a significant change, there is room for an ordinary resolution (a majority) to suffice (art. 18). Which it is a case-by-case judgment based on the scale and manner of the work. For a space with an exclusive-use right where the use is specially affected, the holder's consent is separately required (art. 17(2)). The success of a resolution and legal judgment are a lawyer's field.

**Q. Who should I consult about whether the power-receiving capacity is enough or a subsidy can be used?**
A. Reinforcing the power-receiving capacity (such as adding a cubicle) and the installation work are the field of a registered electrical contractor / electrician, and the power-supply conditions the power company's field. The electrical-contractor registration/notice and the charging-infrastructure subsidy application documents are handled by an administrative scrivener, and tax such as the equipment's depreciation by a tax accountant. These cannot be bundled into one contract; each is an independent business contracted with separately, and we connect them (no referral fees are exchanged). Subsidy amounts and requirements change each fiscal year, so confirm the latest in each scheme's public-offering guidelines.
