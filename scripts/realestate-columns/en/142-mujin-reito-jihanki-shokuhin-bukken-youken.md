## What zoning and property can an unmanned frozen vending machine go in?

The first fork is whether the machine is a "building" under the Building Standards Act. If it is not, zoning use-restrictions tend not to apply; if it is, the zoning restriction on store use, and in some cases change-of-use confirmation, come in.

Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950) art. 2 (1) defines a building as "a structure fixed to land, having a roof and pillars or walls." Simply standing the machine on land, with no roof or pillar/wall structure, generally does not amount to this building. By contrast, the following placements bring in zoning restrictions or confirmation as a building (or as business inside a building).

| Placement | Treatment under the Building Standards Act |
|---|---|
| Standing the machine outdoors only (no roof/pillars/walls) | Unlikely to be a building; zoning use-restrictions tend not to apply |
| Building a hut/prefab (roof and pillars/walls) around the machine | A building; the zoning restriction on retail-store use and building confirmation come in |
| Putting it in an existing vacant store (tenant) | If the building's use is a store, follow that zoning restriction; for conversion from another use, check whether change-of-use confirmation is needed |

Where it is a building, use-restrictions by zone are set by Building Standards Act art. 48 and Appended Table 2. A retail store is, in principle, not permitted in Category I/II Low-rise Exclusive Residential Districts (except parts meeting floor-area and other conditions), is allowed up to two storeys and 500 m² in a Category I Mid/High-rise Exclusive Residential District, and is not permitted in an Exclusive Industrial District—treatment differs by zone. Which zone applies is set by the municipality's city plan. We check the candidate property's zone and the building's current use (store or not), and organise what to confirm with an architect according to how the machine is placed.

Where you convert an existing building to place the machine, a retail store is a special-purpose building under the Building Standards Act, so when you change the use to a special-purpose building (one whose floor area for that use exceeds 200 m²), the confirmation (change-of-use confirmation) applied by art. 87 can be required. Treatment varies by whether there is a completion certificate and by the area, so for completion certificates and change of use, [checks before converting a property without a completion certificate to welfare use (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/kensazumisho-nashi-bukken-fukushi-youto-henko) is a useful reference. The final judgment on whether change-of-use or building confirmation is needed is an architect's and the designated administrative agency's field; we do not assert it.

## For selling frozen food by vending machine, do you need a licence or a notification under the Food Sanitation Act?

Selling frozen food (frozen packaged food) by vending machine can fall under a business notification (art. 57), unlike selling packaged food that keeps at room temperature, under the Food Sanitation Act amended with effect from 1 June 2021. If the machine cooks automatically at the point of sale, it moves into the field of a restaurant-business licence (art. 55). The classification is for the health centre.

The Food Sanitation Act (Act No. 233 of 1947) provides that a business with a significant effect on public health (the business types designated by cabinet order under art. 54) requires the prefectural governor's licence (art. 55), and that a person intending to run a business other than those must, in advance, notify the prefectural governor (art. 57). This notification system began on 1 June 2021 under the amending act (Act No. 46 of 2018). Businesses for which notification is not required include the sale of packaged food that "keeps without risk of quality deterioration causing a food-sanitation hazard when stored by a method that does not rely on freezing or refrigeration"—that is, the sale of packaged food that keeps at room temperature. Frozen food does not fall within this room-temperature exception, so its sale can require a business notification.

| How it is sold | Treatment under the Food Sanitation Act (classification by the health centre) |
|---|---|
| Selling frozen food manufactured/packaged at a licensed facility, unprocessed, by vending machine (buyer takes it away / heats it themselves) | Not room-temperature packaged food, so the sale can require a business notification (art. 57) |
| The machine cooks automatically at the point of sale and serves it | The field of a restaurant-business licence (art. 55); confirm with the health centre |
| Manufacturing frozen food at your own facility and selling it | A licensed manufacturing type (e.g. frozen-food manufacturing) may apply separately |

On vending machines that cook automatically at the point of sale, an old Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare notice (Notice Eishoku No. 39 of 5 March 1999, "On the scope of vending machines requiring a restaurant-business licence") treated a form in which cooking is done automatically at sale as requiring a restaurant-business licence, and a form in which the food is sold unprocessed and the buyer heats it themselves as not requiring one. This, however, is a notice predating the 2021 reform. Which of a notification, a licence, or neither applies to frozen-vending sales varies with the product, the equipment and the serving method, and is ultimately a decision by the health centre with jurisdiction over the installation site. Including the need to appoint a food-sanitation supervisor for a business, tell the health centre the site, the business type and the products handled and confirm with them. ## What should you check about power, drainage and security when choosing the property?

A frozen vending machine keeps freezing at all times, so the capacity and stability of the power supply, a fallback for power cuts/breakdowns, and the security and siting that an unmanned machine needs are the practical checks when choosing a property. These are matters to confirm on site and from drawings and management rules before contracting, ahead of any law.

| Check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Power capacity / contracted power | Whether the property's electrical capacity, outlet shape (single-/three-phase) and contracted amperage suffice for the freezer's power draw; whether capacity can be added |
| Fallback for cuts/breakdowns | Rules for temperature control and product disposal when freezing is lost in a cut or breakdown; whether remote monitoring is possible |
| Installation space and carry-in | Floor load capacity against the machine's dimensions/weight; the carry-in route; the passage space in front |
| Drainage / condensation | Handling the freezer's condensation/drainage (effect on floor or neighbours) |
| Security / unmanned management | Security cameras, lighting, locking, cash/cashless management, and consideration for neighbours in unmanned operation |
| Lease / management rules | For a tenant, whether vending-machine installation, added electrical capacity, and signage/outdoor placement are permitted; the scope of restoration |

Even standing it outdoors, you need to confirm the title to the site land/building (owned or leased), the effect on neighbouring land and passage, and consideration for neighbours over night-time noise and light. For a tenant, confirm before contracting whether the lease permits that use (retail / vending-machine installation) and whether added electrical capacity and signage are possible. We help confirm the points you can check on site and from records before contracting—electrical capacity, carry-in, installation space, and whether the management rules permit it. The capacity-addition work itself is the field of an electrician/equipment contractor.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the Building Standards Act zoning restriction apply even if I just place the machine outdoors?**
A. Simply standing the machine on land, with no roof or pillar/wall structure, is unlikely to amount to a "building" under Building Standards Act art. 2 (1) (a structure fixed to land, having a roof and pillars or walls), so zoning use-restrictions tend not to apply. By contrast, building a hut or prefab (roof and pillars/walls) around it, or putting it in an existing building, brings in zoning restrictions and confirmation for the building's use. The final judgment on whether it is a building is the field of an architect and the designated administrative agency.

**Q. Do I need a licence or a notification to sell frozen food by vending machine?**
A. Under the Food Sanitation Act amended with effect from 1 June 2021, selling packaged food that keeps at room temperature does not require notification, but frozen food (frozen packaged food) does not fall within that exception, so its sale can require a business notification (art. 57). If the machine cooks automatically at the point of sale, it moves into the field of a restaurant-business licence (art. 55). Which applies varies with the product, equipment and serving method, and is the decision of the health centre with jurisdiction over the site. Support with application documents is an administrative scrivener's work.

**Q. Must I appoint a food-sanitation supervisor?**
A. A business under the Food Sanitation Act (whether licence or notification) generally requires a food-sanitation supervisor per facility (business). Which business an unmanned vending operation falls under, and how much of a framework is needed, should be confirmed following the health centre's guidance. This article is general information and does not assert whether a supervisor is required for a particular machine.

**Q. How do I confirm whether the electrical capacity is enough?**
A. Because the freezer is always powered to keep freezing, confirm on site and from drawings before contracting whether the property's contracted power, outlet shape (single-/three-phase) and spare capacity suffice for the machine's power draw. Whether addition work is possible if it is short depends on the lease and management rules and on an electrician's estimate. We help confirm the property-side electrical capacity, carry-in route, installation space, and whether the management rules permit it, and connect you to an electrician for the work itself.
