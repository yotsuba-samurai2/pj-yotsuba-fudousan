**In short:** Japanese inheritance tax is paid in cash as a rule. Only when you cannot pay in full even by instalments (延納) can you apply to pay in kind (物納) with the inherited real estate itself. Not every parcel qualifies. Land with unclear boundaries, property carrying a security interest, or a co-owned share where not all co-owners join is, as a rule, "property unsuitable for management or disposal" and will not be accepted.

Whether you can proceed to payment in kind is a decision for the tax accountant. From the real estate side, the earlier step is to check the property conditions — boundaries, rights and road access. For [inherited property consultations](https://luck428.com/en/souzoku), start by mapping the current state of the inherited land and building and the documents needed for a sale or payment in kind.

## In what order, and when, is payment in kind allowed?

Article 41(1) of the Inheritance Tax Act allows payment in kind, on application, up to the amount that is difficult to pay in cash where, even by instalments, paying in cash is difficult. The order is cash first, then instalments, and payment in kind only for the part that remains hard to pay. If instalments can cover it, payment in kind is not allowed. The procedure is in Article 42, and the value at which the state takes the asset (the收納価額) is, as a rule, the value used as the basis for the taxable amount, under Article 43.

Assets that may be used for payment in kind are ranked (Article 41(2)).

| Rank | Assets that may be used |
|---|---|
| 1st | Real estate, ships, national bonds, local bonds, listed shares, etc. (including those among them that are "subordinate" property) |
| 2nd | Unlisted shares, etc. (including those among them that are "subordinate" property) |
| 3rd | Movables |

A lower-ranked asset is used only where there is no suitable higher-ranked asset of an appropriate value, among other cases. Inherited land and buildings are first rank, but first rank does not mean automatic acceptance: the asset must not be "property unsuitable for management or disposal," discussed next. Whether payment in kind, instalments or a sale is more advantageous bears directly on the tax, so confirm it with a tax accountant.

## What is "property unsuitable for management or disposal"?

Property that the state would find hard to manage or dispose of cannot be used for payment in kind. This is "property unsuitable for management or disposal," set out, under Article 41(2), in Article 18 of the Inheritance Tax Act Enforcement Order and Article 21 of the Enforcement Regulations. The National Tax Agency's guidance gives examples for real estate.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Unstable rights | Property under a security interest; property whose ownership is in dispute |
| Extent not fixed | Land whose boundaries are not clear |
| Cannot be used alone | Property that cannot be used normally without litigation against an adjoining owner |
| Co-ownership | Co-owned property (unless all co-owners apply to pay their entire shares in kind) |

These are excluded not because the value is low but because the state could not manage or sell them as they are after taking them. A similar term, "subordinate property for payment in kind" (Enforcement Order Article 19), refers to land not meeting the frontage requirement (a landlocked parcel), land in an urbanization control area, planned city-planning road sites, and buildings in breach of law with their sites — property that may be paid in kind only where there is no other suitable asset. Treat it as distinct from unsuitable property.

## Why can't land with unfixed boundaries be paid in kind?

For the state to sell or manage the property after taking it, it must be settled exactly where the paid-in-kind land begins and ends. Land whose boundaries are not clear is property unsuitable for management or disposal, so the practice is to settle the boundaries with the adjoining owners present and prepare a cadastral survey map from a confirmed survey before applying. If a building's frame, including its foundation, encroaches on neighboring land, the property is treated as unsuitable unless the encroachment is resolved.

Confirmed-boundary surveying and the identification of parcel boundaries are the work of a land and house investigator (土地家屋調査士) under the Real Property Registration Act. The real estate side reads the register, cadastral map and current survey to assess whether boundary confirmation is needed and refers the matter to the investigator. Where a neighbor will not cooperate with confirmation, or a parcel boundary is in dispute, the parcel-boundary identification system or litigation comes into view, and anything contentious is a lawyer's domain.

## Can leased land (底地) or a co-owned share be paid in kind?

Leased land (底地), as land carrying a leasehold, raises questions about ease of management after the state takes it, depending on the lease situation and any rent arrears. Without organizing the lease contract, the record of rent received and whether the lessee can be identified, it is hard to move the application forward.

A co-owned share cannot, as a rule, be used for payment in kind on its own. Co-owned property is treated as property unsuitable for management or disposal unless all co-owners apply together to pay their entire shares in kind. Having several heirs keep an inherited property in co-ownership and pay only one share in kind is, in principle, not contemplated. Decide first whether to settle it into sole ownership through division of the estate, or to apply with all co-owners. If the co-owners cannot agree, division by agreement or mediation, with a lawyer, may be needed.

## What should surveying and registration settle before the application?

The application window (up to the payment due date) is limited, and starting surveys or registration at the last minute may not finish in time. Prepare the property side in this order, front-loaded.

| Order | Content and who handles it |
|---|---|
| Inheritance registration | Title in the deceased's name lacks a basis for the application. Registration of title on inheritance: judicial scrivener |
| Boundary confirmation / survey | Neighbor attendance, confirmed survey, cadastral survey map. Land and house investigator |
| Sorting out rights | Discharge of security interests such as mortgages; checking leaseholds and encroachments. Registration: judicial scrivener; anything contentious: lawyer |
| Eligibility and documents | Showing cash-payment difficulty, the application documents, the rank and subordination assessment. Tax accountant |

These proceed under separate engagements with independent providers. The eligibility decision, tax and comparison with instalments go to the tax accountant; confirmed-boundary surveying to the land and house investigator; inheritance registration and discharge of security interests to the judicial scrivener; and disputes among co-owners or over leased land to the lawyer — each contracted directly. There are no referral fees among them. The real estate side organizes the current state and documents of the land and building heading toward payment in kind, so it can be weighed alongside a sale.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If it is inherited land, can inheritance tax always be paid in kind with it?**
A. No. Consider cash first, then whether instalments can cover it; payment in kind can be applied for only for the amount hard to pay even by instalments. Land is first rank, but on the premise that it is not property unsuitable for management or disposal.

**Q. Can I at least apply for payment in kind with land that has no boundary markers?**
A. Land whose boundaries are not clear is, as a rule, property unsuitable for management or disposal. Before applying, you need boundary confirmation with neighbors present and a confirmed survey, which is the land and house investigator's work.

**Q. Three siblings co-inherited a parcel. Can I pay only my share in kind?**
A. As a rule, no. Co-owned property is unsuitable unless all co-owners apply to pay their entire shares in kind. You would first need to settle it into sole ownership by division, or similar.

**Q. Who can I ask for the final eligibility decision and the paperwork?**
A. Eligibility, tax and procedure go to the tax accountant, confirmed-boundary surveying to the land and house investigator, and registration to the judicial scrivener. The real estate side prepares the property's current state and documents and does not decide whether payment in kind is granted.
