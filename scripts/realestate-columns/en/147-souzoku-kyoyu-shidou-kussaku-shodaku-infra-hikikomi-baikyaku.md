**The key point**: When an inherited house fronts a private road co-owned by several people, bringing in water/gas or rebuilding—work that digs up the road—in principle needs the other co-owners' involvement. The Civil Code (Act No. 89 of 1896) provides that a change that markedly alters a shared thing needs the consent of all co-owners (art. 251(1)); management not involving a marked change of form or function is decided by a majority of the value of co-ownership shares (art. 252(1)); and acts of preservation may be done by each co-owner alone (art. 252(5)). The Ministry of Justice "Research report on owner consent needed for works on a privately owned road held by several persons—Guideline for responding to private roads with unknown owners (2nd ed., June 2022)" organises road repaving as an act of preservation and much work such as lifeline connection as an act of management (majority), and it is usual practice to secure this consent (excavation consent) before selling. Where a co-owner's whereabouts are unknown, the 2021 reform (in force 1 April 2023) provides for acquisition of the unknown co-owner's share (Civil Code art. 262-2), transfer of the share (art. 262-3), and court decisions to decide management/change excluding that person (art. 251(2), art. 252(2)). ## Why does co-owned private-road excavation consent become necessary before selling?

If the frontage road is a "co-owned private road," that road is a shared thing of several people. Bringing in a new water/gas pipe, replacing an old one, or digging up the road for a rebuild is an act of digging land in which others also hold shares. The Civil Code divides how a shared thing is handled by the nature of the act.

| Type of act | Involvement needed | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Act of preservation (maintaining the status quo; minor repaving, etc.) | Each co-owner may do it alone | Civil Code art. 252(5) |
| Act of management (including minor change) | Decided by a majority of the value of co-ownership shares | Civil Code art. 252(1) |
| Act of change (involving a marked change of form/function) | Consent of all co-owners | Civil Code art. 251(1) |

Further, where a management decision would have a "special effect" on one co-owner, that co-owner's consent is needed (art. 252(3)). The problem is that unless all-consent or a majority is securely in hand at the time of work, the contractor hesitates to start and the buyer cannot obtain financing. So it is widely practised to secure consent (excavation consent) from the relevant co-owners before selling. This both meets the statutory consent requirement and is a practical measure to avoid future trouble and let the buyer start work directly.

## How does the Ministry of Justice private-road guideline organise consent for excavation?

The Ministry of Justice "study group on preservation and management of co-owned private roads" has published a report organising, case by case, how much consent each work needs. The 2nd edition of June 2022 is the latest, reflecting the 2021 Civil Code reform (Act No. 24 of 2021); the 1st edition was January 2018.

The guideline organises work roughly as follows by its nature (an outline; the conclusion changes with individual circumstances).

| Example of work | Act type (the guideline's organisation) |
|---|---|
| Repairing/repaving the private road | Act of preservation (each co-owner may do it alone) |
| Newly laying a private water-supply pipe for one's own use | May, in some cases, be done alone as use according to one's share |
| Newly laying a public sewer pipe | Act of management (decided by a majority of the value of shares) |
| Setting a right of use for a lifeline operator | Usually an act of management (majority) |
| Work with a special effect on some co-owners, such as causing a long water stoppage | Individual judgment (the consent of the co-owner with the special effect is needed) |

The 2021 reform widened the scope of "management" so that a "minor change" not involving a marked change of form/function can also be decided by a majority. Excavation to bring in a lifeline often does not markedly change the road's form/function and is often organised as within management (majority). But which work falls into which type varies with the pipe's route, diameter and the scale of excavation, and whether there is a special effect is an individual judgment per case. Confirm the application of the types and the scope of consent with a lawyer/judicial scrivener.

## What can you do when a co-owner's whereabouts are unknown?

On co-owned private roads, it is not rare that, as inheritances pile up and shares fragment, there is a co-owner one cannot reach (a co-owner of unknown whereabouts). The 2021 reform set up systems to move forward with the court's involvement in such cases (in force 1 April 2023).

| Trouble | System available | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Want to make a change excluding the unknown co-owner | By court decision, a change may be made with the consent of co-owners other than the unknown one | Civil Code art. 251(2) |
| Want to decide management excluding the unknown co-owner | By court decision, management matters may be decided by a majority of shares of co-owners other than the unknown one | Civil Code art. 252(2) |
| Want to acquire the unknown co-owner's share itself | By court decision, the other co-owners may acquire that share | Civil Code art. 262-2 |
| Want to sell to a third party including the unknown co-owner's share | By court decision, a power to transfer the share is granted | Civil Code art. 262-3 |

Each of these requires a petition to the court, premised on investigating that the whereabouts are unknown (resident record, family register, real-estate registration, etc.) and on a deposit. Which procedure to choose and how to frame the petition is the field of a lawyer/judicial scrivener. We read the current state of co-owners and shares from the registry and the official map, and organise the premises of the process—including how easy each is to reach—to hand to you.

## What do you have written in the consent document, and how is it passed to the buyer?

Excavation consent means little orally; a written document is the premise. So that the buyer, the financial institution and the contractor can start work with confidence, it is usual to include the following in the consent document (drafting the text and confirming the rights relations is a judicial scrivener's/lawyer's field).

- Permission to install/excavate water, drainage and gas pipes, and the rough range of their position/route
- Permission for future re-excavation for inspection, repair and replacement
- The method of restoring the road after work and who bears the cost
- That this consent also extends to the transferee (buyer) of the land and their successors (a succession clause)

The succession clause is especially important: even if you, the heir, obtain consent, unless it passes to the buyer the buyer will have to obtain consent again, so it is essential if a sale is in view. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does simply inheriting require consent from the landlord or other co-owners?**
A. Inheriting a share of a co-owned private road itself does not require the other co-owners' consent (inheritance transfers rights by operation of law). Consent becomes an issue when you dig up the road for a water/gas connection or a rebuild, and when you secure consent for the buyer in preparation for a sale. Depending on the nature of the work, it falls under an act of preservation (alone), management (majority) or change (all consent) (Civil Code arts. 251, 252). Confirm the application with a lawyer/judicial scrivener.

**Q. Does excavation need everyone's consent, or is a majority enough?**
A. It depends on the nature of the work. The Ministry of Justice guideline (2nd ed.) organises work such as lifeline connection that does not markedly change the road's form/function as an act of management (a majority of the value of shares), while work involving a marked change needs the consent of all (Civil Code art. 251(1)). Also, where there is a special effect on some co-owners (such as a long water stoppage), that co-owner's consent is needed (art. 252(3)). Since the conclusion changes with the pipe's route and scale, confirm the individual application with a professional.

**Q. If there is a co-owner I cannot reach, does that mean I cannot sell?**
A. Not necessarily. The 2021 Civil Code reform (in force 1 April 2023) set up acquisition of an unknown co-owner's share (art. 262-2) and transfer of the share (art. 262-3), and court decisions to decide management/change excluding that person (art. 251(2), art. 252(2)). Each is premised on a petition to the court and an investigation of the unknown whereabouts. Which procedure to choose is the field of a lawyer/judicial scrivener; we help within the range of brokerage and current-state survey.

**Q. Is the consent document passed to the buyer?**
A. It is not passed automatically, so it is usual to include a succession clause in the consent document stating that "it also takes effect for the transferee (buyer) and their successors." Without it, the buyer has to obtain consent from the co-owners again, which can stall the sale. Drafting the consent document and confirming the rights relations is a judicial scrivener's/lawyer's field; within the sale brokerage we check the presence/scope/succession of consent and connect you to a professional where it is lacking.
