**The key point**: Even if an old "buyback special clause" or a "provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership" remains on the register of inherited land, ownership itself is the heir's and selling is possible. But because a buyer cannot obtain full ownership while these registrations remain, in practice you clear them before handing over. A buyback clause is a device under the Civil Code (Act No. 89 of 1896) art. 579, and the buyback period is at most 10 years under art. 580. The 2021 reform of the Real Property Registration Act (Act No. 123 of 2004) by Act No. 24 of 2021 added art. 69-2, so a buyback-clause registration can now be cancelled by the registration holder alone (the current owner) if 10 years have passed from the contract date (in force 1 April 2023). A provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership is a provisional registration based on a sale pre-contract, etc.; its cancellation can be applied for alone by the provisional-registration holder, or alone by an interested party on the register who has the holder's consent (art. 110). Where the counterparty is of unknown whereabouts or deceased and consent cannot be obtained, cancellation via a dismissal decision (public summons) under Real Property Registration Act art. 70, or a search for the heirs, becomes necessary. ## Why can't I sell when a buyback-clause registration or a provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership remains on inherited land?

As long as ownership is the heir's, you can enter a contract to sell the land. The problem is whether the buyer can obtain "full ownership".

When a buyback clause is registered, under Civil Code art. 581(1) the seller (the buyback holder) can assert the buyback against a third party (a person who bought from the buyer). In theory, room remains on the register for the buyback holder to return the price, etc., rescind the sale and recover ownership. A provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership is a scheme by which, if that provisional registration is later made a definitive registration, a priority rank is secured over registrations made after it. Either way, a registration that "could be asserted by another" remains, so a buyer or a bank normally makes cancellation a condition of handover.

| Remaining registration | The buyer's anxiety |
|---|---|
| Buyback-clause registration | Risk of the buyback holder rescinding the sale and recovering ownership (Civil Code art. 581(1)) |
| Provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership | Risk that, if made definitive, it takes priority over rights registered after the provisional registration |

In many cases these registrations are decades old, and the underlying right has already lost substance. Even so, the registration alone obstructs the sale, so clearing it before handover is the practice. The treatment where an old mortgage remains is covered in [the flow to clear an old mortgage on inherited real estate and sell](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-fudosan-kyu-teitoken-massho-baikyaku), and where a home-loan balance remains in [clearing the home-loan balance and mortgage on an inherited family home and selling](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-jikka-juutaku-loan-zansai-teitouken-massho-baikyaku).

## How can a buyback clause (Civil Code art. 579) and a provisional registration be cleared—by prescription or by cancellation?

Cancellation of a buyback-clause registration became much simpler in the 2021 reform. Under Real Property Registration Act art. 69-2 (in force 1 April 2023), where a buyback-clause registration exists and 10 years have passed from the contract date, the registration holder (the current owner) can apply for its cancellation alone. Before the reform, a joint application with the buyback holder was the rule and the counterparty's cooperation was needed.

| Registration | Line of clearing | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Buyback-clause registration | If 10 years have passed from the contract date, the current owner applies for cancellation alone | Real Property Registration Act art. 69-2 |
| Buyback-clause registration (under 10 years / disputed) | Joint application with the buyback holder, or confirming the buyback period has expired | Civil Code arts. 579–585 / Real Property Registration Act art. 60 |
| Provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership | Application by the provisional-registration holder alone, or by an interested party with consent | Real Property Registration Act art. 110 |

The buyback period is at most 10 years under Civil Code art. 580; a clause longer than this is shortened to 10 years, and if no period was set, it must be exercised within 5 years (art. 580(3)). Where the cause of a provisional registration of a claim for transfer of ownership is a sale pre-contract, the completion right of that pre-contract is said to be subject to a 10-year prescription running from when the right can be exercised, and if the right has been extinguished by prescription, the provisional registration becomes one without substance. However, the start point and whether prescription has run, and whether the provisional-registration holder's consent is needed, divide case by case, so a check by a judicial scrivener/lawyer is needed. This article shows only the line of clearing and leaves individual possibility to qualified professionals.

## What if the registered holder (the counterparty) is of unknown whereabouts or deceased?

In old registrations, the buyback holder or provisional-registration holder is often of unknown whereabouts or deceased, so consent or a joint application cannot be obtained. The response is staged.

| Situation | Line of response |
|---|---|
| Buyback clause, 10 years passed from the contract | Under Real Property Registration Act art. 69-2 the current owner can apply for cancellation alone, so the counterparty's cooperation is unnecessary |
| The counterparty is deceased | Investigate the heirs by family register and proceed against the heirs for cancellation |
| The counterparty / heirs are of unknown whereabouts | Consider cancellation via a dismissal decision (public summons) under Real Property Registration Act art. 70 |

Real Property Registration Act art. 70 is a system by which, where the whereabouts of the registration obligor are unknown, you obtain a court's dismissal decision through a public summons under the Non-Contentious Case Procedures Act and can cancel the registration alone. The 2021 reform organised requirements such as the method of investigating unknown whereabouts. The family-register investigation of heirs, the petition for a public summons, and negotiation or litigation are the field of judicial scriveners and lawyers. We investigate the current state from the register and cadastral map and hand over an assessment of which line applies.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I sell the land even with a buyback clause or provisional registration remaining?**
A. Since ownership is the heir's, you can enter a contract to sell. But when a buyback clause is registered the buyback holder can assert the buyback against a third party (Civil Code art. 581(1)), and a provisional registration takes priority if made definitive, so buyers and banks normally make cancellation a condition of handover. In practice you clear it before handing over. Whether cancellation is possible and the line for it are judged by a judicial scrivener.

**Q. Can a buyback-clause registration be cleared without the counterparty's cooperation?**
A. Under Real Property Registration Act art. 69-2, in force 1 April 2023, a buyback-clause registration can be cancelled by the registration holder (the current owner) alone if 10 years have passed from the contract date. Before the reform a joint application with the buyback holder was the rule. If under 10 years or disputed, confirming the buyback period's expiry or a joint application is needed. Confirm the specific possibility with a judicial scrivener.

**Q. What if the counterparty is deceased or of unknown whereabouts?**
A. If the counterparty is deceased, investigate the heirs by family register and proceed against them. If the counterparty or heirs are of unknown whereabouts, consider cancellation via a dismissal decision (public summons) under Real Property Registration Act art. 70. The family-register investigation, the petition for a public summons and litigation are the field of judicial scriveners and lawyers. For a buyback clause, if 10 years have passed from the contract, cancellation may be possible alone under art. 69-2 without the counterparty's cooperation.

**Q. How much cost and time does cancellation take?**
A. The registration and license tax for a cancellation registration is a flat 1,000 yen per property, and a sole application with the counterparty's cooperation is relatively quick. On the other hand, once a counterparty search or a public summons (dismissal decision) is needed, both period and cost become hard to read because a court procedure is involved. If registration of inheritance is not done, that registration (mandatory from 1 April 2024) comes first. Specific cost and period are calculated by a judicial scrivener/lawyer per case.
