**In short:** a property usable as a certified auto workshop (specified maintenance, Article 78 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act) is decided by three points — (1) whether the use-zone allows an auto repair shop, (2) whether the certification-standard work bay and inspection bay (Article 80) fit with enough width and ceiling height, and (3) whether the floor and frontage road can take lift work and the vehicles. All can be confirmed before the lease is signed.

A "certified workshop" that carries out disassembly maintenance and electronic-control-device maintenance cannot open without the certification of the District Transport Bureau Director. And the certification is granted only once the size, equipment and people meet the standards. "We leased the property, then applied for certification and the bay was too small" — the same rework that hits care facilities and clinics hits auto workshops too. This is for operators starting a specified-maintenance business (certified workshop) in Tokyo and for the real estate staff who handle workshop properties, setting out in order what can be checked before signing. Final confirmation of suitability is made at the District Transport Bureau / Transport Branch Office with jurisdiction over the site.

## What floor area and ceiling height does a certified specified-maintenance workshop need?

It varies with the class of vehicle handled, but for passenger ordinary vehicles and four-wheel light-standard vehicles you need room for the indoor work area (the vehicle work bay and inspection bay), the parts work area and the vehicle storage area together.

Anyone intending to run a specified-maintenance business must, under Article 78, paragraph 1 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act (Act No. 185 of 1951), obtain the **certification** of the District Transport Bureau Director. The standards for certification are set in Article 80 and the Enforcement Regulation, and the scale of the work areas is fixed in the Regulation's tables by class of vehicle. For passenger ordinary and four-wheel light-standard vehicles, the rough guide is as follows.

| Work area | Rough size (passenger ordinary / four-wheel light-standard) |
|---|---|
| Vehicle work bay | width 4m+ × depth 8m+ |
| Inspection bay | width 4m+ × depth 8m+ |
| Parts work area | 8㎡+ |
| Vehicle storage | width 3m+ × depth 5.5m+ |

For large vehicles (gross weight 8t+, etc.) the figures grow, with the vehicle work bay around width 5m+ × depth 13m+. **The ceiling must be high enough to do lift work on the target vehicle**, and the floor must be smoothly paved. If you do electronic-control-device maintenance (below), you also need an electronic-control-device inspection-and-maintenance area, but it may be shared with the vehicle work bay and inspection bay. How use-zone and floor-area ratio drive land price is set out in [Why Japanese land prices change with the "floor-area ratio"](https://luck428.com/column/yosekiritsu-hosei-tochine).

## Are there places, by use-zone or surroundings, where an auto workshop cannot open?

Yes. An auto repair shop is subject to the use-zone restrictions of the Building Standards Act and cannot be built in many residential zones.

Clinics and nurseries do not get stuck on use-zone, but auto repair shops do. Article 48 and Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950) divide the zones where an auto repair shop may be built by **the total floor area of the work area**. Per the MLIT summary of use restrictions, it is roughly as follows.

| Use-zone | Auto repair shop (by total work-area floor area) |
|---|---|
| Cat. 1/2 low-rise & mid/high-rise residential exclusive, agricultural residence, Cat. 1/2 residential | cannot be built |
| Quasi-residential | allowed if total work-area floor area ≤150㎡ |
| Neighbourhood commercial / commercial / quasi-industrial | allowed if ≤300㎡ |
| Industrial / exclusive industrial | no area limit |

The point to watch is that **the "work area" size in the certification standard and the "work-area floor area" in the use-zone restriction are different rulers**. Secure a large indoor work area to obtain certification, and its floor area may exceed the use-zone ceiling (150㎡ in quasi-residential), so it cannot be built there. That is why quasi-industrial and industrial zones suit workshops. Whether you clash with neighbours over noise, paint fumes and queuing vehicles is also a pre-signing point. ## How do property requirements differ between a certified workshop and a designated workshop (private inspection station)?

A designated workshop needs, on top of the certified-workshop requirements, a "completion inspection bay" and inspection machinery. The property is larger and the equipment burden heavier.

A certified workshop brings the maintained vehicle to the Transport Branch Office's inspection lane. A designated workshop (private inspection station, Article 94-2 of the Act) is a certified workshop that also holds the inspection equipment and an automobile inspector and can issue a safety-standard conformity certificate. In exchange for skipping the drive-in, the property requirements grow.

| Category | Property / equipment requirements |
|---|---|
| Certified workshop (Art. 78) | work bay, inspection bay, parts work area, vehicle storage, work machinery, employees engaged in specified maintenance |
| Designated workshop (Art. 94-2) | the certified-workshop requirements plus a completion inspection bay (room and ceiling height for an inspection lane), testers and other inspection machinery, and an appointed automobile inspector |

The usual order is to choose the property as a certified workshop first, then aim for a designated workshop (private inspection station) once the business grows. **Assume a designated workshop from the outset and the required site, ceiling height and investment change greatly.** Deciding which you target at the property-selection stage prevents rework.

## When getting certification on a leased property, what should you confirm with the landlord before signing?

Confirm "may I use it for this purpose" and "will the building take the work and heavy equipment" in writing, before signing.

To open a certified workshop on a lease, look at both the contract and the current condition. The key checks are these.

| Item to confirm | Why before signing |
|---|---|
| Whether "auto maintenance (specified maintenance)" is in the permitted use | A restricted-use contract blocks the later certification application |
| Whether lifts/pits, floor cutting and three-phase 200V power may be installed | Agree the restoration and fit-out scope first, or exit costs balloon |
| Frontage road width and entrance width | Whether the vehicles handled (especially large ones) can enter — tied to the vehicle-storage and work-bay entrance width |
| Use-zone and the inspection certificate / building-confirmation history | Whether an auto repair shop may be built there and whether a use-change confirmation is needed |
| Fire, drainage and paint-booth exhaust | If you handle paint or organic solvents, fire, water-quality and air rules apply |

These can be confirmed at the viewing and before signing. Learn after signing that "that use is not allowed" or "the floor pit cannot be added", and the whole certification design is redone. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. When is specified-maintenance certification needed?**
A. When you carry out, as a business, work amounting to "specified maintenance" under Article 49, paragraph 2 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act. Besides the traditional disassembly maintenance (maintenance involving disassembly of the engine, power-transmission, running, steering, braking, buffer and coupling devices), the amendment in force 1 April 2020 added "electronic-control-device maintenance" — removing and calibrating (aiming) the cameras and radar of automated driving devices and collision-damage-mitigation brakes. Whether it applies varies by the work, so confirm with the Transport Branch Office before you start.

**Q. Besides the work area, how many mechanics does certification need?**
A. Including the maintenance supervisor, two or more employees engaged in specified maintenance are required. A set proportion must be qualified automobile mechanics — as a guide, 1 for 2–4 workers, 2 for 5–8, 3 for 9–12. The maintenance supervisor is in principle appointed from Class 1 or Class 2 automobile mechanics (excluding Class 2 chassis mechanics where the engine is a target). Confirm the human requirements with the Transport Branch Office.

**Q. Can I lease an empty warehouse in a residential district and make it a workshop?**
A. It depends on the use-zone. An auto repair shop may be built only in limited zones under Table 2 of the Building Standards Act, and not in Cat. 1/2 low-rise or residential-exclusive zones. As a guide, quasi-residential allows total work-area floor area ≤150㎡, neighbourhood commercial/commercial/quasi-industrial ≤300㎡, and industrial/exclusive-industrial no limit. A use-change from a warehouse may need a building-confirmation application. Confirm the zone and the building's history before signing.

**Q. How do you choose the property between a certified workshop and a designated workshop (private inspection station)?**
A. A certified workshop brings the maintained vehicle to the Transport Branch inspection lane. A designated workshop (Art. 94-2) needs, on top of the certified-workshop requirements, a completion inspection bay, inspection machinery and an automobile inspector, and completes the inspection in-house. A designated workshop needs a larger site, ceiling height and investment, so the usual order is to choose the property as a certified workshop first and aim for a designated workshop as the business grows. Deciding the target at the property-selection stage reduces rework.
