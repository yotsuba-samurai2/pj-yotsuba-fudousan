**In short:** for a home-visit nursing station, the law first asks two things of the premises — (1) a dedicated office, and (2) the equipment and supplies needed to provide home-visit nursing (Standards for Designated In-Home Services, Ministry of Health and Welfare Ordinance No. 37 of 1999, Art. 62). Because the service goes to the client's own home, you do not need resident rooms as an elderly-care facility or serviced housing would. Zoning, too, is relatively easy: home-visit care and nursing offices are treated by a technical notice (Kokujūgai No. 107, 13 November 2015) as "senior welfare centres and the like," so there is room to set one up even in residential-only zones. Whether a designation is granted is judged by the designating authority (the prefecture / the Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare); we only provide property information.

When you set out to open a home-visit nursing station, the first thing you puzzle over is "what premises should I rent?" Articles on elderly-care facilities and serviced housing for the elderly list detailed standards for room size, barrier-free design and floor area — but home-visit nursing goes to the client's home, so the way to think about premises is fundamentally different. This article is for nurses and medical-corporation staff considering a new home-visit nursing station, and for real-estate practitioners searching for business premises. ## What does a home-visit nursing station first need in its premises?

A home-visit nursing station is an office from which nurses visit clients' homes to provide nursing care and assist with treatment. Because clients are not lodged or housed on the premises, no resident rooms, baths, or dining areas are needed. What the law asks of the premises comes down to three points.

| Point | What is required | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Office | A **dedicated office** of sufficient size to run the business | Standards, Art. 62 |
| Equipment / supplies | The equipment and supplies needed to provide home-visit nursing | Same, Art. 62 |
| Zoning | Home-visit care/nursing offices are treated as "senior welfare centres and the like" | Building Standards Act, Appended Table 2 · Kokujūgai No. 107 |

**What matters first is whether you can secure a dedicated office.** Rather than a floor-area figure, what is asked is whether you can set aside, separately from other businesses, space to store records, manage client information, and let staff stand by and hand over. For how the view of premises differs from "facility-type" care operations, see [an elderly-care office is decided by zoning](https://luck428.com/column/kaigo-jigyousho-bukken-youto-chiiki).

## How large an office, and what infection-control partition, do you really need?

Article 62 of the Standards provides that a designated home-visit nursing station "shall establish a dedicated office of a size sufficient to run the business, and shall provide the equipment and supplies necessary to provide designated home-visit nursing." **No nationwide uniform floor-area figure is written in the text.** In practice, each prefecture's or designated-city's designation-application guide shows a benchmark for "sufficient size" (consultation space, an infection-control perspective, and so on), so you check the guide for your planned location when you start.

It is often misunderstood that "an independent infection-control partition is required," but what the law uniformly mandates as a structural feature of the premises is not a stand-alone infection zone. Prevention of the spread of infection is framed on the operational side (a committee, guidelines, training and drills for measures against infectious disease) and as a hygiene-management requirement to keep equipment and supplies sanitary. So at the premises stage, what you look at is whether the "groundwork for a partition" can be taken.

| What to check at the premises | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Hand-hygiene fixtures | Can a hand-washing basin / sink be secured near the office? |
| Cleaning / disinfection space | Is there a work surface to handle visit equipment, separating clean and unclean? |
| Clean storage area | Can shelving / a partition be taken to keep supplies and records clean? |
| Privacy | Can the office handling client information be separated from visitors and shared areas? |

The figures and the handling of required partitions vary by municipality. **When you start, check the designation-application guide published by the prefecture / designated city of your planned location directly (these guides are revised from time to time).**

## Is zoning a problem (Building Standards Act, Appended Table 2)?

With business premises, zoning is often the first hurdle, but for a home-visit nursing station it is comparatively lenient. Home-visit care/nursing offices were once read as "offices" under Article 48 and Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950), and there was a time when they were treated as unbuildable in residential-only zones.

However, following the Regulatory Reform Implementation Plan, a technical notice from the Urban Building Division, Housing Bureau, Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (**Kokujūgai No. 107, 13 November 2015**) provided that offices for providing home-visit care/nursing and similar services to residents (excluding hospitals, clinics, shops and the like) are to be treated as "senior welfare centres and the like," widening the room to set one up **even in residential-only zones including Category 1 low-rise exclusive residential zones.**

| Zone | Rough treatment of a home-visit care/nursing office |
|---|---|
| Category 1 / 2 low-rise exclusive residential | Room to build as "senior welfare centres and the like" (mind size limits) |
| Mid/high-rise, residential, quasi-residential | Easy to build |
| Neighbourhood commercial, commercial, quasi-industrial | Easy to build |
| Exclusive industrial | Mind the use restriction (confirm by the zoning designation) |

That said, size (floor-area) limits and whether a change-of-use confirmation (Art. 87(1)) is needed when converting an existing building vary by property and zone. **Whether the zoning allows it, and whether a change-of-use confirmation is required, can be confirmed at the specified administrative agency before signing.**

## Can you open in a detached house or one room of a rental apartment?

Sometimes yes. Because a home-visit nursing station needs no resident rooms, part of a detached house or one room of a rental apartment or office building can be a candidate, so long as you can secure a dedicated office and the necessary equipment and supplies. At the premises-selection stage, check the following.

| Point | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lease's purpose of use | Can it be used as "office / business"? A residential-only lease may not allow it |
| Condominium management rules | Is business use barred by a use restriction (residential only) on the exclusive area? |
| Securing a dedicated area | Can a dedicated office be taken, separated from other businesses (if co-located)? |
| Groundwork for hygiene | Can you take a partition for hand-washing, cleaning/disinfection, and clean storage? |
| Barrier-free / access | Staff flow lines and ease of carrying records and equipment in and out |

**"Using a room rented as a residence directly as an office" is where leases and management rules trip you up.** Check the lease's purpose of use and the management rules' use restriction before deciding. On what to read in a lease, see [what to read in a lease agreement](https://luck428.com/column/chintaishaku-keiyakusho-doko-wo-yomu).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is there a set minimum floor area for a home-visit nursing station's premises?**
A. Article 62 of the Standards requires "a dedicated office of a size sufficient to run the business," but no nationwide uniform figure is written in the text. In practice each prefecture's / designated city's designation-application guide shows a benchmark, so check the guide for your planned location when you start. Guides are revised from time to time.

**Q. Is an independent room (partition) for infection control always required?**
A. The law does not uniformly mandate an "independent infection zone" as a structural feature of the premises. Prevention of the spread of infection is an operational-standard requirement (committee, guidelines, training, etc.) and a hygiene-management matter; at the premises stage you look at whether a partition for hand hygiene, cleaning/disinfection and clean storage can be taken within the office. Confirm the details in the guide for your planned location.

**Q. Can you open a home-visit nursing station in a residential-only zone?**
A. There is room to. Home-visit care/nursing offices are treated by the technical notice (Kokujūgai No. 107, 13 November 2015) as "senior welfare centres and the like," and are said to be establishable even in residential-only zones including Category 1 low-rise exclusive residential zones. Since size limits and whether a change of use is needed vary by property and zone, confirm with the specified administrative agency.

**Q. Can you open in one room of a rental apartment?**
A. If you can secure a dedicated office and the necessary equipment and supplies, and the lease's purpose of use and the management rules allow business use, it is a candidate. Because a residential-only lease or a use restriction in the management rules can trip you up, check the contract and rules before deciding. The final decision on the designation is made by the designating authority.
