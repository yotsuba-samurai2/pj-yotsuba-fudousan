## For a property that handles hazardous materials, from what quantity does regulation begin?

The starting point is the "designated quantity." Each item classed as a hazardous material under the Fire Service Act has a designated quantity fixed by Cabinet Order (Order on the Regulation of Hazardous Materials, Appended Table 3). The amount handled divided by the designated quantity is the "multiple of the designated quantity," and this multiple and quantity set the entry point of regulation.

| Quantity band | Regulation | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| At or above the designated quantity | Fire Service Act (manufacturing/storage/handling facility) | Mayoral installation permit + completion inspection (Fire Service Act Art. 11) |
| One-fifth up to below the designated quantity (small quantity) | Municipal fire-prevention ordinance | Notification to the fire department (small-quantity storage/handling site) |
| Below one-fifth of the designated quantity | In principle outside the facility regulation | — (general fire management applies separately) |

The designated quantities of typical items are as follows. For several items, divide each by its designated quantity and add the results; if the total reaches one or more, it is treated as at or above the designated quantity.

| Item (Class 4, flammable liquids) | Designated quantity |
|---|---|
| Petrol (first petroleum, non-water-soluble) | 200 L |
| Kerosene / diesel (second petroleum, non-water-soluble) | 1,000 L |
| Heavy oil (third petroleum, non-water-soluble) | 2,000 L |

So even within "a property that handles hazardous materials," the conditions differ clearly between a scale where a small-quantity notification suffices and a scale that needs an installation permit. Fixing the items and the maximum stored/handled quantity before deciding the property is the starting point. ## In which use-zone can you build a service station or hazardous-materials warehouse?

A building that handles hazardous materials is subject to the use restrictions of Building Standards Act Art. 48 and Appended Table 2. Appended Table 2 sets what may be built in each use-zone, and a building used for the storage or processing of hazardous materials is narrowed by zone according to quantity under Building Standards Act Enforcement Order Art. 130-9.

| Facility | Use-zone that tends to fit |
|---|---|
| Service station (petrol station) | Limited by quantity; residential-exclusive zones barred or heavily restricted, so in practice quasi-industrial, neighbourhood-commercial, commercial and industrial zones |
| Hazardous-materials warehouse (indoor storage, etc.) | The larger the amount, the more it leans industrial; very small amounts may be built in category-2 residential, quasi-residential, etc. |
| Manufacturing / general handling facility | Mainly quasi-industrial, industrial, exclusive-industrial |

Note that the "quantity of hazardous materials" under the Building Standards Act is judged by a different yardstick from the designated quantity under the Fire Service Act. Building Standards Act Appended Table 2 and Enforcement Order Art. 130-9 narrow the zone by the maximum amount of hazardous materials that can momentarily exist in the work area. Because this differs in approach from the Fire Service Act permit categories (the multiple of the designated quantity), you check both against each other. Kyoto City's material likewise organises the relationship between use-zones and hazardous-materials facilities by quantity.

Residential use-zones do not suit a service station or hazardous-materials warehouse. Trouble with neighbours and environmental concerns arise easily, so in practice it is safer to search on the premise of quasi-industrial, industrial and exclusive-industrial zones.

## What conditions attach to the property's structure, safety distance and clear space?

For a manufacturing/storage/handling facility at or above the designated quantity, the Fire Service Act and the Order on the Regulation of Hazardous Materials set technical standards for structure, equipment and siting. What matters at the property-selection stage is the following "site size and layout" conditions.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Safety distance | Manufacturing facilities, indoor storage, outdoor tank storage, etc. must keep a certain distance from objects to be protected — housing, schools, hospitals, important cultural properties (Order Art. 9 and others). Housing about 10 m; schools/hospitals about 30 m |
| Clear space | Open space of a width matching the multiple of the designated quantity must be secured around the facility (for firefighting and to prevent spread) |
| Structure / equipment | Non-combustible materials, oil/liquid retention dikes on the floor, ventilation and firefighting equipment, standards for buried tanks below ground level, etc. |
| Service-station space | A service station has its own standards, such as the fuelling space and dispensing space (Order Art. 17) |

Safety distance and clear space are decided not just inside the building but by "how much margin the site can take." For the same floor area, a site with housing or a school nearby cannot meet the safety distance, and a cramped lot with no surrounding open space fails on the clear space. Property size is judged not by "does the building fit," but by whether it works including separation and open space.

## When using a former service station for another use, what do you check for underground tanks and soil?

A former service station may have buried tanks and piping underground even with no building above. When converting it to another use, check the following before signing.

| What to check | Point |
|---|---|
| Presence/condition of underground tanks | Whether the tank was removed at abandonment or filled with sand/mortar and left; leaving it changes the later works burden |
| Soil / groundwater contamination | History of contamination by oil, benzene, etc.; confirm through a specialist firm's survey (phase 1/2, etc.) |
| Buried objects / piping | Whether fuelling pipes, vent pipes and other buried objects interfere with the foundation work of the new use |
| Records of abandonment | Whether there is a use-abandonment notification (fire) of the hazardous-materials facility, or the completion-inspection certificate at abandonment |

The underground-tank and soil survey and remediation are by a specialist survey firm; our company (real estate) handles interviewing on the property's history and connecting you to the survey firm, as information. We do not judge whether contamination exists or whether measures are needed. Signing without knowing the underground condition can bring unexpected removal and remediation costs later. It is safer to grasp the feasibility and cost of conversion through a survey before signing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I use a warehouse for hazardous-materials storage right after renting it?**
A. Often not right away. To store or handle at or above the designated quantity, you need an installation permit and completion inspection (Fire Service Act Art. 11) as a manufacturing/storage/handling facility, meeting the use-zone, safety distance, clear space and structure standards. For one-fifth up to below the designated quantity, a small-quantity notification under the municipal fire-prevention ordinance suffices. Fix the items and maximum quantity before deciding the property.

**Q. From what quantity is a permit required?**
A. A permit is required at or above the designated quantity. Designated quantities are set item by item in Appended Table 3 of the Order on the Regulation of Hazardous Materials: petrol 200 L, kerosene/diesel 1,000 L, heavy oil 2,000 L as a guide. For several items, divide each by its designated quantity and add the results. One-fifth up to below the designated quantity is a small quantity, subject to notification under the municipal fire-prevention ordinance.

**Q. Does any use-zone do?**
A. No. A building that handles hazardous materials — a service station, a hazardous-materials warehouse — is narrowed in zone and quantity by Building Standards Act Art. 48, Appended Table 2 and Enforcement Order Art. 130-9. Residential-exclusive zones are barred or heavily restricted, and in practice quasi-industrial, industrial and exclusive-industrial are the mainstay. The Building Standards Act's "quantity of hazardous materials" is a different yardstick from the Fire Service Act's designated quantity, so check both against each other.
