## How is a sublease contract succeeded to in an owner change?

A sublease (master lease) is a scheme in which the property owner (lessor) leases the building as a whole to a sublease operator (specified sublessor = lessee), who in turn subleases it to tenants. When a third party buys this building, the position of lessor in principle transfers to the buyer. Civil Code Art. 605-2(1) provides that where a lease has been given a requirement for perfection and the real property is transferred, the position of lessor transfers to the transferee. The requirement for perfection of a building lease is delivery of the building (Art. 31 of the Land and Building Lease Act), and as long as the sublease operator has received delivery of the building and is subleasing and managing it, this requirement is normally satisfied.

Accordingly, the buyer in principle succeeds as it is to the landlord's position in the original contract (the master-lease contract) between the seller and the master-lease operator. The rent (the master-lease rent the owner receives) and the obligation to return the deposit are also succeeded to by the buyer (Art. 605-2(4)). To assert this succession against the lessee (the sublease operator), registration of the transfer of ownership of the property is required (Art. 605-2(3)). Even if the buyer wishes to "lease on new terms," they cannot unilaterally change the terms of the existing original contract, and it is the very content of the existing contract that binds the buyer's return and exit — this is the starting point of owner-change DD.

| Issue | Treatment | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer of the position of lessor | In principle transfers to the buyer | Civil Code Art. 605-2(1) |
| Rent / deposit-return obligation | Succeeded to by the buyer | Art. 605-2(4) |
| Assertion against the lessee | Registration of ownership transfer required | Art. 605-2(3) |
| Requirement for perfection of the building lease | Delivery of the building | Land and Building Lease Act Art. 31 |

## What explanation to the buyer did the 2020 sublease law make mandatory?

Of the Rental Housing Management Business Act enacted in 2020 (Reiwa 2), the sublease-regulation part came into force on 15 December 2020, and the registration system for the rental housing management business came into force on 15 June 2021. For a specified master-lease contract (a master-lease contract; Art. 2(4)), this law imposed on the specified sublessor and the solicitor the prohibition of exaggerated advertising and the like (Art. 28), the prohibition of improper solicitation and the like (Art. 29 — the prohibition of deliberately failing to tell the truth, or telling an untruth, about an important matter affecting judgment), and an important-matters explanation by delivery of a document before the contract (Art. 30).

What should be noted here is that the important-matters explanation under Art. 30 is an obligation that the sublease operator performs, before concluding the master-lease contract, towards the person who is to become the owner (lessor). In other words, who is protected is the owner who leases the property to the sublease operator, and it is not an explanation automatically given to the buyer who buys the property in an owner change. In the buyer's DD, confirm whether the seller received the Art. 30 explanation when they originally concluded this master-lease contract, and whether that important-matters explanation document (which states the conditions for revising the rent, the exemption period, matters concerning termination, and so on) remains. In addition, in the sale to the buyer, the real estate broker explains the content of the lease (the master lease) to be succeeded to, within the important-matters explanation under Art. 35 of the Brokerage Act.

| Regulation | Content | Who is protected |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition of exaggerated advertising (Art. 28) | Prohibition of markedly misleading representations on rent, maintenance, termination, etc. | Owner / solicited person |
| Prohibition of improper solicitation (Art. 29) | Prohibition of deliberate non-disclosure / untrue statement | Owner / solicited person |
| Important-matters explanation before the contract (Art. 30) | Document explanation of rent revision, exemption period, termination, etc. | Owner concluding the master lease |

## Why can a sublease not be easily terminated (the just cause of the Land and Building Lease Act)?

Even if the buyer wishes to end the original contract with the sublease operator and switch to self-management or another operator, it is not easy. The master-lease contract is a building lease, and a notice of refusal to renew or of termination from the lessor (the buyer) requires just cause (Art. 28 of the Land and Building Lease Act). Just cause is judged comprehensively from the circumstances in which both the lessor and the lessee need to use the building, the past course, the state of use and current state of the building, an offer of a payment such as a relocation fee, and so on, and the lessor cannot in principle terminate unilaterally on their own convenience. The sublease operator (lessee) is in a position protected by the Land and Building Lease Act, and this is a risk peculiar to a sublease, different from a rental property that is simply easy to resell.

Another important issue is rent. Art. 32(1) of the Land and Building Lease Act provides that where the rent has become unreasonable owing to changes in economic circumstances and the like, the parties may, regardless of the terms of the contract, request an increase or decrease of the rent for the future. This right to claim a reduction is understood to operate as a mandatory provision even where there is a special agreement for an automatic rent increase or a special agreement advertised as a "rent guarantee." In other words, the sublease operator may, regardless of the rent guarantee in the contract, claim a reduction of the rent from the owner (the buyer). In the buyer's DD, you need to estimate on the premise that the master-lease rent that underpins the income calculation is not guaranteed to be maintained in the future. The final judgment on contract interpretation, whether termination is possible and whether there is just cause lies with a lawyer; a rent-reduction negotiation is for the parties and a lawyer.

| Risk | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Difficulty of termination | A termination / refusal to renew by the lessor requires just cause | Land and Building Lease Act Art. 28 |
| Rent-reduction claim | A reduction may be claimed even with a rent-guarantee clause | Land and Building Lease Act Art. 32(1) |
| Forced succession | The buyer in principle succeeds as it is to the landlord's position | Civil Code Art. 605-2 |

## In the buyer's DD, which parts of the rent roll and the original contract are scrutinised?

In the practice of the buyer's DD, match the master-lease contract (the original contract) against the rent roll and pick out the clauses that bear on return and exit. In the original contract, confirm the master-lease rent the owner receives and the conditions for its revision (how often and by what index it is reviewed), the exemption period (the period during which payment to the owner stops when there is a vacancy), the division of restoration and repair costs, the mid-term termination clause and penalty, the contract term and the renewal provisions, and the reality of the rent guarantee (that the reduction claim under Art. 32 of the Land and Building Lease Act cannot be excluded). In the rent roll, match the rent the sublease operator pays the owner against the sublease rent the operator obtains from the tenants (the operator's margin), the actual occupancy rate and the state of arrears and vacancies, and see whether the rent in the original contract matches the reality of the subleasing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I buy a sublease property, can I change the previous contract terms?**
A. In principle you cannot. The buyer in principle succeeds as it is to the position of lessor (Civil Code Art. 605-2) and is bound by the existing master-lease contract's terms. You cannot unilaterally change the rent or the termination conditions, and a termination or refusal to renew by the lessor requires just cause (Art. 28 of the Land and Building Lease Act). Because the content of the existing contract decides the buyer's return and exit, you need to scrutinise the original contract before acquisition.

**Q. If there is a "rent guarantee," will the rental income not fall in the future either?**
A. It may fall. Art. 32(1) of the Land and Building Lease Act provides that, regardless of the terms of the contract, the parties may claim an increase or decrease of the rent, and this right to claim a reduction is understood to operate even where there is a special agreement for an automatic rent increase or a rent guarantee. Because the sublease operator may claim a reduction regardless of the rent guarantee in the contract, it is important that the buyer not build the income calculation on the premise that the rent will be maintained in the future. The negotiation and judgment are the field of a lawyer.

**Q. Can the buyer also receive the sublease-law important-matters explanation?**
A. The important-matters explanation under Art. 30 of the Rental Housing Management Business Act is an obligation that the sublease operator performs, before concluding the master-lease contract, towards the person who is to become the owner. It is not given automatically to the buyer who buys the property in an owner change. In the buyer's DD, confirm whether the seller originally received this explanation and whether that important-matters explanation document remains. In the sale to the buyer, a real estate transaction specialist explains the content of the lease to be succeeded to in the important-matters explanation under Art. 35 of the Brokerage Act.

**Q. If I want to replace the sublease operator, what should I do?**
A. A termination or refusal to renew of the master-lease contract requires just cause (Art. 28 of the Land and Building Lease Act), and you cannot in principle replace the operator unilaterally on the owner's convenience. Whether termination is possible, whether there is just cause and whether a relocation fee is needed are judged by the individual circumstances, and the final judgment is the field of a lawyer.
