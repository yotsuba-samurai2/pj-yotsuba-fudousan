What the Fill Regulation Act makes subject to permission is construction work—fills, cuts and the like above a certain scale within the area—not transferring ownership (the sale). What affects price and saleability is whether the buyer needs permission to do construction or rebuild there, and whether the existing fill or retaining wall is safe. ## What is the Fill Regulation Act, and what changed and when?

The Fill Regulation Act (Act on Regulation of Residential Land Development and Specified Embankments) thoroughly revised the former Act on Regulation of Residential Land Development by Act No. 55 of 2022 and came into force on 26 May 2023. The aim is to regulate dangerous fills under a nationwide uniform standard regardless of the land's use or purpose. As a result, not only residential land development but also fills onto farmland or forest, and the stockpiling of earth and stones, came within the regulation.

There are two kinds of regulation area.

| Area | What kind of land | Permission for work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential-land-development, etc. work regulation area | Built-up areas or settlements and the like, where a fill would endanger houses, etc. | Work above a certain scale needs permission of the prefectural governor, etc. (Act art. 12 (1)) |
| Specified-embankment, etc. regulation area | Away from built-up areas, but where terrain etc. means a fill could collapse and endanger houses, etc. | Work above a certain scale needs permission of the prefectural governor, etc. (Act art. 30 (1)) |

Separately, there is also a scheme to designate land already graded, where the risk of disaster is large, as a "graded-land disaster-prevention area" (Act art. 45). In all cases the areas are designated by the prefectural governor, etc., and the progress of designation differs by region. The important point is that these regulations attach to "construction work," and do not prohibit the sale of the land itself.

## How do you find out whether your inherited land is in a regulation area?

Whether it is within an area is checked first in the public information of the prefecture (or designated/core city, etc.) that designates the area. Many municipalities publish the designation and make it viewable on a web map or at a counter.

| How to check | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Municipal published map / counter | Check the area at the prefecture's fill-regulation section (erosion control, residential-land disaster prevention, development guidance, etc.) |
| Whether notified | Area designation takes effect by notification; check the date and extent |
| Graded-land disaster-prevention area | Check whether it is separately designated (targets existing graded land) |
| Condition on site | Check on site for past fills/cuts, retaining walls, cliffs, spring water, etc. |

Area designation is being carried out progressively by each prefecture, etc., and designation may differ even within the same city. For land about which we receive a sale inquiry, we check the likelihood of the area applying from the municipality's public information and connect it to the survey toward the important-matters explanation. The final judgment on the area rests with the relevant municipality (we confirm based on the public information as of 23 October 2026).

## Can land with an existing fill or retaining wall be sold, and who judges its safety?

Land with an existing fill or retaining wall can be sold. But if the buyer newly rebuilds or grades, work above a certain scale within an area needs permission, and whether the existing retaining wall conforms to current standards becomes an issue. An old retaining wall (two-tier walls, aged interlocking blocks, unknown structure, etc.) ties directly to the buyer's funding plan and the cost of demolition and rebuilding.

| Axis | Who judges |
| --- | --- |
| Structure, deterioration, legality of the existing retaining wall | Architect / civil-engineering specialist (survey, assessment, design) |
| Whether work within the area needs permission | The relevant prefecture, etc. (permit review) |
| Effect on the land's price | Real-estate transaction business (us; appraisal, brokerage) |

We cannot assert "this retaining wall is safe." Assessing safety and designing a rebuild are the field of an architect or civil-engineering specialist; we organise the condition (presence of a retaining wall, past grading, whether it is cliff land) and reflect it in the sale steps. ## How is the Fill Regulation Act explained in the important-matters explanation?

When a real-estate broker brokers a sale, it explains the restrictions under laws and regulations to the buyer in the important-matters explanation under article 35 of the Real Estate Brokerage Act. Whether land falls within the Fill Regulation Act's residential-land-development work regulation area, specified-embankment regulation area, or graded-land disaster-prevention area is subject to explanation as a "restriction under laws and regulations" set out in article 3 of that Act's Enforcement Order.

| What is explained | Point |
| --- | --- |
| Whether it falls within an area | Whether it is in the work regulation area, specified-embankment regulation area, or graded-land disaster-prevention area |
| Restrictions within the area | That work above a certain scale needs permission (affects the buyer's rebuilding/grading) |
| Existing fill / retaining wall | Explain to the extent ascertainable as the condition; the safety judgment is a specialist's field |

Even if it is within an area, a sale forms if that fact is correctly surveyed and explained. Rather, neglecting the explanation risks later claims of non-conformity or breach of the explanation duty. We reflect the area-check result accurately in the important-matters explanation and explain, including the possibility that the buyer needs permission for rebuilding or grading. Whether permission is actually granted and which work is subject to permission are individual judgments in the field of the relevant municipality and the administrative scrivener who handles the application.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If my inherited land is in a fill-regulation area, can it no longer be sold?**
A. It can be sold. What the Fill Regulation Act makes subject to permission is construction work—fills, cuts and the like above a certain scale within the area—not the sale of the land itself (Act arts. 12 (1) and 30 (1)). What affects price and saleability is whether the buyer needs permission for rebuilding/grading and whether the existing retaining wall is safe. We survey whether the area applies and reflect it in the important-matters explanation.

**Q. Where can I find out whether land is in a fill-regulation area?**
A. The prefectural governor, etc. designates the area, and it is usually viewable on a web map or at a counter together with the notification. You can check at the fill-regulation section (erosion control, residential-land disaster prevention, development guidance, etc.). For land about which we receive a sale inquiry, we also check the likelihood of the area applying from the municipality's public information. The final judgment on the area rests with the relevant municipality.

**Q. The land has an old retaining wall. Can a real-estate company judge whether it is safe?**
A. No. Judging whether a retaining wall or fill is safe, its conformity with current standards, and designing a rebuild are the field of an architect or civil-engineering specialist. We organise the condition (presence of a retaining wall, past grading, whether it is cliff land) and reflect it in the sale, and connect you to a specialist for the safety assessment. Whether the buyer needs permission when doing work is the field of the relevant municipality and the administrative scrivener.

**Q. How is land in a fill-regulation area treated in the important-matters explanation?**
A. Whether land falls within a Fill Regulation Act area is subject to the important-matters explanation as a "restriction under laws and regulations" under Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35 and art. 3 of that Act's Enforcement Order. Even if it is within an area, a sale forms if it is correctly surveyed and explained. Because neglecting the explanation risks later liability, we reflect the area-check result accurately and explain it.
