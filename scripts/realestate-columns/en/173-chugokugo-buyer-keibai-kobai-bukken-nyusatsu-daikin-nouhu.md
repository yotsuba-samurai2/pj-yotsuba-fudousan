## How do auction, public sale and negotiated sale differ in Japan?

The three routes, often talked of together as "a cheap way to buy," differ entirely in governing law, procedure and the risk the buyer bears. Confusing them means taking on the overlooked risk as it is.

An auction is a procedure where, on the application of a creditor (a mortgagee or a creditor who has won a judgment), the court (execution court) attaches and sells the debtor's real estate; the basis is the Civil Execution Act. A public sale is a delinquency disposition where the state (tax office) or a local government attaches and sells the property of a tax delinquent; the basis is the National Tax Collection Act and the Local Tax Act. A negotiated sale is a sale based on the agreement of an ordinary seller and buyer, where the important-matters explanation under the Real Estate Brokerage Act (art. 35) and the liability for non-conformity apply.

| Route | Governing law | Seller / operator | Buyer-side feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auction | Civil Execution Act | Execution court (creditor's application) | Self-judgment from the three-piece set. As-is; no non-conformity liability |
| Public sale | National Tax Collection Act / Local Tax Act | Tax office / local government | Delinquency disposition. Separate payment and rights-transfer procedure |
| Negotiated sale | Civil Code / Brokerage Act | Ordinary seller | Important-matters explanation and non-conformity liability apply |

The biggest feature common to auction and public sale is that the seller's cooperation cannot be assumed. You cannot view the interior and judge at your own responsibility from the materials the court or administration makes public and the exterior at the site. The seller's liability for non-conformity under the Civil Code (formerly defect-warranty liability) does not apply in principle, and as-is acquisition is the basis. For a Chinese-speaking buyer, this "no seller explanation or warranty" is the biggest difference from a negotiated sale. The buyer-side due-diligence viewpoint for a negotiated sale is organised separately in [the key points of the important-matters explanation to read before introducing a Japanese income property to a Chinese-speaking buyer](https://luck428.com/en/column/chugokugo-buyer-juyojiko-check-toushi-bukken).

## Where do you read the three-piece set (property description, status-survey report, appraisal)?

At an auction, in place of the seller's explanation, the court prepares and makes public three documents called the "three-piece set." These can be viewed and downloaded on the court's real-estate-auction property information site "BIT," and are materials everyone considering a bid is expected to read. The basis provisions and roles are as follows.

| Document | Author / basis | What to read |
|---|---|---|
| Property description | Court clerk (CEA art. 62) | Rights not extinguished by sale (rights the buyer takes on), outline of statutory superficies, occupation status |
| Status-survey report | Execution officer's survey (art. 57) | Current state, occupation relationship, use. Photos and floor plan |
| Appraisal | Appraiser (art. 58) | Basis for the appraised value, state of land and building, market discounts |

The knack in the reading order is to first confirm in the property description whether there are "rights the buyer takes on." Rights that disappear by sale (such as a mortgage) and rights that remain and are taken on by the buyer (a lease with countervailing power, or superficies in certain cases) are divided, and missing this leaves you bearing an unexpected burden after winning. Next, confirm the presence and attributes of occupants in the status-survey report and the premise of the price (as-is, market discounts) in the appraisal.

The three-piece set is a document where law, real estate and on-site reading intersect. The legal assessment of rights is the field of a lawyer / judicial scrivener and the adequacy of the appraised value a real estate appraiser's field, and we do not assert whether rights are taken on or the winning price. ## What are the deadlines and order for the guarantee, bid and payment of the price?

The basic order of an auction (period bid) is: provide the guarantee → bid → opening → sale-permission decision → payment of the price. Each has a basis provision and a deadline.

A person making a purchase offer provides, before bidding, the guarantee in the amount and method set by the execution court (the bid guarantee; CEA art. 66). The guarantee amount is set by the execution court, and for a period bid, by the rules, two-tenths of the standard sale price is the principle. The bid amount must be at or above the "minimum purchase price" (art. 60), which is the standard sale price less two-tenths. At the opening, the highest bidder is fixed, and on the sale-decision date the permission or non-permission of the sale is pronounced (art. 69). Once the sale-permission decision becomes final, the buyer pays, by the deadline set by the court clerk, the price less the guarantee from the bid amount (art. 78). The guarantee is applied to the price.

| Stage | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Standard sale price / minimum purchase price | Set the standard sale price from the appraisal; bid at 80% or more of the standard sale price | CEA art. 60 |
| Bid guarantee | Provide the guarantee before bidding (for a period bid, by the rules, 20% of the standard sale price in principle) | CEA art. 66 |
| Sale-permission decision | Pronounce permission/non-permission on the sale-decision date; execution appeal possible until final | CEA arts. 69, 74 |
| Payment of the price | After the permission decision is final, pay by the clerk's deadline; the guarantee is applied to the price | CEA art. 78 |

Note that even winning (becoming the highest bidder) means that if you do not pay the price by the deadline, the sale permission loses effect and the guarantee provided is not returned. In a public sale, under the National Tax Collection Act and the Local Tax Act, the flow is bid (or auction-sale) → sale decision → payment of the purchase price, and the handling of the public-sale guarantee is also provided for. If the buyer does not pay the price by the deadline and the sale decision is cancelled, the public-sale guarantee is applied to the delinquent tax, etc. — a different rule from an auction. Because the deadlines and order differ by procedure, in an individual case confirm with the court, tax office or local government's guidance and note the reference date.

## How do you have a property with a remaining occupant delivered up?

The foremost inherent risk of an auction property is the case where an occupant remains. Unlike a negotiated sale, the seller does not evict them, so the buyer must realise the delivery. What is used here is the delivery order.

A delivery order is a system where, on the application of the buyer who has paid the price, the execution court orders the debtor or the occupant of the real estate to deliver the real estate to the buyer (CEA art. 83(1)). However, a person recognised in the record as occupying by a title that can be asserted against the buyer (for example an occupant with a lease having countervailing power) is excluded from the delivery order. This application has a deadline: once six months (nine months for the buyer of a building that a mortgage-building user occupied at the time of purchase) have passed from the date of payment of the price, it can no longer be made (art. 83(2)).

| Point | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Subject of the delivery order | After payment, on the buyer's application, order the debtor/occupant to deliver | CEA art. 83(1) |
| Excluded occupant | A person occupying by a title that can be asserted against the buyer (a lease with countervailing power, etc.) | CEA art. 83(1) proviso |
| Deadline for application | Six months from the date of payment (nine months for a building a mortgage-building user occupied) | CEA art. 83(2) |

The attribute of the occupant (whether there is a title that can be asserted), the judgment of whether a delivery order can be used, and the actual delivery / compulsory execution are a lawyer's field. ## When a non-resident wins the bid, how are registration and tax arranged?

When a Chinese-speaking investor wins as a non-resident (a person with no domicile or residence in Japan), registration and tax must be arranged after payment of the price. These are procedures separate from the acquisition of the property itself; they cannot be bundled into one contract, and **each is an independent business contracted with separately.** We handle the real-estate-side information and traffic control, connecting you from one and the same point of contact to each expert. On registration: when the price is paid at an auction, by the court clerk's commission the ownership-transfer registration and the cancellation registration of the disappearing mortgage, etc. are done; but registration procedures the buyer needs, and the arrangement of rights in a public sale and afterward, are a judicial scrivener's field. The identity verification and necessary documents (signature certificate, address certificate, etc.) when a non-resident becomes the party to a sale/acquisition are also confirmed with a judicial scrivener as registration practice. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. What is the difference between an auction and a public sale?**
A. The governing law and operator differ. An auction, under the Civil Execution Act, is a procedure where on a creditor's application the execution court attaches and sells the debtor's real estate. A public sale, under the National Tax Collection Act and the Local Tax Act, is a delinquency disposition where the tax office or local government attaches and sells a tax delinquent's property. Both acquire by bidding and share that the seller's explanation or warranty cannot be assumed (as-is; no non-conformity liability in principle), but the rules for the guarantee, payment and rights transfer differ. Confirm the individual procedure with the court, tax office or local government's guidance.

**Q. What is the three-piece set, and where can I see it?**
A. The three documents the court makes public at an auction: the property description (prepared by the court clerk; CEA art. 62), the status-survey report (the execution officer's survey; art. 57) and the appraisal (the appraiser's appraisal; art. 58). They can be viewed and downloaded on the court's real-estate-auction property information site "BIT." First confirm in the property description whether there are rights the buyer takes on, then read the occupation status in the status-survey report and the price premise in the appraisal. The legal assessment of rights is a lawyer / judicial scrivener's field and the adequacy of the appraised value a real estate appraiser's field.

**Q. What happens if I win but do not pay the price? What happens to the guarantee?**
A. At an auction, before bidding you provide the guarantee the execution court sets (the bid guarantee; CEA art. 66; for a period bid, by the rules, 20% of the standard sale price in principle). If, after the sale-permission decision becomes final, you do not pay by the deadline set by the court clerk and the sale permission loses effect, the guarantee provided is not returned. In a public sale too, if you do not pay by the deadline and the sale decision is cancelled, there is a rule that the public-sale guarantee is applied to the delinquent tax, etc. Because the deadlines and order differ by procedure, confirm with the court, tax office or local government's guidance.

**Q. How do I have an auction property that an occupant lives in delivered up?**
A. There is a delivery-order system where, on the application of the buyer who has paid the price, the execution court orders the debtor/occupant to deliver (Civil Execution Act art. 83(1)). However, a person recognised in the record as occupying by a title that can be asserted against the buyer is excluded. The application can no longer be made once six months (nine months for a building a mortgage-building user occupied at the time of purchase) have passed from the date of payment (art. 83(2)). The assessment of the occupant's attribute, the judgment of whether a delivery order can be used, and the actual delivery / compulsory execution are a lawyer's field, and we do not judge whether delivery is possible.
