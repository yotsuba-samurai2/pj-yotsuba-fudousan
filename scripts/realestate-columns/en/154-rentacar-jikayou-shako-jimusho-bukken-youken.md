**The key point**: When you start a rent-a-car business (the "paid lending of private vehicles" permit under the Road Transport Act [Act No. 183 of 1951] art. 80(1)), the premises that matter are the office and the parking space (garage) for each vehicle. Unlike the freight-haulage business (green plates), the permit's review standards do not squarely impose numeric requirements such as the distance between office and garage or the garage's capacity. Art. 80(1) says: "a private vehicle may not be lent for hire as a business without the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's permission—unless the borrower is the user of that private vehicle" (so a lease where the borrower is the user needs no permit). Art. 80(2) says the Minister must grant permission unless the manner of the lending is found to resemble running a motor-transport business. The permit application goes to the Transport Branch Office with jurisdiction over your principal office (the application form is under Road Transport Act Enforcement Regulation art. 52), and the standard processing period is about one month. What bites on the property side is the parking space for each vehicle lent. The Act on Securing Parking Spaces, etc. for Vehicles (Act No. 145 of 1962, the "Parking Space Act") art. 3 obliges the holder to secure a parking space, and an ordinary vehicle needs a police chief's parking certificate (a "garage certificate") at registration. By Enforcement Order art. 1, the parking space must be within 2 km straight-line of the base of use, allow the vehicle to enter/exit from the road and hold the whole vehicle, and be one you have the right to use. Drafting the permit documents is an administrative scrivener's; the permit itself the Transport Branch Office's; the parking certificate the police's; a dispute over the lease or an accident a lawyer's. ## How far do premises and a garage matter for a rent-a-car permit?

A rent-a-car business (paid lending of private vehicles) runs on the permit under Road Transport Act art. 80(1). What is easily confused here is that the garage requirement of the general freight-haulage business (green-plate haulage) and the property requirement for rent-a-car are different things. For haulage, the permit's review standards state in numbers the distance between office and garage and that the garage can hold all vehicles; the rent-a-car standards do not ask that squarely.

| Business | Basis | What is squarely asked on the property side |
|---|---|---|
| Rent-a-car (paid lending of private vehicles) | Road Transport Act art. 80(1) | An office and a parking space for each vehicle (Parking Space Act) |
| General freight-haulage (green plates) | Motor Truck Transport Business Act | Office–garage distance, garage capacity, front-road width, etc. |

What a rent-a-car permit reviews is that the applicant and officers have no grounds for disqualification, that they have not been disciplined for acts resembling a transport business within a set period, and that they carry enough vehicle insurance—set in each Regional Transport Bureau's public notice (the review standards). So the centre is the operator, the insurance and the lending plan, rather than the property's pass/fail itself. Still, the application form (Enforcement Regulation art. 52) records the office where the lending business is carried out, so the office's real existence and your right to use it are a premise. And to register and run each vehicle lent, a parking space must be secured per vehicle. The property requirements for a haulage office and garage are covered in [the property requirements for a haulage office and garage](https://luck428.com/en/column/unsou-eigyosho-shako-bukken-yoken); this article limits itself to the rent-a-car-specific "office" and "parking space".

## How do I check the parking space (garage)—area, location, right to use?

What bites as property for rent-a-car is the parking space for each vehicle lent. Parking Space Act art. 3 obliges the vehicle's holder to secure a parking space in a place other than the road. An ordinary vehicle needs a parking certificate issued by the police chief at new/transfer/change registration (art. 5). A light vehicle needs only a post-registration notification of the parking space, and only in certain areas (art. 13).

| Requirement | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Location (distance) | Within 2 km straight-line of the base of use | Parking Space Act Enforcement Order art. 1 |
| Entry/exit, capacity | Can enter/exit from the road without trouble and hold the whole vehicle | Enforcement Order art. 1 |
| Right to use | You have the right (ownership, lease, use-consent, etc.) to use it as a parking space | Parking Space Act art. 4 / Enforcement Order art. 1 |

The "base of use" means, for an individual, the address or residence, and for a company, the office location, with a premise that there is in fact living or business there. So if the office (the base of use) and the garage are too far apart, the 2 km distance requirement blocks the parking certificate. The right to use is proven by a self-declaration for your own land, or a parking-space use-consent certificate for another's land/building. If you use leased garage land, whether the lease permits that use (vehicle storage / business use) is the dividing point to check before signing. The parking certificate is applied for at the police station with jurisdiction over the parking space's location. We organise how a candidate property looks against the 2 km requirement, entry/exit and capacity, and the use restriction (the final judgment on suitability rests with the police).

## Can the office be a virtual office or my home?

For a rent-a-car office, the application form under Enforcement Regulation art. 52 records the name and location of the office where the lending business is carried out. What often becomes an issue is whether a virtual office or your home can be the office. It splits into two points. One is whether that place actually carries out the lending business (a virtual office that only rents an address tends to be found to lack business substance). The other is whether it is recognised as the "base of use" under the Parking Space Act. As above, the base of use requires actual living/business, and the parking space must be within 2 km of it.

| Form of office | What to check in property/contract | Who judges |
|---|---|---|
| Leased office | Does the lease permit business use / the lending business | Lessor / Transport Branch Office |
| Home | Is business use not barred by the lease or management rules; can a garage be secured within 2 km | Lessor / management association / police |
| Virtual office | Is there substance actually carrying out the lending business (address-only tends to lack it) | Transport Branch Office |

The final judgment on where the office can be is the Transport Branch Office's; whether a parking certificate can be obtained as the base of use is the police's. ## How do property requirements change for unattended / ICT lending (car-sharing)?

For unattended lending using ICT (rent-a-car-type car-sharing), the way you read the property changes. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism organised the treatment of rent-a-car-type car-sharing, including the one-way (drop-off) method, in a notice (27 March 2014, Kokujijо No. 205 / Kokujiryo No. 609, etc.): those found able to grasp the vehicle's status accurately through ICT may make an unattended off-street car park the vehicle's placement location (placement office), and treat it as the parking space under the Parking Space Act and the "base of use" under the Road Transport Vehicle Act.

| Issue | Treatment under unattended / ICT lending | Basis / window |
|---|---|---|
| Placement location (unattended car park) | Those able to grasp status by ICT may make an unattended off-street car park the placement office = parking space = base of use | Kokujijо No. 205 / Kokujiryo No. 609 |
| Notification | Notify the location of the vehicle's placement location (placement office) to the Transport Branch Office head with jurisdiction over the principal office | The notice / Transport Branch Office |
| Distance requirement | The parking space and the base of use within 2 km straight-line | Parking Space Act Enforcement Order art. 1 |

So even if you make a bay in an unattended monthly car park or a coin-parking a car-sharing vehicle location, the parking-space requirements (2 km, entry/exit and capacity, right to use) and the notification of the placement location follow. At the property-choosing stage, the point is to check whether that bay may be used for lending/storing vehicles (the lease and the car park's management rules). Whether it falls under the notice, and whether an unattended car park is recognised as a placement office, is the Transport Branch Office's judgment. The issues of monthly/coin-parking land itself are touched on in [turning inherited land into monthly/coin parking to sell or lease](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-tsukigime-coin-parking-tochi-baikyaku). This article shows only the framework and leaves the individual judgment to the Transport Branch Office and police.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does a rent-a-car permit have garage-size or distance standards like haulage?**
A. The rent-a-car review standards (Road Transport Act art. 80(1), paid lending of private vehicles) do not squarely ask the office–garage distance or garage capacity in numbers, as the general freight-haulage business does. The review centres on no grounds for disqualification, no disciplinary history for resembling-a-transport-business acts, and carrying enough vehicle insurance. But each vehicle lent separately needs a parking space secured under the Parking Space Act (a garage certificate for ordinary cars), and this is what bites on the property side. The detailed standards are in each Regional Transport Bureau's public notice, and the judgment is the Transport Branch Office's.

**Q. How far from the office may the garage (parking space) be?**
A. By Parking Space Act Enforcement Order art. 1, the parking space must be within 2 km straight-line of the base of use (for an individual the address/residence, for a company the office location). It must also allow entry/exit from the road without trouble, hold the whole vehicle, and be one you have the right to use. The parking certificate (garage certificate) is applied for at the police station with jurisdiction over the parking space, and suitability is judged by the police.

**Q. Can a virtual office or my home be the rent-a-car office?**
A. It is not decided uniformly. The points are whether that place actually carries out the lending business (an address-only virtual office tends to be found to lack substance), whether it is recognised as the base of use under the Parking Space Act (actual living/business is a premise), whether a garage can be secured within 2 km of it, and whether the lease or management rules bar business use. The final judgment on the office is the Transport Branch Office's; whether a parking certificate can be obtained is the police's.

**Q. For unattended car-sharing (one-way), can a coin-parking be the garage?**
A. The Ministry's notice (2014, Kokujijо No. 205 / Kokujiryo No. 609, etc.) treats rent-a-car-type car-sharing found able to grasp the vehicle's status accurately through ICT as able to make an unattended off-street car park the vehicle's placement location (placement office) and the parking space / base of use under the respective Acts. The placement location is notified to the Transport Branch Office head with jurisdiction over the principal office. The 2 km distance requirement and a check that the bay may be used for lending/storage are needed, and whether it falls under the notice is the Transport Branch Office's judgment.
