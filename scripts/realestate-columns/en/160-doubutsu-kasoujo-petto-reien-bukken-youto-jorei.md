## What kind of property lets you open a pet crematorium / animal cemetery?

The first thing to grasp is that animal cremation and burial fall outside the Graveyard and Burial Act. Its art. 2 defines "burial" as interring a corpse in the ground and "cremation" as burning a corpse to inter it, and "grave," "graveyard," "ossuary" and "crematorium" are all built around human remains. Animals do not fit this framework, and no national law squarely regulates the animal-cemetery business.

Far from being unregulated, the absence of a national law means siting is decided by **municipal ordinances/guidance** and by the **soot and odour rules** for the incinerator. A human funeral hall is considered within the Graveyard and Burial Act / Building Standards Act framework, but animal cremation leaves that and lines up a different set of yardsticks. Property selection for a human funeral hall is covered in [conditions for a property usable as a funeral hall](https://luck428.com/en/column/sogi-ceremony-hall-bukken-youto-chusha-kinrin); this article narrows to the issues specific to animal cremation.

The four things to look at first on the property side:

| Check item | Why it matters | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinance/guidance exists? | Many municipalities make pet-cemetery siting a permit / prior-consultation matter | Inquire with the municipality's environment / sanitation division |
| Zoning and Building Standards Act use | Incinerator-bearing facilities are classified variably by municipality | Prior consultation with the competent building authority |
| Distance from neighbours | An ordinance may require a set distance from homes/schools | Measure the distance; confirm the ordinance's distance requirement |
| Soot / odour rules | The incinerator's scale/location brings in the Air Pollution / Offensive Odor Acts | Confirm furnace capacity and designation of regulated areas with the authority |

## Can a pet crematorium be built given the zoning?

This is a point to avoid asserting flatly. A human crematorium is treated as a "crematorium" under Building Standards Act art. 51 and, in principle, cannot be newly built or extended unless its location is fixed in the city plan. But a pet crematorium is not a "crematorium" under the Graveyard and Burial Act, so **how its use is classified under the Building Standards Act (akin to a crematorium? a factory or other treatment facility?) is not fixed uniformly nationwide, and the competent building authority's judgment varies**.

So one cannot declare at the advertising stage that "this zoning definitely allows / forbids it." What is reliable is, once a candidate site is set, to confirm the Building Standards Act use with the competent building authority and to pin down the City Planning Act area classification (urbanisation-promotion or urbanisation-control area). Building is in principle restricted in an urbanisation-control area, and an incinerator-bearing facility raises the bar further.

At the property stage, what we can back up is the objective condition—zoning, area classification, frontage road, site shape, distance to neighbours. Fitting that into the judgment of "can it be permitted as a use akin to a crematorium" belongs to the architect who handles the building confirmation and to the competent authority.

## What is examined in an ordinance's siting permit / prior consultation?

In municipalities that make siting a pet cemetery / animal-cremation facility a permit or prior-consultation matter, roughly the following are examined (a generalisation from municipalities with ordinances such as Kyoto, Takatsuki and Gyoda; the details differ by municipality).

| Commonly examined | Content often seen in ordinances |
|---|---|
| Siting location | Distance from homes, schools, hospitals, water sources; whether the applicant holds title to the land |
| Explanation to neighbours | Explaining to nearby residents in advance and recording/reporting the consent or consultation |
| Structure / equipment | Furnace structure, screening walls/planting, drainage/smoke treatment |
| Prior consultation | Finishing consultation with the municipality before applying (starting without it is sent back) |

Especially heavy are the **prior explanation/consent to neighbours** and the **distance requirement**. If you secure a property and then find "the distance to homes falls short of the ordinance" or "consultation with neighbours cannot be settled," the contract is wasted. That is why inquiring with the municipality about the ordinance/guidance and distance requirement before deciding on a property is, though it looks like a detour, the shortcut.

## How do the soot, odour and neighbour-distance rules bite on property choice?

Once you place an incinerator, soot and odour are unavoidable. But the national laws do not necessarily apply uniformly—**applicability shifts with scale and location**.

| Rule | Basis | How it bites on property choice |
|---|---|---|
| Soot (Air Pollution Control Act) | A waste incinerator is a regulated "soot-emitting facility" at a grate area of 2 m² or more **or** an incineration capacity of 200 kg/h or more (Air Pollution Control Act Enforcement Order, Appended Table 1) | A small pet-cremation furnace often falls below the national scale threshold. If it meets it, notification and emission standards apply |
| Odour (Offensive Odor Control Act) | The prefectural governor (the mayor within a city) designates a "regulated area," within which concentration or odour-index regulation applies (arts. 3, 4) | Whether the site is in a regulated area, and which standard applies, changes the needed measures |
| Municipal soot/odour standards | An ordinance/guidance may add standards separate from the national ones | Even below the national threshold, an ordinance may impose soot/odour standards |

So do not jump to "it's a small furnace, so no national rule applies." Confirm with the authority: (1) whether it meets the Air Pollution Control Act's scale threshold; (2) whether the site is in an Offensive Odor Control Act regulated area; and (3) whether a municipal ordinance adds its own standards. The actual soot/odour design (furnace, dust collection, deodorisation) is a licensed equipment contractor's work; on the property side we look at whether the site can carry those measures—where the furnace and chimney can go and how high, distance to neighbours, exhaust direction. ## How do I confirm whether a candidate property can be used?

Running the pre-contract checks in this order reduces rework:

1. **Inquire about the ordinance/guidance** (with the municipality's environment / sanitation division—permit system? prior-consultation system? distance requirement?).
2. **Confirm the Building Standards Act use and area classification with the competent authority** (treated as akin to a crematorium? not an urbanisation-control area?).
3. **Confirm the soot/odour rules** (does the furnace scale meet the Air Pollution Control Act threshold? is the site in an Offensive Odor Control Act regulated area? any ordinance add-on?).
4. **Confirm the distance to and prospects for explaining to neighbours** (distance to homes/schools; is prior explanation likely to settle?).
5. **Property survey and brokerage** (we confirm zoning, frontage, site shape and existing building, then the important-matters explanation).

Use (1)–(4) to gauge "is this a property you can go and obtain a permit for," and leave the yes/no judgment to the administrative scrivener, architect and municipality—this order is the surest way to avoid getting stuck after securing a property. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does a pet crematorium need a permit under the Graveyard and Burial Act?**
A. No. The Graveyard and Burial Act (the Act on Graveyards and Burial) targets human remains, and animal cremation/burial is outside it. The government indicated in a 2004 statement that the Act does not apply to animals and that no national law directly regulates the animal-cemetery business. Instead, siting is decided by each municipality's ordinance/guidance and by the soot (Air Pollution Control Act) and odour (Offensive Odor Control Act) rules. Inquiring with the municipality is the starting point.

**Q. If the zoning is set, can I conclude a pet crematorium can be built?**
A. Zoning alone is not enough. A human crematorium is settled under Building Standards Act art. 51, but a pet crematorium is not a "crematorium" under the Graveyard and Burial Act, so its Building Standards Act use classification is not fixed uniformly nationwide and the competent authority's judgment varies. Once a site is chosen, confirm the use with the building authority and pin down whether it is an urbanisation-control area. This judgment is the field of the architect who handles the building confirmation.

**Q. Will the real-estate company handle the ordinance siting permit or prior consultation for me?**
A. No. What a real-estate company handles is the property-suitability survey—zoning, area classification, distance to neighbours, the condition of any existing building—and brokerage up to the important-matters explanation (Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35). Preparing the siting-permit application and running prior consultation is an administrative scrivener's work; building confirmation an architect's; soot/odour design and work an equipment contractor's; neighbour disputes a lawyer's—each an independent business contracted with separately. **Q. If it's a small cremation furnace, may I assume the soot and odour rules don't apply?**
A. Don't jump to that. Under the Air Pollution Control Act a waste incinerator becomes regulated at a grate area of 2 m² or more or a capacity of 200 kg/h or more, and a small pet-cremation furnace does often fall below this threshold. But whether the site falls in an Offensive Odor Control Act regulated area, and whether a municipal ordinance adds soot/odour standards over the national ones, must be checked separately.
