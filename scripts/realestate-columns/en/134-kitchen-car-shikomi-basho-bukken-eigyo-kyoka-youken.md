## Why does a food truck need a "commissary" at all?

A food truck (mobile food sales) operates under the licence of Article 55(1) of the Food Sanitation Act (restaurant business, etc.) for the vehicle itself. But the amount of water a vehicle can carry is limited, and health-centre facility standards generally divide what cooking can be done on board by the capacity of the supply and drainage tanks.

The guidance of health centres such as the Higashimatsuyama Health Centre (Saitama) and the Hitachi Health Centre (Ibaraki) explains the tank-capacity guideline roughly as follows (capacities and conditions differ by locality, so it is safest to confirm with the health centre for the intended site, showing a drawing).

| Supply/drainage tank capacity (guideline) | Cooking assumed on board |
| --- | --- |
| About 40L or more | Simple cooking only, or a single item; disposable containers assumed |
| About 80L or more | Simple cooking of up to about two steps, multiple items; disposable containers assumed |
| About 200L or more | Multi-step cooking, or a business using ordinary tableware |

For this reason, "prep"—preliminary processing, cutting, marinating, manufacturing—is hard to complete on board, and the Hitachi Health Centre (Ibaraki) advises that "where prep is needed, it must be done in a facility that has obtained a food business licence." This is why a prep-base (commissary) property is needed separately from the vehicle.

## What equipment does a commissary property require? (water supply, drainage, handwashing, zoning of areas)

A commissary is itself a "facility" that obtains a business licence. The facility standards are set, under Article 54 of the Food Sanitation Act, by each prefecture etc. by ordinance, taking into account the standards set by Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare order (the standards in the appended tables of the Food Sanitation Act Enforcement Regulations). With the 1 June 2021 enforcement of the 2018 amendment, the framework of these standards was levelled nationwide. At the property stage, the following points generally matter.

| Item to check | Point to see in the property |
| --- | --- |
| Water supply / drainage | Whether the necessary supply and drainage can be installed; a system that can treat drainage properly |
| Handwashing | Whether a dedicated handwashing station (with disinfection) can be set in the work area |
| Work area | Whether the preliminary-processing / cooking area can be clearly separated from living quarters and other uses |
| Floor, walls, ceiling | Whether they can be finished in easy-to-clean, impermeable materials |
| Ventilation / lighting | Whether sufficient ventilation and lighting can be secured in the workroom |
| Storage | Whether storage of ingredients and utensils, and refrigeration/freezing, can be placed |

Specific figures and details vary by ordinance and health-centre practice. Rather than deciding the details on the property side, it is safest to show a drawing to the health centre and consult in advance. ## What licence is needed, and who applies?

The business categories requiring a licence are set by Article 35 of the Food Sanitation Act Enforcement Order, under Article 55(1) of the Act. With the 1 June 2021 enforcement of the amendment, the licence categories were reorganised from the former 34 to 32. Which category a commissary falls into changes with the food made and the process.

| Likely category | What case (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Restaurant business | Prepping food in general that is cooked and served |
| Prepared-food (sōzai) manufacturing | Manufacturing side dishes such as simmered, grilled or fried food in bulk |
| Notification category | Some businesses not falling under a licence category (notification under Article 57) |

Which category applies, and whether to obtain the vehicle's licence and the commissary's licence separately or handle them in the same facility, is an individual judgment. Judging this category and preparing the licence application / health-centre consultation are the field of the administrative scrivener and the health centre. ## What should you check on zoning and the lease?

A commissary is a fixed facility, so unlike the vehicle it is subject to zoning restrictions. Article 48 and appended table 2 of the Building Standards Act set the uses of buildings that may be built in each zone. If you prep as a restaurant business it is treated as a "shop / restaurant"; if you manufacture in bulk as a prepared-food manufacturing business it approaches "factory" treatment, and depending on the scale of manufacture may be restricted in residential zones.

| Item to check | Point to see |
| --- | --- |
| Zoning | Whether the intended business use is permitted in that zone |
| Change of use | For an existing building, whether a confirmation application is needed (by scale) |
| Lease | Whether "manufacturing/cooking of food" can be included in the purpose of use; the scope of restoration |
| Neighbourhood | Whether odour, drainage or loading/unloading will clash with neighbours |

Conformity with zoning and whether a change-of-use confirmation application is needed are ultimately the judgment of the designated administrative agency and an architect. We check in advance the property's zoning and the lease's purpose of use, and connect you to an architect where a confirmation application is involved.

## Can you use your home or an existing restaurant as a commissary?

We often hear "can I prep in my home kitchen," but a commissary needs to be a licensed facility, and unless living quarters and the work area are separated it is hard to meet the standards. Even when using part of a home, the dividing line is whether the work area, handwashing and water supply/drainage can be set to standard and separated from the living space.

To use an existing restaurant as a prep base, you need to confirm with the health centre whether that restaurant's licence category covers prep as well, and—if used as a separate business outside opening hours—how to organise the licence holder and category. In either case, feasibility depends on the individual facility and operation, and no unqualified person may assert "it can be used." At the viewing stage, we check the zoning and lease conditions and connect you to an administrative scrivener and the health centre for the licence feasibility.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If the vehicle is licensed, do I not need a licence for the commissary?**
A. The vehicle and the commissary are generally treated as separate facilities, and many health centres advise that where prep is needed it must be done in a licensed facility (Hitachi Health Centre, Ibaraki, and others). How much can be completed on board varies with supply/drainage tank capacity, so confirm with the health centre for the intended site, showing a drawing.

**Q. What licence category will the commissary be?**
A. Depending on the food made and the process, it divides into restaurant business, prepared-food manufacturing and so on. The licence categories are set by Article 55(1) of the Food Sanitation Act and Article 35 of its Enforcement Order, reorganised from 34 to 32 with the 1 June 2021 amendment. Judging the category and the application is the field of the administrative scrivener and the health centre, so confirm the specific category there.

**Q. Can I make my home kitchen a commissary?**
A. A commissary needs to be a licensed facility, and the dividing line is whether you can separate living quarters from the work area and set handwashing and water supply/drainage to standard. In many cases a dedicated area separated from the living space is required. Feasibility depends on the individual facility and health-centre practice, so it cannot be asserted.

**Q. I'd be in trouble if the licence isn't granted after I rent. What order should I follow?**
A. Before deciding on a property, it is safest to confirm that the intended business is permitted under zoning and that the layout can meet the facility standards, and to consult the health centre in advance before signing. We handle the property use check and the lease, and connect you to an administrative scrivener and the health centre for the category and consultation.
