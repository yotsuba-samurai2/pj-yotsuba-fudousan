## As which type is a certified child center, and where do the property standards double up?

Certified child centers come in four types—combined (yoho-renkei), kindergarten, nursery, and locally-determined—and only the combined type is a single facility holding both the legal character of a "school" and a "child-welfare facility." This is where the property standards double up.

A certified child center is a facility under the Act on Advancement of Comprehensive Provision of Education, Care, etc. of Preschool Children (Certified Child Center Act; Act No. 77 of 2006), providing education and care together and offering regional childcare support. The combined type is positioned in art. 2 (7) as "both a school and a child-welfare facility"—a single facility, unlike the other three types (where an existing kindergarten/nursery is certified by meeting standards).

| Type | Structure | How to read the property |
|---|---|---|
| Combined | A single facility that is both a school and a child-welfare facility (Certified Child Center Act art. 2 (7)) | Both school and nursery facility/use standards overlap |
| Kindergarten | A licensed kindergarten adds care and is certified | Kindergarten (school) standards are the base |
| Nursery | A licensed nursery adds education and is certified | Nursery (child-welfare facility) standards are the base |
| Locally-determined | An unlicensed facility, etc. is certified | By the prefecture's certification standard |

This article covers the combined type, where the overlap is largest. On the property side, you check whether you can satisfy, at the same time, the "school" side (buildable even in a low-rise residential zone, but with use and lighting constraints) and the "child-welfare facility" side (facility standards such as a cooking room and area). For how to read a licensed-nursery (20+ capacity) property see [How a licensed nursery (20+) property meets the use-zone and yard (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/ninka-hoikusho-20nin-bukken-youto-engei), and for small-scale childcare [How a small-scale childcare property is checked for use-zone and area (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/hoiku-shoukibo-bukken-youto-chiiki).

## Where in the use-zones can a combined type open?

By the amendment of Building Standards Act art. 48 and Appended Table 2, a combined-type certified child center can be built in every use-zone, from a Category I low-rise exclusive residential zone to an exclusive industrial zone. A property is, in principle, not ruled out by the use-zone itself.

Building Standards Act art. 48 provides that in a Category I low-rise exclusive residential zone, "no building other than those listed in Appended Table 2 item (i) may be built." A combined-type certified child center was once treated as subject to the stricter of the school (kindergarten) and nursery rules, so it could not be built in industrial or exclusive-industrial zones where a school cannot be built. The Building Standards Act was then amended to align the combined type with the nursery—buildable in every use-zone.

| Use-zone | Combined-type certified child center | Ref.: school (kindergarten) |
|---|---|---|
| Category I/II low-rise exclusive residential | Buildable | Buildable |
| Residential / commercial / quasi-industrial | Buildable | Buildable |
| Industrial / exclusive industrial | Buildable (post-amendment) | Not buildable |

But "buildable" and "suited to opening" are different. A place whose surroundings differ greatly from a residential environment, such as an exclusive-industrial zone, can be unsuitable in terms of the yard's sunlight, noise and safety, or the parent drop-off/pick-up flow. Use-zone applicability is confirmed from city-planning information (municipal web/counter); the final confirmation of buildability is the field of the designated administrative agency and the architect. We check a candidate's use-zone, frontage road and access, and help organise what to confirm with the architect.

## How much nursery-building and yard area, and what cooking facilities, are needed?

The facilities of a combined-type certified child center are set by the "Standard concerning class organisation, staff, facilities and operation of combined-type certified child centers" (Cabinet Office / MEXT / MHLW Ordinance No. 1 of 2014). The nursery-building and yard areas are set from the number of classes and children, and a cooking room is, in principle, mandatory.

Art. 7 (1) of the standard lists, as facilities for the nursery-building: a staff room, an infant room or crawling room (if under-2s are accepted), classrooms, a play room, a health room, a cooking room, toilets, and so on. The area framework is as follows (confirm the details and the latest with the ordinance in force and the ordinance of the prefecture/city where the property is).

| Item | Framework | Basis (ordinance) |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery-building area | Per class (1 class = 180 m²; 2+ = 320 + 100 × (classes − 2) m²) + a portion for under-3s | Art. 6 (6) |
| Yard (outdoor play area) area | The larger of ① per class (≤2 classes = 330 + 30 × (classes − 1) m²; 3+ = 400 + 80 × (classes − 3) m²) and ② 3.3 m² × number of children aged 3+; plus 3.3 m² × number aged 2 to under 3 | Art. 6 (7) |
| Classroom / play room | 1.98 m² × number of children aged 2+ | Art. 7 (6) |
| Infant room | 1.65 m² × number of under-2s who do not crawl | Art. 7 (6) |
| Crawling room | 3.3 m² × number of under-2s who crawl | Art. 7 (6) |

On in-house cooking, art. 7 (1) vi makes a cooking room mandatory in principle, with two exceptions: where meals for children aged 3+ are provided by external delivery (art. 7 (3), applying mutatis mutandis art. 32-2 of the Standard for child-welfare facilities), and where the number of children fed by in-house cooking is under 20 (art. 7 (4)); a cooking room may then be omitted, but facilities with the necessary cooking function (heating, storage) are still required. When looking at a property, "whether a cooking room with water supply/drainage and ventilation can be placed" and "whether outdoor space for a yard can be secured" matter alongside the area calculation. ## When converting an existing building, how do you check change of use and lighting?

When converting an existing office or shop into a certified child center, the right order is to confirm with an architect whether a change-of-use confirmation is required and whether the classroom/infant-room lighting and other room standards are met.

A certified child center (including kindergartens and nurseries) is a "special building" under the Building Standards Act. Under art. 87, if you change a building to a special-building use and the floor area of that use exceeds 200 m², a change-of-use confirmation is required (the 200 m² threshold was raised from 100 m² by the 2018 amendment). Rooms such as classrooms and infant rooms are subject to lighting and ventilation standards (art. 28, etc.), which an existing building's windows may fail to meet.

| What to check | Content | Whose field |
|---|---|---|
| Change-of-use confirmation | Confirmation if converting to a special-building use over 200 m² | Architect / designated administrative agency |
| Room lighting / ventilation | Window/opening standards for classrooms and infant rooms | Architect |
| Two-way evacuation / fire | Evacuation, interior and fire standards as a special building | Architect / fire department |
| Non-conforming / inspection certificate | Whether the current state is lawful and an inspection certificate exists | Architect |

These are judged by the architect (designer) and the designated administrative agency; we (a real-estate transaction business) do not act as agent. For a case that stalls on converting a building without an inspection certificate to a welfare use, see [Can a property without an inspection certificate be used for a welfare purpose (Japanese)](https://luck428.com/en/column/kensazumisho-nashi-bukken-fukushi-youto-henko). ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is there any use-zone where a combined-type certified child center cannot be built?**
A. In principle, no. By the amendment of Building Standards Act art. 48 and Appended Table 2, a combined type can be built in every use-zone, from a Category I low-rise exclusive residential zone to an exclusive industrial zone (the same as a nursery). But "buildable" and "suited to opening" differ—an exclusive-industrial zone, for example, can be unsuitable for sunlight, noise and the drop-off/pick-up flow. Use-zone applicability is confirmed from city-planning information; the final confirmation of buildability is the field of the designated administrative agency and the architect.

**Q. How large a yard (outdoor play area) is needed?**
A. Under art. 6 (7) of the combined-type facility standard (ordinance), the area must be at least the larger of ① a per-class area (≤2 classes = 330 + 30 × (classes − 1) m²; 3+ = 400 + 80 × (classes − 3) m²) and ② 3.3 m² × the number of children aged 3+, plus 3.3 m² × the number aged 2 to under 3. The treatment of rooftops and substitute sites has requirements; confirm with the prefecture/city ordinance and the latest text.

**Q. Must meals always be cooked in-house?**
A. The principle is in-house cooking with a cooking room (ordinance art. 7 (1) vi). But where meals for children aged 3+ are provided by external delivery (art. 7 (3)), or the number fed by in-house cooking is under 20 (art. 7 (4)), a cooking room may be omitted. In both cases, facilities with the necessary cooking function (heating, storage) are still required. On a property, you check whether a section for a cooking room with water supply/drainage and ventilation can be secured.

**Q. When converting an office into a certified child center, is a confirmation required?**
A. A certified child center is a special building under the Building Standards Act. If you convert an existing building to a special-building use and the floor area of that use exceeds 200 m², a change-of-use confirmation is required (200 m² was raised from 100 m² by the 2018 amendment). Classroom/infant-room lighting, ventilation and evacuation standards must also be met. Whether it is required and whether it conforms are the field of the architect and the designated administrative agency; we help confirm things such as whether an inspection certificate exists, and connect the judgment to the architect.
