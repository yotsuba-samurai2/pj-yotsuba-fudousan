**In short:** a rental apartment building can be built in timber up to three storeys. Tokyo subsidises newly built zero-emission homes at up to 2 million yen per unit for apartment buildings — around 20 million yen for a ten-unit building. This article is for landowners and investors considering land utilisation and rental management in Tokyo: the structural options, the Tokyo subsidy, and impressions from visiting a three-storey wooden rental building.

## Can a three-storey rental apartment building be built in timber?

Yes. A three-storey wooden apartment building (known in Japan as *moku-san-kyo*) can be built without being a fire-resistant building, provided it meets certain standards — principal structural parts of one-hour quasi-fire-resistant construction, a balcony usable for evacuation for each unit, and so on. In addition, the 2018 amendment to the Building Standards Act (Act No. 67 of 2018, in force 25 June 2019) widened the range of three-storey buildings under 200 square metres of total floor area that need not be fire-resistant buildings, and Japan's major housebuilders are productising mid-rise timber construction.

Which standard applies depends on the site's fire-prevention zoning (fire zone or quasi-fire zone) and the building's size. The reliable approach is to **confirm with a designer at the land-selection stage**.

## How does timber compare with reinforced concrete?

| Item | Timber | Reinforced concrete (RC) |
|---|---|---|
| Construction cost | Comparatively easier to contain | Comparatively higher |
| Construction period | Easier to shorten | Tends to run longer |
| Statutory useful life (residential) | 22 years | 47 years |
| Sound insulation | Structure alone tends to be weaker; compensated by floor and wall specifications | Generally considered stronger |

A shorter statutory useful life (National Tax Agency useful-life table) means larger annual depreciation charges. How that plays into loan terms and tax treatment depends on the property and the financing, so we point you to a licensed tax accountant (*zeirishi*) for the specifics, under a direct engagement.

## How much can be claimed under the Tokyo subsidy?

Under the Tokyo Zero-Emission House promotion programme for fiscal 2026 (Reiwa 8), newly built homes certified to the insulation and energy-saving standards set by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government receive a construction subsidy (Cool Net Tokyo website, viewed 15 August 2026; applications open).

| Grade | Apartment buildings | Detached houses |
|---|---|---|
| Grade A | ¥2,000,000 per unit | ¥2,400,000 per unit |
| Grade B | ¥1,300,000 per unit | ¥1,600,000 per unit |
| Grade C | ¥300,000 per unit | ¥400,000 per unit |

The main conditions and framework are as follows.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Eligible | Newly built homes in Tokyo (detached and apartment buildings), with total floor area under 2,000 m² |
| Condition for apartment buildings | Every unit must reach Grade C or higher; each unit is certified and subsidised at its own grade |
| Applicant | The building owner commissioning the construction (individual or business) |
| Application period (FY2026) | 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027; electronic filing as a rule |
| Precondition | Design confirmation and certification by a certification body registered with the Tokyo Metropolitan Government |

There are further subsidies for installing solar power equipment (¥120,000–130,000 per kW up to 3.6 kW) and storage batteries (¥100,000 per kWh, capped at ¥1,200,000 per unit), and a reduction of real estate acquisition tax — up to the full amount — where certain conditions are met. Note that for storage batteries, where the design-confirmation review is applied for on or after 1 October 2026, only battery systems on SII's registered product list for FY2026 qualify (projects that completed the design-confirmation application by 30 September 2026 are excluded from the change).

Budgets and conditions change by fiscal year and over time; nothing here promises any amount or approval. Always check the latest conditions on the official pages.

## What was the actual three-storey wooden rental building like?

In August 2026 we visited a three-storey, ten-unit wooden rental building in Kamata, Ota-ku, built by Misawa Homes.

![The three-storey, ten-unit wooden rental building we visited (Kamata, Ota-ku; photographed August 2026)](https://luck428.com/assets/images/column-mokuzo-chintai-kengaku-202608.jpg)

The honest impression: rental housing built to the standard of a custom detached home. The walls are thick, and you can see on a visit that materials have been used properly inside the frame — in places tenants never see. It overturned the assumption that rental housing is always built down to a cost.

According to the explanation given on the visit, the building received a subsidy of roughly 20 million yen. Grade A (¥2,000,000 per unit) times ten units matches that figure. In other words, even in timber, raising the insulation and energy-saving grade and obtaining certification can, depending on scale, bring subsidies in the tens of millions of yen.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How many storeys can a wooden rental apartment building have?**
A. Up to three storeys can be built without being a fire-resistant building where the *moku-san-kyo* standards are met — one-hour quasi-fire-resistant principal structure, evacuation-usable balconies and so on. Above that, or inside a fire zone, the required performance changes. The answer turns on the site's fire-prevention zoning and the building's size, so confirm with a designer and the local building authority at the land-selection stage.

**Q. Can I apply for the Tokyo Zero-Emission House subsidy if my purpose is rental management?**
A. The applicant is the building owner commissioning the new home (individual or business), and apartment buildings are within scope. Conditions include every unit reaching Grade C or higher and total floor area under 2,000 m². Check eligibility for your project and the latest conditions on the Cool Net Tokyo official pages and the programme guidelines.

**Q. Isn't a 22-year statutory useful life a disadvantage for timber?**
A. Not necessarily. A shorter useful life means larger annual depreciation charges, and it also bears on loan terms and the timing of an eventual sale. It can work either way, so take it to a licensed tax accountant with your specific project and financing, under a direct engagement.
