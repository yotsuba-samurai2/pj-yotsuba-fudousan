## What kind of property lets you open a dental clinic?

Start with zoning. The conclusion is that a dental clinic, as a bedless clinic, can be built in every zone under Building Standards Act Appended Table 2. A hospital is restricted in the Category-1/Category-2 low-rise exclusive residential zones, the agricultural-residential zone, the exclusive industrial zone and so on, but a clinic faces no such restriction. In other words, a property where "zoning forbids a dental clinic" is, in principle, almost non-existent.

What trips you up is the building-side conditions. The four to look at before signing:

| Check item | Why it matters | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray room shielding | A room for dental/panoramic/CT units needs 画壁 shielding (Enforcement Regulation art. 30-4) | Any existing shielding, dose measurement outside the 画壁, the apparatus you will install |
| Change-of-use confirmation | Bedless = not a special building, so in principle unnecessary; beds / large scale may require it | Presence of beds, floor area of the changed part; inquire with the building authority |
| Water / electrical capacity | Units, suction, sterilisers and CT use a lot of water and power | Diameter/position of existing piping, contract power, capacity of the distribution board |
| Barrier-free | A property at or above a set scale, or under a barrier-free ordinance, must meet the standard | Floor area; the barrier-free ordinance's threshold in the municipality |

Of these, the one specific to dentistry and costly to overlook is the X-ray room shielding. We take it in turn.

## What standards govern the X-ray room's protective structure?

A dental clinic almost always installs a dental X-ray, a panoramic unit, or a dental CT. A room where such diagnostic X-ray apparatus is used in a fixed position (an X-ray room) must meet the Enforcement Regulation's structural standards.

At the centre of the shielding is Enforcement Regulation art. 30-4(1). The X-ray room's 画壁 (walls, floor, ceiling, doors) must be shielded so that the effective dose outside them stays at or below the regulatory standard, and the measurement is taken outside the 画壁 under normal use. In other words, the test is not "how much lead or concrete you wrapped around it" but "is the dose measured outside the 画壁 at or below the standard." The specific numeric standard and measurement details are fixed in the regulation text and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's notices, so decide the apparatus (dental or CT) and the room size, then apply the latest provision and confirm with the competent public health centre.

| Point to confirm | Content | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 画壁 shielding | Do walls/floor/ceiling/doors hold the outside effective dose at or below the standard (art. 30-4(1))? | Architect / shielding-design contractor |
| Dose measurement | Measure the dose outside the 画壁 and attach the result to the installation notification | Measurement contractor |
| Difference by apparatus | A dental CT is higher-output than a dental unit and tends to need thicker shielding | Apparatus maker / architect |

At the property stage, what we can back up is the objective condition—the size of the area usable as an X-ray room, its position relative to adjoining rooms, floors above/below and the exterior, the structure of existing walls. Fitting that into the shielding design/calculation belongs to the architect and the shielding-design contractor; if the work needs a building confirmation, a designated confirmation body reviews it.

## When, and to whom, are the opening notification and the X-ray installation notification submitted?

The procedure splits at the entrance by who opens the clinic and whether there are beds.

| Scene | Basis | Procedure | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| A dentist opens a bedless clinic themselves | Medical Care Act art. 8 | Clinic opening notification (prefectural governor = in practice the public health centre) | Within 10 days of opening |
| A medical corporation opens / there are beds | Medical Care Act art. 7 | Clinic opening permit (permit in advance) | Obtain the permit before opening |
| An X-ray apparatus is installed | Medical Care Act art. 15(3); Enforcement Regulation art. 24-2 | X-ray installation notification (prefectural governor = in practice the public health centre) | Within 10 days of installation |

What to grasp here is that the most common case—a dentist opening their own bedless clinic—is satisfied by a notification, whereas making a medical corporation the opener or providing beds requires a permit in advance. Because a permit is before opening and a notification is within 10 days after, settling the form of opening before you secure a property reduces rework.

Separately from the clinic notification/permit, once an X-ray apparatus is installed, an installation notification is required within 10 days. A mobile or portable apparatus is notified the same way. ## What should you check when using a former dental fit-out property?

A former-dental fit-out property, with units, piping and an X-ray room still in place, looks attractive. But "the previous dentist could use it" does not mean "your opening can use it." Points to check:

- **The previous opener's notifications do not carry over.** Both the clinic opening notification and the X-ray installation notification are per-opener, so you re-file.
- **Does the old X-ray room's shielding fit the current standard and your apparatus?** Shielding for a dental unit may fall short for a panoramic or dental CT. Re-measure the dose outside the 画壁 and re-evaluate.
- **Position and diameter of water/unit piping.** Changing the layout may require re-running the piping.
- **Electrical capacity.** Check whether the contract power suffices for added CT or sterilisers, and the capacity of the distribution board.

Of this, the objective condition (area, piping, electrical capacity, existing equipment) is ours to confirm; whether the shielding meets the standard, and any refurbishment design, belongs to the architect and the shielding-design contractor. ## What to watch for on water, electrical capacity and unit expansion?

A dental clinic depends on infrastructure more than its visible layout. Each unit gathers water, drainage, suction, air and power, and adding a CT and sterilisers raises the electrical and water load further.

| Equipment | What to check on the property |
|---|---|
| Water / drainage | Diameter/position of existing piping, feasibility of re-running when adding units, drainage slope |
| Electrical capacity | Contract power, spare circuits on the board, whether CT/steriliser/compressor can run simultaneously |
| Air-conditioning / ventilation | Ventilation for areas handling dental technical work or chemicals; compressor noise/location |
| Barrier-free | Entrance steps/corridor width; whether it falls under the municipality's barrier-free ordinance threshold |

On barrier-free: under the national law (the Act on Promotion of Smooth Movement of Elderly Persons, Disabled Persons, etc.), new construction at or above a set floor area must meet the standard, and many dental clinics fall below that, while a municipal ordinance may lower the threshold. Confirming the municipality's ordinance at the property-selection stage saves panic at the interior-design stage.

A. In principle, almost none. A dental clinic, as a bedless clinic, can be built in every zone under Building Standards Act Appended Table 2. What trips you up is not zoning but building-side conditions: the X-ray room shielding, whether a change-of-use confirmation is needed (beds vs. no beds), water/electrical capacity, and barrier-free rules.
