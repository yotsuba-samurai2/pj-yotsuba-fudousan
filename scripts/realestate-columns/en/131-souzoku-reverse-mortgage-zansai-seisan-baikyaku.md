When you inherit a home where a parent used a reverse mortgage, you worry about the remaining debt, whether you must sell to repay it, and whether to disclaim the inheritance. A reverse mortgage is a housing loan where, during the borrower's life, only interest is paid, and the principal is repaid in a lump sum when the borrower dies. The timing of repayment and the structure of settlement differ from an ordinary housing loan.

The Japan Housing Finance Agency's "Re-Bars 60" (リ・バース60) is a product for people aged 60 and over, offered by private financial institutions with the agency's housing loan insurance attached. The principal is repaid, when the borrower dies, by the heirs in a lump sum or from the sale of the collateral property (the home and land). The starting point is to confirm whether the property you inherited fits this structure.

## How is a reverse-mortgage balance treated on inheritance?

Article 896 of the Civil Code provides that an heir succeeds, from the time inheritance commences, to all rights and duties attached to the decedent's property (except those exclusively personal). Accordingly, the reverse-mortgage balance (the duty to repay the loan) is, in principle, succeeded to by the heirs. At the moment of inheritance, you take on the collateral property together with the repayment duty.

Re-Bars 60 is a product in which the principal is repaid in a lump sum at death. Repayment is either a lump-sum repayment by the heirs or repayment from the sale proceeds of the collateral property. Whether to settle by sale or to repay with your own funds and keep the property is for the heirs to choose.

| To confirm | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Balance | The principal balance and the interest arising until settlement |
| Deadline | By when, after death, lump-sum repayment or sale will be required (depends on the institution/contract) |
| Type of liability | Whether it is non-recourse or recourse |
| Joint liability | Whether a spouse is a co-obligor; the lump-sum repayment time may be when both have died |

First contact the handling financial institution and confirm the balance, deadline and contract type in writing. ## How does the burden on heirs differ between the non-recourse and recourse types?

Re-Bars 60 has two ways of handling a balance that cannot be fully repaid from the sale proceeds of the collateral property. This is where it differs greatly from an ordinary housing loan.

| Type | Treatment of a balance not fully repaid from the sale proceeds |
| --- | --- |
| Non-recourse | The heirs are not required to repay the remaining debt |
| Recourse | The heirs bear the duty to repay the remaining debt |

With the non-recourse type, after applying the sale proceeds of the collateral property to repayment, any shortfall is not claimed from the heirs. With the recourse type, the heirs must repay any shortfall after sale. Which type your inherited contract is determines whether a burden remains after the sale.

Which type it is was fixed in the contract; heirs cannot re-choose later. Evaluating whether it is non-recourse or recourse, and the legal matters in negotiating with the financial institution, are the lawyer's field. ## What is the flow and the deadline for selling to settle the balance?

When selling to settle the balance, "lump-sum repayment of the balance" and "release of the mortgage" are added to an ordinary sale. Because the financial institution's mortgage is registered on the collateral property, the basic approach is to repay the balance from the sale price at settlement and release the mortgage at the same time.

## Disclaiming the inheritance or settling by sale—which to choose, and whom to consult? (Separate mandates)

We often hear, "If there is a balance, should I disclaim the inheritance?" The choice changes depending on whether it is non-recourse or recourse, whether there are other debts, and whether you want to keep the property.

Article 915(1) of the Civil Code provides that acceptance or disclaimer of inheritance must be made within three months from when you learn that inheritance has commenced for you (the period may be extended in some cases). If you disclaim, under article 939 you are treated as never having been an heir, and can receive none of the inherited property, including the collateral property.

| Option | When it tends to fit (general) |
| --- | --- |
| Settle by sale | Non-recourse type, where you are willing to give up the property; premised on no balance remaining after sale |
| Repay and keep | Where you can repay in a lump sum with your own funds and wish to keep the home |
| Disclaim | Recourse type with a large balance, or large other debts; but you also lose the other property |

With the non-recourse type and no other debts, selling to settle leaves, in principle, no balance, so you may not need to disclaim. With the recourse type or other debts, consider disclaiming too. The deliberation period is a short three months, so early confirmation is needed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I inherit a reverse-mortgage balance, must I always pay it myself?**
A. Under Civil Code article 896 the repayment duty is, in principle, succeeded to by the heirs. Re-Bars 60 is a product in which the principal is repaid in a lump sum at death, and you may choose a lump-sum repayment by the heirs or repayment from the sale proceeds of the collateral property. With the non-recourse type, a shortfall after applying the sale proceeds is not claimed.

**Q. Can I re-choose between non-recourse and recourse after inheriting?**
A. No. The type was fixed in the contract. Non-recourse leaves no balance after sale; recourse requires the heirs to repay the shortfall. Confirm with the institution which type your inherited contract is. Legal evaluation of the type is the lawyer's field.

**Q. Which is better, disclaiming or selling?**
A. With the non-recourse type and no other debts, selling to settle leaves no balance, so you may not need to disclaim. With the recourse type or other debts, consider disclaiming too. Disclaim within the three-month deliberation period; you also lose the other inherited property. Confirm feasibility with a lawyer.

**Q. How do settlement and mortgage release proceed on a sale?**
A. Because the financial institution's mortgage is registered on the collateral property, the basic approach is to repay the balance in a lump sum from the sale price at settlement and register the mortgage release at the same time. The release and succession registration are handled by a judicial scrivener. We organise the appraisal, brokerage and the settlement structured on the premise of the balance.
