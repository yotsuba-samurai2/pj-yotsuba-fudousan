**In short:** foreigners and foreign companies can acquire land in Japan without nationality-based restriction. But when you carry out a transaction of land above a certain area, the "post-transaction notification" under Art. 23 of the National Land Use Planning Act is required. The person obliged to notify is the side that acquired the right (the buyer), who must notify the prefectural governor — via the mayor of the municipality where the land is located — of the intended land use, the consideration and other matters, within two weeks counting from the day the contract is concluded. ## Can foreigners or foreign companies freely buy land in Japan?

**In Japan there is no law that generally prohibits or restricts the acquisition of land on the ground that one is a foreigner or a foreign company.** Regardless of nationality or place of residence, a person can acquire ownership and other rights. This is a basic premise of the Japanese system.

But "not restricted by nationality" and "no procedure needed" are two different things. Even where the buyer is a non-resident of Japan, certain land transactions trigger the post-transaction notification under the National Land Use Planning Act described below, and in specific areas such as around defense facilities, prior notification under the Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities may apply. These are not restrictions imposed only on foreigners; they are imposed by the area of the transaction and the location of the land, regardless of the parties' nationality.

So when an overseas investor buys land in Japan, the first thing to confirm is not "can I buy" but "which notification applies." How large is the area, in what area is the land located, what right is being acquired — these determine the notification required.

## For what transactions does the post-transaction notification under the National Land Use Planning Act become required (area thresholds)?

**Art. 23 of the National Land Use Planning Act requires the acquirer to file a post-transaction notification when they acquire a right to land through a "land sale or similar contract" above a certain area.** It covers contracts that transfer or create, for consideration, ownership, superficies, lease rights and the like — sales, and also exchanges and certain right-creations. An acquisition without consideration, such as inheritance or gift, is in principle outside this notification.

Whether a notification is required is determined by whether the area of the land acquired reaches the following thresholds.

| Area of the land | Area at which notification becomes required (at or above) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Urbanization promotion area | 2,000 sqm or more | National Land Use Planning Act Art. 23(2)(i) |
| City planning area other than an urbanization promotion area (urbanization control area / non-demarcated city planning area) | 5,000 sqm or more | Same item |
| Outside city planning areas (other areas) | 10,000 sqm (1 hectare) or more | Same item |

**The area is judged on a "block of land" basis.** Even if a single contract is below the threshold, where the acquirer successively acquires adjoining land for the same intended use and the whole reaches the threshold area (so-called "add-on buying"), each contract may fall within the notification. The idea is that splitting into small contracts does not avoid the notification if the whole exceeds the threshold. Confirm whether your transaction falls within the thresholds from the area in which the land is located and the aggregate area.

## Who files it, by when, and to whom?

The "who, when, to whom, and what" of the post-transaction notification is fixed by the articles as follows.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Person obliged to notify | The person who acquired a right to the land (the buyer / the acquirer) | Art. 23(1) |
| Deadline | Within two weeks counting from the day the contract is concluded | Art. 23(1) |
| Recipient | The prefectural governor, via the mayor of the municipality where the land is located (the city mayor for a government-designated city) | Art. 23(1) |
| Matters to notify | Parties' names and addresses, contract-conclusion date, location and area of the land, the type and content of the right, the intended use of the land, and the consideration | Each item of Art. 23(1) |

**The key point is that only the buyer (the acquirer) notifies; the seller has no notification obligation.** This is in contrast to the prior notification under the Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities described below, which requires notification from both seller and buyer. Also, because the post-transaction notification is filed "after" the contract, failing to file does not make the contract void. Having received a notification, the prefectural governor may — where they find the intended use of the land is not appropriate in light of the land use master plan and the like — recommend a change of the intended use after hearing the opinion of the land use examination board (Art. 24). The recommendation is in principle made within three weeks of the notification date.

For an overseas buyer, two weeks feels short. Before contracting, confirm whether the land to be acquired is subject to notification, and if so decide who will prepare the notification documents (the buyer, or a domestic agent or professional) — that makes filing within the deadline easier.

## How does this differ from the Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities or a Foreign Exchange Act report?

When an overseas investor buys land in Japan, similarly named but separate systems are easily confused, so distinguish them by governing law, purpose and recipient.

| System | Governing law / article | Prior or post | Recipient | Person obliged | Main area / target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-transaction notification | National Land Use Planning Act Art. 23 | After contract (within two weeks) | Prefectural governor (via municipal mayor) | Buyer (acquirer) | 2,000 sqm or more in an urbanization promotion area, etc. |
| Important-land transaction notification | Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities Art. 13 | Before contract (in advance) | Prime Minister | Both seller and buyer | Land etc. of 200 sqm or more in a special watch area |
| Foreign Exchange Act report | Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act | Depends on the transaction | Minister of Finance (via the Bank of Japan), etc. | The party (resident / non-resident) | Whether an inward direct investment report etc. is required depends on the transaction |

**The post-transaction notification under the National Land Use Planning Act aims to curb speculative land transactions and secure appropriate, rational land use, and is imposed by area threshold.** By contrast, the [Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities](https://luck428.com/column/juyo-tochi-chosa-ho-chushi-tokubetsu-chushi-torihiki-todokede) is a national-security system to prevent land use that impairs the function of "important facilities" such as defense-related facilities and remote border islands; only in designated watch areas and special watch areas, and in advance, both seller and buyer notify the Prime Minister. Which areas are designated can be checked on the Cabinet Office's "Important Land Web Map" (accessed 8 November 2026).

The inward-direct-investment report under the Foreign Exchange Act (the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act) is a separate framework focused on the movement of funds and the nature of the investment, administered by the Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Japan. **Whether a report is required on a real estate acquisition varies with the buyer's residence, the purpose of the transaction and the form of the investment — it is not uniform.** Confirm the need and the reporting procedure with the handling bank and a tax/foreign-exchange professional. The practicalities of remittance and settlement are set out at [capital controls and remittance/settlement](https://luck428.com/column/chuka-shihon-kisei-soukin-nihon-fudosan-kessai). Because these three differ in purpose, governing law and recipient, more than one may apply to a single transaction, or none may apply.

## What happens if you fail to notify?

Failing to file the post-transaction notification, or filing a false one, carries a penalty.

| Violation | Penalty | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Failing to file the post-transaction notification under Art. 23(1) | Imprisonment of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 1 million yen | National Land Use Planning Act Art. 47 |
| Making a false post-transaction notification | Imprisonment of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 1 million yen | Same article |

**Failing to file the post-transaction notification does not make the contract itself void, but a breach of the notification obligation carries the above penalty.** Because notification is the buyer's (acquirer's) obligation, even an overseas resident must file within the deadline for a subject transaction. Bearing in mind the two-week deadline, confirm before contracting whether the land to be acquired is subject to notification, and if so decide who prepares and files the documents.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can foreigners or foreign companies buy land in Japan? Is a notification always required?**
A. In Japan there is no law that generally restricts land acquisition on the ground of being a foreigner or a foreign company, and acquisition is possible regardless of nationality. A notification is not always required: the post-transaction notification under Art. 23 of the National Land Use Planning Act applies to a land transaction above a certain area (2,000 sqm or more in an urbanization promotion area, 5,000 sqm or more in a city planning area other than that, 10,000 sqm or more outside city planning areas). Below the threshold, the post-transaction notification is not required. However, land etc. of 200 sqm or more in a special watch area, such as around defense facilities, may require prior notification under the separate Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities.

**Q. Who files the post-transaction notification, and by when?**
A. The one who notifies is the side that acquired a right to the land (the buyer / the acquirer). The seller has no notification obligation. The deadline is within two weeks counting from the day the contract is concluded, and it is filed with the prefectural governor via the mayor of the municipality where the land is located. The matters notified are the parties' names and addresses, the contract-conclusion date, the location and area of the land, the type and content of the right, the intended use of the land, and the consideration.

**Q. How does the National Land Use Planning Act notification differ from the Act on the Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities notification?**
A. They differ in purpose, governing law and recipient. The post-transaction notification (Art. 23) aims to curb speculative transactions and secure appropriate land use; for a transaction meeting the area threshold, the buyer files with the prefectural governor within two weeks after the contract. The important-land transaction notification (Art. 13) is for national security around defense facilities and remote border islands; for land etc. of 200 sqm or more in a special watch area, both seller and buyer file with the Prime Minister in advance, before the contract. Both may apply to one transaction, or neither.

**Q. What happens if I forget to notify? Does the contract become void?**
A. Failing to file the post-transaction notification does not make the contract itself void. However, Art. 47 of the National Land Use Planning Act provides imprisonment of up to 6 months or a fine of up to 1 million yen for a person who fails to file the Art. 23(1) notification or makes a false notification. Because notification is the buyer's obligation, you must file within the deadline for a subject transaction. Confirm whether a notification is required, and who handles it, with the transaction specialist before contracting.
