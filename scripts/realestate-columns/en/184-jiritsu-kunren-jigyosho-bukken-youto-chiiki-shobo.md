**In short:** for a self-reliance-training (jiritsu-kunren: life training / functional training) office, three things matter first — (1) the use zone (the office is treated under the Building Standards Act as a "child-welfare facility etc.," buildable outside the industrial-exclusive zone); (2) whether a training/work room, consultation room, washroom, toilet and multipurpose room can be secured (Art. 168 and Art. 158→81 of the standard on personnel, equipment and operation of designated disability services, MHW Ordinance No. 171 of 2006); and (3) the fire classification under Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order (self-reliance training is, in principle, item (6)-ha) and whether an automatic fire-alarm is required. The training room has no numeric area standard such as "3 sqm × capacity" — the Ordinance says only "a size that does not impede training." Confirm the use zone, equipment, fire, change-of-use and the lessor's stance before you sign.

You lease a place, and only then find the consultation room cannot be partitioned, or added fire equipment delays the opening — the same rework that hits restaurants and clinics hits self-reliance-training offices. This article is for operators (companies or sole proprietors) looking for rented premises to open a self-reliance-training office (the "training-type benefit" under the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities), and for the owners who lease to them: what you can confirm before signing, set out in order from the standard on personnel, equipment and operation of designated disability services (MHW Ordinance No. 171 of 2006) and the Building Standards Act and Fire Service Act. We do not go into full-time-equivalent staffing (labour), designation-application documents, or fee calculation. Final confirmation that the premises can meet the standards rests with the designating authority (prefecture/municipality), the specified administrative authority and the fire department.

## How does a self-reliance-training office differ from other disability services?

First, self-reliance training has two types — **functional training** (training to maintain/recover physical function) and **life training** (training to maintain/improve living ability) — both being "training-type benefits" under Article 5(12) of the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities. They are siblings of employment-transition support, employment-continuation support A/B, daily-life care, after-school day service and child-development support, but **the equipment and personnel standards are set type by type in different articles**, and the points that bear on premises differ too.

From the property side, three features stand out.

| Aspect | How it works for self-reliance training |
|---|---|
| Use zone | Treated under the Building Standards Act as a "child-welfare facility etc."; in principle possible outside the industrial-exclusive zone |
| Training-room area | **No numeric area standard.** The Ordinance says only "a size that does not impede training" (no "3 sqm × capacity" rule like adult day care) |
| Fire classification | In principle item (6)-ha of Appended Table 1; a facility mainly admitting hard-to-evacuate users is (6)-ro |

**So reasoning "work the area back from capacity," as with adult day care or a group home, misreads it.** The training/work room has no numeric standard; instead you must design a size adequate for the training content yourself. Adult day care in general works the area back from capacity ("3 sqm × capacity"), but self-reliance training does not. Note too that life training comes in a day-attendance type and a residential type (designated residential self-reliance training); the residential type additionally needs living rooms and a bath, so the equipment logic changes. This article is mainly about the day-attendance type. ## What are the minimum area and equipment standards for the training room, consultation room, toilet and so on?

The core of the equipment standard is **Article 81 (applied by Article 158)** for functional training, and **Article 168** for life training (both MHW Ordinance No. 171 of 2006; **accessed 19 November 2026**). Both require the office to provide the following.

| Equipment | Standing under the standard | The makings to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Training/work room | Required; **a size that does not impede training** or work, with the necessary machinery/tools | No numeric standard; a size adequate for the training content and room for tools |
| Consultation room | Required; **partitioning etc. to prevent conversation being overheard** | Can it be a private room or partitioned |
| Washroom | Required; suited to users' characteristics | A washroom that can suit wheelchairs/characteristics |
| Toilet | Required; suited to users' characteristics | A toilet that can be made barrier-free |
| Multipurpose room | Required; other equipment needed for operation | May be shared with the consultation room (if no impediment) |

**Note that the training/work room has no numeric standard such as "○ sqm × capacity."** Adult day care caps its dining/function-training room at "3 sqm × user capacity or more," but self-reliance training says only "a size that does not impede training." So you **work the needed size back from capacity and the training programme yourself**, and it is safest to confirm the effective floor area (measured net of columns and wet areas) at the viewing.

Another lever is **sharing the consultation room and multipurpose room.** Article 168(4) (for functional training, the applied Article 81(3)) allows them to be shared where this does not impede support. Saving space with a single floor is possible, but the consultation room presupposes partitioning that prevents conversation being overheard.

If you run the **residential type (designated residential self-reliance training)** under life training, Article 168(3) additionally requires **living rooms (one person each, at least 7.43 sqm excluding storage) and a bath.** The 7.43 sqm living room is one of the few numeric standards in this field, so if you consider the residential type, check the layout early. Design of zoning, plumbing and barrier-free, and the judgment of whether a change-of-use application is required, are the work of an architect/designer. **We (the real estate agent) assist with the groundwork of whether the property can meet these conditions, and with negotiating terms with the lessor.** We do not do the equipment design or structural-conformity judgment itself.

## Which use zones allow it, and is a change-of-use confirmation required?

A self-reliance-training office is, under the Building Standards Act, treated not as a "clinic" or a "shop" but as a **"child-welfare facility etc."** Article 19(1) of the Enforcement Order of the Building Standards Act expressly lists, within "child-welfare facilities etc.," "a facility used for a disability welfare service business (limited to daily-life care, self-reliance training, employment-transition support or employment-continuation support)," so a self-reliance-training office falls here. The use-zone restriction follows the treatment of child-welfare facilities etc. in Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (**accessed 19 November 2026**).

| Use zone | Feasibility for a self-reliance-training office (as a child-welfare facility etc.) |
|---|---|
| Industrial-exclusive zone | Not buildable |
| Category-1/2 low-rise exclusive residential zone | In principle possible (area limits apply by scale) |
| Other residential, commercial and industrial zones | In principle possible |

**So the use zone itself really only blocks you in the industrial-exclusive zone; even a low-rise residential neighbourhood can work.** But the classification can vary with the scale, form and the specified administrative authority's operation. **Confirm feasibility at the specified administrative authority (building guidance section).**

A **change-of-use confirmation application** also matters. Converting a former shop or office to a self-reliance-training office (a child-welfare facility etc.) requires an application once that use area exceeds 200 sqm (Building Standards Act Arts. 6 and 87). **Whether it applies is an architect's judgment,** worth scoping before you sign. For a property in an urbanisation-control area, the question of development/building permission under the City Planning Act is layered on, so confirm the area classification first.

## How do you check the fire classification and the automatic fire alarm / sprinklers?

Since users attend daily, **fire equipment is a gate** in the property choice. First, the classification. Item (6)-ha(5) of Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order expressly lists a **facility carrying out self-reliance training** under Article 5(12) of the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities, so a day-attendance self-reliance-training office is, in principle, classified as **item (6)-ha** (**accessed 19 November 2026**). A disability-support facility that mainly admits hard-to-evacuate persons, or a short-stay / communal-living-assistance facility for such persons, is item (6)-ro.

Once the class is fixed, whether an automatic fire alarm is required can be read from the text (Art. 21(1)).

| Form of office | Fire class | Automatic fire alarm |
|---|---|---|
| Day-attendance (no residence/overnight) | (6)-ha | Required at **a total floor area of 300 sqm or more** (Art. 21(1)(iii)-ro) |
| Residential (users reside / stay overnight) | (6)-ha | Required **regardless of area** (Art. 21(1)(i)-ro) |
| Facility mainly admitting hard-to-evacuate persons | (6)-ro | Required regardless of area |

**Whether sprinklers, equipment reporting to the fire authority, and guide lights are required turns on the class (ro or ha), the total floor area and the occupant load,** and the text alone does not settle any individual property. Which equipment, and how far, is judged and installed by the fire department (prevention section) and a fire-equipment contractor. Confirming the current fire equipment and the likely additions before you sign avoids surprise work after opening.

## What should you clear at the property before signing?

As a pre-signing checklist:

| Confirm | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Use zone | Not the industrial-exclusive zone; for low-rise residential, check scale limits with the specified authority |
| Change of use | Confirmation application needed once the use area exceeds 200 sqm (consult an architect) |
| Training/work room | A size adequate for the training content and room for tools; confirm the effective area by measurement |
| Consultation room | Can it be partitioned/private to prevent conversation being overheard |
| Washroom / toilet | Can they be made barrier-free to suit users' characteristics |
| Multipurpose room | Share with the consultation room, or secure separately |
| Residential type | Can living rooms (one person, ≥ 7.43 sqm) and a bath be secured |
| Fire | Is it (6)-ha; an automatic fire alarm from 300 sqm; confirm likely additions with the fire department |
| Lessor's stance | Leasing to a welfare business, fit-out/equipment work, scope of restoration |

Full-time-equivalent staffing (for functional training, the combined number of nurses, physical/occupational/speech therapists and life-support workers at the office must be the user count ÷ 6 or more, Art. 156; for life training, life-support workers at day-attendance ÷ 6 plus residential ÷ 10 or more, Art. 166) belongs to the labour design, not the property. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do you work the training-room area back from capacity?**
A. No. Adult day care caps its dining/function-training room at "3 sqm × user capacity or more," but the self-reliance-training training/work room has no numeric area standard. The designated standard (MHW Ordinance No. 171 of 2006) says only "a size that does not impede training." So you work the needed size back from capacity and the training programme yourself, and it is safest to confirm the effective floor area (net of columns and wet areas) at the viewing. One of the few numeric standards is that a residential-type living room must be at least 7.43 sqm per person (excluding storage).

**Q. Can a self-reliance-training office open in a residential area?**
A. There is room to. A self-reliance-training office falls within "child-welfare facilities etc." under Article 19(1) of the Enforcement Order of the Building Standards Act (a facility used for a disability welfare service business carrying out self-reliance training), and the use-zone restriction follows that treatment. It is buildable outside the industrial-exclusive zone and possible even in Category-1/2 low-rise exclusive residential zones with scale limits. Because the classification can vary with scale, form and the specified authority's operation, confirm feasibility at the specified administrative authority (building guidance section). Converting an existing building requires a confirmation application once the use area exceeds 200 sqm.

**Q. How is self-reliance training classified under the Fire Service Act?**
A. Item (6)-ha(5) of Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order expressly lists a "facility carrying out self-reliance training" under Article 5(12) of the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities, so a day-attendance office is, in principle, item (6)-ha. An automatic fire alarm is required from a total floor area of 300 sqm for the day-attendance type, and regardless of area for the residential type (users residing/staying overnight) (Art. 21(1)). Whether sprinklers and guide lights are required varies by class and scale, so confirm with the fire department (prevention section). A facility mainly admitting hard-to-evacuate persons is item (6)-ro.

**Q. Can the consultation room and multipurpose room be shared?**
A. Yes. The designated standard allows the consultation room and multipurpose room to be shared where this does not impede support (Article 168 for life training; the applied Article 81 for functional training). But the consultation room presupposes partitioning etc. that prevents conversation being overheard, and the washroom and toilet must suit users' characteristics. Saving space with a single floor is possible, but confirm confidentiality of consultations and barrier-free access at the property-selection stage.
