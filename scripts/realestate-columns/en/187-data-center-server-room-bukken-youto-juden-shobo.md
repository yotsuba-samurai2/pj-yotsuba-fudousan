## In which zoning districts can a data centre or server room be placed?

Art. 8 of the City Planning Act sets the kinds of zoning district, and Art. 48 and Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950) restrict building uses by district. Appended Table 2 has no express "data centre" or "server room" category, so feasibility turns on whether the building's actual use is evaluated as an "office," "factory," "warehouse" or the like — judged on actual use rather than the name.

In practice, using one room of an existing building as a server room is often treated as part of an office, while a building wholly devoted to large-scale IT equipment, with generators and transformers, may be evaluated as closer to a "factory." Offices can generally be built except in exclusive industrial districts, but for a facility with large-scale equipment, a prior enquiry to the specified administrative agency is the sure route. We do not assert this (unverified) and leave it to the specified administrative agency and an architect.

| What to check | Basis / approach | Whom to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Use category in Appended Table 2 | No express "data centre"; evaluated on actual use (unverified) | Specified administrative agency |
| Part of an office or a stand-alone facility | Feasibility differs by office/factory treatment | Specified administrative agency / architect |
| Whether it is an urbanization-control area | If so, City Planning Act restrictions apply separately | Specified administrative agency |

## What figures for floor load and power supply (extra-high voltage) should you check on the property?

Server racks carry a large load per unit area, so the design live load is central to choosing a property. Art. 85 of the Enforcement Order of the Building Standards Act sets live loads by use, but a server room's actual design load often far exceeds the figure for ordinary office use, and whether the structure can bear it is calculated case by case by an architect. What a real estate company can check is property documents — the live load noted in the design drawings and any history of added floors or reinforcement.

Power supply is seen by voltage class. Art. 2 of the Ministerial Ordinance on Technical Standards for Electrical Installations (Ordinance No. 52 of 1997) sets three classes: low voltage (AC 600 V or below / DC 750 V or below), high voltage (above low voltage up to 7,000 V), and extra-high voltage (over 7,000 V). A large data centre often receives extra-high voltage, which as a self-use electrical installation requires appointing a chief electrical engineer (Art. 43(1) of the Electricity Business Act). The contractor doing the receiving-and-transforming work must hold the registration under Art. 3 of the Act on the Proper Operation of Electrical Construction Business (Act No. 96 of 1970).

| What to check | Basis | In charge |
|---|---|---|
| Design live load / structural margin | Enforcement Order Art. 85 / structural calculation | Architect |
| Voltage class of receiving (extra-high = over 7,000 V) | Ministerial Ordinance on Technical Standards Art. 2 | Chief electrical engineer |
| Appointment of a chief electrical engineer | Electricity Business Act Art. 43(1) | Chief electrical engineer |
| Electrical-contractor registration | Act on the Proper Operation of Electrical Construction Business Art. 3 | Administrative scrivener (application) / [where and who applies for electrical-contractor registration](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/denki-koji-gyo-touroku-eigyosho-shunin) |

## Why inert gas rather than water, and what do you look at on the property side?

IT equipment is vulnerable to water damage, so a gas-based system that does not wet the equipment when it operates is chosen. Art. 17 of the Fire Service Act requires those concerned with a property under fire prevention to install and maintain fire-fighting equipment, and Art. 13 of the Enforcement Order of the Fire Service Act requires, for a part used as a telecommunications-equipment room with a floor area of 500 square metres or more, one of an inert-gas, halogenated or powder extinguishing system. A part where generators, transformers or similar electrical equipment are installed, with a floor area of 200 square metres or more, is likewise covered by these systems.

Which system to choose, and how to manage occupancy and unstaffed hours, are for the fire department with jurisdiction and the fire-equipment technician. What a real estate company can check is property documents — the current fire-equipment drawings and inspection reports (the inspection and reporting of Art. 17-3-3 of the Fire Service Act); installation itself is left to the fire-equipment technician.

| What to check | Basis | Whom to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of the duty to install equipment | Fire Service Act Art. 17 | Fire department |
| Equipment for a telecom room (500 m² or more) | Enforcement Order Art. 13 | Fire department / fire-equipment technician |
| Equipment for electrical rooms (200 m² or more) | Enforcement Order Art. 13 | Fire department / fire-equipment technician |
| Current equipment / inspection reports | Fire Service Act Art. 17-3-3 | Fire-equipment technician |

## When converting an existing building or warehouse, when is a change-of-use building confirmation needed?

When converting an existing office building or warehouse into a server room or data centre, Art. 87(1) of the Building Standards Act applies: where the use is changed to a special building under Art. 6(1)(i) (one where the total floor area used for that purpose exceeds 200 square metres), the confirmation provisions are applied mutatis mutandis. Whether a server room or data centre is a special building (a use in Appended Table 1) is not express and is judged case by case; in principle a confirmation application is required where it so qualifies and the converted part exceeds 200 square metres. We do not assert this qualification (unverified) and confirm it with the specified administrative agency and an architect.

Also worth checking are whether the existing building has a certificate of inspection and the current state of floor load, power supply and fire equipment. Without a certificate of inspection, proving legality on conversion and updating equipment tend to cause rework. These are the fields of the architect, the chief electrical engineer and the fire-equipment technician; we handle obtaining the property's current documents (confirmation and inspection certificates, equipment inspection reports, etc.) and organising the conditions.

|---|---|
| Whether a change-of-use confirmation is needed | Building Standards Act Art. 87(1) / specified administrative agency (unverified) |
| Whether there is a certificate of inspection | Existing building ledger / specified administrative agency |
| Excess or shortfall in floor load, power, fire equipment | Architect / chief electrical engineer / fire-equipment technician |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a server room be placed in any zoning district?**
A. Not uniformly. Appended Table 2 of the Building Standards Act has no express "data centre" or "server room" category; treated as part of an office it is generally possible except in exclusive industrial districts, while a stand-alone facility with large-scale equipment may be evaluated as closer to a factory. Because the judgment is on actual use rather than the name, confirm with the specified administrative agency per property in advance. This article does not assert it (unverified).

**Q. From what voltage is it extra-high-voltage receiving?**
A. Art. 2 of the Ministerial Ordinance on Technical Standards for Electrical Installations sets extra-high voltage as over 7,000 V (high voltage is above 600 V up to 7,000 V; low voltage is AC 600 V or below / DC 750 V or below). A large data centre often receives extra-high voltage, which as a self-use electrical installation requires appointing a chief electrical engineer (Art. 43(1) of the Electricity Business Act).

**Q. Why an inert-gas system rather than sprinklers?**
A. Because IT equipment is vulnerable to water damage. Art. 13 of the Enforcement Order of the Fire Service Act requires, for a telecommunications-equipment room of 500 square metres or more, one of an inert-gas, halogenated or powder system, and covers electrical rooms with generators, transformers and the like of 200 square metres or more. Which system to choose is judged by the fire department and the fire-equipment technician.

**Q. When converting an existing building to a server room, what do I look at first?**
A. Zoning, floor load, power supply, fire equipment and whether there is a certificate of inspection. Changing the use to a special building (over 200 square metres of floor area) may require a change-of-use confirmation (qualification judged case by case; unverified). Structure goes to an architect, power to a chief electrical engineer, fire to a fire-equipment technician; we handle obtaining the property's current documents and organising the conditions.
