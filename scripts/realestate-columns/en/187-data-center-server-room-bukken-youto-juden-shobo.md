**In short:** Ask three questions before signing: can the premises be used as planned, can the floor and electrical supply support the proposed racks, and what fire protection does each room need? “Office use allowed” and “high-voltage supply” answer none of those questions on their own.

## Does permission for office use cover a server room?

Japan does not classify every data centre as an office. In its October 2026 reply to the Diet, the Cabinet cited facilities classified as offices or warehouses according to their actual use. Size alone does not turn a facility into a factory. Check the local use district under Article 48 and Appended Table II of Japan’s Building Standards Act (建築基準法), any district plan, the building rules and the landlord’s consent. These are separate checks.

Ask for the building confirmation and inspection certificates, plans and zoning information. Mark the racks, cooling equipment and standby power on a plan, and note the hours of operation and how many people will work there. An architect can compare the proposal with the existing use and consult a building official or designated confirmation and inspection body.

## Which figures matter for the floor and power supply?

| Question | Ask the landlord for | Bring to the discussion |
|---|---|---|
| Will the floor support the racks? | Structural drawings, design live loads, floor construction | Fully loaded rack weights, support points, layout and delivery route |
| At what voltage is power supplied? | Single-line diagram and supply voltage (V or kV) | Input specifications for the equipment |
| How much power can the tenant actually use? | Transformer rating (kVA), contracted demand (kW), existing loads and tenant allocation | Combined peak demand of servers and cooling, and expansion plans |

Article 85(1) of the Enforcement Order of the Building Standards Act (建築基準法施行令) starts from the actual use of the building. An average floor load on a drawing does not establish that a heavy rack can be placed at a particular point. Give the architect the weight of the installed equipment, not just the empty rack.

Under Article 2(1)(iii) of the Ministerial Ordinance on Technical Standards for Electrical Installations (電気設備に関する技術基準を定める省令), extra-high voltage means **more than 7,000 V**. Exactly 7,000 V is not in that category. Voltage does not tell you what capacity is available. Ask the building’s electrical specialist to distinguish the total building supply from the capacity assigned to your unit.

## Does the fire protection threshold refer to the whole building?

Article 13 of the Enforcement Order of the Fire Service Act (消防法施行令) sets out the following thresholds for **particular parts** of premises listed in Appended Table I. Where these rows apply, inert gas, halogenated-agent or dry-chemical extinguishing systems are options; inert gas is not the only one.

| Part of the premises | Floor area in the relevant row |
|---|---|
| Telecommunications equipment room | 500 m² or more |
| Part containing qualifying electrical equipment such as generators or transformers | 200 m² or more |

Not every server room is a telecommunications equipment room for this purpose. The fire agency also lists equipment excluded from the electrical-equipment category, including distribution boards. A room below either threshold may still be subject to other requirements. Show the floor plan, equipment specifications and existing inspection records to the local fire department and a qualified fire-equipment specialist.

## When can changing the use of an existing building require building confirmation?

Under Article 87(1) of the Building Standards Act, the confirmation provisions apply to certain changes to a special-use building covered by Article 6(1)(i). The questions include whether the total floor area used for a purpose in Appended Table I, column (i), **exceeds** 200 m², and whether the change is between uses designated as similar. This is a different rule from the fire threshold of *200 m² or more*. Not needing an application does not dispense with substantive building or fire standards.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. The listing says “office.” Is that enough to sign?**
A. No. The building-use classification and the landlord’s agreement are separate. Set out the equipment and operating plan and check both against the existing drawings.

**Q. Does a supply above 7,000 V mean there is enough power?**
A. No. That is a voltage class. Compare transformer capacity, the tenant allocation and existing demand with the peak demand of the servers and cooling equipment together.

**Q. Must every server room have gas suppression?**
A. No. The room’s use, the equipment and the floor area of the relevant part matter. Where the cited rows of Article 13 apply, dry chemical is another option. Total building area is not the test.

**Q. What should I prepare for a viewing?**
A. Bring the number of racks, their fully loaded weights, power demand including cooling, and a layout. Compare these with the landlord’s structural, electrical and fire drawings to identify what specialists still need.
