## Since when has the flood hazard map been part of the important-matters explanation?

The explanation of the location on the flood hazard map was added to the important-matters explanation by the 2020 amendment of the Enforcement Regulation of the Real Estate Brokerage Act, in force on 28 August 2020. The basis is Art. 16-4-3(iii-2) of that Regulation. Art. 35(1)(xiv)(a) of the Real Estate Brokerage Act requires that matters set by the ministry's ordinance be explained as important matters, and this was added as one such matter.

Specifically, where the property is shown on the map (flood hazard map) that the mayor of the municipality (including a special ward) provides under Art. 11(i) of the Enforcement Regulation of the Flood Control Act, a real estate transaction specialist explains the property's location on that map. The ministry's Q&A describes the practice of presenting each of the flood, inland (rainwater) flooding and storm-surge hazard maps and indicating the property's approximate position (MLIT, "Q&A on the partial amendment of the Enforcement Regulation of the Real Estate Brokerage Act (adding flood-risk information to the important-matters explanation)"). If the municipality has not prepared a hazard map, it suffices to explain that fact.

The key point is that this explanation "points to the property's position on the map" and does not assert whether or not it floods or that it is safe. Do not stop at hearing the explanation; read it against the municipality's original hazard map.

| What to check | Basis | How the buyer reads it |
|---|---|---|
| Timing of enforcement | In force 28 Aug 2020; Reg. Art. 16-4-3(iii-2) | Transactions from this date are covered |
| The target map | Mayor-provided map under Flood Control Reg. Art. 11(i) | Check all three: flood, inland, storm surge |
| Content of the explanation | Property's location on the map (approximate) | Not a guarantee of flooding or safety |
| If not prepared | The absence is explained | Supplement with other disaster information |

## Which zones — maximum-scale flood, inland (rainwater) flooding and storm surge — do you check?

The Flood Control Act defines inundation-assumption zones in three types: the flood inundation-assumption zone (Art. 14), the inland (rainwater) flooding inundation-assumption zone (Art. 14-2), and the storm-surge inundation-assumption zone (Art. 14-3). Since the 2015 amendment of the Act, these are in principle designated on the premise of the "largest scale that can be assumed" of rainfall or storm surge, so a wider area is coloured than under the former planning scale. What the buyer should see is which zone and which inundation depth the property falls in for each of the three.

Flood means river overflow, inland flooding means inundation where drainage such as sewers cannot keep up, and storm surge means a sea-level rise from a typhoon; different causes mean inundation arises differently. Even away from a river, some zones flood from inland water or storm surge, so judging a place safe by looking at only one type is dangerous. For these inundation-assumption zones, the mayor takes measures such as distributing a hazard map to inform residents of what is needed to secure evacuation (Art. 15(3) of the Flood Control Act).

Separately from the important-matters explanation, sediment-disaster alert zones and special alert zones (the so-called yellow and red zones under the Sediment Disaster Prevention Act) are explained as "restrictions under laws" under Art. 35(1)(ii) of the Real Estate Brokerage Act. Because building is restricted within a special alert zone, check it together with the flood hazard map.

| Type of hazard | Provision | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Flood inundation-assumption zone | Flood Control Act Art. 14 | Depth and zone on river overflow |
| Inland (rainwater) flooding zone | Flood Control Act Art. 14-2 | Check even away from a river |
| Storm-surge inundation zone | Flood Control Act Art. 14-3 | Flooding in coastal and low areas |
| Sediment-disaster (special) alert zone | Sediment Disaster Prevention Act; Brokerage Act Art. 35(1)(ii) | Whether building is restricted |

## How do inundation-assumption zones and sediment-disaster zones affect value and financing?

Falling within an inundation-assumption or sediment-disaster alert zone does not by itself prohibit sale or building. However, zones of deep inundation or a special alert zone (red zone) may, in practice, affect a lender's collateral valuation, insurance premiums, and future saleability. This is a general tendency; individual loan availability or appraised value is left to the lender and the real estate appraiser. This article does not assert it (unverified).

For the buyer, it is practical to read hazard information not only as a "reason not to buy" but as material for "how to prepare after buying." Organise the assumed inundation depth, evacuation sites, the building's structure and foundation height, and the terms for fire insurance and flood coverage from the important-matters explanation and municipal materials. Where a non-resident buyer settles or remits the price, confirmation under the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act may also apply.

| Flood coverage in fire insurance | Insurer | Terms confirmed with the insurer |
| Building restrictions | Specified administrative agency (special alert zones, etc.) | Legal restrictions confirmed in the explanation |

## What can a Chinese-speaking buyer obtain by themselves before the important-matters explanation?

Because the important-matters explanation is given just before the contract, gathering hazard information yourself beforehand lets you calmly cross-check the explanation. The main information a buyer can obtain is as follows; much of it is free or inexpensive and available online.

First, MLIT's "overlay hazard map" (Hazard Map Portal Site), where you can overlay flood, inland, storm-surge and sediment-disaster information by address (record the access date). Second, the originals of the flood, inland and storm-surge hazard maps published by the property's municipality. Third, city-planning information and the designation status of sediment-disaster alert zones. These back up the information shown in the important-matters explanation. Viewing methods and update timing differ by municipality, so confirm with the guidance of the property's municipality (the counter and publication timing differ by municipality; unverified).

That said, online maps show an approximate position; the final identification of the property's location and its explanation as an important matter are done by a real estate transaction specialist. Treat the information you gather as preparation for a dialogue rather than taking the explanation at face value.

| Source | What it reveals | Cost / method |
|---|---|---|
| Overlay hazard map (MLIT) | Overlays flood, inland, storm surge, sediment disaster | Free, online (record access date) |
| Municipal hazard map originals | Each inundation assumption and evacuation sites | Differs by municipality (unverified) |
| City-planning / sediment-zone designation | Building restrictions, presence of alert zones | Municipal counter / online |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is the important-matters explanation deficient without the flood hazard map explanation?**
A. For transactions from 28 August 2020, if the property is shown on the flood hazard map that the mayor provides under Art. 11(i) of the Enforcement Regulation of the Flood Control Act, Art. 16-4-3(iii-2) of the Enforcement Regulation of the Real Estate Brokerage Act requires explaining its location on that map. If the municipality has not prepared one, explaining that fact suffices. Confirm whether and how it is explained before contracting.

**Q. Is it enough to look only at the flood hazard map?**
A. No. Inundation-assumption zones come in three types: flood (Flood Control Act Art. 14), inland (rainwater) flooding (Art. 14-2) and storm surge (Art. 14-3). Because some zones flood from inland water or storm surge even away from a river, check the property's position on all three.

**Q. If it is within an inundation-assumption zone, can I not buy the property?**
A. Being within a zone does not itself prohibit sale or building. But depending on the inundation depth and whether it is a special alert zone, it may affect financing, insurance and future saleability. Individual loan availability and valuation are for the lender and the real estate appraiser; this article does not assert them. Consider post-purchase preparation as well.

**Q. Can I research the hazards myself before the important-matters explanation?**
A. Yes. Using MLIT's "overlay hazard map" or the municipality's published hazard maps, you can check flood, inland, storm-surge and sediment-disaster information by address. Record the access date and cross-check it with the important-matters explanation so you can read it calmly. The final identification and explanation of the location are done by a real estate transaction specialist.
