We increasingly receive enquiries from people who want to open a family-funeral hall or a rental ceremony hall using a vacant warehouse, a roadside building or their own land—or to lease such a property to a funeral operator. However, not every building can become a funeral hall. Most plans stall at the stage of zoning restrictions, road access, parking, or explanation to neighbours.

A common first stumble is confusing a funeral hall with a crematorium. The two have different frameworks for building and permits, so searching for a property as if they were the same "funeral facility" often reveals later that you cannot build or lease it.

## How does a ceremony hall differ from a crematorium or cemetery, and what permit framework applies?

The framework first divides according to whether the facility has a crematory furnace or stores remains. A funeral hall that only holds ceremonies such as wakes and farewell services, without cremation or the interment or storage of remains, is generally not subject to the business permit under the Act on Cemeteries, Burial, etc. ("Cemetery and Burial Act").

| Facility | Main basis | Siting/permit framework (outline) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funeral hall (no crematory furnace) | Building Standards Act (assembly hall) | Zoning building restrictions (Act art. 48 and Appended Table 2). Where there is no cremation, the Cemetery and Burial Act business permit does not apply |
| Crematorium | Building Standards Act art. 51 and Cemetery and Burial Act | Within a city planning area, the site location must be decided in the city plan, or the designated administrative agency's permission etc. is required. A separate business permit under the Cemetery and Burial Act is also needed |
| Cemetery/charnel house | Cemetery and Burial Act | Subject to a business permit because it involves interment/storage of remains (separate article—the administrative scrivener's field) |

The Cemetery and Burial Act defines burial, cremation, graves, cemeteries, charnel houses and crematoria in article 2, and in article 10 provides that operating a cemetery, charnel house or crematorium requires the permission of the prefectural governor etc. A plan that includes a crematory furnace involves both this business permit and Building Standards Act article 51, which greatly raises the siting hurdle.

Even a hall without cremation is still subject to zoning restrictions and fire-safety standards as an "assembly hall". The final judgment on whether the Cemetery and Burial Act applies, and the feasibility of plans involving cremation or storage, should be confirmed with the relevant municipal section and an administrative scrivener who handles permits. The business permit for cemeteries and charnel houses is covered separately in [Business permits for cemeteries and charnel houses](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/bochi-noukotsudo-keiei-kyoka-youken-nagare).

## In which zoning districts can a funeral hall operate, and in what properties can it not be built?

A funeral hall without cremation is generally treated as an "assembly hall" and is subject to the building restrictions of each zoning district. Article 48 of the Building Standards Act and its Appended Table 2 set out, for each zoning district, which buildings may and may not be built. The City Planning Act (articles 8 and 9) defines the types and purposes of zoning districts, while the specific building restrictions are governed by the Building Standards Act.

| Example zoning district | Treatment of an assembly hall (funeral hall) (general tendency) |
| --- | --- |
| Category I/II low-rise exclusive residential; Category I medium-/high-rise exclusive residential | Assembly halls generally cannot be built. Exceptions by permission of the designated administrative agency may exist, but are not guaranteed |
| Category II medium-/high-rise residential through neighbourhood commercial, commercial, quasi-industrial, etc. | Building may be possible depending on scale and district, but floor-area requirements and attached conditions must be checked |
| Industrial/exclusive industrial | Restrictions apply from a different angle, so each plan must be checked |

This is only a general tendency. Even within the same zoning district, the conclusion can change with the size of the building, whether it is a legal non-conforming existing building, and overlay regulations such as district plans. Whether a use applies and whether building is feasible are matters for the designated administrative agency (building guidance section) and an architect to judge. Do not decide "it can be built" on assumption; present the scale of the plan and check individually.

## What does a hall property need in terms of the Building Standards Act, Fire Service Act and parking obligations?

Even after clearing zoning, the standards for the building itself and its equipment remain. Because an assembly hall is a use where many people gather, evacuation, fire prevention and the handling of visitors' vehicles become issues.

| Aspect to check | Main content |
| --- | --- |
| Building Standards Act (assembly hall) | Evacuation and fire-prevention standards as a special building, and rules according to floor area. For existing buildings, whether there is a completion inspection certificate or non-conforming status |
| Fire Service Act | Fire-fighting equipment and notifications according to the fire-defence-object classification in Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order (assembly halls fall within a category). Consult the fire station with jurisdiction in advance |
| Parking | Entry and exit of mourners, hearses and shuttle buses. If the scale triggers the municipality's parking-provision ordinance, confirm the obligation |
| Surrounding roads | Entry and turning of hearses and buses, stopping space in front of the building, interference with neighbouring daily routes |

Parking-provision obligations are set by municipal ordinance according to the building's use and total floor area, among other factors. Standards differ by municipality, so check the ordinance for the property's location together with the date of reference. Because mourners' cars lingering on the street can become a source of friction with neighbours, look at the actual number of vehicles in operation, separately from the statutory obligation.

When converting an existing building into a hall, also check for a completion inspection certificate and whether a use change is required. For the handling of use change, [Converting a property without a completion inspection certificate to a welfare use](https://luck428.com/en/column/kensazumisho-nashi-bukken-fukushi-youto-henko), and for fire and noise issues, [Karaoke-box properties, zoning and fire safety](https://luck428.com/en/column/karaoke-box-tenpo-bukken-youto-shoubou-soon), are useful for the reasoning. Confirm conformity with evacuation and fire-prevention standards with an architect and the fire station with jurisdiction.

## When converting an existing building in an urbanisation-control area into a hall, how are development permits and use changes handled?

Roadside buildings and suburban warehouses are often in urbanisation-control areas. In such areas, building and use change are restricted under the City Planning Act.

Article 29 of the City Planning Act requires the governor's etc. permission for development activities; article 34 limits, by exhaustive list, the siting criteria for development permits in urbanisation-control areas; and article 43 requires the governor's etc. permission to newly build or rebuild a building, or to change its use to a different kind of building, in areas other than those with a development permit. In other words, even merely "changing the use" of an existing warehouse into a funeral hall may require consideration of a permit in an urbanisation-control area.

Whether it can be treated as rebuilding of an existing building, or requires a new permit as a use change, depends on the identity of use and scale with the former building and on the municipality's operating criteria. Judging whether the siting criteria for a permit apply, and applying for a development permit or use-change permit, are the administrative scrivener's field. For the reasoning on properties in urbanisation-control areas, see also [Using an existing building in an urbanisation-control area for business](https://luck428.com/en/column/kensetsugyo-shizaioki-shako-chosei-kuiki-bukken).

Which area classification a property falls in is investigated as a premise of sale or lease. Do not assume that an urbanisation-control area is always impossible or an urbanisation-promotion area always possible; check the area classification, zoning and road access together.

## What should you confirm about neighbour explanation and residents' association before choosing a property?

A funeral hall may fail to progress because of relations with neighbours even when it meets the legal standards. Concerns about the coming and going of mourners, shuttle vehicles and night-time movement have led to opposition and disputes in reported cases. Before deciding on a property, confirm the following.

- The zoning and area classification, and the location of surrounding homes and facilities
- Whether the municipality has its own ordinance, guidance outline or prior-consultation scheme for funeral facilities
- The custom of prior explanation to the residents' or neighbourhood association, and the scope of explanation expected
- Whether the routes of mourners and vehicles interfere with neighbours' daily routes or school routes

Some municipalities set ordinances or outlines requiring distance limits, prior notification or consultation for funeral halls. Check the ordinance or outline for the property's location together with the date of reference, and consult the prior-consultation point if needed. If discussion with neighbours becomes a dispute and turns into a legal matter such as damages or an injunction, that is the lawyer's field. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I turn a vacant house in a residential neighbourhood into a ceremony hall?**
A. It depends on the zoning. A hall without cremation is generally treated as an "assembly hall", which tends to be prohibited in Category I/II low-rise exclusive residential and Category I medium-/high-rise exclusive residential districts. Whether a use applies and whether it is feasible are judged by the designated administrative agency and an architect, so present the scale of the plan and check individually.

**Q. Can I convert a roadside warehouse (in an urbanisation-control area) into a hall?**
A. In an urbanisation-control area, even a use change may require consideration of a permit under the City Planning Act. Judging whether the siting criteria apply, and applying for permits, are the administrative scrivener's field. Decide after checking the area classification, zoning and road access together.

**Q. I want to build a crematorium too. Does that change how I search for a property?**
A. Yes. Adding a crematory furnace brings in the business permit under the Cemetery and Burial Act and the siting framework of Building Standards Act article 51, greatly raising the hurdle. The business permit is the administrative scrivener's field and siting feasibility the designated administrative agency's, so consider it separately from a hall-only plan.

**Q. If I only lease to a funeral operator without running it myself, are permits unnecessary?**
A. The lessor is in the lessor's position, and permits and neighbour relations for the hall are generally the lessee's responsibility. However, the use conforming to zoning, building and fire standards is a premise of the lease. Make the use limitation and restoration clear in the lease agreement.
