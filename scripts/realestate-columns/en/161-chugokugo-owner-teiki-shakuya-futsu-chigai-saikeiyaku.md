**The key point**: When you lease out a Japanese income property, the contract comes in two types—an "ordinary building lease" and a "fixed-term building lease." The biggest difference is whether there is renewal. An ordinary lease is in principle renewed even after the term expires, and the landlord needs "just cause" to refuse renewal (Land and Building Lease Act arts. 26 and 28). A fixed-term lease ends definitively at expiry, with no renewal. However, a fixed-term lease is not formed unless two conditions are met: (1) the contract is in writing (or an electromagnetic record) and defines that there is "no renewal"; and (2) before contracting, the landlord delivers a written document stating that there is no renewal and the lease ends at the end of the term, and explains it (Land and Building Lease Act art. 38; the amendment effective 18 May 2022 also allowed electronic contracts and electromagnetic means). Neglecting this prior explanation voids the no-renewal clause, and the lease is treated as an ordinary one. For a non-resident owner, a fixed-term lease—which lets you control renewal—is an option, but it has the flip side of vacancy risk and the trouble of re-contracting. ## What is the difference between a fixed-term and an ordinary lease?

The decisive difference is "whether it is renewed." An ordinary lease is deemed renewed on the same terms as before even after the term expires (art. 26), and for the landlord to refuse renewal or give notice of termination, "just cause" is required (art. 28). Just cause is judged by weighing the parties' need for the building, the past course of dealings, the state of use, an offer of eviction money, etc., and is not easily recognised.

A fixed-term lease (fixed-term building lease) ends definitively at expiry, with no renewal. In exchange, the formalities for its formation are strict.

| Item | Ordinary lease | Fixed-term lease |
|---|---|---|
| Renewal | In principle renewed (art. 26) | No renewal (ends at expiry) |
| Refusing renewal | Landlord needs just cause (art. 28) | Just cause unneeded (no renewal from the start) |
| Contract form | May be formed orally | A written or electromagnetic-record contract is required (art. 38(1), (2)) |
| Prior explanation | Not needed | Deliver a written explanation that there is no renewal and it ends at expiry (art. 38(3)) |
| Minimum term | Under 1 year is deemed no fixed term | Valid as a fixed-term lease even under 1 year |
| Early termination | By special agreement | For residential use under 200 m², the tenant may terminate on unavoidable grounds (art. 38(7)) |

A fixed-term "building lease" and a fixed-term "land lease" are different things. It is easy to confuse the fixed-term land lease that leases land (art. 22 et seq.), so the land side is covered in [selling a building when inherited land turned out to be on a fixed-term land lease](https://luck428.com/en/column/souzoku-teiki-shakuchiken-tatemono-baikyaku). This article narrows to the "fixed-term building lease" that leases a building.

## Where are the merits and risks for a non-resident owner who chooses a fixed-term lease?

For an owner living abroad, the appeal of a fixed-term lease is that you "can control renewal yourself." Under an ordinary lease, without just cause you cannot get the tenant to leave, but a fixed-term lease ends definitively at expiry, making it easier to plan a future sale, own use or rebuilding. On the other hand, because there is no renewal, tenants settle less readily, and each term brings the trouble of re-contracting or re-advertising.

| Perspective | Merit of a fixed-term lease | Risk / trouble of a fixed-term lease |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty of recovery | Ends definitively at expiry; just cause / eviction money in principle unneeded | To keep leasing, a re-contracting procedure is needed |
| Rent setting | Easier to set a term matched to the market without assuming renewal | Tenants may be harder to attract than at market |
| Future plans | Easier to match the timing of sale, own use or rebuilding | Vacancy risk may arise at each expiry |
| Managing from abroad | Easier to plan management with a bounded term | Managing termination notice / re-contracting deadlines is a burden remotely |

Which is better depends on the property's location, rent market and the owner's exit strategy. ## What happens if you neglect the prior delivery and explanation of the document?

This is where accidents happen most with a fixed-term lease. Land and Building Lease Act art. 38(3) requires the landlord, before contracting, to **deliver a written document and explain** that "there is no renewal, and the lease ends at the end of the term." With the amendment effective 18 May 2022, provision by electromagnetic means became possible with the tenant's consent (art. 38(4)), but the structure of "in advance, by a separate document (or electromagnetic record), with explanation" is unchanged.

What happens if you neglect it? Under art. 38(5), the **no-renewal clause becomes void**. In other words, what was meant to be a fixed-term lease becomes an ordinary lease; what should have ended at expiry is renewed, and without just cause you cannot have it vacated. Even if the contract states "this is a fixed-term building lease," this conclusion does not change without the prior delivery of the explanatory document.

| Common pitfall | Article | What happens if neglected |
|---|---|---|
| Not contracting in writing (electromagnetic record) | art. 38(1), (2) | Not formed as a fixed-term lease |
| Not delivering the prior explanatory document | art. 38(3), (5) | The no-renewal clause is void = ordinary lease |
| Combining contract and explanation in one sheet | Case law requires a separate document | The fixed-term lease may be denied |

For an owner abroad, this prior explanation is in practice often designed to be done by the rental-housing manager or broker to whom management is entrusted. Settling, before contracting, who delivers the explanatory document and when is the key to avoiding the void risk.

## How should early termination and re-contracting be designed?

**Early termination** is treated differently in ordinary and fixed-term leases. In a fixed-term lease, for a residential building under 200 m², if the tenant can no longer continue living there on unavoidable grounds such as a job transfer, medical treatment or caring for a relative, the tenant may give notice of termination, and the contract ends one month after the notice (art. 38(7)). This is a tenant-protection provision, and any special agreement disadvantageous to the tenant contrary to it is void. Termination by the landlord in principle cannot be done without a special agreement.

**Termination notice** is also easily forgotten. In a fixed-term lease of 1 year or more, the landlord cannot assert the termination against the tenant unless it notifies, between one year and six months before expiry, that the lease ends at expiry (art. 38(6)). If notified after that period, it can be asserted six months after the date of notice. Managing this notice deadline is especially important for management from abroad.

**Re-contracting** is not "renewal." Because a fixed-term lease is not renewed, to keep leasing to the same tenant you must conclude a new fixed-term lease after expiry (re-contract). In principle, the written contract and prior explanation under art. 38 are redone each time. MLIT publishes a "Standard Fixed-Term Residential Lease Contract," which can be referred to as a template for termination-notice and re-contracting clauses.

| Scene | Deadline / requirement | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Termination notice (term ≥ 1 year) | Notify between 1 year and 6 months before expiry | art. 38(6) |
| Tenant's early termination (residential under 200 m²) | Unavoidable grounds + 1 month from notice | art. 38(7) |
| Re-contracting | New contract, not renewal; redo the written contract + prior explanation | art. 38(1)–(5) |

## Can I contract and renew while overseas?

Yes. The 2022 amendment made it possible to carry out both the fixed-term-lease contract and the prior explanation by electromagnetic record / means (art. 38(2), (4)). An electronic contract system, or provision of the explanatory document by email with the tenant's consent, is possible, making it easier to proceed even when the owner is not in Japan.

Two cautions, however. First, electromagnetic prior explanation needs the tenant's consent, premised on the recipient being able to output the record to paper. Second, because a fixed-term lease has no "renewal," to keep leasing you need to re-contract at each expiry, redoing the written contract and prior explanation in principle. The more remote the owner, the more important it is to pin down—dividing roles with the manager—the termination notice (between 1 year and 6 months before expiry) and the re-contracting deadlines. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. What is the single biggest difference between a fixed-term and an ordinary lease?**
A. Whether there is renewal. An ordinary lease is in principle renewed even after the term expires, and the landlord needs just cause to refuse renewal (Land and Building Lease Act arts. 26, 28). A fixed-term lease ends definitively at expiry, with no renewal. In exchange, the conditions for its formation are that the contract (in writing or an electromagnetic record) defines "no renewal," and that before contracting the landlord delivers a written document to that effect and explains it (art. 38). For a non-resident owner, the ability to control renewal is the fixed-term lease's hallmark.

**Q. What happens to a fixed-term lease if I don't hand over the prior explanatory document?**
A. The no-renewal clause becomes void (Land and Building Lease Act art. 38(5)). Even if the contract says "fixed-term building lease," if you did not, before contracting, deliver and explain a separate document stating that there is no renewal and it ends at expiry, it is treated as an ordinary lease; it is not ended at expiry but renewed, and you cannot seek eviction without just cause. The 2022 amendment allows electromagnetic means too, but the tenant's consent is required.

**Q. Can I contract or re-contract a fixed-term lease even while living abroad?**
A. Yes. With the amendment effective 18 May 2022, both the contract and the prior explanation can be done by electromagnetic record / means (art. 38(2), (4)). An electronic contract, or provision of the explanatory document by email with the tenant's consent, is possible. But because a fixed-term lease has no renewal, to keep leasing you need to re-contract at each expiry, in principle redoing the written contract and prior explanation. Don't forget to manage the termination-notice deadline (between 1 year and 6 months before expiry).

**Q. How is tax handled when a non-resident receives rent?**
A. When a non-resident leases domestic real estate in Japan and receives rent, the tenant who pays in principle bears the duty to withhold 20.42% and pay it by the 10th of the following month (Income Tax Act arts. 212, 213). However, where an individual rents it for the residence of themselves or a relative, withholding is not required (Income Tax Act Enforcement Order art. 328). The owner too in principle needs to file a tax return in Japan, and adjustment under a tax treaty is possible. For the specific calculation and filing, consult a tax accountant (an independent business under a separate contract).
