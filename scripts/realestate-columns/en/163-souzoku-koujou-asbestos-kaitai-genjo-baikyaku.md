Manufacture and use of asbestos-containing building materials were comprehensively banned in 2006, so a building built or refurbished before then may contain it. ## Where can you tell whether an inherited factory/warehouse may contain asbestos?

Start with the age. Manufacture, import and use of asbestos-containing building materials were regulated in stages and, in principle, comprehensively banned in 2006. So a factory or warehouse built—or refurbished—before then may contain asbestos-containing materials. In a steel-frame factory, typical examples are sprayed material on beams/columns, insulation on boilers/piping, and moulded board (slate) for roofs/walls.

But age and material type tell you only a "possibility." To determine it reliably, you estimate the materials from the design documents, as-built drawings and past refurbishment records, then take samples and run an analysis.

| Clue | What it tells you | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Year of construction/refurbishment | Pre-2006 means possible content | Registration/building-confirmation records; confirmed in our survey |
| Design documents / material list | Estimate of the materials used | Certified building-asbestos surveyor |
| Analysis | Confirmation of content and level (1–3) | Certified building-asbestos surveyor / analysis body |

We can gather clues for this "estimate of possibility" as part of the property survey, but confirming the presence and level is the field of a certified building-asbestos surveyor. Soil-side contamination (a Soil Contamination Countermeasures Act survey of a former factory site) is a separate issue from asbestos, organised separately for the sale of an inherited factory/warehouse. This article narrows to asbestos in the building.

## What must the seller disclose when selling as-is?

Even selling on an as-is basis, the seller (and the brokering real-estate operator) have things to explain. The centre is the important-matters explanation.

Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35(1)(xiv) and Enforcement Regulation art. 16-4-3(iv) provide that, in a sale etc. of a building, where "a record of a survey of the presence or absence of asbestos use exists," its content is explained as an important matter. The point easily misunderstood is that this is **not a duty to run a new asbestos survey**. The structure is: if a survey-result record already exists, explain its content. If there is no record, you explain that there is none.

Separately from whether a record exists, Civil Code non-conformity liability applies to a sale. Where the delivered object does not conform to the contract, the buyer may seek cure, price reduction, damages or rescission (Civil Code art. 562 et seq.). Even with a clause stating the seller bears no non-conformity liability, the seller is not relieved of liability for a fact the seller knew and did not disclose (Civil Code art. 572). Further, where a real-estate operator is itself the seller, there are limits on clauses restricting non-conformity liability (Brokerage Act art. 40).

| Scene | What the seller must note | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A survey record exists | Explain its content (body, scope, date, presence of asbestos) as an important matter | Brokerage Act art. 35(1)(xiv); Enforcement Regulation art. 16-4-3(iv) |
| A fact you know | Not disclosing it leaves liability even under a disclaimer | Civil Code art. 572 |
| A real-estate operator is the seller | Limits on clauses restricting non-conformity liability | Brokerage Act art. 40 |

So selling as-is does not mean "selling without investigating"; it presupposes "selling by conveying correctly what you know." The brokerage including the important-matters explanation is ours; handling a specific non-conformity dispute is a lawyer's field.

## How much do the cost and time run if you demolish/remove first?

Removal-first lets you hand the buyer vacant land (or a sound building) free of asbestos worry, but the seller bears the demolition/removal cost and time first. The cost varies greatly with the asbestos level and quantity, so it is inappropriate to cite a single market rate. Grasp the procedure and the factors that move the cost, not a "ballpark."

| Step | Content | Basis / who |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-survey | Check design documents and site survey, analyse if needed; from 1 Oct 2023 a certified professional performs it | Asbestos Rules art. 3; Air Pollution Control Act art. 18-15 / certified building-asbestos surveyor |
| Report of pre-survey result | Work at or above a set scale is reported before starting via the electronic system | Air Pollution Control Act art. 18-15; Asbestos Rules / prime contractor or self-executing party |
| Removal work | Methods for isolation/sheeting vary by level (1 = sprayed, 2 = insulation etc., 3 = moulded board) | Specialist with an asbestos work chief |
| Demolition/disposal | Asbestos-containing waste is separated and disposed of properly | Demolition contractor / industrial-waste disposal |

The biggest factor moving cost is the asbestos level. Sprayed material (level 1) tends to be expensive because of negative-pressure isolation and dedicated sheeting; moulded board (level 3) tends to be relatively contained. Time, too, is longer than selling as-is because it goes through survey, report, isolation/sheeting, removal and disposal. Designing/executing this is a specialist's field; preparing the report an administrative scrivener's; calculating capital gains (acquisition-cost add-on, treatment of transfer expenses) a tax accountant's.

## Who bears the pre-survey reporting duty?

This is a point inherited sellers easily misunderstand. From 1 April 2022, for demolition/refurbishment work at or above a set scale, there is a duty to report the result of the asbestos pre-survey before starting, via the electronic system (the asbestos pre-survey result reporting system) (Air Pollution Control Act art. 18-15; Asbestos Hazard Prevention Rules).

The party bearing this reporting duty is the **prime contractor or self-executing party = the executor** of the work, not the party ordering the work (= the inherited owner = seller). The report goes to both the prefecture etc. and the labour standards inspection office. The scale guide for when a report is needed:

| Type of work | Guide to the reportable scale |
|---|---|
| Building demolition | Total floor area of the demolished part is 80 m² or more |
| Building refurbishment | Contract price is 1,000,000 yen or more (tax included) |
| Structure demolition/refurbishment | Contract price is 1,000,000 yen or more (tax included) |

Further, from 1 October 2023, a building pre-survey must be performed by a certified professional such as a building-asbestos-containing-material surveyor. The inherited owner does not report themselves; the executor they commissioned reports—mistaking this role can lead to "no one reported," so confirm who carries the pre-survey and report at the demolition-contract stage. Preparing the report is an administrative scrivener's field; the survey a certified building-asbestos surveyor's.

## How is the price set when the buyer assumes demolition?

Many factory/warehouse buyers value the land rather than the building and consider demolition/rebuilding. With asbestos, removal cost is added on top of this demolition cost, so it affects the price.

The axis is a subtraction: from the land price as vacant land, deduct the demolition/removal cost the buyer will bear. The higher the asbestos level, the larger the removal cost and the larger the deduction. This is where the fork arises between the seller removing first and then selling (removal-first) and selling as-is with the cost priced in (as-is).

- **As-is**: the seller does not bear the removal cost directly but effectively bears it in the priced-in form. Disclosure of known facts and the important-matters explanation are required.
- **Removal-first**: the seller bears the cost and time first, but the buyer's anxiety is reduced and the transaction may proceed more easily.

Which is advantageous depends on the asbestos level, the buyer's profile (demolition- or use-assuming) and whether there is urgency to sell. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Must I run an asbestos survey before selling an inherited factory/warehouse?**
A. There is no provision uniformly requiring a new asbestos survey before selling. Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35(1)(xiv) and Enforcement Regulation art. 16-4-3(iv) are structured so that "where a record of an asbestos survey exists," its content is explained as an important matter; if there is no record, you explain that there is none. But known facts must be disclosed, and failing to disclose a fact you knew leaves non-conformity liability even under a disclaimer (Civil Code art. 572). Whether to survey is decided together with the as-is vs. removal-first choice.

**Q. If I sell as-is, is the seller free of asbestos liability?**
A. Selling as-is does not remove liability. Even with a clause stating no non-conformity liability, the seller is not relieved for a fact the seller knew and did not disclose (Civil Code art. 572). Where a real-estate operator is itself the seller, clauses restricting non-conformity liability are further limited (Brokerage Act art. 40). As-is is not "selling without investigating" but "selling by conveying correctly what you know." Responding to any dispute is a lawyer's field.

**Q. When demolishing, does the owner (seller) file the asbestos pre-survey report?**
A. No. From 1 April 2022, the asbestos pre-survey reporting duty for work at or above a set scale falls on the prime contractor or self-executing party = the executor, not the owner who orders it. The reportable guide is a total demolished floor area of 80 m² or more for building demolition, and a contract price of 1,000,000 yen or more for building refurbishment or structure demolition/refurbishment. From 1 October 2023 a building pre-survey must be done by a certified professional. Confirm who carries the pre-survey and report at the demolition-contract stage. Preparing the report is an administrative scrivener's field.

**Q. How much does asbestos lower the sale price?**
A. No single market rate can be given. The axis is a subtraction: from the land price as vacant land, deduct the demolition/removal cost the buyer will bear. The higher the level (1 = sprayed, 2 = insulation etc., 3 = moulded board), the larger the removal cost and the deduction. Whether you remove first (removal-first) or price it in as-is changes your net proceeds and timing. Confirm the content judgment with a certified building-asbestos surveyor, the removal estimate with a specialist, and the capital-gains estimate with a tax accountant; the market sense and brokerage are ours.
