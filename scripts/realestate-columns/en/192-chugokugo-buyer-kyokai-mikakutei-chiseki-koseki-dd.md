## How do the parcel boundary and the ownership boundary differ?

The "parcel boundary" (筆界) means the two or more points, and the straight lines connecting them, that were deemed to constitute the border of a parcel of land at the time it was registered (Art. 123(i) of the Real Property Registration Act); it is a boundary fixed publicly by registration and cannot be moved by the parties' agreement alone. By contrast, the "ownership boundary" is a private-law boundary of how far ownership of the neighbouring parcels extends, and it can diverge from the parcel boundary through acquisitive prescription or the parties' agreement. The boundary a buyer understands from "the line of a wall or ditch" is closer to the ownership boundary and may not match the registered parcel boundary — grasp this difference in premise first.

This difference divides whom to consult. A dispute over the parcel boundary is handled by parcel-boundary identification by a parcel-boundary registrar at the Legal Affairs Bureau (Art. 131(1)) or by a boundary-fixing lawsuit in court. A dispute over the ownership boundary — the extent of ownership — is a civil matter handled by a lawyer. Where, after parcel-boundary identification has been made, a judgment in a boundary-fixing lawsuit becomes final, the parcel-boundary identification loses its effect to the extent it conflicts with the judgment (Art. 148). In other words, the court's judgment is superior for the final fixing of the parcel boundary.

| Term | Nature | Basis / in charge |
|---|---|---|
| Parcel boundary | Public registered boundary (cannot be moved by agreement) | Act Art. 123(i) / land and house investigator, Legal Affairs Bureau |
| Ownership boundary | Private-law extent of ownership (moves by agreement/prescription) | Civil matter / lawyer |
| Parcel-boundary identification | Administrative identification of the parcel boundary's on-site position | Art. 131(1) / parcel-boundary registrar |

## What happens after the handover if you buy land with an unfixed boundary?

Acquiring land while the boundary is unfixed can bring, after the handover, a mismatch between the registered and actual area, a later-discovered encroachment with the neighbour (wall, eaves, trees, pipes), and a demand to fix the boundary at the time of subdivision, security creation or resale. Where the registered area and the measured area diverge, the owner and the like must, when there is a change in the area, apply within one month for a change registration of the area (Art. 37); and the correction of an error or omission in a registered matter is done as a "correction registration" (Art. 2(xvi)) through an area-correction registration.

In the buyer's DD, before the sale contract, confirm "whether the boundary is fixed" and "whether there is a confirmed survey map, a parcel-boundary confirmation and a land-area survey map." Where it is not fixed, make clear in the contract clauses whether to make a confirmed survey at the seller's cost a condition of handover, or to proceed as a registered-area sale with no settlement of any area difference. Organising these contract clauses is handled by the broker within the important-matters explanation and contract, but the actual survey and registration and disputes with the neighbour are the fields of a land and house investigator, a judicial scrivener and a lawyer respectively.

| What may happen after handover | Background | In charge of response |
|---|---|---|
| Area difference between registered and measured | Change or error in the area | Area-correction registration (Art. 37; Art. 2(xvi)) / land and house investigator |
| Encroachment with the neighbour | Overhang of wall, eaves, pipes | Current-state survey, negotiation, lawyer |
| Obstacle to subdivision, security, resale | Unfixed boundary | Confirmed boundary survey / land and house investigator |

## Who handles the confirmed survey, parcel-boundary identification and area-correction registration?

Procedures relating to the boundary are divided among qualified professionals. A confirmed boundary survey that measures the boundary in the presence of the neighbouring owners and prepares a parcel-boundary confirmation and a land-area survey map (whose recorded matters are set by Art. 77 of the Regulation for Real Property Registration — the lot number, direction, scale, area and its calculation method, distances between boundary points, coordinate values, indication of boundary markers, and so on), and an area-correction registration that corrects the registered area, are the work of a land and house investigator. Where the neighbour's cooperation cannot be obtained and the parcel boundary is in dispute, you can apply to the Legal Affairs Bureau for parcel-boundary identification (Art. 131(1)). Parcel-boundary identification is made by a parcel-boundary registrar of the Legal Affairs Bureau or District Legal Affairs Bureau having jurisdiction over the location of the target land (Arts. 124, 125), and a parcel-boundary identification document is prepared in light of the opinion of the parcel-boundary examiner (Art. 143).

An ownership-transfer registration or a mortgage-creation registration lies with a judicial scrivener; a dispute over the ownership boundary — the extent of ownership — or representation in a boundary-fixing lawsuit lies with a lawyer; and tax on transfer and acquisition (including withholding where the seller is a non-resident) lies with a tax accountant. ## How do a measured-area sale and a registered-area sale differ, and how is an area difference settled?

A sale of land can be a "measured-area sale," which sets the price on the area measured before handover, or a "registered-area sale," which fixes the price on the registered area. In a measured-area sale, the difference between the measured area obtained in the confirmed survey and the registered area is customarily settled at the unit price set in the contract. In a registered-area sale, the premise is that the price is not increased or decreased even if there is a difference from the measured area. Which is adopted changes the cost burden, timing and location of the area-difference risk of the confirmed boundary survey, so make it clear in the contract clauses.

Even in a registered-area sale, a large divergence between the registered and measured area can work against the buyer at the stage of resale, subdivision or security valuation, so confirm the presence of the land-area survey map and the date of the survey before acquisition. For land that has only an old land-area survey map, or none, it is safer to have a land and house investigator confirm consistency with the current state. The unit price and cap of area-difference settlement, and the treatment where the confirmed survey is not completed by handover, are organised by the broker within the contract and important-matters explanation. The survey and registration themselves lie with a land and house investigator and a judicial scrivener.

| Method | How the price is set | Treatment of area difference |
|---|---|---|
| Measured-area sale | Measured area × unit price | Settle the difference from registered area at unit price |
| Registered-area sale | Fixed on the registered area | In principle no increase/decrease |
| Common check | Presence of land-area survey map; survey date | Have a land and house investigator confirm current-state consistency |

## What drawings and documents should be checked by handover?

In the practice of the buyer's DD, assemble the following drawings and documents before acquisition and check them against the current state: the land-area survey map (meeting the recorded matters of Art. 77 of the Regulation for Real Property Registration), the confirmed survey map, the parcel-boundary confirmation signed by the neighbouring owners, the presence of on-site boundary markers (stone or metal pins), and a current-state survey map showing any encroachment. Where these are missing or old, set in the contract whether to make a confirmed survey at the seller's cost a condition of handover, or to proceed as a registered-area sale with no area-difference settlement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How do the parcel boundary and the ownership boundary differ?**
A. The parcel boundary is the points and lines deemed to constitute the border of a parcel when it was registered — a public registered boundary (Art. 123(i)) that cannot be moved by agreement alone. The ownership boundary is a private-law boundary of how far ownership extends, and it can diverge from the parcel boundary through prescription or agreement. A parcel-boundary dispute is handled by parcel-boundary identification at the Legal Affairs Bureau (Art. 131(1)) or a boundary-fixing lawsuit; an ownership-boundary dispute is handled by a lawyer as a civil matter.

**Q. What becomes a problem if I buy with the boundary unfixed?**
A. An area difference between registered and measured, encroachment with the neighbour, and a burden of being required to fix the boundary at subdivision, security creation or resale may surface after handover. A change in area carries an application duty for a change registration within one month (Art. 37), and an error or omission in the register is corrected by a correction registration — an area-correction registration (Art. 2(xvi)). Confirm the presence of a confirmed survey map, a parcel-boundary confirmation and a land-area survey map before acquisition.

**Q. Who should I ask for the confirmed survey or the area-correction registration?**
A. The confirmed boundary survey in the presence of neighbours, the preparation of the parcel-boundary confirmation and land-area survey map, and the area-correction registration are the work of a land and house investigator. Where the neighbour's cooperation cannot be obtained and the parcel boundary is in dispute, you can apply for parcel-boundary identification at the Legal Affairs Bureau (Art. 131(1)), and a parcel-boundary registrar prepares the parcel-boundary identification document (Art. 143). The ownership-transfer and mortgage registrations lie with a judicial scrivener, disputes with a lawyer and tax with a tax accountant, engaged separately as independent entities.

**Q. Which is more favourable to the buyer, a measured-area sale or a registered-area sale?**
A. It cannot be said uniformly. A measured-area sale settles the difference between the measured area from the confirmed survey and the registered area at a unit price, while a registered-area sale fixes the price even if there is a difference. Because the cost burden, timing and location of the area-difference risk of the confirmed survey change by method, make it clear in the contract clauses. Even in a registered-area sale, a large divergence between registered and measured can be unfavourable at resale or security valuation, so confirm the presence of the land-area survey map and the survey date before acquisition.
