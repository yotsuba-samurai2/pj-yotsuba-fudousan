**The key point**: Under the Important Land Survey Act, if a property sits in a "Special Monitored Area" and is at or above a set size—land of 200 m² or more per parcel, a building of 200 m² or more in total floor area—a prior notification must be filed before concluding a contract to transfer or set ownership and similar rights, and it is the parties to the contract, the seller and the buyer both, who file it in advance with the Prime Minister (the window is the Cabinet Office) (Act on the Review and Regulation of the Use of Real Estate Surrounding Important Facilities and on National Border Remote Islands, etc. = Act No. 84 of 2021, art. 13(1); Enforcement Order art. 4). A plain "Monitored Area," by contrast, carries no notification duty at the point of sale; there the state surveys how land is used and may issue recommendations and orders against acts that impede an important facility's function (art. 6, art. 9). Concluding a contract without notifying, or filing a false notification, is punishable by up to 6 months' confinement or a fine of up to ¥1 million (art. 26). What a real-estate company (we) handle is checking—from the published designation and the Cabinet Office list—whether the property falls in a Monitored / Special Monitored Area and, if so, whether it meets the size threshold, providing that in the important-matters explanation (Real Estate Brokerage Act art. 35), and brokerage. The notification itself is for the party or an administrative scrivener; we do not file it. ## What is the Important Land Survey Act, and since when has it been fully in force?

The Act's full name is the "Act on the Review and Regulation of the Use of Real Estate Surrounding Important Facilities and on National Border Remote Islands, etc." (Act No. 84 of 2021). It was promulgated on 23 June 2021 and came fully into force on 20 September 2022. Around defense-related facilities, Japan Coast Guard facilities, life-related facilities such as nuclear power plants, and national border islands, it lets the state survey how land and buildings are used, and regulate that use if necessary, to prevent them being used in ways that impede a facility's function.

The Act sets its regulated areas in two tiers: the "Monitored Area" (art. 5) and, within it, the "Special Monitored Area" designated for the most important of them (art. 12). What changes most for brokerage practice is that only the Special Monitored Area carries a "prior notification" at the point of a transaction.

| Area | Basis | What the state does | Prior notification on a deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monitored Area | art. 5 (roughly within 1,000 m of an important facility's site; border islands) | survey of use (art. 6); recommendations/orders against function-impeding acts (art. 9) | none |
| Special Monitored Area | art. 12 (a Monitored Area tied to a designated important facility / border island) | same as above, plus receiving and reviewing notifications of deals above a set size (art. 13) | yes (both seller and buyer) |

Both areas are designated by the Prime Minister after hearing the Real Estate Use Council, and take effect when published in the Official Gazette. Which places are designated is published each time and listed on the Cabinet Office "Important Land Survey Act" page.

## How do you check whether your property is in a Monitored / Special Monitored Area?

Whether an area applies is fixed objectively from the property's address. A Monitored Area is an area, designated and published by the government, that lies roughly within 1,000 m of an important facility's site or within a border-island area (art. 5). A Special Monitored Area is designated where, among Monitored Areas, the facility's or island's function is especially important, or where impeding it is easy and substitution is difficult (art. 12).

The order of checking is below. The objective property-side condition—whether the address falls within a published area—is what we survey and provide in the important-matters explanation.

| What to confirm | Where |
|---|---|
| Whether any designated area exists and which one the property falls in | Cabinet Office "Important Land Survey Act" area list and the Official Gazette |
| The area boundary against the property's position | Compare the published maps/area diagrams with the property's address and lot number |
| If a Special Monitored Area, whether the size threshold is met | Land area (land) / floor area (building) on the registration certificate |
| Where to inquire when unsure | The Cabinet Office call centre (number on the official page); the municipality |

Designations are added and changed, so note the date you consulted. The final judgment on whether an area exists and its boundary belongs to the Cabinet Office; we handle the fact-check against the published information.

## In a Special Monitored Area, who notifies what and when?

Where you conclude a contract (including a pre-contract) to transfer or set ownership or a right aimed at acquiring ownership over land etc. of a certain size or more inside a Special Monitored Area, the parties to the contract must notify certain matters to the Prime Minister in advance (art. 13(1)). The "parties" are both seller and buyer, not one of them. As a rule the notification is made before the contract is concluded.

The matters notified include each party's name and address (and nationality etc. for non-Japanese), the location and area of the land etc., the type and content of the right transferred/set, and the intended use of the land. The window is the Cabinet Office; by post it must arrive by the day before the planned contract date.

As exceptions, contracts specified by Cabinet Order—such as where the counterparty is the state or a local government—fall outside the notification. Where the contract arises from grounds specified by Cabinet Order, such as civil conciliation, the prior-notification rule does not apply (art. 13(2)); in that case you notify within two weeks of the day the contract is concluded (art. 13(3)). Land etc. inside a plain Monitored Area is not subject to this transaction notification at all.

## Where is the line on the area or the deal that triggers notification?

Even in a Special Monitored Area, not every small piece of land etc. triggers notification. It applies to land of 200 m² or more, and a building of 200 m² or more counting the total floor area of each storey including annexes (art. 13(1); Enforcement Order art. 4). This area is judged per parcel for land and per building for buildings, even when several are sold together.

| The line | Content |
|---|---|
| Area | Inside a Special Monitored Area (a Monitored Area is out of scope) |
| Size | Land ≥ 200 m² / building ≥ 200 m² total floor area (judged per parcel / per building) |
| Contract | A contract to transfer or set ownership etc. (sale, exchange, pre-contract, etc.) |
| Timing | Before concluding the contract (the civil-conciliation exception: within two weeks after) |

Note that while the land area and floor area that decide the threshold can be confirmed by us from objective property-side information such as the registration certificate, the final judgment on which land/building those figures correspond to inside the area is fixed against the Cabinet Office that receives the notification. Where boundaries or actual survey are involved, a land/house surveyor's survey may be a premise.

## When the buyer is foreign capital or a non-resident, what extra care does the deal need?

The prior notification under the Important Land Survey Act applies to both parties whenever the Special Monitored Area and the size threshold are met, regardless of the buyer's nationality or residence. Whether the buyer is foreign capital, a non-resident, or Japan-resident, this notification duty itself does not change. The framing is "notify because it is a deal of a certain size in a Special Monitored Area," not "notify because the buyer is foreign."

At the same time, when the buyer is a non-resident, other systems overlap the same deal apart from this notification—for example the broker's identity verification under the Act on Prevention of Transfer of Criminal Proceeds, the post-facto report of inward direct investment etc. under the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act, and each country's rules on the international remittance of the price. These have a different basis and window from the Important Land Survey Act here, and get missed if confused with it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. When selling or buying property in a Monitored Area, is notification required?**
A. No prior notification at the point of sale. The prior notification applies only to land etc. of a certain size or more (land ≥ 200 m², building ≥ 200 m² total floor area) inside a Special Monitored Area; land etc. inside a plain Monitored Area is not subject to it (Important Land Survey Act art. 13(1); Enforcement Order art. 4). Even in a Monitored Area, though, the state surveys use and may issue recommendations/orders against acts that impede an important facility's function (art. 6, art. 9). Which area a property falls in is confirmed on the Cabinet Office area list and the Official Gazette.

**Q. For a Special Monitored Area notification, does the seller or the buyer file it?**
A. Both the seller and the buyer, as parties to the contract, file it (art. 13(1)). One of them is not enough. The notification is made to the Prime Minister (the Cabinet Office window) before the contract is concluded. Where the contract arises from grounds specified by Cabinet Order such as civil conciliation, the prior-notification rule does not apply by way of exception, and you then notify within two weeks of the contract date (art. 13(2), (3)). Preparing and filing the notification is for the party or an administrative scrivener; a real-estate company (we) does not file it.

**Q. What happens if you conclude a contract without notifying?**
A. Concluding a contract without the art. 13(1) notification, failing to make the art. 13(3) notification, or filing a false notification is punishable by up to 6 months' confinement or a fine of up to ¥1 million (art. 26). Where a breach occurs in a corporation's business, the corporation too may be fined besides the actor (art. 28). Separately, breaching a state order in a Monitored Area (art. 9(2)) draws a heavier penalty—up to 2 years' confinement or a fine of up to ¥2 million (art. 25).

**Q. If the buyer is foreign or a non-resident, does the notification change?**
A. The Important Land Survey Act prior notification applies to both parties whenever it is a deal of a certain size in a Special Monitored Area, regardless of the buyer's nationality or residence. The framing is "notify because it is a covered deal in a Special Monitored Area," not "notify because the buyer is foreign." When the buyer is a non-resident, however, other systems overlap the same deal—identity verification under the Criminal Proceeds Act, the inward-direct-investment report under the Foreign Exchange Act, and international-remittance rules. Their basis and window differ, so confirm each separately.
