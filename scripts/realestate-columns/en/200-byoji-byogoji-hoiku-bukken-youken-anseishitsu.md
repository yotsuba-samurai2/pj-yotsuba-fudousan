## Why is a layout that separates a "rest room" from the ordinary childcare room needed in a sick-child room?

Sick-child care is a business defined in Article 6-3, paragraph 13 of the Child Welfare Act, caring for infants and the like who are ill at a nursery, certified childcare centre, hospital, clinic or other facility. The national operating standard is set by the Children and Families Agency's "Sick-Child Care Business Implementation Guideline" (Ko-Sei-Ho No. 180, dated 30 March 2024, applied from 1 April 2024, first amended 31 March 2025). For the place of implementation of the "sick-child response type," the guideline requires meeting the following (a)–(c).

- (a) Having a childcare room and an observation or rest room with a function to rest or isolate the child
- (b) Having a kitchen (a dedicated sick-child kitchen is preferable, but sharing the main facility's kitchen is acceptable)
- (c) A place suited to raising children, with care for accident prevention and hygiene

A rest room (observation room) is set apart from the childcare room so an unwell child can be laid down to rest, their condition watched, and kept from contact with other children. The idea differs from an ordinary nursery's "near-single-room childcare room," and the property too must be checked for whether "a separate room to lay a child down" can be taken in the layout. Note that the national guideline has no numeric area standard for the childcare room, observation room or rest room; some municipalities set their own per-capacity area guides (for example, 1.98 sqm per child for the childcare room, 1.65 sqm per child for the observation or rest room). Because the actual area treatment is decided by the local guideline and manual, this article does not assert a uniform figure and leaves it as unverified.

| Business type | Main target | Idea of property / place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sick-child response type | Children in the "acute phase" | A dedicated space/facility with a childcare room + observation or rest room + kitchen |
| Convalescent-child response type | Children in the "recovery phase" | Temporary care in a dedicated space/facility, as with the sick-child type |
| Unwell-child response type | Children who become unwell during care | Emergency/health response for children attending the nursery (attached to the nursery) |

## Where does the property's use zone, change of use and building confirmation get stuck?

A nursery is listed in the Building Standards Act Appended Table 2 (i) item 6 as "a home for the aged, a nursery, a welfare home and the like," and can be built even in the most restrictive Category I exclusively low-rise residential zone. That is why a sick-child room, set up as a child welfare facility, rarely gets stuck on the use zone. What gets stuck is rather "change of use" and "fire service."

When converting a property that was an office or shop into a sick-child room, which is a child-welfare-facility-class (special building), Article 6, paragraph 1, item 1 of the Building Standards Act makes "a special building in the use listed in column (i) of Appended Table 1, where the total floor area of the part used for that purpose exceeds 200 sqm" subject to a confirmation application, and Article 87 applies this to a change of use. In other words, a conversion where that use part exceeds 200 sqm in principle needs a change-of-use confirmation application (except changes among similar uses designated by Cabinet Order). You also need to check whether the building is not non-conforming, whether there is an inspection certificate, and whether two-direction evacuation and interior-finish restrictions are met. These building-law judgments and confirmation applications are an architect's field; we do not act for them. Where attached to a hospital or clinic, it is treated as a medical use, and the way of looking at the property changes again.

## What does the property need for the equipment to care for an infectious child (isolation, cooking, hand-washing, ventilation)?

A sick-child room cares for children in the acute or recovery phase and children who become unwell during care, so a design that does not spread infection is the premise. The guideline contemplates isolation and the like where children with different infectious diseases are cared for in the same time slot, and the rest room (observation room) carries this isolation function. On the property side, what to check is whether the observation/rest room can be partitioned from the childcare room, whether multiple hand-washing points can be secured, whether there is a ventilation route, and whether the kitchen can be kept hygienic.

A kitchen is said to be preferably dedicated to sick-child care, while sharing the main facility's kitchen is acceptable. Whether water supply/drainage, air-conditioning or ventilation needs extending is a matter of building equipment, and the feasibility of works and the confirmation application are judged by an architect. Medical judgments—methods of disinfection, health response, which infectious child to isolate how—are the field of nurses and physicians, not of real estate or building. We organise the property-side fact of "whether that layout can secure partitioning, hand-washing and ventilation," connecting equipment works to an architect and the hygiene operation to medical professionals.

## How far does a hospital's attachment or cooperation affect the property choice (who should you consult)?

The guideline sets the place of implementation of the sick-child and convalescent-child types as "a dedicated space attached to a hospital/clinic or nursery, or a dedicated facility for this business." There is an attached-to-a-hospital/clinic form, an attached-to-a-nursery form, and an independent dedicated-facility form, and which you choose changes how you look at the property's use and location. A paediatric clinic adjacent or attached makes a sudden change in condition easier to handle, while a dedicated-facility type gives more weight to the operational design of securing a cooperating medical institution in advance.

On staffing, the guideline requires placing "a nurse and the like (a nurse, assistant nurse, public health nurse or midwife) in charge of nursing the sick child, at least one per roughly 10 children using the service" and "a childcare worker at least one per roughly 3 children." Whether such a staffing system can be built, which medical institution to cooperate with, and how to design transport in a sudden change of condition are medical-system judgments, the field of a physician and a medical institution. The property's location (distance to the nearest medical institution, ease of transport) affects the property choice, but the feasibility of the cooperation system and the scope of medical care itself are left to qualified persons. We convey the property-side facts of location and layout, referring the medical cooperation to a physician and medical institution.

## How do the property requirements change between the commissioned type and the facility type (unlicensed notification)?

Sick-child care is positioned, by Article 59, item 11 of the Child and Childcare Support Act, as a regional child and childcare support business carried out by the municipality. In practice the municipality often commissions an operator to run it, in which case a commission contract is concluded with the municipality and it is run under a system meeting the guideline standards. On the other hand, if run independently rather than under the municipal business, you need to consider, separately, whether notification of an unlicensed childcare facility under Article 59-2 of the Child Welfare Act is required, as a facility providing childcare. Which form you take changes the required contracts, notifications and submitted documents, but the property-side skeleton—"separating the childcare room from the observation/rest room," "providing a kitchen"—is common.

Administrative procedures such as concluding the commission contract, notifying the unlicensed childcare facility, and preparing documents to the facility standards along the implementation guideline are an administrative scrivener's field; we do not act for them. The necessity and forms of notification differ by municipality and may be revised mid-year, so confirm the actual procedure with the local municipality and an administrative scrivener. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does a sick-child room always need a rest room?**
A. The Children and Families Agency's Sick-Child Care Business Implementation Guideline requires, for the place of the sick-child response type, "having a childcare room and an observation or rest room with a resting or isolation function." It is a room to lay an unwell child down, watch their condition, and separate them from other children. Whether it can be taken separately from the childcare room in the layout is the pivot of the property choice. There is no numeric area standard in the national guideline; it may be set by municipal guidelines, so confirm in the area of implementation.

**Q. When converting an office into a sick-child room, is a change-of-use confirmation always required?**
A. Not uniformly. When the use is changed to a child-welfare-facility class (special building) and the total floor area of that use part exceeds 200 sqm, a confirmation application is in principle required under Building Standards Act Art. 6(1)(i) and Art. 87 (except among similar uses designated by Cabinet Order). Even at 200 sqm or less, fire-service and evacuation checks are needed. An architect judges the necessity and feasibility of the confirmation.

**Q. Is there a place where a nursery cannot be built by use zone?**
A. A nursery is listed in Building Standards Act Appended Table 2 (i) item 6 and can be built even in the most restrictive Category I exclusively low-rise residential zone. A sick-child room set up as a child welfare facility usually rarely gets stuck on the use zone. However, where attached to a hospital or clinic it is treated as a medical use and the way of looking at the property changes. We check the current use.

**Q. Can I consult a real estate company about nurse staffing or the cooperating medical institution?**
A. We organise the property's location (distance to the nearest medical institution) and layout, but how many nurses to place, which medical institution to cooperate with, and the response to a sudden change in condition are medical-system judgments, the field of a physician and medical institution. The guideline sets nurses at least one per roughly 10 children and childcare workers at least one per roughly 3, but building the system is for qualified persons.
