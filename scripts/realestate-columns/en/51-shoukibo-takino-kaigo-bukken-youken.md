**In short:** small-scale multifunctional home care (shotaki) cannot be run in just any property. Four things narrow the field. First, whether overnight rooms can be secured — enough rooms of single occupancy and 7.43 m² or more. Second, whether the building's size can meet the scale: registered capacity of 29 or fewer, commuting up to 18 a day, overnight up to 9 a day. Third, whether that use zone and building classification allow the use — it cannot go in an exclusive industrial zone, and because it has an overnight function the use classification must be checked. Fourth, whether the fire equipment (sprinklers, automatic fire alarm) that the overnight function calls for can be installed. Unlike an ordinary care office, the "overnight" makes the property conditions a notch stricter. Much of this can be narrowed from the documents before any viewing.

## Why does a small-scale multifunctional home-care property need an overnight room?

Because it is a service that centres on "commuting" and combines "visiting" and "overnight stay" as needed. That "overnight" is the decisive difference from day-service offices when choosing a property.

Small-scale multifunctional home care is defined in Article 8, paragraph 19 of the Long-Term Care Insurance Act (Act No. 123 of 1997): centring on "commuting" to the office, combined with short "overnight stays" and "visits" to the user's home, providing bathing, toileting, meals and other daily-living care and functional training according to the user's condition and wishes. It is one of the "community-based services" in Article 8, paragraph 14, used in principle by residents of the municipality where the office is located, and it is the mayor — not the prefectural governor — who grants the designation (Article 78-2, paragraph 1).

Seen from the property side, on top of the living room, dining room and kitchen for "commuting", an overnight room is needed for "overnight stay" — and that is what differs from day care. Article 67 of the Ministerial Ordinance on Standards for Personnel, Facilities and Operation of Designated Community-Based Services (Ministerial Ordinance No. 34 of 2006) requires the office to have a living room, dining room, kitchen, overnight room, bathroom, fire-extinguishing equipment and other equipment needed in a disaster. **Whether such an overnight room exists is the first gate in choosing a property.**

## How do registered capacity and commuting/overnight capacity bear on the building's size?

The number of overnight rooms and the size of the living/dining room cap how many people can be accepted. First, fix the capacity framework.

Article 66 of the same standard sets the registered capacity (the cap on the number of registered users) of a designated small-scale multifunctional home-care office at 29 or fewer (18 or fewer for a satellite type). On that basis, it sets the daily commuting and overnight capacity within the following ranges.

| Category | Daily user capacity | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Registered capacity | 29 or fewer (satellite type 18 or fewer) | Ministerial Ordinance No. 34 of 2006, Art. 66 |
| Commuting service | Half of registered capacity up to 15; up to 16 at registered 26-27, 17 at 28, 18 at 29 | Same, Art. 66 |
| Overnight service | One-third of the commuting capacity up to 9 | Same, Art. 66 |

For the overnight room, Article 67 sets occupancy at one person (two is allowed where needed for the user's care) and floor area at 7.43 m² or more. The living and dining room is "of a suitable size to fully perform its function" — judged not by a fixed number but by "can the commuting users spend time there".

Read back into the property — **to accept up to 9 overnight, you need enough space to secure nine overnight rooms of 7.43 m² or more each (assuming no shared rooms).** In a second-hand house or tenancy, whether the layout can be renovated to this number of overnight rooms and the size of the living/dining room caps how many people can be accepted.

## How far can a house or tenancy in a residential area be used under the use zone and Building Standards Act?

Because it has an overnight function, the use classification is somewhat more complex than a day-service office. This is a point to confirm with the specified administrative authority and an architect.

On use zones, because a small-scale multifunctional home-care office has an overnight function, it may be treated under the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950) as a "dormitory" or as a "child welfare facility, etc." Which one it is changes the permitted use zones and the required measures. What holds in common is that **in an exclusive industrial zone, dwellings, dormitories and child welfare facilities cannot be built, so a shotaki office cannot go there either.** Whether it can be used elsewhere depends on how the building is classified, so before committing, confirm with the municipality's city-planning and building-guidance sections and an architect.

When converting an existing building, check whether a change-of-use confirmation application is required. Article 87, paragraph 1 applies the confirmation procedure where a building's use is changed into one of the special buildings under Article 6, paragraph 1, item 1, which covers "special buildings used for a purpose listed in column (i) of Appended Table 1, where the total floor area for that purpose exceeds 200 m²".

| Floor area used for that purpose | Change-of-use confirmation application |
|---|---|
| 200 m² or less | In principle not required |
| Over 200 m² | Required (Building Standards Act Art. 87 (1) and Art. 6 (1) (i)) |

But — **"no confirmation application" and "no need to comply with the Act" are two different things.** Article 87, paragraph 2 applies Article 48 (use-zone restrictions) and the evacuation and fire-prevention provisions to any change of use; substantive requirements — daylighting, smoke exhaust, means of escape, interior-finish restrictions — apply regardless. The use classification, and the requirement and design of a change of use, belong to architects and designated confirmation and inspection bodies; we handle obtaining and delivering the property documents (registration records, drawings and so on).

## With an overnight function, how does the fire equipment (automatic fire alarm, sprinklers) change?

Because there is "overnight", the fire-equipment burden can be heavier than for a day-service. This ties directly to the property's renovation cost.

Small-scale multifunctional home care falls within category (6)-ro or (6)-ha of Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order (Cabinet Order No. 37 of 1961). Which one applies is judged by how many residents need help to evacuate (whether it mainly houses people who find self-evacuation difficult). If it is (6)-ro — a social-welfare facility housing people who find self-evacuation difficult — the fire-equipment burden grows.

Sprinklers used to be required for (6)-ro only from a total floor area of 275 m². An amendment (in force 1 April 2015) removed that area threshold, so **(6)-ro in principle now requires sprinklers regardless of total floor area.** A special exception remains where the total floor area is under 275 m² and the building meets requirements such as a fire-spread-suppressing structure.

| Category | Sprinklers |
|---|---|
| (6)-ro, 275 m² or more | Required |
| (6)-ro, under 275 m² | In principle required; exempt if it meets a fire-spread-suppressing structure and other requirements |
| (6)-ha | Required at 275 m² or more, etc. (varies by category and area) |

Category (6)-ro also calls for an automatic fire alarm system, a fire alarm that reports to the fire authority, fire extinguishers and the like, depending on area and other factors. **When converting a second-hand house or small tenancy, this sprinkler and alarm cost can be a large part of the opening budget**, so consult the fire station with jurisdiction before you commit, to establish what that building actually needs. The categorisation and equipment design belong to the fire station and fire-protection engineers; we do not judge them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does small-scale multifunctional home care always need an overnight room?**
A. Yes. It is a service that centres on "commuting" and combines "visiting" and "overnight stay", and Article 67 of the Ministerial Ordinance on Standards for Personnel, Facilities and Operation of Designated Community-Based Services (Ministerial Ordinance No. 34 of 2006) requires equipment including an overnight room. The overnight room is single occupancy (two allowed where needed for care) and 7.43 m² or more. Unlike day care, whether an overnight room can be secured is the first gate in choosing a property.

**Q. How does the registered capacity of 29 relate to the building's size?**
A. Registered capacity is 29 or fewer (satellite type 18 or fewer); daily commuting is half the registered capacity up to 18, and overnight is one-third of the commuting capacity up to 9 (Art. 66). From the property side, how many overnight rooms of 7.43 m² or more can be secured caps the overnight number, and the size of the living/dining room caps commuting. Size determines the acceptance cap.

**Q. Can it be opened in a house in a residential area?**
A. It depends on the use zone and the building's classification. Because shotaki has an overnight function, it may be treated under the Building Standards Act as a "dormitory" or a "child welfare facility, etc." It cannot go in an exclusive industrial zone; elsewhere it depends on the classification. If, in converting an existing building, the floor area used for that purpose exceeds 200 m², a change-of-use confirmation application is required (Art. 87 (1)). Confirm the classification and design with an architect and the specified administrative authority.

**Q. With an overnight function, are sprinklers always required?**
A. Shotaki is category (6)-ro or (6)-ha of Appended Table 1 of the Fire Service Act Enforcement Order, and the category is judged by how many residents need help to evacuate. For (6)-ro, the amendment in force on 1 April 2015 made sprinklers in principle required regardless of total floor area (with an exception under 275 m² where a fire-spread-suppressing structure and other requirements are met). Because the category and the requirement vary by building, consult the fire station with jurisdiction first.
