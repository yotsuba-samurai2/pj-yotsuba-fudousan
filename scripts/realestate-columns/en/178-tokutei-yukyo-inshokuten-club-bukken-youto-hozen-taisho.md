## What is a specified entertainment restaurant, and how does it differ from "hospitality" (hostess clubs) and an ordinary restaurant?

Even among "night-time restaurants," a different category under the Entertainment Business Act changes both the premises required of the property and where you apply. Fixing which category your own business falls into is the starting point of property selection.

Article 2(11) of the Entertainment Business Act defines a "specified entertainment restaurant business" as a business — a nightclub or other business with fixtures that lets customers enjoy entertainment and serves them food and drink (limited to those serving alcohol) — other than one operated only in the hours from after 6 a.m. to before midnight. In short: a specified entertainment restaurant lets customers enjoy entertainment such as dancing or shows, serves alcohol, and extends into late night (after midnight). A hostess/host club with "hospitality" is an "entertainment business (hospitality food-and-drink business)" under art. 2(1)(i), which is a different licence. A bar that offers no hospitality and no late-night entertainment, merely serving alcohol and food, falls neither under a specified entertainment restaurant nor an entertainment business, and stays within the scope of a restaurant business.

| Example business | Entertainment | Hospitality | Late night (after midnight) | Category |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Club / lounge / dance bar | Yes | No | Yes | Specified entertainment restaurant (art. 2(11)) |
| Hostess / host club | — | Yes | — | Entertainment business = hospitality food-and-drink (art. 2(1)(i)) |
| Late-night bar (no hospitality/entertainment) | No | No | Yes | Late-night alcohol-serving restaurant (notification) |
| Daytime café / restaurant | No | No | No | Restaurant business (licence only) |

"Letting customers enjoy entertainment" is understood to mean the operator actively entertaining customers (letting unspecified customers dance, showing shows or live music, letting unspecified customers sing with karaoke equipment, etc.). Because which category applies is judged by the actual state of the business, the order is to confirm your category against the police station's community-safety division or an administrative scrivener before you look for a property. ## In which areas can a licence be obtained (zoning and the "permitted area for business premises")?

What most strongly governs viability for a specified entertainment restaurant is the area regulation. Note that this regulation has two layers.

One is Building Standards Act zoning — a building-side restriction on "whether a restaurant can be placed in that building." Restaurants are allowed in relatively broad zones, though floor area can limit this. The other is the Entertainment Business Act's "permitted area for business premises," which is the strict restriction specific to specified entertainment restaurants. Article 4(2)(ii), applied via art. 31-23, provides that where the premises are not within an area that the prefectural ordinance designates — following the Cabinet Order standard — as an area where establishment is specifically permitted because it would not impair a sound morals environment, no licence may be granted. Whereas an entertainment business (hospitality) is "in principle anywhere, but not in the ordinance's restricted areas" (a subtraction), a specified entertainment restaurant is "operable only in an area the ordinance has designated as permitted" (an addition, i.e., opt-in). This difference is decisive.

Article 22 of the Enforcement Order sets the standard for areas that may be designated as permitted. Roughly: (1) it must be a dense entertainment-business district, or an area where, late at night, at most roughly 100 persons per square kilometre reside; and (2) it must not be a housing-clustered area, an area comparable to it that needs special care for late-night morals environment, an area adjacent to those, or around a protected facility. As a result, permitted areas are limited to entertainment districts centred on commercial zones, and places near housing are the first to be excluded.

| Character of the area | Could it be a permitted area for a specified entertainment restaurant? |
|---|---|
| Entertainment district in a commercial zone (dense entertainment district, etc.) | Possible (if the ordinance designates it as a permitted area) |
| Area thinly populated late at night | Possible (if it meets the Enforcement Order art. 22 population-density guide) |
| Housing-clustered area / comparable area | In principle no |
| Around a protected facility (within roughly 100 m of the site) | In principle no |

Permitted areas are drawn concretely by prefectural ordinance, and even within the same city one street can divide inside from outside. Whether a property is inside that designated area should be confirmed with the police station with jurisdiction (public safety commission) or the prefecture's division (we do not conclude applicability at the lot level here). ## How is the distance from protected facilities (schools, hospitals, etc.) measured?

Among the elements that exclude an area from being permitted, the one that bites at the lot level is the distance from protected facilities. Article 22 of the Enforcement Order provides that, in designating a permitted area, areas within a zone of up to roughly 100 metres around the site of a protected facility are, in principle, excluded from the permitted area.

A "protected facility" here is limited to those the prefectural ordinance designates as facilities needing protection of a sound late-night morals environment around them. Generally, schools, hospitals, clinics, libraries and child-welfare facilities are listed as protected facilities, but which facilities are covered, how many metres the distance is, and at what point it is judged (whether limited to clinics with beds, etc.) differ by ordinance. Distance is usually measured as the shortest distance between the site of the protected facility and the site of the premises, commonly in a straight line even if buildings or roads lie between — but the start/end points also need confirmation under the ordinance and practice.

| Check item | General content | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Type of protected facility | Schools, hospitals, clinics, libraries, child-welfare facilities, etc. (designated by ordinance) | Police station with jurisdiction / prefectural ordinance |
| Standard distance | Up to roughly 100 metres around the site (Enforcement Order art. 22) | Police station / ordinance |
| How distance is measured | Shortest / straight-line distance between sites is common | Police station |
| Planned new facilities | Whether a school, child-welfare facility, etc. is planned nearby | Municipal urban-planning / child-welfare division |

Note that protected facilities can include "land decided to be used for that purpose," and that an ordinance amendment or a new facility can change the situation after you contract. Even with no protected facility right in front of the property, a facility a little away that the ordinance covers and that falls within range can exclude the area from being permitted. ## What property meets the standards for room floor area and view-blocking structure?

Even if you clear the area conditions, the structure/equipment standards for the building interior (the room) come next. Article 4(2)(i), applied via art. 31-23, provides no licence where the structure/equipment of the premises fails to meet the technical standards set by National Public Safety Commission regulation, and that standard is specified by art. 75 of the Enforcement Regulations.

Article 75 lists these technical standards for the premises of a specified entertainment restaurant: (1) the floor area of a room shall be 33 m² or more per room; (2) no fixtures that block the view inside the room shall be installed; (3) no photographs, advertisements, decorations or other fixtures that harm good morals or a sound morals environment, or impair the sound upbringing of minors, shall be installed; (4) no lock shall be installed on a room door (except a door leading directly outside the premises); (5) the structure/equipment shall maintain the illumination inside the premises not falling to 10 lux or below; and (6) the structure/equipment shall maintain noise and vibration below the values set by ordinance.

| Standard (Enforcement Regulations art. 75) | Content | Focus in property selection |
|---|---|---|
| Room floor area | A room of 33 m² or more per room | Cannot be chopped into small partitions; is a single room of that size achievable? |
| View | No view-blocking fixtures inside the room | No tall partitions or private rooms; affects layout freedom |
| Locks | No lock on a room door | No private-room type that locks from inside |
| Illumination | Maintained not below 10 lux | Sets the dimming floor; affects lighting design |
| Noise / vibration | Structure/equipment meeting the ordinance values | Is there room for sound/vibration insulation work? |

The point is that the combination of "one room of 33 m² or more" and "no view-blocking" sits poorly with properties premised on small private rooms. If the existing layout is finely partitioned, removing walls becomes a premise, and you must confirm with an architect whether that is structurally possible (whether they are principal structural parts). At the viewing stage we form a first read from the floor plan of whether one room of 33 m² can be secured and whether a non-view-blocking layout is possible, and organise the flow of confirming conformity of structure/equipment and renovation design with an administrative scrivener / an architect.

## Will operating hours (late night) and neighbourhood noise regulation knock the property out?

A specified entertainment restaurant presumes late-night operation, but not without limit. Time and noise regulations can, depending on location, effectively knock a property out.

On operating hours, art. 31-23 does not apply the late-night operation ban for entertainment businesses (art. 13(1)), so a specified entertainment restaurant is built to operate late at night. On the other hand, art. 24 of the Enforcement Order sets the standard by which a prefecture may restrict operating hours by ordinance: it may designate, as hours in which operation is prohibited late at night, a time within 5 a.m. to 6 a.m., or designate a time within the band from after 6 a.m. to 10 a.m., restricting the early-morning band. Because how late you can operate changes by area, you must check the ordinance of the property's location.

On noise and vibration, art. 25 of the Enforcement Order provides that noise from late-night operation is at or below the values per area, and vibration within a range not exceeding 55 decibels as set by ordinance. Article 75 of the Enforcement Regulations above also makes structure/equipment that can maintain noise and vibration below the ordinance values a licence requirement. In other words, a property near housing or protected facilities is not only excluded from being a permitted area but also, even where it meets the area conditions, tends to carry a heavy burden for sound/vibration insulation.

| Point | Content | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Operating-hour restriction | Ordinance may restrict the early-morning band (within 5–6 a.m., or after 6 a.m. to 10 a.m.) (Enforcement Order art. 24) | Police station / prefectural ordinance |
| Noise value | Noise from late-night operation at or below the per-area value (Enforcement Order art. 25) | Police station / municipal environment division |
| Vibration value | Set by ordinance within a range not exceeding 55 decibels (Enforcement Order art. 25) | Police station / municipal environment division |
| Neighbourhood | Whether the floor above / adjacent is housing; room for soundproofing | Management company / architect |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I have a hostess-club (hospitality) licence, is it fine to let customers dance at a club?**
A. Generally, a hostess club with hospitality is an entertainment business (hospitality food-and-drink) under art. 2(1)(i), while a club/lounge/dance bar that lets customers enjoy entertainment and serves alcohol and food late at night is a specified entertainment restaurant under art. 2(11) — a different licence. Which applies is judged by the actual state of the business: presence of hospitality, presence of entertainment, operating hours. Changing the business form may require obtaining the applicable licence anew. Confirm which category your business falls into against the police station's community-safety division or an administrative scrivener before you look for a property. We do not conclude the category or the need for a licence; we organise the property-side checks and the referral to an administrative scrivener.

**Q. If the property is in a commercial zone, can I get a specified-entertainment-restaurant licence?**
A. Commercial zoning is only one building-side premise and does not by itself mean a licence can be obtained. A specified entertainment restaurant cannot be licensed unless it is within the "permitted area for business premises" that the prefecture designates by ordinance (art. 4(2)(ii) applied via art. 31-23; Enforcement Order art. 22), and the permitted area is further limited within commercial zones, dividing inside from outside by a single street. Whether a property is inside that designated area and not near a protected facility should be confirmed with the police station (public safety commission) or the prefecture's division. We match published materials against the property's location for a first read, and organise the flow of confirming final applicability with an administrative scrivener / the police station.

**Q. How far from a school or hospital is enough?**
A. Article 22 of the Enforcement Order provides that, in designating a permitted area for a specified entertainment restaurant, areas within a zone of up to roughly 100 metres around the site of a protected facility are, in principle, excluded from the permitted area. However, which facilities are protected (schools, hospitals, clinics, libraries, child-welfare facilities, etc.), how many metres the distance is, and how it is measured are set by prefectural ordinance and differ by locality. "Land decided to be used for that purpose" can also be covered. Confirm the specific applicability with the ordinance of the property's location and the police station.
