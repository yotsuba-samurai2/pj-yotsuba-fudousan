## Even with a seizure registration left on it, can inherited real estate be sold?

The contract and registration procedures themselves are possible, but handing it over as is is usually avoided. Because an heir succeeds, from the time the inheritance begins, to all rights and duties that belonged to the decedent's property (Civil Code Art. 896), a seizure registration placed on the decedent, and the delinquent tax or debt, are carried over as they are. Even with a seizure registration, ownership lies with the heir, and the law does not forbid concluding a sale contract and registering the transfer of ownership itself.

The problem is the disposition-restricting effect of the seizure. A buyer who acquires ownership by sale after the seizure cannot stand against the seizing creditor or the seizure procedure, and if an auction proceeds afterwards, the buyer loses ownership. So neither buyers nor banks can usually buy a property with a seizure left on it. In practice, you build the deal in this order: establish a prospect that the seizure can be removed; at settlement, pay off from the price to have the seizure released or withdrawn; receive the removal registration; and only then hand over. It is the same thinking as selling a property with a mortgage left on it.

| Point | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Attribution of ownership | Succeeded to the heir (Civil Code Art. 896); ownership exists despite the seizure registration |
| Contract / transfer registration | The procedures themselves are possible |
| Disposition-restricting effect of seizure | Buyer cannot stand against the procedure; may lose ownership at auction |
| Order in practice | Pay off from price → release/withdraw seizure → remove → hand over |

The outlook on whether it can be sold varies with the seized-claim amount, the expected sale price and the heirs' intentions. Negotiation or disputes with creditors go to a lawyer, registration to a judicial scrivener, and the arrangement of the sale to us.

## How do a seizure by delinquency disposition, a seizure by civil execution, and a provisional seizure differ?

The governing law differs, and so does the way to remove them. Broadly, they split into a seizure by tax-delinquency disposition, a seizure by civil execution based on a title of obligation such as a final judgment (compulsory auction), and a provisional seizure that secures future execution before the main case. The registry records "seizure" or "provisional seizure," and the treatment splits by who (the tax office / municipality, or the court) seized it.

A delinquency-disposition seizure is carried out when, among others, the national tax subject to the demand is not fully paid by the day on which 10 days have passed from the day the demand letter was issued (National Tax Collection Act Art. 47); real estate is seized by service of the seizure document, and the district director of the tax office commissions the seizure registration (Art. 68). A civil-execution seizure is where the court declares, in the commencement order for a compulsory auction, that it seizes the target real estate (Civil Execution Act Art. 45), and the seizure registration is commissioned (Art. 48). A provisional seizure is issued where, for a monetary claim, there is a risk that compulsory execution will become impossible, etc. (Civil Provisional Remedies Act Art. 20), and the execution of a provisional seizure against real estate is by the method of making a provisional-seizure registration or by compulsory administration (Art. 47(1)).

| Kind | Basis | Line of removal |
|---|---|---|
| Delinquency-disposition seizure | NTC Act Arts. 47, 68 (local taxes: Local Tax Act) | Full payment / deferral → tax authority releases |
| Civil-execution seizure | Civil Execution Act Arts. 45, 48 | Pay the creditor → withdrawal / cancel procedure |
| Provisional seizure | Civil Provisional Remedies Act Arts. 20, 47 | Payment / cancellation / deposit of a release sum, etc. |

Which seizure it is changes the counterpart and the steps. Instalment or deferral of tax is negotiation by the person / with the tax authority; debt-repayment negotiations with creditors and disputing a provisional seizure go to a lawyer; and we support from the angle of the sale outlook.

## How do you build the step of paying off from the sale price at settlement and removing it?

As with removing a mortgage, you build it as a concurrent performance. In the sale contract, make removing the seizure by the time of handover / transfer registration the seller's obligation, and place removal in a suspensive-condition or concurrent-performance relationship so the buyer is not made to pay with the seizure still on. On settlement day, apply the sale price the buyer pays to repay the delinquent tax / debt, receive the release/withdrawal document from the tax authority / creditor, and the judicial scrivener files the removal registration and the transfer-of-ownership registration as consecutive applications.

In a delinquency disposition, the district director of the tax office releases the seizure on full payment, etc. (NTC Act Art. 79) and commissions removal of the seizure registration. In civil execution, when the creditor is paid, the petition is withdrawn and the auction procedure is cancelled, and removal of the seizure registration is commissioned. A provisional seizure is removed by payment of the secured claim, cancellation of the provisional remedy, deposit of a release sum, and so on. Whether the sale amount is enough for the seized-claim amount, and the order of distribution or any shortfall where there are several seizures / mortgages, need to be worked out case by case; the negotiation goes to a lawyer and the execution of the registration to a judicial scrivener. Where the sale price is not expected to pay off the debt in full, the prospect of removal does not stand and the deal may not close.

## Do you need to enter inheritance registration first? Who do you ask?

As a premise for selling, you in principle complete inheritance registration first. A seizure is often placed while still in the decedent's name, and inheritance registration (the transfer of the name by inheritance) is a premise for the transfer-of-ownership registration by sale. Inheritance registration became mandatory from 1 April 2024: an heir who acquired real estate by inheritance must apply within three years from the day they came to know that the inheritance had begun for them and that they had acquired ownership (Real Property Registration Act Art. 76-2). The presence of a seizure does not hinder the inheritance-registration application itself.

The one who can act as agent for the inheritance-registration or seizure-removal registration application is a judicial scrivener. The drafting of the estate-division agreement goes to an administrative scrivener, the inheritance-tax return and the estimate of capital gains on the sale to a tax accountant, and instalment or deferral of the delinquency and debt-repayment negotiations with creditors or disputing a provisional seizure to a lawyer — the hands divide. For inherited real estate with a seizure left on it, the step of matching the order of inheritance registration / removal and the settlement date is the crux, so coordinating with a judicial scrivener and us early prevents rework. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can inherited real estate with a seizure registration not be sold at all?**
A. It is not that it cannot be sold. The contract and transfer-registration procedures themselves are possible, and in practice you pay off the delinquent tax or debt from the sale price at settlement, have the seizure released or withdrawn, and remove it before handing over. But a seizure has a disposition-restricting effect, and handing it over with the seizure left on it risks the buyer losing ownership at auction, so the deal is usually built on removal as a premise. Repayment negotiation goes to a lawyer / tax authority, and registration to a judicial scrivener.

**Q. Do a seizure by tax delinquency and a court seizure / provisional seizure differ in how they are removed?**
A. They do. A delinquency-disposition seizure is released by the district director of the tax office on full payment, etc. (NTC Act Art. 79), who commissions removal of the seizure registration. A civil-execution seizure is removed when the creditor is paid, the petition is withdrawn and the procedure cancelled. A provisional seizure is removed by payment of the secured claim, cancellation of the provisional remedy, deposit of a release sum, and so on. Which it is changes the counterpart and steps, so confirm with a lawyer / the tax authority.

**Q. What happens if the sale price cannot repay the delinquent tax or debt in full?**
A. The prospect of removing the seizure may not stand, and the deal may not close. Where there are several seizures / mortgages, it becomes a matter of the order of distribution and any shortfall, and the negotiation of the repayment amount and whether distribution is possible are the realm of the lawyer / tax authority / court. We provide information from the angle of the expected sale price, but we do not step into judging whether the debt can be paid off in full. We recommend first confirming the current situation with a lawyer / the tax authority.

**Q. Which comes first, inheritance registration or removal of the seizure?**
A. As a premise for selling, the transfer of the name by inheritance (inheritance registration) is in principle done first. A seizure is often placed while still in the decedent's name, and inheritance registration can be applied for even with a seizure on it. Inheritance registration became mandatory from 1 April 2024 and must be applied for within three years from the day the acquisition is known (RP Registration Act Art. 76-2). A judicial scrivener and we coordinate the order of inheritance registration / seizure removal / ownership transfer and the settlement date.
