## When does a self-service 24-hour gym need a "change-of-use confirmation application"?

Changing how an existing building is used is called a "change of use". Not every change of use needs a confirmation application; the requirement turns on area and use.

Building Standards Act art. 87(1) provides that where a building's use is changed into a special building under art. 6(1)(i) (one where the part used for that purpose exceeds 200 m²), the confirmation rules apply. So a confirmation application is in principle required when "the post-change use is a special building in Appended Table 1 column (i)" and "that part exceeds 200 m²". However, changes between the "similar uses" set out in Building Standards Act Enforcement Order art. 137-18 are exempt from a confirmation application.

| Axis of judgment | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Post-change use | Whether it is a special building in Appended Table 1 column (i) | Building Standards Act art. 87(1), art. 6(1)(i), Appended Table 1 |
| Area | Whether the part used for that use exceeds 200 m² in total floor area | Building Standards Act art. 6(1)(i) |
| Similar-use exception | Changes between similar uses under Enforcement Order art. 137-18 need no confirmation | Building Standards Act Enforcement Order art. 137-18 |

This 200 m² threshold was raised from over 100 m² by the reform in Act No. 67 of 2018, in force 25 June 2019. A change of use has a building confirmation but no completion inspection; on finishing the work it is read as a notification to the building official (art. 87(1)). Even at a scale needing no confirmation, the duty to conform to the Building Standards Act itself remains, so whether the evacuation, lighting and fire provisions are met must be checked separately. Whether the use can be changed by interior work alone, and the final judgment on the confirmation requirement and the design, are an architect's and the specified administrative agency's field. Confirmation and use issues when using containers or simple structures are also covered in [confirming building certification and use for container/trailer-house shop properties](https://luck428.com/en/column/container-trailer-house-tenpo-bukken-kenchiku-kakunin-youto).

## How is converting an office or shop into a sports facility treated under the Building Standards Act?

An office is not a special building, but a fitness gym (a sports facility) can fall under Appended Table 1 column (i) item (4), "a bowling alley, ski ground, skating rink, swimming pool or sports practice facility", a special building. Whether it is a "sports practice facility" is judged by the actual use, not the name, so a check with the specified administrative agency and an architect is the premise.

The use-zone restriction must also be checked. Building Standards Act art. 48 and Appended Table 2 set which buildings may be built per use zone, and a sports facility is allowed or not depending on the use zone and scale (it cannot be built in an exclusive industrial zone, for example). The concrete area brackets are set by Appended Table 2 and the Order, so confirm the property's use zone with an architect and the specified administrative agency.

| Item to confirm | What happens office→gym | Where to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Falling under a special building | A sports facility can fall under Appended Table 1 item (4). Over 200 m² means a change-of-use confirmation | Architect / specified administrative agency |
| Use-zone restriction | Art. 48 and Appended Table 2 allow or bar it by use zone and scale | Architect / specified administrative agency |
| Existing non-conformity / retroactivity | The range of evacuation, fire, etc. provisions required with the change of use | Architect |
| Presence of a completion certificate | Without one, a change-of-use confirmation is harder to proceed | Architect / specified administrative agency |

For a building without a completion certificate, a survey of legal-conformity status may be needed before the change-of-use confirmation. Change-of-use issues for a property without a completion certificate are organised in [confirming a change of use to a welfare use for a property without a completion certificate](https://luck428.com/en/column/kensazumisho-nashi-bukken-fukushi-youto-henko). This article shows only the line of confirmation and leaves the individual conformity judgment to an architect.

## In 24-hour unmanned operation, where does the fire service look? Automatic fire alarms, guide lights, escape routes

A fitness gym is generally classed under Fire Service Act Enforcement Order Appended Table 1 item (15) (a workplace not falling under the preceding items), a "non-specified" fire-defence target rather than a specified one. If a sauna (steam bath / hot-air bath) is set up inside the gym, that part can fall under Appended Table 1 item (9)(i), so the classification may change. Classification is judged by the actual use, so a check with the competent fire station is the starting point.

| Equipment | Skeleton of the standard for item (15) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic fire alarm | Item (15) carries a duty at total floor area of 1,000 m² or more. Under 300 m² in certain cases a small-scale-facility automatic fire alarm may be usable | Fire Service Act Enforcement Order art. 21 |
| Guide lights | Required in the basement, windowless storeys and 11th floor and above, etc. Whether a storey is "windowless" is the issue | Fire Service Act Enforcement Order art. 26 |
| Fire extinguishers | Installed per total floor area and use | Fire Service Act Enforcement Order art. 10 |
| Installing/notifying fire equipment | On installation, notify and have the competent fire station inspect | Fire Service Act art. 17, art. 17-3-3 |

If a storey counts as "windowless" (one with openings below a level effective for evacuation and firefighting), the need for guide lights and automatic fire alarms can change. Converting a basement former office or a window-poor tenant into a gym makes this windowless-storey judgment drive the cost. The final call on which equipment is needed at what scale is the competent fire station's and a fire-equipment officer's. 24-hour and late-night operation overlaps the fire and noise issues of other sectors such as karaoke, so [karaoke-box shop properties and use/fire/noise](https://luck428.com/en/column/karaoke-box-tenpo-bukken-youto-shoubou-soon) and [internet-café private-room properties, fire and identity checks](https://luck428.com/en/column/net-cafe-kobetsu-shitsu-bukken-shobo-honnin-kakunin) are also helpful. ## With no staff at night, how do I sort the fire-prevention manager and crime prevention?

Even in unmanned operation, the fire-prevention duty is judged not by whether people are present but by capacity. A non-specified fire-defence target (item (15)) needs a fire-prevention manager appointed and a fire-defence plan drawn up and notified once capacity reaches 50 or more (Fire Service Act art. 8, Fire Service Act Enforcement Order art. 1-2(3), Order art. 3).

| Issue | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity calculation | Not the number of staff but the method in Fire Service Act Enforcement Regulations art. 1-3 (number of staff plus floor area divided by a set area, etc.) | Fire Service Act Enforcement Regulations art. 1-3 |
| Appointing a fire-prevention manager | Item (15) needs appointment and a fire-defence plan notification at capacity of 50 or more | Fire Service Act art. 8, Order art. 3 |
| Unlocking / escape route in a fire | Entrances locked by member card, etc. must be able to be unlocked promptly in a fire. Management of facilities needed for evacuation | Fire Service Act art. 8-2-4 |
| Measures for unmanned hours | Inform users, remote monitoring/notification, placement of initial fire extinguishers, written into the fire-defence plan | Tokyo Fire Department's guideline for fire safety when parties are absent |

Because capacity is calculated by regulation, not by the number of people actually present, even with no permanent staff a property may be calculated at 50 or more by its floor area and need a fire-prevention manager. Operation assuming unmanned hours—how a fire is detected, how evacuation is guided, how the fire service is notified—is helped by the Tokyo Fire Department's guideline for facilities where parties are absent. Late-night unmanned crime-prevention equipment (remote monitoring cameras, entry/exit control, emergency reporting) is a specialist's field, separate from fire. The final call on whether a fire-prevention manager is needed and the content of the fire-defence plan rests with the competent fire station.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. To turn an office building into a 24-hour gym, is a change-of-use confirmation application always required?**
A. Not always. Building Standards Act art. 87(1) applies the confirmation rules where the post-change use is a special building in Appended Table 1 column (i) and the part used for that purpose exceeds 200 m² in total floor area. A fitness gym can fall under item (4) as a "sports practice facility" and thus a special building, so over 200 m² a confirmation application is in principle required. Changes between similar uses (Enforcement Order art. 137-18) are not required. The actual applicability is judged by the specified administrative agency and an architect.

**Q. Even in unmanned operation, must I appoint a fire-prevention manager?**
A. Appointing a fire-prevention manager is judged by capacity, not by the number of people present. Item (15), a non-specified fire-defence target, needs appointment and a fire-defence-plan notification at capacity of 50 or more (Fire Service Act art. 8, Order art. 3). Capacity is calculated by the method in Fire Service Act Enforcement Regulations art. 1-3, so even with no permanent staff a property may be calculated at 50 or more by floor area and need an appointment. The final call on need rests with the competent fire station.

**Q. Are automatic fire alarms and guide lights mandatory for a 24-hour unmanned gym?**
A. In item (15), to which a fitness gym belongs, an automatic fire alarm is a duty at total floor area of 1,000 m² or more (Fire Service Act Enforcement Order art. 21). Guide lights are needed in the basement, windowless storeys and the 11th floor and above, etc. (Order art. 26). For a window-poor tenant or a basement, whether a storey is "windowless" changes the need. Which equipment is needed at what scale is judged by the competent fire station and a fire-equipment officer.

**Q. How should I think about fire measures for the hours with no staff at night?**
A. The Tokyo Fire Department publishes a fire-safety guideline for facilities where parties are absent, illustrating informing users of unmanned hours, a mechanism to promptly unlock entrances locked by member card in a fire, remote monitoring/notification and placement of initial fire extinguishers, written into the fire-defence plan. Management of facilities needed for evacuation rests on Fire Service Act art. 8-2-4. Confirm the specifics with the competent fire station, and consult a specialist separately on crime-prevention equipment.
