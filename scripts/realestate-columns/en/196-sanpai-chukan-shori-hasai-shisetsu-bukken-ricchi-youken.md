## What kind of property can hold an industrial-waste intermediate-treatment facility?

An intermediate-treatment facility such as crushing/sorting is not decided by zoning suitability alone. The first things to check in property selection are whether the site is within a city planning area and the treatment under Article 51 of the Building Standards Act. That article provides that, within a city planning area, a building used for a wholesale market, a crematorium, a refuse incineration plant and other treatment facilities prescribed by Cabinet Order (which include industrial-waste treatment facilities) must not be newly built or extended unless the location of its site has been fixed in the city plan. By a proviso, it may be built where the specified administrative agency, after the deliberation of the prefectural city planning council and the like, recognises that the location of the site causes no hindrance to city planning and grants permission.

In other words, a treatment facility such as a crushing facility can in principle be built only where the location is fixed in the city plan; to place it elsewhere, you must pass a separate gate, the permission of the specified administrative agency (the so-called Article 51 proviso permission). Note that Article 130-2-3 of the Order for Enforcement of the Building Standards Act prescribes small-scale treatment facilities that do not require a location designation, so the treatment changes with the scale of the facility. Whether a facility qualifies is the field of the specified administrative agency, and we cannot assert "permission is / is not required."

| Layer to check | What to look at | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| City planning area | Whether within the area; urbanization promotion / control area | City Planning Act |
| Facility location | Whether fixed in the city plan / whether an Art. 51 proviso permission is needed | Building Standards Act Art. 51 |
| Facility scale | Whether it is a small-scale facility not requiring location designation | Building Standards Act Enforcement Order Art. 130-2-3 |

## Can a crushing facility be built in an urbanization control area or a zoning district?

In terms of zoning, siting a treatment-type building is realistic only in limited districts such as industrial and exclusive industrial districts, and it does not fit residential districts. The specific possibility depends on the building restrictions for each zoning district and the local government's practice. A heavier gate is the urbanization control area. An urbanization control area is an area where urbanization is to be restrained; in principle a new building requires a development permission / building permission under the City Planning Act, and siting an industrial-waste facility is not easy.

In practice, cheap land in an urbanization control area tends to draw attention, but the Article 51 proviso permission, the development permission and the disposal-business and facility-installation permissions overlap, and costs can mount while the prospects remain unclear. Which areas are realistic starts with a prior consultation with the specified administrative agency and the prefecture that have jurisdiction over the intended site. ## How do the facility-installation permission and the disposal-business licence differ?

To crush and sort industrial waste as a business, you first need a waste-disposal business licence. Article 14(6) of the Waste Management Act provides that a person who intends to carry on the disposal of industrial waste as a business must obtain the licence of the prefectural governor (the mayor for a designated city) having jurisdiction over the area. This is a licence for the "business."

Separately, a crushing facility of a certain scale or more needs an installation permission for the facility itself. Article 15(1) of the Waste Management Act provides that a person who intends to install an industrial-waste treatment facility prescribed by Cabinet Order must obtain the permission of the prefectural governor having jurisdiction over the installation site, and Article 7 of the Order lists the target facilities. Among crushing facilities, a crushing facility for waste plastics (Order Art. 7(7)) and a crushing facility for debris and the like (Art. 7(8)-2) are each subject to the installation permission where the processing capacity exceeds 5 tonnes per day. Even at 5 tonnes or less, outside the installation permission, the disposal-business licence and the Building Standards Act regulation still apply separately.

| Permission | Target | Basis | Window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Waste-disposal business licence | Carrying on crushing/sorting as a "business" | Waste Management Act Art. 14(6) | Prefectural governor (designated-city mayor) |
| Industrial-waste treatment facility installation permission | Crushing facility (waste plastics / debris etc. over 5 t/day) | Waste Management Act Art. 15(1); Enforcement Order Art. 7 | Prefectural governor |
| Building confirmation / Art. 51 proviso permission | Construction and location of the facility building | Building Standards Act Arts. 6, 51 | Building official / specified administrative agency |

The windows and the bases differ, and the disposal-business and facility-installation permission applications are handled by an administrative scrivener, while building confirmation and structural design are handled by an architect. We do not act for these.

## How does the living-environment impact survey change property selection?

The application for the facility-installation permission must be accompanied by the results of a living-environment impact survey. Article 15(3) of the Waste Management Act provides that the application must, as prescribed by Order of the Ministry of the Environment, be accompanied by a document stating the results of a survey on the impact that installing the facility will have on the living environment of the surrounding area. For a crushing facility, noise, vibration, dust and wastewater are among the survey items, and the Ministry of the Environment's "Guideline for living-environment impact surveys concerning the installation of industrial-waste treatment facilities" is the practical handbook.

From the property-selection view, the closer the surrounding land is to housing, schools and hospitals, the more likely an impact will show in the survey, and the heavier the prospects of installation and the burden of dealing with neighbours. Conversely, land in an industrial cluster where a buffer zone is easy to secure tends to have clearer prospects. Measurement and surveying are the work of a specialist survey organisation; we organise the property-side facts of the surrounding situation (neighbouring uses, distances, the road and the state of wind direction and the like).

| Surrounding environment | Burden of the impact survey / neighbour response (general tendency) |
| --- | --- |
| Housing, schools and hospitals close by | Impact likely to show; heavy burden; siting prospects are poor |
| Industrial cluster / buffer zone securable | Easier to contain impact; clearer prospects |
| Close to a river, waterway or groundwater | Additional checks needed for wastewater and water quality |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a crushing facility be built as long as the zoning fits?**
A. Zoning suitability is only the entrance. Within a city planning area, under Article 51 of the Building Standards Act, you cannot newly build or extend unless the facility's location is fixed in the city plan or you have the specified administrative agency's permission (the Art. 51 proviso). In an urbanization control area, the development-permission wall also overlaps. The judgment on possibility is the field of the specified administrative agency, the administrative scrivener and the architect; we organise the property-side facts.

**Q. If I have the disposal-business licence, do I not need the facility-installation permission?**
A. They are separate permissions. To crush and sort as a "business" you need a waste-disposal business licence (Waste Management Act Art. 14(6)), and separately, a crushing facility for waste plastics or debris and the like whose processing capacity exceeds 5 tonnes per day needs a facility-installation permission (Art. 15(1); Enforcement Order Art. 7). Which is required varies by item and scale, so confirm with an administrative scrivener and the competent local government.

**Q. Is the living-environment impact survey required before buying the property?**
A. The results of a living-environment impact survey must accompany the facility-installation permission application (Waste Management Act Art. 15(3)). Measurement and surveying are handled by a specialist survey organisation. At the property-selection stage, whether housing, schools and hospitals are near governs the survey prospects, so we organise that surrounding situation and refer the survey itself to a survey organisation.

**Q. Can a crushing facility be placed on cheap land in an urbanization control area?**
A. It is not easy. An urbanization control area is to be restrained from urbanization; in principle building requires a development / building permission, and the Art. 51 proviso permission and the installation permission overlap. Choosing by price alone risks mounting costs while the permission never stands. The possibility is for the specified administrative agency and the prefecture to judge; we organise the property's city-planning position and connect you to each qualified professional.
