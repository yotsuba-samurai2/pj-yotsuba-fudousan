**In short:** for a grooming salon or pet shop, three things trip you up first — whether the premises can meet the animal-facility standards (cages, washing, disinfection, ventilation, drainage), whether odour and barking will clash with the neighbours, and whether the lease bans "keeping animals." First-category animal-handling registration is granted "per business establishment," so the premises being able to meet the facility standards is a precondition. Confirm the use zone, drainage, ventilation and the lessor's stance before you sign, and you avoid the rework.

You lease a place because it is "a pet-friendly building," and then, at the registration stage, the ventilation or drainage of the animal facility falls short and the fit-out has to be redone. The same rework that hits restaurants and salons hits animal businesses. This article is for those opening a grooming salon or pet shop in Tokyo, and for those leasing to them: what you can confirm before signing the lease, set out in order from the Act on Welfare and Management of Animals and the guidance of the Ministry of the Environment and local governments. Final confirmation rests with the local government that grants the registration.

## What do you confirm first, before signing?

First, check whether your trade needs registration. Article 10 of the Act on Welfare and Management of Animals (Act No. 105 of 1973) provides that a person who intends to run, as a business, the sale, keeping, lending, training or display of animals (mammals, birds or reptiles) must obtain **first-category animal-handling registration** from the prefectural governor (or the head of a designated/core city) with jurisdiction over the location, **for each business establishment** (**accessed 25 August 2026**).

Which category your shop falls under:

| Trade | Category (example) |
|---|---|
| Pet shop (live sales) | Sale |
| Grooming salon (take in and treat) | Keeping |
| Pet hotel | Keeping |
| Training school | Training |
| Cat café / interaction | Display |

**Registration is "per business establishment."** So if the premises change, you register again, and the premises being able to meet the facility standards is a precondition of registration. That is the starting point of choosing premises.

The registration has a term and must be renewed every five years (Article 13). In addition, certain grounds — links to organised crime, a past revocation and so on — bar registration (Article 12).

## How do facility size, cages and odour control depend on the premises?

A first-category operator must comply with the standards set by Ordinance of the Ministry of the Environment for the structure and scale of the animal facility and the method of managing the animals (Article 21). The detail is in the Regulation for Enforcement of the Act (Ordinance of the Ministry of the Environment No. 1 of 2006); the amendment in force from 1 June 2021 added numerical standards for dogs and cats (**accessed 25 August 2026**).

What matters on the property side:

| Item | The regulation's approach |
|---|---|
| Cages etc. | Sized to the species, number and build of the animals; for dogs/cats, standards differ by whether the exercise space is separated or combined; wire-mesh floors are in principle prohibited (unless paw-protected) |
| Feeding/water | Equipment giving constant access to clean water |
| Washing/disinfection | Equipment to wash and disinfect facilities and tools |
| Drainage | Drainage that properly discharges waste and dirty water |
| Ventilation | Ventilation that removes odour, humidity and dust and manages temperature/humidity |
| Light-blocking/lighting | Daylight and lighting suited to the animals |
| Staff numbers | For dogs/cats, a cap on animals per staff member (dogs 20, of which 15 breeding; cats 30, of which 25 breeding; the staff-number standard is phased in via transitional measures) |

**Note that these are standards "for the animals," but where each thing goes in the building is decided by the property's plumbing and ventilation makings.** Fitting a large washing sink or heavy ventilation into a one-room unit later moves plumbing-upgrade and electrical-capacity costs.

What is worth confirming on the property side is less the figure itself than **whether the place has the makings to meet it.**

| Confirm before signing | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Position and bore of water supply/drainage | Essential to washing/disinfection/water; grooming uses much hot water |
| Ventilation/exhaust route | Odour is the main cause of neighbour trouble; direction and capacity matter |
| Electrical capacity | Water heating, dryers, HVAC and washers draw capacity |
| Waterproof, cleanable floors/walls | The makings of "keeping clean"; tied to restoration scope |
| Room for soundproofing | Barking control; timber and light-steel frames carry sound |

## What kind of premises tend to be refused over use zone and neighbours?

For an animal business, unlike a restaurant or clinic, **odour, barking and drainage — the impact on neighbours — heavily shape the choice of premises.**

First, the lease. Many apartment buildings and multi-tenant buildings ban "keeping animals" or bringing in pets. Even a "pet-friendly" building permits a resident to keep an animal — not necessarily to run a business of repeatedly taking animals in. **You need to read how the lease treats keeping animals and business use before you apply.** Which parts of the lease to read is at [reading a lease](https://luck428.com/column/chintaishaku-keiyakusho-doko-wo-yomu).

Second, the use zone. A pet shop or grooming salon is treated as a "service-business shop" under the Building Standards Act and, like a beauty salon, is subject to use-zone restrictions. In a Category-1 low-rise exclusive residential zone a standalone shop is hard to place; in a Category-2 low-rise exclusive residential zone there are limits such as a total floor area within 150 m². Feasibility is confirmed at the specified administrative authority. How the use zone and floor-area ratio move pricing is at [why Japanese land prices change with the floor-area ratio](https://luck428.com/column/yosekiritsu-hosei-tochine).

Third, the neighbours. Apart from registration, odour and noise affect the shop's survival through local environmental ordinances and complaints. **Confirm the exhaust direction, opening hours and soundproofing, and the state of the lessor and neighbours, before you sign.**

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If it is a pet-friendly apartment, can I open a grooming salon?**
A. Not necessarily. "Pet-friendly" permits a resident to keep an animal, not necessarily to run a business of repeatedly taking animals in. Many leases restrict "keeping animals" or business use. Confirm the lease terms and the lessor's stance before you apply, and check whether the ventilation, drainage and disinfection equipment can meet the enforcement regulation.

**Q. What happens to first-category registration if I move the shop?**
A. Registration is granted per business establishment (Article 10). If you move, you register again for the new premises. Since the new premises must be able to meet the facility standards, confirm the plumbing, ventilation and partition before signing.

**Q. Are there set figures for cage size or staff numbers?**
A. For dogs and cats, the enforcement regulation (amendment in force from 1 June 2021) sets numerical standards such as cage size and a cap on animals per staff member (dogs 20 per person, of which 15 breeding; cats 30, of which 25 breeding; the staff-number standard is phased in). Application and interpretation follow local operation, so confirm with the animal welfare centre with jurisdiction.
