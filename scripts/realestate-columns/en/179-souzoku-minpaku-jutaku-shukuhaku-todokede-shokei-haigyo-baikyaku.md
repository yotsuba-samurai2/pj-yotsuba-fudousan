**The key point**: When you lose a parent who ran a minpaku (residential-lodging business) and inherit the property and the business, you cannot simply take over the "notification" the parent filed. The Housing Accommodation Business Act contains no "succession" provision by which an heir takes over the operator's position on death (there is no succession article in the Act). What the law provides is the opposite: when a residential-lodging business operator who is an individual dies, the heir must file a closure (etc.) notification within 30 days of learning the fact (art. 3(6)(i)). The parent's death is treated as ending the business, and to keep operating the heir must re-file a new notification (art. 3(1)) in the heir's own name. This contrasts with the Inns and Hotels Act (art. 3-4), where an heir who applies within 60 days of death and obtains approval can succeed to the position. The heir's choice comes down to two: (1) stop, file the closure notification, and if continuing, file a new notification to keep operating; or (2) stop operating and sell. ## When you inherit a minpaku, can you take over the notification the parent filed?

In short, no. This is the biggest difference from the inn/hotel business (hotels, inns, simple-lodging), and mishandling the period right after inheritance risks lodging guests without a notification.

The Housing Accommodation Business Act, in art. 3(1), provides that a person who notifies the prefectural governor (or, in a public-health-centre-establishing city, its head) may run a residential-lodging business. This notification is personal to the person who filed it (the operator). The Act contains no "succession" article by which an heir takes over the position on the operator's death. Instead, art. 3(6)(i) provides that when a residential-lodging business operator who is an individual dies, the heir must notify the prefectural governor within 30 days of learning the fact. In other words, the law is built around "death = end of the business (subject to a closure notification)," not "death = succession to the heir."

| System | Treatment on death | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Residential-lodging business (minpaku) | No succession provision. Heir files a death notification (within 30 days). To continue, a new notification | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 3(6)(i), art. 3(1) |
| Inn/hotel business (hotels, inns, simple-lodging) | If the heir applies within 60 days of death and obtains approval, they succeed to the position | Inns and Hotels Act art. 3-4 |

For a licensed hotel or inn, an heir who wishes to continue the business must apply for approval within 60 days of the operator’s death. An heir who obtains approval succeeds to the operator’s status (Hotel Business Act, Article 3-4). Even for the same "business of lodging people," minpaku has no such succession mechanism, so you cannot keep operating using the parent's notification. If the inherited property was a minpaku, the starting point is to decide whether to continue or stop, on the premise that the notification is not inherited. For the property requirements when continuing, see [Minpaku notification and property checks](/en/legal/column/minpaku-jutaku-shukuhaku-todokede-yoken); for special-zone minpaku, [Special-zone minpaku property requirements/certification](/en/column/tokku-minpaku-bukken-youken-nintei).

## If the notification is not inherited, what must you do by when to keep operating (closure + new notification)?

Since succession is not possible, an heir who wants to keep operating takes a two-stage procedure: "a notification closing the parent's business" and "a notification opening one's own business." If left unaddressed, you end up lodging guests with no notification in the heir's name — a risk of unnotified operation.

First, under art. 3(6)(i), the heir files a death (closure, etc.) notification within 30 days of learning of the death. This settles the parent's notification. Then, to keep operating, the heir files a new notification under art. 3(1). The new notification submits a notification form stating the heir's trade name/name and address, the dwelling's location, and, where management is entrusted, the residential-lodging management operator, together with drawings and a written pledge of not falling under grounds for disqualification (art. 3(2), (3)). In effect, the heir re-files, in their own name, the same notification the parent filed when first starting the minpaku.

| Procedure | By when | Who | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Death (closure, etc.) notification | Within 30 days of learning of the death | Heir | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 3(6)(i) |
| New notification (if continuing) | Before resuming operation | Heir (in the heir's own name) | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 3(1) |
| Inheritance registration | Within 3 years of learning of acquisition | Heir who acquired ownership | Real Property Registration Act art. 76-2 |

Note that until the new notification is accepted and the heir becomes the operator, you cannot lodge guests on the basis of the parent's notification. To avoid a gap in the notification, discuss the timing of the death notification and the new notification with an administrative scrivener early. ## By when is inheritance registration required, and how do you align it with the notification holder?

Separately from the minpaku notification, the name on the property itself (registration) must also be transferred to the heir. Because inheritance registration became a duty on 1 April 2024, there is a deadline here too.

Article 76-2(1) of the Real Property Registration Act provides that when an inheritance begins for a registered owner, a person who acquires ownership by inheritance must apply to register the transfer of ownership within 3 years of learning both that the inheritance has begun for themselves and that they have acquired the ownership. Failing to apply without justifiable grounds is subject to a non-penal fine of up to 100,000 yen under art. 164(1). This registration duty arises from the fact of inheriting the property, regardless of whether you file a minpaku notification or keep operating.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Registration deadline | Within 3 years of learning of acquisition | Real Property Registration Act art. 76-2(1) |
| If neglected | Non-penal fine up to 100,000 yen without justifiable grounds | Real Property Registration Act art. 164(1) |
| Effective date | From 1 April 2024 (with transitional measures applying to past inheritances) | Mandatory inheritance registration |
| Alignment with the notification holder | Clearer when the new notification's operator = the property's rights holder | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 3 |

Filing the new minpaku notification in the heir's name and transferring the property's registration to the heir aligns the operator and the property's rights holder, smoothing later management and sale. The application for inheritance registration is a judicial scrivener's exclusive work, including collecting registration records and family registers. If there are several heirs and who takes the property is undecided, both the registration and the notification holder change with the content of the estate-division agreement, so fixing the acquirer first is the order. ## If you continue operating, how does the need to entrust management (a residential-lodging management operator) change?

If the heir lives somewhere different from the parent, or has a separate main job and cannot be resident, the operating structure changes from the parent's era, and entrustment to a residential-lodging management operator may become necessary.

Article 11(1) of the Housing Accommodation Business Act provides that where (1) the number of guest rooms in the notified dwelling exceeds the number set by MLIT/MHLW ordinance, or (2) the operator is absent (other than temporarily) while lodging guests — the so-called "host-absent type" — the residential-lodging management work must be entrusted to one residential-lodging management operator (except where the operator is itself a residential-lodging management operator managing it themselves). If the parent ran a "host-resident type" while living in the property, and the heir lives far away, making it a "host-absent type," this entrustment duty may newly arise. The entrusted management operator is also an item to state in the new notification form (art. 3(2)(vi)).

| Operating structure | Need to entrust management (general) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Heir resides and manages themselves (host-resident; rooms within the ordinance number) | In principle no entrustment duty | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 11(1) |
| Heir is far away and absent (host-absent) | Entrustment to a residential-lodging management operator required | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 11(1)(ii) |
| Number of guest rooms in the notified dwelling exceeds the ordinance number | Entrustment to a residential-lodging management operator required | Housing Accommodation Business Act art. 11(1)(i) |

Note that a residential-lodging business is defined as one where the number of days people are lodged does not exceed 180 days per year (art. 2(3)), and this cap does not change after inheritance. The 180-day limit applies to each notified dwelling, not each operator. Days already used within the same statutory annual period are carried over when the operator changes; a new notification by the heir does not reset the count. Confirm the dwelling’s prior lodging record with the notification authority ([Japan Tourism Agency FAQ](https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/minpaku/content/001396589.pdf), checked 18 November 2026). A form where a non-resident entrusts management and operates is organised separately in [Entrusting minpaku management for non-resident owners](/en/column/hikyojusha-chuka-owner-minpaku-jutaku-shukuhaku-kanri-itaku). ## If you stop operating and sell, how do the minpaku track record affect valuation and the buyer?

If you decide not to continue, you file the closure notification and proceed to sale. When selling a dwelling that was used as a minpaku, it is viewed a little differently from an ordinary second-hand home.

First, even at the point of deciding to sell, the heir must complete the death (closure, etc.) notification (art. 3(6)(i)). Whether a buyer who uses the property after the sale can continue the minpaku depends, since there is no succession mechanism, on whether the buyer re-files a new notification in their own name. In other words, even if the seller hands over the property as "minpaku-ready," the notification is tied to the person, not the property, so the buyer must re-file their own notification. In a condominium whose management rules prohibit minpaku, the next buyer may not be able to continue a minpaku at all, and this is a matter subject to the important-matters explanation.

|---|---|---|
| Closure notification | The death notification is needed even on sale (because it is not succeeded) | Administrative scrivener / prefecture's division |
| Buyer's continuation | Because the notification is tied to the person, the buyer re-files a new notification | Administrative scrivener |
| Management rules | A condominium may have rules prohibiting minpaku | Management company / management association |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can I keep operating using my parent's minpaku notification number?**
A. No. The Housing Accommodation Business Act has no succession provision by which an heir takes over the position on the operator's death. When the parent dies, the heir must file a death (closure, etc.) notification within 30 days of learning the fact (art. 3(6)(i)), and to keep operating must re-file a new notification (art. 3(1)) in the heir's own name. The build differs from art. 3-4 of the Inns and Hotels Act, where an heir who obtains approval can succeed to the position. Lodging guests using the parent's notification number as is carries a risk of unnotified operation, so consult an administrative scrivener early on the notification procedure. We organise the material for the decision and connect the notification to an administrative scrivener.

**Q. When I inherit a minpaku, what must I notify by when?**
A. Generally, you first file the death (closure, etc.) notification under art. 3(6)(i) within 30 days of learning of the death. To keep operating, the heir files a new notification under art. 3(1) before resuming operation. In addition, the property's inheritance registration is required to be applied for within 3 years of learning of acquisition (Real Property Registration Act art. 76-2). The notification and the registration are separate procedures with different professionals. Confirm the specific start date of the deadline and the attached documents with the prefecture / public-health-centre-establishing city where you notify. We do not carry out the procedures; we connect the notification to an administrative scrivener and the registration to a judicial scrivener.

**Q. Can I keep running a minpaku even if the heir lives far away?**
A. You may be able to, but depending on the operating structure, entrustment to a residential-lodging management operator may become necessary. Article 11(1) of the Housing Accommodation Business Act requires entrusting the residential-lodging management work to one residential-lodging management operator when it is a host-absent type with the operator absent while lodging guests, or when the number of guest rooms in the notified dwelling exceeds the ordinance number. If the parent ran a host-resident type and the heir is far away and becomes an absent type, this entrustment duty may newly arise. The entrustment target is also stated in the new notification form.
