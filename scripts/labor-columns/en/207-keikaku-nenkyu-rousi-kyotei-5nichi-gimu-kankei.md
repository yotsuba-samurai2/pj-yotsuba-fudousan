**Bottom line first**: Planned grant of annual paid leave lets the employer decide in advance, by a labour-management agreement, when leave is taken (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)). Only the part exceeding 5 days of each person's leave can be planned; 5 days must remain for the worker to choose freely. Days given by planned grant count toward the employer's 5-day designation duty (Article 39(7)). The agreement need not be filed with the Labour Standards Inspection Office, but a provision in the work rules is the premise. The agreement and work rules belong to a Shakai Hoken Roumushi, and negotiation or litigation over unused leave or disadvantageous treatment to an attorney, each contracted separately as an independent business entity.

During a planned summer shutdown, some employees may have sufficient leave for an agreed allocation and others may not. This guide explains what an agreement can cover and how the leave counts toward the five-day obligation.

## What is planned grant, and how does it differ from ordinary paid leave?

Planned grant fixes in advance, by a labour-management agreement, when annual paid leave is taken, and leave is then used up according to that schedule (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)). Ordinary paid leave is, in principle, taken on a date the worker designates (Article 39(5)), but for days made subject to planned grant, neither side may exercise the right to designate or change the date, and leave is taken on the day set in the agreement.

| Item | Ordinary paid leave (worker designates) | Planned grant |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Labour Standards Act Article 39(5) | Labour Standards Act Article 39(6) |
| Who fixes the date | Worker designates; employer may change | Fixed in advance by labour-management agreement |
| Range that can be covered | All held days | Only the part exceeding 5 days |
| Labour-management agreement | Not needed | Needed (no filing with the Inspection Office) |

The methods of planned grant are a workplace-wide closure, a rotating grant by group, and an individual grant (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, "Planned grant system for annual paid leave" leaflet, accessed 14 November 2026). Which method to use turns on the ebb and flow of work and the duty roster, so we do not give one conclusion here (**unverified**). The 5-day duty itself is in [What happens if the 5-day duty is not met](/en/labor/column/nenji-yukyu-5nichi-torikirenai), and leave taken in hourly units in [Introducing hourly paid leave and the labour-management agreement](/en/labor/column/jikantan-nenkyu-dounyu-roushi-kyotei-shugyokisoku).

## Which part of the leave can be covered by planned grant?

Planned grant can cover the "part exceeding 5 days" of each worker's held paid leave (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)). The 5 days must stay in the worker's hands so they can use them freely for illness or personal matters. A worker holding 10 days can have at most 5 days planned; one holding 20 days, at most 15 days.

| Held paid-leave days | Cap that can be planned | Minimum days free for the worker |
|---|---|---|
| 10 days | 5 days | 5 days |
| 15 days | 10 days | 5 days |
| 20 days | 15 days | 5 days |

For planned leave, include unused, unexpired days carried forward. For example, three carried-over days plus seven newly granted days, all unused, allow up to five days to be scheduled in advance. Do not use that combined balance for the separate ten-day test for compulsory annual take-up. See [From how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## What must the labour-management agreement set out?

To carry out planned grant you need a written labour-management agreement with the majority union (or, if none, a representative of the majority of workers) (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)). Unlike a 36 Agreement, this agreement does not have to be filed with the Labour Standards Inspection Office. Still, it is usual to operate with a basis provision in the work rules.

| Main items in the agreement | Example |
|---|---|
| Who is covered | The range of workers covered, and those excluded |
| Days covered | Days of each person's leave put to planned grant (part exceeding 5 days) |
| Method and actual dates | Workplace-wide / by group / individual, and the actual dates of planned grant |
| Treatment of those with little leave | How to deal with workers who lack enough days to cover |
| Procedure to change a planned date | The procedure to change a planned date for business reasons |

The above is the general view in the Ministry's "Planned grant system for annual paid leave" leaflet (accessed 14 November 2026); how far each agreement sets out the details differs by workplace. Handling a labour-management agreement shares reasoning with the 36 Agreement on overtime; the difference in whether filing is needed is in [How far can a 36 Agreement allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made).

## Does planned grant count toward the 5-day acquisition duty?

It does. The employer has a duty, for a worker given 10 or more days of paid leave, to have 5 days taken by designating the date within a year of the base date (Labour Standards Act Article 39(7)). Article 39(8) provides that days the worker designated and took, and days given by planned grant (Article 39(6)), are deducted from those 5 days. The ten-day threshold uses the statutory entitlement newly granted on the reference date, not the balance including carry-over. Whole days and half days actually taken count; hourly leave does not count toward the five days.

| What can be credited to the 5-day duty | Basis |
|---|---|
| Days the worker designated and took | Labour Standards Act Article 39(5) and (8) |
| Days given by planned grant | Labour Standards Act Article 39(6) and (8) |
| Days the employer designated and had taken | Labour Standards Act Article 39(7) main text |

So if planned grant has 5 or more days taken in a year, the employer need not separately designate 5 days. Conversely, at a workplace where planned grant is under 5 days, the shortfall must be made up by the employer's designation so that 5 days are reliably taken. How to count the 5-day duty and the risk of shortfall is in [What happens if the 5-day duty is not met](/en/labor/column/nenji-yukyu-5nichi-torikirenai).

## How do you treat new hires or those with little leave?

Because only the "part exceeding 5 days" can be put to planned grant, a worker within six months of joining who has no leave yet, or one with 5 or fewer days, lacks enough leave to cover. With a workplace-wide closure there are days such workers cannot be made to work.

| Option | Content |
|---|---|
| Give special paid leave | Grant special paid leave, separate from statutory leave, and let them rest |
| Pay a leave allowance | For a closure, pay a leave allowance (Labour Standards Act Article 26; at least 60% of average wage) |
| Grant leave earlier | Provide in the work rules for granting leave before the statutory base date |

These are the general responses in the Ministry's leaflet (accessed 14 November 2026); which to use differs by workplace circumstances. We do not conclude on the individual design here (**unverified**). Prompting individual date designation without planned grant is also possible, and the design that evens out peaks in duty is the labour area of a Shakai Hoken Roumushi.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Must the planned-grant agreement be filed with the Labour Standards Inspection Office?**
A. No filing is needed. Planned grant must be set by a written labour-management agreement with the majority union (or, if none, a representative of the majority of workers) (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)), but, unlike a 36 Agreement, there is no duty to file it with the Inspection Office. Still, it is usual to operate with a basis provision in the work rules.

**Q. How far of the leave can planned grant cover?**
A. The "part exceeding 5 days" of each person's leave (Labour Standards Act Article 39(6)). The 5 days must stay in the worker's hands for free use for illness or personal matters. A worker holding 10 days can have at most 5 days planned; one holding 20 days, up to 15 days.

**Q. Are days used up by planned grant counted toward the 5-day acquisition duty?**
A. Yes. The employer's 5-day designation duty (Labour Standards Act Article 39(7)) is calculated by deducting days the worker designated and days given by planned grant (Article 39(6)) (Article 39(8)). If planned grant has 5 or more days taken in a year, no separate designation of 5 days is needed.
