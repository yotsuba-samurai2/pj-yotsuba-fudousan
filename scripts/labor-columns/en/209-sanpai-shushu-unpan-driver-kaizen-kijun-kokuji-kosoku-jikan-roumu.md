**In short:** The Improvement Standards Notice (Standards for Improving the Working Hours, etc., of Motor Vehicle Drivers) applies **regardless of whether the employer holds a transport licence — it applies to employees who mainly drive four-wheel-or-larger vehicles** (Notice, Article 1). So even where you need neither a waste-collection licence nor a transport licence just to haul your own industrial waste in your own trucks, the Improvement Standards Notice still reaches those drivers. From April 1, 2024 (Reiwa 6), truck drivers' on-duty hours are managed within **3,300 a year / 284 a month / 13 a day (up to 15)**, and the rest period is **based on a continuous 11 hours and does not fall below 9**. This page sorts out the line between licensing and labour management, the on-duty ceilings, where to fix the 36 Agreement and work rules, and whom to ask for what.

The view that "we don't hold a transport licence, so the rules on drivers' working hours don't concern us" is dangerous. This page is for operators who employ truck drivers under an industrial-waste collection-and-transport licence, and for those who haul their own waste in their own vehicles. From the viewpoint of a shakai hoken roumushi's labour management, it sets out the scope of the Improvement Standards Notice, on-duty hours and rest periods, and where to fix the work rules and the 36 Agreement.

## Does the Improvement Standards Notice apply even without a transport licence?

It does. Whether the Improvement Standards Notice applies turns **not on "what licence you hold" but on "whether the employee mainly engages in the work of driving a motor vehicle."**

Article 1 of the Improvement Standards Notice defines the "motor vehicle driver" it covers as a worker under Article 9 of the Labor Standards Act (excluding workers at businesses using only live-in relatives, and domestic workers) who **mainly engages in the work of driving a four-wheel-or-larger vehicle** (excluding those designated by the Director-General of the Labour Standards Bureau). There is no limitation to "someone employed by an operator holding a trucking licence."

Whether a licence is required is decided by other laws.

| How you haul | Licence required | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Hauling another party's industrial waste as a business | Industrial-waste collection-and-transport licence (prefectural governor) | Waste Management Act, Article 14, paragraph 1 |
| Hauling your own (the operator's own) industrial waste | **No licence required** | Waste Management Act, Article 14, paragraph 1, proviso (where the operator itself transports its own industrial waste) |
| Hauling freight for another party for a fee | General trucking business licence | Motor Truck Transport Business Act, Article 2 |
| Hauling your own cargo (waste) in your own vehicles | No transport licence required (not "in response to another party's demand") | Motor Truck Transport Business Act, Article 2 |

In other words, if you only haul your own waste in your own trucks, you need neither a waste-collection licence nor a transport licence. **But "no licence required" and "the rules on drivers' working hours do not reach us" are two different things.** Once you employ someone who mainly engages in driving, the Improvement Standards Notice must be managed, licence or no licence. For the general rules on working hours for staff other than drivers, see [The 36 Agreement and the overtime ceiling](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made).

## How did on-duty hours (year, month, day) change from April 2024?

The Improvement Standards Notice was amended by Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare Notice No. 367 of December 23, 2022 (Reiwa 4) and has applied since April 1, 2024 (Reiwa 6). The main standards for truck drivers are as follows.

| Item | Principle | Exception / limit |
|---|---|---|
| On-duty hours per year | 3,300 hours | Up to 3,400 hours for up to 6 months a year under a labour-management agreement |
| On-duty hours per month | 284 hours | Up to 310 hours for up to 6 months a year under a labour-management agreement (over 284 hours for no more than 3 consecutive months; strive to keep monthly overtime and holiday work under 100 hours) |
| On-duty hours per day | 13 hours | Up to 15 hours (long-distance freight: up to 16 hours twice a week) |
| Rest period | Based on a continuous 11 hours or more after duty | Not below a continuous 9 hours |
| Continuous driving time | Within 4 hours | May be extended by up to 30 minutes where unavoidable |

On-duty hours means the time from start to finish of duty, being working hours plus break time (including standby time). The Improvement Standards Notice is not the Labor Standards Act itself but a notice of the Minister; still, a breach is subject to corrective guidance from the Labor Standards Inspection Office. It is also a different yardstick from the overtime cap (for motor-vehicle driving, up to 960 hours a year even under a special clause; Labor Standards Act, Supplementary Provisions, Article 140, paragraph 1), and **both must be satisfied at the same time**.

## Does a waste driver's "loading and waiting at the collection site" count as on-duty time?

It does. On-duty hours cover **not only working hours but also break and standby time — the whole time from start to finish of duty.**

Industrial-waste collection involves loading at the site that produces the waste, unloading at disposal or intermediate-treatment facilities, and waiting in line to be received — so "time not spent driving" tends to run long. If such time is under the employer's command and cannot be freely used, it counts as standby time within on-duty hours. If you count only driving time and judge "there's still room," you can find that actual on-duty hours exceeded 15.

| Type of time | Counts as on-duty? |
|---|---|
| Time spent driving | Yes (working hours) |
| Loading and unloading work | Yes (working hours) |
| Waiting to be received / waiting in line at the disposal site | Yes if under command (standby time) |
| Break time | Yes (counted in on-duty hours; not working hours) |
| Rest period between shifts | No (outside on-duty hours; a continuous 11 hours is the base) |

The starting point for management is to **record actual on-duty hours with a digital tachograph or daily log, and to separate driving, work, and waiting.** The longer the waiting at a site, the more important it is to check that records match reality.

## How do you set up the 36 Agreement and work rules?

Satisfying both the management of on-duty hours and the overtime cap requires careful drafting. The points to fix are as follows.

| What to do | Point to review |
|---|---|
| 36 Agreement (special clause) | Design annual overtime for motor-vehicle driving within 960 hours, and state the frequency of the special clause (up to 6 times a year), the reasons, and the procedure (Labor Standards Act, Article 36; Supplementary Provisions, Article 140, paragraph 1) |
| Work rules and wage rules | Reflect the thinking on on-duty hours and rest periods, and align them with the calculation of premium wages (25%+ for overtime, 35%+ for holiday work, 25%+ for late-night work) (Labor Standards Act, Article 37) |
| Records of on-duty and working hours | Run day-to-day management against the lines of 15 hours a day, 284 hours a month, and a 9-hour rest, and make the treatment of standby time clear |
| Selection of the majority representative | Select someone who is not in a managerial position through a proper procedure, and keep a record (Ordinance for Enforcement of the Labor Standards Act, Article 6-2) |

Work rules must be drawn up and filed at workplaces that regularly employ 10 or more workers. For details, see [How many employees make work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo). The whole picture of the "2024 problem" for general trucking operators that hold a transport licence is covered in [The trucking 2024 problem and the Improvement Standards Notice](/en/labor/column/unso-2024-mondai-kaizen-kijun-kokuji).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do we have to follow the Improvement Standards Notice even just to haul our own waste in our own trucks?**
A. Yes. The Improvement Standards Notice applies based not on holding a transport or waste-collection licence but on whether the employee mainly engages in driving a four-wheel-or-larger vehicle (Notice, Article 1). Even where hauling your own waste needs no licence, if that driver mainly engages in driving, on-duty hours and rest periods must be managed.

**Q. Since waste collection needs a licence, do we also need a transport licence on top of the Improvement Standards Notice?**
A. It depends on how you haul. Hauling another party's industrial waste as a business needs an industrial-waste collection-and-transport licence (Waste Management Act, Article 14, paragraph 1), but hauling your own waste yourself needs no licence (same paragraph, proviso). A transport (general trucking) licence is needed when hauling freight "for another party for a fee"; just hauling your own cargo in your own vehicles does not need one. Whether a licence is required is a separate issue from the working-hour rules.

**Q. Does time waiting to be received at the disposal site count as on-duty time?**
A. If it is under the employer's command and cannot be freely used, it counts as standby time within on-duty hours. On-duty hours are the time from start to finish of duty — working hours plus breaks and standby — and include waiting time when not driving. Where waiting is long, grasping the reality through records is the starting point for management.

**Q. Can you handle the driver's licence and labour together?**
A. Labour management is the work of a shakai hoken roumushi, and the papers for a waste-collection or transport licence are the work of a gyoseishoshi.
