**In short:** Staffing a self-reliance training (living/functional training) office starts by reading the numbers set in the designation standard (Standards concerning personnel, facilities and operation of designated disability welfare service businesses under the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities, Ministerial Ordinance No. 171 of 2006), Article 156 (functional training) and Article 166 (living training), as **"how many full-time-equivalent hours must be met."** Functional training counts the combined nursing staff, therapists and living support workers at a full-time equivalent of at least the number of users divided by 6 (Article 156, paragraph 1, item 1); living training counts living support workers at the sum of daytime users divided by 6 and residential-type users divided by 10 (Article 166, paragraph 1, item 1) — each with a service manager and a full-time requirement. This article organises the labour side — how full-time equivalents are counted, social-insurance and employment-insurance thresholds, working-hours management where night and visiting support is involved, and work rules and employment contracts — and separates interpreting the standard itself and preparing the designation application to a Gyoseishoshi.

"We meet the standard's numbers, but how do we build shifts that don't fall below when someone is on leave or in training?" and "If we place night support in the residential type, what happens to working hours and premium pay?" — these are questions we often hear from labour staff who open and run a self-reliance training office. This page organises the labour design — full-time equivalents, social insurance, working-hours management and work rules — on the premise of the office's staffing. Whether the standard is met and whether the application succeeds is the domain of the designating authority (prefecture, municipality, designated city, etc.) and application practice; this article focuses on the labour thinking.

## In labour terms, how do you read the self-reliance training staffing standard?

Self-reliance training is two types — living training and functional training — within the training-type benefits of the Act on Comprehensive Support for Persons with Disabilities. The number of staff is set, type by type, in separate articles of the standard (Ministerial Ordinance No. 171 of 2006). The outline is as follows (fine points and handling change with reform and local operation, so final conformity is confirmed with the designating authority's guidance).

| Type | Main roles and approach |
|---|---|
| Functional training (Article 156, para. 1, item 1) | The **combined** nursing staff, physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech-language-hearing therapists and living support workers, at a full-time equivalent of **at least the number of users divided by 6**. Nursing staff, therapists and living support workers are each 1 or more per office |
| Functional training (same, item 2) | Service manager: 1 or more for 60 users or fewer; above 60, plus 1 for each 40 (or fraction) over 60 |
| Living training (Article 166, para. 1, item 1) | Living support workers, at a full-time equivalent of at least the sum of **daytime users divided by 6** and **residential-type self-reliance training users divided by 10** |
| Living training (same, items 2–3) | Where the residential type is run, a community-transition support worker, 1 or more. The service manager is the same approach as functional training |
| Manager | In principle full-time and dedicated (may hold concurrent duties within a range causing no hindrance) |

The point to grasp is that **most of the staffing standard is set not by "headcount" but by "full-time equivalent — the total volume of working hours."** That is exactly why how shifts are built directly governs conformity. Alongside this, **functional training requires at least one each of nursing staff, living support workers and the service manager to be full-time** (Article 156, paragraphs 6–8), and **living training requires at least one each of the living support workers and the service manager to be full-time** (Article 166, paragraphs 6 and 7) — tying directly to employment contracts and rosters. What a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles is not "the number to place" but "how to build the work that meets that number" — shift design, employment contracts, work rules and wage regulations, working-hours management, and social-insurance acquisition and loss. Interpreting the standard and preparing the designation application are a Gyoseishoshi's work, governed by different laws.

## How do you count full-time equivalents, and how far can you use part-timers?

The full-time-equivalent number is **the total scheduled working hours of the office's staff divided by the hours a full-time staff member should work.** The required numbers for functional and living training are met by this thinking, as a "total volume of working hours." The full-time-equivalent counting common to disability welfare and care is organised in [how do you calculate full-time equivalents in disability welfare and care](/en/labor/column/shogai-fukushi-kaigo-jokin-kansan-keisan-jinin).

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Formula | FTE = (total scheduled hours of all staff) ÷ (hours a full-time worker should work) |
| Full-time hours | Prescribed hours set in work rules (e.g. 40/week). The ceiling under Article 32 is 8/day, 40/week |
| Part-time staff | Counted into the total by their actual hours; they count by time, not by head |
| Full-time requirement | Even if the full-time equivalent is met, there is a separate requirement that "at least one of each role be full-time," which part-timers alone cannot satisfy |
| Fractions/margin | Build with a margin so as not to fall below even when hours drop for leave, absence or training |

Note that **the full-time equivalent is seen by the total volume of working hours, not by headcount.** One full-time worker at 40 hours and two part-timers at 20 hours each are the same "1." On the other hand, **the full-time requirement (at least one of each role) cannot be substituted by time.** Even if you thicken part-timers to meet the full-time equivalent, falling short of a full-time worker can be a shortfall against the standard. How far you can use part-timers is decided on both faces — "the total full-time-equivalent volume" and "the full-time requirement" — and that is the starting point of labour design.

## From which staff does social insurance and employment insurance apply?

Staff social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) and labour insurance (employment insurance and workers' accident insurance) have thresholds set by laws separate from the staffing standard. A corporate office is in principle a compulsorily covered workplace for health insurance and employees' pension. Workers' accident insurance in principle covers every worker you employ, including part-timers.

| Insurance | Main threshold for coverage |
|---|---|
| Health insurance / employees' pension (general) | Those whose prescribed weekly hours and monthly working days are **roughly three-quarters or more** of a regular worker's ([details of the three-quarters test](/en/labor/column/tanjikan-koyo-shakaihoken-4bunno3)) |
| Health insurance / employees' pension (short-time) | At specified covered workplaces (including enterprises with at least 51 insured employees), the usual criteria include 20 or more scheduled hours a week, expected employment of more than two months and, in general, not being a student. See the pay exception below. |
| Employment insurance | Those with 20+ prescribed weekly hours and a prospect of 31+ days of employment (Employment Insurance Act, Article 6) |
| Workers' accident insurance | In principle every worker employed (the employer handles the procedure collectively) |

Here, **short-time workers' social-insurance coverage is widening in stages.** According to Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare materials, the company-size (insured-person) requirement is scheduled to fall from 51 or more to 36 or more (October 2027), 21 or more (October 2029) and 11 or more (October 2032), and be abolished in October 2035. The minimum monthly scheduled pay of ¥88,000 ceased to be a general eligibility condition on October 1, 2026. An employee with authorized minimum-wage reduction and scheduled pay below ¥88,000 a month is normally excluded but can apply to join. Self-reliance training offices often use many part-time living support workers, so **check at opening whether the design will bring staff into coverage later as the size requirement falls**, even if they are out of scope now. The whole staged picture is organised in [the expansion of social-insurance coverage (from 2027)](/en/labor/column/shakaihoken-tekiyo-kakudai-2027).

## Where night or visiting support is involved, how do you design working-hours management?

Residential-type self-reliance training may place community-transition support workers at night, and daytime functional/living training may visit users' homes to provide training. Where working-hours management trips up:

| Issue | Labour handling |
|---|---|
| Night work | Work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. carries a late-night premium (Labor Standards Act, Article 37; 25% or more). As long as it is actual work, it is subject to wages |
| On-call / day duty | If the posture is one of almost no work as a rule, it may be treated as on-call/day duty with the labour standards inspection office head's permission (Article 41, item 3, and Article 23 of the Act's enforcement regulations). Working at night as normal duty without permission is subject to the premium |
| Travel time for visits | Travel under the office's direction can be working time. Record the distinction between visit, travel and training on the roster |
| Variable working-hours system | If there is busy/slack by day or week, fit it within statutory hours with a monthly-unit (Article 32-2) or yearly-unit (Article 32-4) variable system. For choosing, see [how do you choose between a variable working-hours system and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata) |
| Breaks | 45 minutes over 6 hours, 1 hour or more over 8 hours (Article 34). If there is a duty to respond during a break, it can be hand-waiting time and thus working time |

The misunderstanding that calling night work "on-call" removes the premium is dangerous. **On-call permission presupposes a posture of almost no work in reality,** and if support work is routine at night it is subject to the premium as ordinary working time. The distinction between night shift and on-call at group homes and the like is organised in [how do night shifts and on-call duty differ](/en/labor/column/group-home-yakin-shukuchoku-roudou-chigai). To have staff work overtime or on holidays, you must conclude and file a 36 Agreement (Article 36); the ceilings are in principle 45 hours/month and 360 hours/year.

## What should you decide in work rules and employment contracts before opening?

To keep meeting the staffing standard, fixing the framework of work in work rules and employment contracts in advance is key. Before opening, decide:

- **Preparation and filing of work rules.** At establishments that regularly employ 10 or more workers, there is a duty to prepare and file them (Article 89). Even under 10, setting rules on hours, days off and wages stabilises operation. For how many workers, see [from how many workers must you prepare and file work rules](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo)
- **Notice of working conditions.** At the employment contract, state prescribed hours, days off, wages, whether renewed, etc. in writing (Article 15). For part-time living support workers, make clear in the contract the actual hours counted into the full-time equivalent
- **The full-time/part-time split and the full-time-requirement allocation.** Decide whose contract meets the "at least one of each role full-time" requirement
- **Rules for the variable system and night support.** For the residential type, reflect the night work system, whether there is on-call, and how premiums are calculated in the wage regulations
- **A structure to record work results objectively.** Keep start/end times, breaks and the duty distinction (when concurrent) on rosters and time cards, so full-time-equivalent conformity can be verified later

The extinctive prescription of a wage claim is 3 years for the time being (Article 115 and Supplementary Provisions), and an error in premium calculation risks three years' worth being claimed together. The more an office has night and variable work, the more getting the premium design right is a safeguard.

## FAQ

**Q. Can you staff self-reliance training with part-timers alone?**
A. The total full-time-equivalent volume can be met with part-timers' actual hours, but there is a separate full-time requirement that "at least one of each role be full-time" (functional training: Ministerial Ordinance No. 171 of 2006, Article 156, paragraphs 6–8; living training: same Ordinance, Article 166, paragraphs 6 and 7). You cannot meet it with part-timers alone without placing full-time staff, so decide first whose contract meets the full-time requirement. This is a matter of employment-contract and shift design, within a Shakai Hoken Roumushi's range.

**Q. Can the full-time equivalent be met just by headcount?**
A. No. The full-time equivalent is seen by the total volume of working hours — the total scheduled hours of all staff divided by the hours a full-time worker should work. When total hours fall due to paid leave, absence or training, there is a risk of falling below the standard, so a shift design with a margin is a premise.

**Q. If residential night support is treated as on-call, is premium pay unnecessary?**
A. It can be treated as on-call only where the posture is one of almost no work in reality and the labour standards inspection office head's permission is obtained (Labor Standards Act, Article 41, item 3, and Article 23 of the enforcement regulations). If support work is routine at night, it is ordinary working time, and 10 p.m.–5 a.m. is subject to the late-night premium (Article 37; 25% or more). A design matched to the reality is needed.

**Q. Can conformity with the staffing standard and the labour work be entrusted to the same person?**
A. Because the governing laws differ, they are considered separately. Interpreting the staffing standard and preparing the designation application are for a Gyoseishoshi; shift design, work rules, working-hours management and social insurance are for a Shakai Hoken Roumushi. At Yotsuba, 四葉行政書士事務所 and 四葉社会保険労務士事務所 are independent business entities, dividing the work under separate contracts.
