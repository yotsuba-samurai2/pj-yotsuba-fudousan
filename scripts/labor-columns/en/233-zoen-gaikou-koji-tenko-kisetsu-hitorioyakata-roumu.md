**Bottom line first**: Landscaping and exterior work is outdoors, so three issues overlap: rain cancellations, seasonal busy-slack swings, and how the one-person master works. ## How is pay handled on a day when work is interrupted or canceled by rain?

In outdoor landscaping and exterior work, rain cancellations are unavoidable. How pay is handled splits on "whose fault the cause is" and "how the wage is set (monthly or daily)."

Labour Standards Act Article 26 requires, for a leave of absence due to a reason attributable to the employer, a leave allowance of at least 60 percent of average wages. Weather such as rain, snow, or typhoons is generally treated as force majeure (akin to a natural disaster) and not a reason attributable to the employer, so in that case no leave allowance is owed. Still, even the same "rain cancellation" may be assessed as the employer's responsibility if the employer's own circumstances pile on, such as poor scheduling or late delivery of materials, and the conclusion changes with the facts. Under a monthly wage with no absence deduction, the wage is paid as is; under a daily wage or daily-monthly wage, a day not worked is unpaid in principle (no work, no pay).

| How the wage is set | Principle on a rain-cancellation day |
|---|---|
| Monthly wage (no absence deduction) | Wage paid as is (no deduction) |
| Daily wage / daily-monthly wage | A day not worked is unpaid in principle (no work, no pay) |
| Leave due to a reason attributable to the employer | Leave allowance of 60% or more of average wages (Article 26) |

Writing the treatment of rain-cancellation days (whether standby is ordered or the day is shifted) into the work rules and wage rules in advance prevents confusion on site. Whether a particular case is a reason attributable to the employer is determined by a qualified professional after a consultation.

## How do you level spring-autumn peaks and winter slack with the one-year unit variable working-hours system?

Landscaping and exterior work concentrates in spring and autumn for planting and pruning, and drops in deep winter. This seasonal swing can be leveled by reorganizing scheduled working hours with the one-year unit variable system.

Labour Standards Act Article 32-4 allows, within a range where the weekly hours averaged over a period of more than one month and up to one year do not exceed 40, having workers work beyond 40 hours in a particular week and beyond 8 hours on a particular day. Using it, hours can be set heavier in the busy season and lighter in the slack season. But a labor-management agreement must be concluded and filed, hours may not exceed 10 a day and 52 a week, and where the period exceeds three months the number of workdays must be kept within 280 a year and consecutive workdays within six in principle (Enforcement Regulation Article 12-4). Even with the variable system, hours beyond the annual total, and night and holiday premium wages, arise separately.

| Main frame of one-year variable | Content (basis) |
|---|---|
| Labor-management agreement | Conclusion and filing mandatory (Article 32-4) |
| Daily / weekly cap | 10 hours a day, 52 a week (Enforcement Regulation Article 12-4) |
| Workdays where period exceeds 3 months | Within 280 a year |
| Consecutive workdays | Within six in principle |
| Average | Within 40 hours a week averaged over the period |

It is also important not to confuse this with industries excluded from working-hours rules, such as agriculture (Article 41, item 1). Landscaping is not agriculture, and the working-hours rules apply as usual. The thinking on one-year variable hours in construction is set out in [the 2024 construction time-limit rules and holidays](/en/labor/column/kensetsugyo-2024-jikangai-jougen-kyujitsu-roumu).

## How does a gardener working as a one-person master prepare for workers' accident insurance?

On landscaping sites, a gardener (one-person master) who takes on work alone without being employed is not unusual. A one-person master is not a worker and so is outside ordinary workers' accident insurance, but there is a route called special enrollment.

Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act Article 33 sets out those eligible for special enrollment, and the "one-person masters, etc." of items 3 and 4 may take Type 2 special enrollment under Article 35. It covers a one-person master who performs the construction business (building, remodeling, preserving, repairing, altering, demolishing, or dismantling civil-engineering and architectural and other structures, or the preparatory work therefor) without using workers, and landscaping work is treated as included in this construction business. Indeed, landscaping cooperatives in various places are listed in the Labour Bureau's registry as special-enrollment associations for construction one-person masters (checked 27 November 2026). Special enrollment is taken through an approved association, not directly by an individual. Whether a landscaping one-person master is eligible and which association to use are best confirmed with the association or the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office.

| Item | Basis / content |
|---|---|
| Those eligible for special enrollment | Industrial Accident Insurance Act Article 33, items 3 and 4 = one-person masters, etc. |
| Special enrollment of one-person masters | Article 35 = Type 2 special enrollment (through an association) |
| Position of landscaping | Included in the construction business (building and preserving structures, etc.) |
| Entry point | Approved special-enrollment association (landscaping cooperative, etc.); confirm with the Inspection Office |

The whole picture of social insurance and workers' accident insurance when a construction business hires workers is set out in [construction social insurance enrollment and the one-person master's workers' accident special enrollment](/en/labor/column/kensetsugyo-shakaihoken-hitorioyakata-rosai-tokubetsu). ## What should you do for heat-illness measures in outdoor work, now mandatory since 2025?

Midsummer landscaping and exterior work carry a high risk of heat illness. From 1 June 2025, heat-illness measures became an employer's duty for certain heat-exposed work. Outdoor landscaping sites can fall within scope.

Industrial Safety and Health Regulation Article 612-2 (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare Ordinance No. 57 of 2025, promulgated 15 April 2025 and in force 1 June 2025) made it a duty, for heat-exposed work meeting certain requirements, to put in place a system to detect early the workers at risk of heat illness, to create procedures for when symptoms suggesting heat illness arise, and to make these known to the workers concerned. Specifically, work carried out continuously where the WBGT (heat index) is at or above a certain level is in scope, and the duty is to turn emergency contacts and transport to a medical institution into a system usable on site. Violations engage Industrial Safety and Health Act Articles 22 and 119, item 1 (the specific type and ceiling of the penalty are unverified in this article). The scope of covered work and operation are covered in [the duty to take heat-illness measures and the on-site system](/en/labor/column/necchusho-taisaku-gimuka-2025-06-genba-roumu).

| Pillar of the duty | Content (Regulation Article 612-2) |
|---|---|
| Detection system | A mechanism to detect early the workers at risk of heat illness |
| Response procedures | Create procedures for treatment, emergency contact, and transport when symptoms arise |
| Notification | Make the system and procedures known to the workers concerned |

Outdoor landscaping work must grasp that, on top of the traditional measures of stopping work, securing breaks, and replenishing water and salt, this system-building is now required.

## Is landscaping work subject to the construction working-hours limit?

The landscaping trade is one of the construction businesses included in the construction-license trade classification (Construction Business Act, Appended Table 1). It is therefore subject to the overtime limit that applied to construction from April 2024.

The penalty-backed overtime limit under the work-style reform legislation applied to large enterprises from 1 April 2019 and to SMEs from 1 April 2020, and to construction from 1 April 2024 after a five-year grace period. The principle is 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year (42 a month and 320 a year for one-year variable hours where the period exceeds three months), and even with a special clause there are frames of 720 hours a year, under 100 hours in a single month, and an average of 80 hours or less over multiple months (Labour Standards Act Article 36, paragraphs 4 to 6). As a construction business, landscaping too must arrange its Article 36 agreement within these frames.

| Item | Content (Article 36) |
|---|---|
| Principle limit | 45 hours/month, 360 hours/year (42/month, 320/year for one-year variable) |
| Special-clause ceiling | 720 hours/year, under 100 hours in a single month, average 80 hours or less over multiple months |
| Start of construction application | 1 April 2024 (after a five-year grace) |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. On a day work is canceled by rain, must we pay the gardener a leave allowance?**
A. Not necessarily. The leave allowance under Labour Standards Act Article 26 arises for a leave due to "a reason attributable to the employer." A cancellation caused by weather such as rain or a typhoon is generally treated as force majeure (akin to a natural disaster) and not attributable to the employer, so in that case there is no obligation to pay a leave allowance. The assessment can change, however, if the employer's own circumstances such as poor scheduling pile on. The treatment also differs between monthly and daily wages, so it helps to set the treatment of rain-cancellation days in the work rules and wage rules.

**Q. If we introduce the one-year unit variable working-hours system, can we work people any number of hours in the busy season?**
A. No. The one-year unit variable working-hours system (Article 32-4) requires a labor-management agreement to be concluded and filed, may not exceed 10 hours a day and 52 hours a week, must keep workdays within 280 a year where the period exceeds three months, and limits consecutive workdays to six in principle (Enforcement Regulation Article 12-4). It is a system that keeps hours within an average of 40 a week, and hours beyond the total, and night and holiday premium wages, arise separately.

**Q. Can a gardener who takes on work alone enroll in workers' accident insurance?**
A. A one-person master is not a worker and so is outside ordinary workers' accident insurance, but there is a framework called special enrollment. The one-person masters, etc. under Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act Article 33, items 3 and 4 may take Type 2 special enrollment under Article 35. Landscaping work is treated as included in the construction business, so enrollment can be considered through a construction one-person-master special-enrollment association (landscaping cooperative, etc.). Whether you are eligible and which association to use should be confirmed with the association or the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office.

**Q. Is the landscaping trade also subject to the construction overtime limit?**
A. It is. The landscaping trade is a construction business included in the construction-license trade classification, and the overtime limit (Article 36) has applied since 1 April 2024. The principle is 45 hours a month and 360 a year, and even with a special clause there are frames of 720 hours a year, under 100 hours in a single month, and an average of 80 hours or less over multiple months. Arranging the Article 36 agreement is for the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant; obtaining the construction license is for the administrative scrivener (an independent entity, contracted separately).
