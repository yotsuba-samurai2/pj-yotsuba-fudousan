**Bottom line first**: Unlike agriculture, forestry is not within the Labour Standards Act Article 41(i) exemption. The working-hours, break and holiday rules apply in principle, and felling, clearing hung-up trees and bucking with a chainsaw require special education (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 36(viii)). Permits are a Gyoseishoshi's area, registration a judicial scrivener's and tax a tax accountant's.

The assumption that "agriculture is exempt from working-hours rules, so forestry must be the same" leads to unpaid overtime and accidents. For forestry operators doing timber production, afforestation and felling, forest co-operatives, and owners who have incorporated and begun hiring, this article sets out what must be observed in working hours, safety and health, and workers' compensation, with the contrast against agriculture as the axis.

## Is forestry exempt from the working-hours rules like agriculture?

No. Agriculture, livestock, sericulture and fisheries are exempt from the working-hours, break and holiday rules under Labour Standards Act Article 41(i), but forestry is outside that exemption. So in forestry the statutory hours of 8 a day / 40 a week (Article 32), breaks (Article 34) and holidays (Article 35) apply in principle, and to work beyond them you need a filed Article 36 labour-management agreement and premium pay for overtime and holiday work (Article 37).

This is the mirror image of the agriculture article ([agriculture is exempt from working hours](/en/labor/column/nogyo-roudoujikan-jogai-41jo-tokutei-ginou-roumu)). The same pattern of work that triggers no overtime premium in agriculture does trigger premium pay in forestry.

## Why is forestry left out of the Article 41 exemption?

Article 41(i) exempts those engaged in the businesses in Appended Table 1 items 6 and 7. Item 6 reads "cultivation or clearing of land, or the planting, cultivation, gathering or felling of plants, and other agriculture/forestry businesses," but the item carries a **parenthetical "excluding forestry,"** so forestry workers are not in the exemption.

| Business | Exempt under Art. 41(i)? | Working-hours / holiday rules |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture (cultivation, etc.) | Table 1 item 6 (excluding forestry) — **exempt** | Do not apply (no overtime premium) |
| Livestock, sericulture, fisheries | Table 1 item 7 — **exempt** | Do not apply |
| **Forestry (felling, afforestation, timber production)** | **Expressly excluded** from item 6 — not exempt | **Apply in principle** (Art. 36 agreement and premiums needed) |

Forestry resembles agriculture in depending on weather and season, but because it involves mechanised felling and yarding with high accident risk, the working-hours framework is kept the same as for ordinary businesses. Forestry also has no special overtime cap like construction or drivers (the 960-hours-a-year type set out in [construction's overtime cap](/en/labor/column/kensetsugyo-2024-jikangai-jougen-kyujitsu-roumu)); the ordinary cap applies (the 45-hours-a-month / 360-hours-a-year principle).

## What special education or qualifications do felling and bucking require?

Dangerous and harmful work requires completion of special education before the work (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 59(3); Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 36). The core one in forestry is the special education for chainsaw felling work, consolidated by an amendment effective 1 August 2020.

| Work | Basis | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Felling, clearing hung-up trees and bucking of standing trees using a chainsaw | Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Art. 36(viii) | The 1 Aug 2020 amendment **merged two trainings divided by trunk diameter into a single special education regardless of diameter**. Felling without a chainsaw was taken out of scope |
| Operation of travelling yarders, simple cable yarding systems, yarding machines, etc. | Related items of Art. 36 | Special education needed for operating the machines used in yarding and hauling |
| Wearing lower-limb cut-protection clothing | Industrial Safety and Health Regulations (amendment effective 1 Aug 2019) | In chainsaw felling work, the employer must **have workers wear** protective clothing, and workers must wear it |

Chainsaw work also raises vibration disorder, and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Guidelines on Chainsaw Handling Work" (notice Kihatsu 0710 No. 1 of 10 July 2009) sets the choice of anti-vibration machines, inspection and maintenance, and management of daily work time (a guide based on daily vibration exposure). The specific items and cycle of the health check for vibration work vary by case, so this article gives no individual conclusion (**unverified**).

## How do you manage work hours that shift with season and weather?

Forestry has large busy/slack swings and weather interruptions, so a flat 8 hours a day fits poorly. Since the working-hours rules apply in principle, a one-year unit variable working-hours system (Labour Standards Act Article 32-4) or a one-month unit system (Article 32-2) can absorb the swings within the limits: set longer scheduled hours in the busy season and shorter in the slack season to keep premiums down within the total. See [choosing a variable working-hours system](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata).

Even with a variable system, the statutory total, the overtime cap, the late-night premium (at least 25% for 10 p.m.–5 a.m.; Article 37(4)) and annual paid leave (Article 39) remain. You need a labour-management agreement and work rules, with start and finish times specified; work rules must be drawn up and filed at 10 or more regular staff (Article 89). Rule in advance the wage treatment of a day cut short by weather (where the leave allowance under Article 26 can come into play).

## What accidents are common in forestry, and how are compensation and reporting handled?

Forestry has the highest casualty rate per thousand workers a year of any industry. The Forestry Agency's materials put forestry's 2022 rate at 23.5, about ten times the all-industry 2.3, with most fatal accidents occurring during felling. Workers' compensation applies regardless of nationality, residence status or form of employment, and work-related injury or illness draws medical and leave (compensation) benefits.

| Item | Basis / content |
|---|---|
| Workers' compensation coverage | Workers' Accident Compensation Insurance Act. Covered once you employ even one worker; benefits irrespective of nationality or status |
| Casualty report | Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Art. 97. A casualty with 4+ days off reported to the Labour Standards Inspection Office without delay (under 4 days reported periodically) |
| Safety and health education for foreign workers | Education in the native language or plain Japanese matters ([safety education for foreign workers](/en/labor/column/gaikokujin-anzeneisei-kyoiku-bogoku-roudo-saigai-boshi)) |
| Sole proprietors / operators | Non-workers (sole proprietors, operators) are not covered; forestry sole proprietors may be eligible for special enrolment |

The claim procedure and the flow of medical and leave benefits are set out in [workers' compensation procedures](/en/labor/column/rousai-tetsuzuki-shishobyo-houkoku). Where a person is employed by more than one company in a side job, the aggregation of benefits is the issue in [workers' compensation for multiple-employment workers](/en/labor/column/fukusu-jigyo-roudosha-rosai-kyufu-fukugyo).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is forestry, like agriculture, free from paying overtime?**
A. No. Agriculture, livestock, sericulture and fisheries are exempt from the working-hours, break and holiday rules under Article 41(i), but item 6 of Appended Table 1 carries a parenthetical excluding forestry. These rules apply to forestry in principle, so working beyond 8 hours a day / 40 a week needs a filed Article 36 agreement, and overtime and holiday work draw premium pay (Article 37).

**Q. Do you need a qualification or training to fell trees with a chainsaw?**
A. Yes. Felling, clearing hung-up trees and bucking standing trees with a chainsaw require completion of the special education under Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 36(viii). The amendment effective 1 August 2020 merged the two trainings previously divided by trunk diameter into one. Wearing lower-limb cut-protection clothing in felling work is also mandatory.

**Q. The swing between busy and slack seasons is large; 8 hours a day doesn't work. What can we do?**
A. A one-year or one-month unit variable working-hours system (Labour Standards Act Articles 32-4 and 32-2) lets you set longer scheduled hours in the busy season and shorter in the slack season, keeping premiums down within the total. It requires a labour-management agreement and work rules, and the late-night premium, annual leave and the overtime cap remain even under a variable system.
