## Does on-call (call standby) time count as working time, and how do you design the allowance?

Because a birth can start at any hour, a birth center cannot avoid on-call (call standby). Whether standby time is working time is fixed objectively by the actual restraint, not by what the arrangement is called.

Whether something is working time is fixed objectively by whether the worker's conduct can be assessed as being under the employer's command, not by the work rules or the contract (Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Nagasaki Shipyard case, Supreme Court judgment of 9 March 2000). Waiting at the workplace where immediate response is required ("time on hand") is working time because the worker is not released from work. By contrast, on-call standby at home, where one may spend time freely except for responding to a call, may be held not to be working time if release from work can be assessed as guaranteed. There is case law denying working-time status for a resident's off-site on-call standby, on the ground that calls were infrequent and attendance brief (Chiba District Court judgment of 22 February 2023). That said, where calls are frequent, prompt arrival is required, and conduct is strongly restricted, the conclusion can change. Time spent actually responding to a call and working is, of course, working time.

| Form of standby | Treatment as working time (general outline) |
|---|---|
| Time on hand at the workplace (immediate response required) | Counts as working time |
| At-home on-call (free to spend time, calls infrequent) | May be held not to be working time |
| Time actually spent working on a call | Counts as working time |

Even where standby itself is not working time, setting an on-call allowance (standby allowance) as compensation for the restraint is unobjectionable. Whether to treat standby as working time, or how to reward it by allowance, is best clarified in the work rules and wage rules. Whether a particular standby is working time is determined by a qualified professional after a consultation.

## Does night or day duty at a birth center need Labour Office permission, and what must it meet?

Where a birth center with inpatient facilities places someone overnight, the calculation of working hours differs depending on whether it is treated as ordinary work or as night duty. To have night or day duty performed outside the statutory working-hours frame, permission from the head of the Labour Standards Inspection Office is required.

Labour Standards Act Article 41, item 3 provides that the provisions on working hours, breaks, and days off do not apply to a person engaged in monitoring or intermittent work who has received permission from the administrative agency. The permission criteria for night and day duty rest on Enforcement Regulation Article 23, with details set out by administrative notices. The main requirements are that it be after complete release from the restraint of ordinary working hours, that the work during night or day duty be limited to light or short-time work, and that in the case of night duty sufficient sleep can be had at night. The rough guide for frequency is once a week for night duty and once a month for day duty. Even with permission, time spent during night duty on work of the same nature as ordinary duty requires the Article 36 procedure for overtime and payment of the premium wage (Article 37). While separate detailed criteria exist for physicians' night and day duty, a midwife's night and day duty follows the general criteria.

| Main requirement for night/day-duty permission | Content (Enforcement Regulation Article 23, notices) |
|---|---|
| Complete release | After complete release from the restraint of ordinary working hours |
| Degree of work | Limited to light or short-time work |
| Securing sleep | Night duty allows sufficient sleep at night |
| Frequency guide | Night duty once a week, day duty once a month |
| Application | Form No. 10 to the head of the Inspection Office |

The thinking on night- and day-duty permission in medical and care settings is set out in [the criteria for medical and care night- and day-duty permission](/en/labor/column/iryo-kaigo-shukujitchoku-kyoka-kijun). Where the reality comes to exceed the scope of the permission, fresh permission may be required.

## How does a midwife in solo practice prepare for labor insurance?

While an opening midwife handles deliveries or visiting midwifery alone, she is not a "worker" but a business owner. Labor insurance (workers' accident insurance and employment insurance) is a system for workers, so she is in principle outside it.

Workers' accident insurance covers workers used in a business, so it does not reach a solo-practice midwife herself who employs no workers. Employment insurance too is a system for workers, and a business owner is not an insured person. During this time, the midwife's own illness, injury, and old age are prepared for in principle with National Health Insurance and the National Pension. Special enrollment in workers' accident insurance for work injuries covers categories of workers that the law lists exhaustively, such as construction one-person masters, and there is no one-person-master category ready-made for a midwife. However, if she hires midwives or part-timers so the business becomes covered by labor insurance and entrusts the affairs to a labor-insurance affairs association, the owner herself may consider special enrollment of small-business owners, etc. (Type 1) (Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act Article 33, items 1 and 2, and Article 34). Which framework the owner can use requires confirmation on the premise of whether she employs and whether she uses an affairs association.

| Owner's position | Labor / social insurance preparation |
|---|---|
| Solo practice (no workers employed) | Outside workers' accident and employment insurance; prepare with National Health Insurance and the National Pension |
| Hires workers and entrusts to an affairs association | May consider special enrollment of small-business owners, etc. (Type 1) (Act Article 33, items 1 and 2, Article 34) |

In a solo practice, where hours tend to run long, health management itself is a premise of running the business. Arranging the way of working and designing insurance can be discussed with the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant.

## Once you start hiring midwives or part-timers, from where do social and employment insurance begin?

The moment you start hiring staff, the system changes completely from when you were alone. Note that the entry point (the conditions on number and industry) differs between labor insurance and social insurance.

Workers' accident insurance applies compulsorily regardless of industry once even one worker is hired, with premiums fully borne by the employer. Employment insurance makes a worker an insured person where requirements such as scheduled weekly hours of 20 or more are met. Health insurance and employees' pension insurance (social insurance), on the other hand, apply compulsorily to an individual birth center too where five or more regular workers are used, because the medical business is one of the 17 statutory industries. An individual business with fewer than five regular workers is voluntarily covered and can become a covered workplace if the owner applies with the consent of at least half the employees and receives approval. From October 2029, an amendment is planned to apply coverage in principle at five or more regular workers regardless of industry (the 2025 pension-reform act, with a transitional measure for existing workplaces for the time being). Where the birth center is run as a corporation, it becomes a covered workplace regardless of the number.

| Insurance | Entry point |
|---|---|
| Workers' accident insurance | Compulsory once even one worker is hired (all industries; fully employer-borne) |
| Employment insurance | Insured where requirements such as 20+ scheduled weekly hours are met |
| Health / employees' pension | Individual birth center is medical (a 17-industry) so compulsory at 5+ regular workers; under 5 is voluntary; a corporation is covered regardless of number |

The thinking on labor in the medical field, such as the physicians' working-hours limit, is also covered in [the physicians' overtime limit and health assurance](/en/labor/column/isha-jikangai-jougen-2024-suijun-kenko-kakuho). The industry classification and the number of regular workers are best confirmed finally with the competent Pension Office and Labour Standards Inspection Office.

## How do you arrange the partnership system and procedures when you yourself take leave for childbirth?

Where the opening midwife herself takes leave for pregnancy and childbirth, two things must be arranged: how to run the birth center (securing people) and the midwife's own protection during leave (procedures). Both benefit from advance design.

Securing people is handled by labor design. Staff shifts, arranging a substitute midwife, and the operating system during leave are set in the work rules and labor-management arrangements. The owner's own protection during leave varies with the medical insurance she is enrolled in. If she is an insured person of health insurance she may be eligible for the maternity allowance, but National Health Insurance has in principle no maternity allowance (the lump-sum birth allowance is payable). The system of a contracted doctor and partner hospital, on the other hand, is a matter the birth center must arrange under the Medical Care Act, and medical judgment and whether to provide care are the realm of the doctor and the medical institution. The birth center's opening notification and Medical Care Act procedures on the contracted doctor and partner hospital (notification to the prefectural governor within ten days of opening, Medical Care Act Article 8) are for the administrative scrivener and the medical institution; the property and facility requirements are for the real estate agent and architect, each contracted separately as an independent entity. There is no referral fee. Medical judgment on midwifery itself is left to the qualified professional.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is all the time I am on home on-call standby working time and subject to wages?**
A. Not uniformly. Whether it is working time is fixed objectively by whether one can be assessed as under the employer's command (Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Nagasaki Shipyard case, Supreme Court judgment of 9 March 2000). On-call standby at home, where one may spend time freely except for responding to a call, has been held not to be working time where release from work is guaranteed (Chiba District Court judgment of 22 February 2023). On the other hand, time on hand at the workplace with required immediate response, and time actually spent working on a call, are working time. Even where standby is not working time, you can reward it with an on-call allowance (standby allowance).

**Q. If we place staff at the birth center overnight, can we treat it as night duty?**
A. To have night or day duty performed outside the statutory working-hours frame, permission from the head of the Labour Standards Inspection Office is required (Labour Standards Act Article 41, item 3, and Enforcement Regulation Article 23). Permission requires that it be after complete release from the restraint of ordinary duty, that the work be limited to light or short-time work, and, for night duty, that sufficient sleep can be had at night; the frequency guide is once a week for night duty and once a month for day duty. Even with permission, time spent during night duty on work of the same nature as ordinary duty requires the Article 36 agreement and the premium wage (Article 37).

**Q. Can a midwife in solo practice enroll in workers' accident insurance?**
A. While employing no workers in solo practice, the owner is a business owner, not a worker, and so is outside workers' accident insurance and employment insurance; during this time she prepares with National Health Insurance and the National Pension. There is no one-person-master special-enrollment category ready-made for a midwife, but if she hires staff so the business becomes covered by labor insurance and entrusts the affairs to a labor-insurance affairs association, the owner herself may consider special enrollment of small-business owners, etc. (Type 1) (Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act Article 33, items 1 and 2, and Article 34).

**Q. Once I hire staff, from when does social insurance apply?**
A. The entry point differs by insurance. Workers' accident insurance applies compulsorily regardless of industry once even one worker is hired; employment insurance makes a worker insured where requirements such as 20+ scheduled weekly hours are met. Health insurance and employees' pension, because the medical business is one of the 17 statutory industries, apply compulsorily to an individual birth center too at five or more regular workers, and under five is voluntary (a corporation is covered regardless of number). The industry classification and number of regular workers are best confirmed with the competent Pension Office and Labour Standards Inspection Office.
