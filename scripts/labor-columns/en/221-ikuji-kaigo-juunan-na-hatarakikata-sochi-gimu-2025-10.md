**Bottom line first**: Under the amended Child Care and Family Care Leave Act effective 1 October 2025, an employer must choose and provide two or more of five flexible-working measures for workers raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school (Article 23-3, paragraph 1). When choosing, the employer must give the majority union or representative an opportunity to state an opinion, set the chosen measures in the work rules, and give targeted workers individual notice and a confirmation of their intention to use them.

## What exactly are the measures for flexible working?

The Child Care and Family Care Leave Act (Act No. 76 of 1991) was amended by Act No. 42 of 2024 (promulgated 31 May 2024). From 1 October 2025, for a worker raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school, the employer must provide "measures to realize flexible working" (Article 23-3, paragraph 1). The employer chooses and provides two or more of the following five, and the targeted worker may then choose one of the provided measures to use. One alone is not enough, and all five are not required.

| Measure | Content and numeric threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Change of start time, etc. | Flextime, staggered hours, etc. | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 1; Ordinance |
| (2) Telework, etc. | Available 10 or more days a month | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 2; Ordinance |
| (3) Child-care facilities, etc. | Setting up and running child-care facilities or equivalent benefits | Measure set by Ordinance |
| (4) Child-raising leave | Granted 10 or more days a year (excluding nursing-care leave, family-care leave and annual paid leave) | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 4; Ordinance |
| (5) Shortened working hours | A measure shortening prescribed working hours for childcare | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 3 |

A point easy to confuse is the telework-type measure for workers raising a child under 3. That is an effort duty effective 1 April 2025 and is a separate system from the flexible-working measures (a legal duty) for age 3 to the start of elementary school. The age band and whether it is a duty or an effort duty differ. For the whole amendment organized by effective date, see [what changed in the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act in 2025](/en/labor/column/ikuji-kaigo-kyugyo-ho-2025-kaisei); for the duty to prepare work rules itself, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## Which employees and child ages fall within scope?

The scope is a worker raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school. That runs from the day the child turns 3 to the end of the year before the child enters elementary school. Before the child turns 3, the worker falls under the shortened-hours measure for children under 3 (Article 23); at age 3 the available systems switch.

| Division | Child's age | Main system |
|---|---|---|
| Under 3 | Birth to the day before the 3rd birthday | Shortened-hours measure, etc. (Article 23, duty); telework, etc. (effort duty) |
| Age 3 to start of elementary school | 3rd birthday to the start of elementary school | Measures to realize flexible working (Article 23-3; two or more; duty) |

The range of workers who may be excluded also needs checking. Day laborers are outside the scope. By a labor-management agreement, a worker employed for less than one year, or one with a markedly small number of prescribed working days a week (as set by Ordinance), may be excluded in certain cases. Workers in scope must be tracked by the child's age in practice, and a procedure arises each time someone qualifies.

## How do you decide to choose two or more of the five options?

Which two (or more) to choose is up to the employer according to the realities of the work, but the choosing process has a statutory step. When choosing the measures, the employer must give the majority union (or, where there is none, a person representing the majority) an opportunity to state an opinion. The employer cannot decide unilaterally, skipping the hearing.

| Viewpoint | What to check |
|---|---|
| Nature of the work | If much work can be done at home, (2) telework fits; for on-site work, (1) start-time change or (5) shortened hours fit |
| Number and working style of those in scope | Few: the burden of designing a system; many: securing substitute staff |
| Relation to existing systems | Where flextime or shortened hours already exist, whether they can be repositioned as a measure |
| Continuity of operation | A chosen measure is set in the rules and continued; do not choose by ease of introduction alone |

Once provided, the measures are set in the work rules or their equivalent and made known to targeted workers. Measures with numeric thresholds — (2) telework (10 or more days a month) and (4) child-raising leave (10 or more days a year) — must be set to meet the threshold, or the measure is not deemed provided. The final judgment of which measure suits the company must be made after checking the realities of the work and the situation of those in scope.

## What must be added to the individual hearing and the work rules?

Flexible-working measures are not complete just by revising the rules. At an appropriate time before the child turns 3, there is a duty to give individual notice of the provided measures and to confirm the intention to use them (Article 23-3, paragraph 5). There is also a duty to hear the worker's intentions on balancing work and childcare and to give consideration to those intentions (Article 23-3, paragraph 7, applying Article 21, paragraph 2). This is new practice from 1 October 2025 and must be organized by distinguishing what to do in each situation.

| What to do | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Individual notice | Give individual notice of the content, point of application and conditions of the provided flexible-working measures | Article 23-3, paragraph 5 |
| Intention confirmation | Confirm whether the targeted worker intends to use a measure | Article 23-3, paragraph 5 |
| Hearing of intentions | Hear individually about working time band, work location, period of using support systems, workload, working conditions, etc. | Article 23-3, paragraph 7 (applying Article 21, paragraph 2) |
| Consideration | The employer examines its response in light of the intentions heard | Article 21, paragraph 3 |

Individual notice and intention confirmation are done by interview (including online), delivery of a document, fax, email, etc., and fax/email only where the worker so wishes. They must not be done in a way that discourages taking or using the measure. "Consideration" is not a duty to grant exactly the conditions the worker wishes; it means examining a response in light of the intentions, and where it is hard to meet them, a careful response such as explaining the reason matters. In the work rules, add for each chosen measure the persons in scope, content, usable period, procedure for applying and the point of application; a workplace with 10 or more regular workers must file with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office (Labor Standards Act, Article 89).

## What are the risks of not providing the measures?

Flexible-working measures are a legal duty, not an effort duty. If not performed, the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (the power delegated to the head of the Prefectural Labour Bureau) may require a report, or give advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56). Where an employer who received a recommendation does not follow it, the Minister may publish that fact (Article 56-2). The publication covers a violation of Article 23-3. Further, a person who fails to make the report under Article 56, or makes a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66).

| Stage | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Advice, guidance, recommendation | The Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) seeks correction | Article 56 |
| Publication | If the recommendation is not followed, the fact can be published | Article 56-2 |
| Civil fine | A person who fails to report, or reports falsely, up to 200,000 yen | Article 66 |

Publication means the company name becomes known externally, with unavoidable effects on hiring and transactions. A flawed system is also a source of individual labor disputes. Note that dismissal or other disadvantageous treatment on the ground of applying for or using a measure is prohibited. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can the company choose any two of the five measures it likes?**
A. The employer chooses two or more, but when choosing must give the majority union (or, where there is none, the majority representative) an opportunity to state an opinion (Article 23-3, paragraph 1; Ordinance). The company cannot decide unilaterally, skipping the step. Which to choose is judged by the realities of the work and the situation of those in scope — whether much work is done at home, whether it is on-site work, whether existing flextime or shortened hours can be repositioned. (2) Telework must be 10 or more days a month and (4) child-raising leave 10 or more days a year; unless set to meet these in the work rules, the measure is not deemed provided.

**Q. If we already have a shortened-hours system, does that satisfy the duty?**
A. The flexible-working measures are a duty to provide two or more of the five. Shortened hours (5) alone is just one; you must choose and provide one or more others. Position the existing shortened hours as one, add at least one from start-time change, telework and so on to make a system of two or more, set it in the work rules and notify those in scope. Note that the under-3 shortened-hours measure (Article 23) is a different system, so the switch at age 3 must also be organized.

**Q. If no targeted employee applies, can we skip revising the work rules?**
A. No. The flexible-working measures are a duty on the employer to provide two or more in advance, regardless of whether a targeted worker applies. Even where there is no current target, you must set the system in the work rules so it can be used when a worker raising a child from age 3 to the start of elementary school arises. And once a target arises, give individual notice and confirm the intention to use at an appropriate time before the child turns 3 (Article 23-3, paragraph 5).

**Q. If we do not provide the measures, is there an immediate fine?**
A. There is no immediate penalty. First the Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) requires a report, or gives advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56), and if the recommendation is not followed, can publish that fact (Article 56-2). However, failing to make the report under Article 56, or making a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66). Publication makes the company name known externally and affects hiring and transactions. Whether a particular case qualifies, and how to correct it, must be judged after checking the company's own situation.
