**Bottom line first**: Under the amended Child Care and Family Care Leave Act effective 1 October 2025, an employer must choose and provide two or more of five flexible-working measures for workers raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school (Article 23-3, paragraph 1). The employer must first hear the views of the union representing a majority at the workplace, or the workers’ majority representative if no such union exists (Article 23-3, paragraph 4). It must then set the chosen measures in the work rules or an equivalent document, and give targeted workers individual notice and a confirmation of their intention to use them.

## What exactly are the measures for flexible working?

The Child Care and Family Care Leave Act (Act No. 76 of 1991) was amended by Act No. 42 of 2024 (promulgated 31 May 2024). From 1 October 2025, for a worker raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school, the employer must provide "measures to realize flexible working" (Article 23-3, paragraph 1). The employer chooses and provides two or more of the following five, and the targeted worker may then choose one of the provided measures to use. One alone is not enough, and all five are not required.

| Measure | Content and numeric threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Change of start time, etc. | Flextime or staggered start or finish times without changing daily scheduled hours | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 1; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-2 |
| (2) Telework, etc. | At least 10 working days a month for employees scheduled to work five days a week. For other schedules, the threshold depends on regular or average scheduled days per week | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 2; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-3, paragraph 1 |
| (3) Child-care facilities, etc. | Setting up and running childcare facilities or providing equivalent support | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 5; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-4 |
| (4) Work-and-childcare support leave | At least 10 working days a year (not child nursing and related leave, family-care leave or annual paid leave) | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 4; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-3, paragraph 4 |
| (5) Shortened working hours | Must include an option with six scheduled hours a day in principle | Article 23-3, paragraph 1, item 3; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-3, paragraph 3 |

A point easy to confuse is the telework-type measure for workers raising a child under 3. That is an effort duty effective 1 April 2025 and is a separate system from the flexible-working measures (a legal duty) for age 3 to the start of elementary school. The age band and whether it is a duty or an effort duty differ. For the whole amendment organized by effective date, see [what changed in the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act in 2025](/en/labor/column/ikuji-kaigo-kyugyo-ho-2025-kaisei); for the duty to prepare work rules itself, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## Which employees and child ages fall within scope?

The scope is a worker raising a child from age 3 until the start of elementary school. That runs from the day the child turns 3 to the end of the year before the child enters elementary school. Before the child turns 3, the worker falls under the shortened-hours measure for children under 3 (Article 23); at age 3 the available systems switch.

| Division | Child's age | Main system |
|---|---|---|
| Under 3 | Birth to the day before the 3rd birthday | Shortened-hours measure, etc. (Article 23, duty); telework, etc. (effort duty) |
| Age 3 to start of elementary school | 3rd birthday to the start of elementary school | Measures to realize flexible working (Article 23-3; two or more; duty) |

The range of workers who may be excluded also needs checking. Day laborers are outside the scope. By a labor-management agreement, a worker employed for less than one year, or a worker scheduled for two days or fewer per week (Article 23-3, paragraph 3, items 1 and 2; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-6), may be excluded in certain cases. Workers in scope must be tracked by the child's age in practice, and a procedure arises each time someone qualifies.

## How do you decide to choose two or more of the five options?

Before introducing the measures, the employer must hear the views of the union representing a majority of workers at that workplace. If there is no such union, it must hear the workers’ majority representative (Article 23-3, paragraph 4). Hearing their views is not the same as obtaining their consent.

| Viewpoint | What to check |
|---|---|
| Nature of the work | If much work can be done at home, (2) telework fits; for on-site work, (1) start-time change or (5) shortened hours fit |
| Number and working style of those in scope | Few: the burden of designing a system; many: securing substitute staff |
| Relation to existing systems | Where flextime or shortened hours already exist, whether they can be repositioned as a measure |
| Continuity of operation | A chosen measure is set in the rules and continued; do not choose by ease of introduction alone |

Once provided, the measures are set in the work rules or an equivalent document and made known to targeted workers. Measures with numeric thresholds — option (2) telework (at least 10 working days a month for a five-day week; different schedules are treated according to their scheduled days) and option (4) work-and-childcare support leave (at least 10 working days a year) — must be set to meet the threshold, or the measure is not deemed provided. The final judgment of which measure suits the company must be made after checking the realities of the work and the situation of those in scope.

## What must be added to the individual hearing and the work rules?

During the one-year period ending one month before the child’s third birthday, the employer must notify the worker individually of the measures and ask whether they intend to use them (Article 23-3, paragraph 5; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-8). Separately, it must hear the worker’s preferences about working conditions that would help them combine work and childcare, and consider those preferences when setting those conditions (Article 23-3, paragraph 6 applies Article 21, paragraphs 2 and 3). The duty to hear and consider preferences about working conditions also applies when the worker reports their or their spouse’s pregnancy or childbirth, or an equivalent event (Article 21, paragraphs 2 and 3).

| What to do | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Individual notice | Explain the selected measures, where to apply, and the systems for limiting work beyond scheduled hours, overtime and late-night work | Article 23-3, paragraph 5; Enforcement Regulation, Article 75-9 |
| Intention confirmation | Confirm whether the targeted worker intends to use a measure | Article 23-3, paragraph 5 |
| Hearing of intentions | Hear individually about working time band, work location, period of using support systems, workload, working conditions, etc. | Article 23-3, paragraph 6 (applying Article 21, paragraph 2) |
| Consideration | Consider the worker’s stated preferences when setting their working conditions | Article 23-3, paragraph 6 (applying Article 21, paragraph 3) |

Individual notice and intention confirmation are done by interview (including online), delivery of a document, fax, email, etc., and fax/email only where the worker so wishes. They must not be done in a way that discourages taking or using the measure. "Consideration" is not a duty to grant exactly the conditions the worker wishes; it means examining a response in light of the intentions, and where it is hard to meet them, a careful response such as explaining the reason matters. In the work rules, add for each chosen measure the persons in scope, content, usable period, procedure for applying and the point of application; a workplace with 10 or more regular workers must file with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office (Labor Standards Act, Article 89).

## What are the risks of not providing the measures?

Flexible-working measures are a legal duty, not an effort duty. If not performed, the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (the power delegated to the head of the Prefectural Labour Bureau) may require a report, or give advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56). Where an employer who received a recommendation does not follow it, the Minister may publish that fact (Article 56-2). The publication covers a violation of Article 23-3. Further, a person who fails to make the report under Article 56, or makes a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66).

| Stage | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Advice, guidance, recommendation | The Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) seeks correction | Article 56 |
| Publication | If the recommendation is not followed, the fact can be published | Article 56-2 |
| Civil fine | A person who fails to report, or reports falsely, up to 200,000 yen | Article 66 |

Publication means the company name becomes known externally, with unavoidable effects on hiring and transactions. A flawed system is also a source of individual labor disputes. Employers must not dismiss or otherwise disadvantage a worker because the worker requested a measure, was provided with a measure, or expressed a particular preference about working conditions (Article 23-3, paragraph 7). There is no referral fee. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can the company choose any two of the five measures it likes?**
A. The employer picks two or more measures. Before introducing them, it must hear the views of the union representing a majority at that workplace, or the majority representative if there is no such union (Article 23-3, paragraph 4). Which to choose is judged by the realities of the work and the situation of those in scope — whether much work is done at home, whether it is on-site work, whether existing flextime or shortened hours can be repositioned. For option (2), telework must be available for at least 10 working days a month on a five-day schedule. On other schedules, the threshold depends on scheduled days. Option (4), work-and-childcare support leave, requires at least 10 working days a year. The selected measures must meet the applicable thresholds.

**Q. If we already have a shortened-hours system, does that satisfy the duty?**
A. The flexible-working measures are a duty to provide two or more of the five. Shortened hours (5) alone is just one; you must choose and provide one or more others. Position the existing shortened hours as one, add at least one from start-time change, telework and so on to make a system of two or more, set it in the work rules or an equivalent document and notify those in scope. Note that the under-3 shortened-hours measure (Article 23) is a different system, so the switch at age 3 must also be organized.

**Q. If no targeted employee applies, can we skip revising the work rules?**
A. No. The flexible-working measures are a duty on the employer to provide two or more in advance, regardless of whether a targeted worker applies. Even where there is no current target, you must set the system in the work rules or an equivalent document so it can be used when a worker raising a child from age 3 to the start of elementary school arises. In the one-year period ending one month before the child’s third birthday, notify the worker and confirm whether they intend to use a measure (Article 23-3, paragraph 5). Separately, hear their preferences about working conditions and consider them when deciding those conditions (Article 23-3, paragraph 6, applying Article 21, paragraphs 2 and 3).

**Q. If we do not provide the measures, is there an immediate fine?**
A. There is no immediate penalty. First the Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) requires a report, or gives advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56), and if the recommendation is not followed, can publish that fact (Article 56-2). However, failing to make the report under Article 56, or making a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66). Publication makes the company name known externally and affects hiring and transactions. Whether a particular case qualifies, and how to correct it, must be judged after checking the company's own situation.
