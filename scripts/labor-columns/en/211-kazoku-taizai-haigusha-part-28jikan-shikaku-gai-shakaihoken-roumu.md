**In short:** To hire the spouse of a "Dependent" resident as a part-timer, **first check on the residence card whether they hold permission to engage in activity outside their status (in principle within 28 hours a week, excluding adult-entertainment work), then design the working hours and social insurance.** Up to 28 hours a week is covered by a comprehensive permission, and the total across several workplaces must stay within 28 hours (Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Article 19, paragraph 2; its Enforcement Regulations, Article 19, paragraph 5). For social insurance, in October 2026 the wage requirement (monthly 88,000 yen, the so-called "1.06-million-yen wall") was abolished, so a worker who meets requirements such as 20 hours a week becomes a Health Insurance and Employees' Pension insured person regardless of wage (2025 Pension Reform Act). Because 28 hours exceeds 20 hours, at a company with more than 51 insured persons the spouse generally becomes insured rather than remaining a dependent. This page sets out how to count the hours, the relation to the 1.3-million-yen dependent wall, the effect on the visa, and the division of roles.

This page is for the HR and labour staff of companies that wish to hire, as a part-timer, the spouse (residence status "Dependent") of a foreign employee working under a status such as "Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services" or "Highly Skilled Professional." Whether the residence status fits and whether the activity-outside-status permission is granted are decisions of the Immigration Services Agency; what the company does is check the residence card and put working conditions and social insurance in place.

## Can the spouse of a Dependent resident not work without permission for activity outside their status?

Correct — in principle they cannot. **The "Dependent" residence status is for the daily life of a spouse or child who is supported, and income-earning work is not allowed as it stands.** To work, the person must obtain "permission to engage in activity outside the scope permitted by their status of residence" (Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Article 19, paragraph 2).

There are two kinds: a **comprehensive permission** that allows a certain range without specifying each activity, and an individual permission that specifies the activity. The usual case of hiring a part-timer is the comprehensive permission, and the back of the residence card carries a note such as "Permitted (in principle within 28 hours a week; excluding work in adult-entertainment businesses)." At hiring, the company checks this note on the residence card. For checking the card and the practice of not facilitating illegal work, see [Checking the residence card and the crime of promoting illegal work](/en/labor/column/zairyu-card-kakunin-fuho-shuro-josei-koyonushi-gimu).

| Where to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Front of the residence card | That the status is "Dependent" and the period of stay has not expired |
| Back of the residence card | That the activity-outside-status field reads "Permitted (in principle within 28 hours a week; excluding adult-entertainment work)" |
| Passport / designation document | For an individual permission, the scope of activity and hours |

Letting someone work without permission can expose the employer to the crime of promoting illegal work (same Act, Article 73-2). The company must always confirm that permission exists.

## How do you count the 28 hours a week (multiple workplaces, long holidays)?

The cap for a comprehensive permission is **the total of all activity outside status, within 28 hours a week** (Enforcement Regulations of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Article 19, paragraph 5). If the person holds several jobs, the total must stay within 28 hours; it is not enough that your company alone is within 28 hours.

A point often confused here is the difference from international students.

| Item | Spouse on "Dependent" status | International student |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly cap | Within 28 hours a week (all year) | Within 28 hours a week |
| Long-holiday exception | **None** (28 hours all year) | Yes (up to 8 hours a day during the school's long holidays) |
| Adult-entertainment work | Not allowed | Not allowed |

International students have an exception allowing up to 8 hours a day during the school's long holidays, but **"Dependent" status has no such exception; it is 28 hours a week all year, including summer and year-end.** The time management of students is in [Managing the student 28-hour week](/en/labor/column/ryugakusei-arubaito-28jikan-kanri). Decide in advance which day the "week" starts on, so that shifts spanning a week boundary do not exceed the cap.

## What happens to social insurance at 28 hours (the 1.06-million-yen wall was abolished in October 2026)?

**In October 2026, the wage requirement for short-hours workers to join social insurance (monthly 88,000 yen, the so-called "1.06-million-yen wall") was abolished** (2025 Pension Reform Act). As a result, regardless of wage, a short-hours worker who meets the following requirements becomes a Health Insurance and Employees' Pension insured person.

| Requirement | Content (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|
| Scheduled weekly hours | 20 hours or more |
| Expected employment | More than 2 months |
| Not a student | In principle, daytime students are excluded |
| Company size | More than 51 insured persons (to be lowered in stages from October 2027) |

The spouse on "Dependent" status can work up to 28 hours a week, but **28 hours exceeds 20 hours a week.** So if the workplace has more than 51 insured persons and the person is expected to be employed for more than 2 months, they generally become a Health Insurance and Employees' Pension insured person even on a low wage. The old design of "stay under 1.06 million yen and remain a dependent" no longer holds at companies with more than 51 insured persons. For the abolition of the 1.06-million-yen wall and what the company does, see [The abolition of the 1.06-million-yen wall](/en/labor/column/106man-no-kabe-teppai-jigyousha-yaru-koto); for the overall picture of the expansion, see [The expansion of social insurance](/en/labor/column/shakaihoken-tekiyo-kakudai-2027). The company-size requirement is to be lowered in stages from October 2027, and the final decision on insured status is made by the insurer (Japan Pension Service, health insurance society).

## What way of working lets them stay a dependent (the 1.3-million-yen wall)?

To remain a **dependent** of the insured foreign employee rather than becoming insured themselves, the person must meet the dependent-certification standard under the Health Insurance Act. The core of the standard is **an annual income of less than 1.3 million yen** and, in principle, less than half the insured person's annual income (Health Insurance Act, Article 3, paragraph 7; the dependent-certification notice).

| Way of working | Social-insurance result |
|---|---|
| More than 51 insured persons, 20+ hours a week, over 2 months | The person is insured (regardless of wage; from October 2026) |
| 50 or fewer insured persons, or under 20 hours a week | Not subject to short-hours coverage; can remain a dependent if annual income is under 1.3 million yen |
| Annual income of 1.3 million yen or more | Falls out of dependent status (joins National Health Insurance / National Pension, etc.) |

So if you want to keep them as a dependent, the way of working becomes **choosing a workplace with 50 or fewer insured persons, or keeping it under 20 hours a week**, while staying under 1.3 million yen in annual income. Dependent certification on "Dependent" status also has other points, such as the domestic-residence requirement. See [Foreign dependents and the domestic-residence requirement](/en/labor/column/fuyousha-kokunai-kyoju-gaikokujin). The 1.3-million-yen test looks at expected annual income including commuting allowance and bonuses, so where there are monthly fluctuations, confirm with the insurer.

## How do work and income affect the person's visa renewal and the continuation of dependency?

Note here that **social-insurance dependency and the residence-status (visa) requirements are separate things.**

Social-insurance dependent certification is judged by the Health Insurance Act standard (1.3 million yen, etc.). The "Dependent" residence status, on the other hand, presumes **living while being supported** by the spouse who holds the principal residence status. Working within the scope of the activity-outside-status permission (within 28 hours a week) is itself no problem, but if the income or the reality of the work departs greatly from the premise of "being supported," an explanation may be sought at the renewal of the period of stay.

That said, no uniform monetary standard such as "income above a certain figure means the Dependent status cannot be renewed" is published; it is a decision of the Immigration Services Agency on the individual circumstances. **Because there is no uniform standard that can be verified, this article does not assert one.** Whether the status fits and the outlook for renewal are best confirmed with a gyoseishoshi (administrative-procedures specialist) qualified to submit applications. Working beyond the scope of the permission raises not only the person's residence but also the company's promotion-of-illegal-work issue, so the 28-hour management is done by both the company and the person.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can we hire, as a part-timer, a Dependent resident whose residence card shows no activity-outside-status permission?**
A. If a comprehensive permission has been granted, the activity-outside-status field on the back of the residence card carries a note such as "Permitted (in principle within 28 hours a week; excluding adult-entertainment work)." If there is no such note, the person may not hold permission or may have an individual permission, and letting them work as is can expose the employer to the crime of promoting illegal work (Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Article 73-2). Always confirm before hiring, and when unsure, check with a gyoseishoshi qualified to submit applications or with the Immigration Services Agency.

**Q. At our company it is 20 hours a week, but combined with another job it exceeded 28 hours. Is that a problem?**
A. The cap for a comprehensive permission is the total of all workplaces, within 28 hours a week (Enforcement Regulations of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Article 19, paragraph 5). If the combined total exceeds 28 hours, it goes beyond the scope of activity outside status and can be a problem for both the person's residence and the company's promotion of illegal work. Do not judge by your company's share alone; confirm the person's work situation at other workplaces and manage it.

**Q. From October 2026, can we keep them as a dependent by hiring under 1.06 million yen?**
A. Because the wage requirement (monthly 88,000 yen, the "1.06-million-yen wall") was abolished in October 2026, hiring at a company with more than 51 insured persons for 20+ hours a week and an expected period of over 2 months makes the person a Health Insurance and Employees' Pension insured person even under 1.06 million yen. To keep them as a dependent, choose a workplace with 50 or fewer insured persons or keep it under 20 hours a week, and stay under 1.3 million yen in annual income. The final insured status and dependent certification are decided by the insurer.

**Q. If they stay within the social-insurance dependent range (1.3 million yen), is the visa renewal safe?**
A. Social-insurance dependent certification (1.3 million yen, etc.) is a Health Insurance Act standard, separate from the renewal standard for the "Dependent" residence status. Working within the scope of the permission (within 28 hours a week) is itself no problem, but if the income or the reality of the work departs greatly from the premise of "being supported," an explanation may be sought at renewal. No uniform monetary standard is published, so confirm the renewal outlook with a gyoseishoshi qualified to submit applications.
