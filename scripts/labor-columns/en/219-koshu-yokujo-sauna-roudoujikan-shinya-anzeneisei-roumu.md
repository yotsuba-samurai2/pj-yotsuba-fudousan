**Bottom line first**: Saunas, super-sento and bathhouses tend to open long hours and year-round, so working time is built on a monthly (Labor Standards Act Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hour system, with the provisions in the work rules and the labor-management agreement as validity requirements. Work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. carries the night premium (Article 37, paragraph 4, 25% or more), and if a shift allowance is to cover it, the wage rules must show it distinctly. In high-heat areas, the heat-illness measures effective 1 June 2025 (Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health, Article 612-2) require a reporting structure and procedures to be in place and made known. Those under 18 cannot in principle work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. (Article 61).

## What system do you use to build working time for a sauna or public bathhouse?

Article 32 of the Labor Standards Act sets the statutory limit at 40 hours a week and 8 hours a day. A sauna, super-sento or bathhouse opens long hours, from the morning bath to the late-night sauna, and opens on weekends and holidays too, so the busy periods swing widely by day and by week. The variable working-hour system distributes working time to match these swings.

| System | Basis | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly variable working-hour system | Article 32-2 | Averaged over a period of up to one month to 40 hours a week, you may work over 8 hours on specified weeks and days. Set it in the work rules or a labor-management agreement; file it if by agreement |
| Yearly variable working-hour system | Article 32-4 | Averaged over a target period of over one month and up to one year to 40 hours a week. Set the scope of workers, target period, working days and hours per day in a labor-management agreement and file it with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office |

Note that the weekly non-fixed variable working-hour system (Article 32-5) is limited to certain industries. Article 12-5, paragraph 1 of the Enforcement Ordinance of the Labor Standards Act limits its target businesses to "retail, inns, restaurants and eating houses" with fewer than 30 regular workers. The bath business is not on this list, so for a sauna or public bathhouse you build working time around the monthly or yearly variable system. For how to choose, see [choosing between variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata). Because how these documents are set is itself a requirement for the system's validity, an omission makes the variable system void and the excess is all recalculated as overtime. For the basic duty to prepare work rules, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## How do you design the night premium and shift system?

Work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. carries a statutory night premium. Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act provides that where work is done in this band, a premium of 25% or more of the ordinary wage must be paid. This is required even for scheduled work, and it is a statutory duty separate from whether the company voluntarily sets a "night allowance" or "shift allowance."

| Kind of work | Premium | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Night (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) | 25% or more | Article 37, paragraph 4 |
| Overtime (over statutory hours) | 25% or more | Article 37, paragraph 1 (Cabinet Order) |
| Overtime extending into the night | 50% or more combined | Article 37, paragraphs 1 and 4 |
| The part of monthly overtime over 60 hours | 50% or more | Article 37, paragraph 1 proviso |

If you pay a shift allowance to run the facility at night and it is to cover the night premium, the wage rules must show distinctly that the allowance includes the equivalent of the night premium and that it does not fall below the premium for the actual night-work hours. Its name alone does not extinguish the duty. For the variable system and how premiums overlap when running on shifts, see [shift work in manufacturing: variable working hours and the night premium](/en/labor/column/seizogyo-kotai-kinmu-henkei-roudoujikan-shinya-roumu). To have workers do overtime you must conclude an Article 36 agreement and file it; its ceiling is set out in [how far overtime can go under an Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). The regional minimum wage, the floor for wages, applies regardless of the time band (Minimum Wage Act), and the night premium is added on top of the minimum-wage amount.

## What health management and breaks are needed in a hot, humid work environment?

Sauna rooms and bathing rooms are hot and humid, and staff may enter them for cleaning, inspection or loyly service. The amendment effective 1 June 2025 made heat-illness measures mandatory for certain hot-work tasks.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare and make known a reporting structure | Set up in advance a structure by which a worker with symptoms, or one who suspects heat illness in another, can report, and make it known to the workers concerned | Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health, Article 612-2, paragraph 1 (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare Ordinance No. 57 of 2025, effective 1 June 2025, based on Article 22 of the Industrial Safety and Health Act) |
| Prepare and make known worsening-prevention procedures | Set, per workplace, procedures such as leaving the work, cooling the body and a physician's examination and treatment as needed, and make them known to the workers concerned | Same Article, paragraph 2 |

The target benchmark is a WBGT (heat index) of 28 or more, or an air temperature of 31 or more, for work continuing an hour or more or exceeding four hours a day (Circular Kihatsu 0520 No. 6). For how to set this up, see [the heat-illness measures made mandatory from June 2025](/en/labor/column/necchusho-taisaku-gimuka-2025-06-genba-roumu).

A point easy to misread is work-environment measurement. The work-environment measurement for a "hot indoor workplace" under Article 65 of the Industrial Safety and Health Act and Article 21, item 2 of the Enforcement Order (measuring temperature, humidity and so on once every half-month) is limited to the workplaces listed in Article 587 of the Ordinance (blast furnaces, glass melting, drying rooms and the like), and saunas and public bathhouses are not on this list. So being a sauna does not in itself trigger the statutory work-environment measurement; you design around the heat-illness measures (Article 612-2) and general duty of care. For breaks, Article 34 of the Labor Standards Act requires at least 45 minutes for work over 6 hours and 1 hour for work over 8 hours, given during working time. Securing cool-down time before and after hot work is desirable but is organized separately from statutory breaks. A worker constantly engaged in work that includes night work is subject to the specified-work examination (on assignment and once every six months) under Article 45, paragraph 1 and Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3 (ヌ) of the Ordinance. For the basics of examinations, see [the duty, cost and time of periodic health examinations](/en/labor/column/teiki-kenko-shindan-jisshi-gimu-hiyo-jikan).

## How far can workers under 18 work, by night and by time?

Public bathhouses and saunas often use student part-timers, and those under 18 (minors) carry added protection. Article 61, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act provides that a person under 18 must not be used between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. It is not enough to pay the night premium; employing them at all in that band is prohibited.

| Division | Basis | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Ban on night work | Article 61, paragraph 1 | In principle no work 10 p.m.–5 a.m.; but a male aged 16 or over on shift work is excluded |
| Non-application of the variable system and the Article 36 agreement | Article 60, paragraph 1 | Overtime and holiday work under an Article 36 agreement and the variable working-hour systems in principle cannot be used |
| Keeping proof-of-age documents | Article 57, paragraph 1 | Keep a family-register certificate proving age at the workplace |

With a shift-work business's permission from the administrative agency, there is room to work them until 10:30 p.m. and from 5:30 a.m. (Article 61, paragraph 3), but the shift-work proviso for night work applies only to males aged 16 or over. Women and those under 16 cannot be made to work at night even on shift. Note that even being a few minutes past 10 p.m. for closing work means you cannot keep a minor in that band. The limits on minors' hours, night work and hazardous work are set out in detail in [the night and overtime limits for part-timers under 18](/en/labor/column/nenshousha-18sai-miman-shinya-jikangai-seigen-roumu).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. We open year-round from early morning to late night. How should we build working time?**
A. The practical way is a monthly (Labor Standards Act Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hour system that distributes working days and hours to match the swings in trade. You set a provision averaging to 40 hours a week over a period of up to one month, or over one month to one year, in the work rules or a labor-management agreement, and file the monthly or yearly agreement with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office. The weekly non-fixed variable system (Article 32-5) is limited to retail, inns, restaurants and eating houses with fewer than 30 workers, so the bath business is excluded. Because how it is set is a validity requirement, an omission voids the system and the excess is recalculated as overtime.

**Q. If we pay staff who work at night a shift allowance, do we still need a separate night premium?**
A. Yes. The night premium is a statutory premium under Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act, and work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. must be paid at 25% or more above the ordinary wage. A shift or night allowance is one the company sets voluntarily, and its name alone does not extinguish the night-premium duty. If a shift allowance is to cover the night premium, the wage rules must show distinctly that the allowance includes the equivalent of the night premium and that it does not fall below the premium for the actual night-work hours.

**Q. The sauna room is hot. Do we need the statutory work-environment measurement?**
A. Saunas and public bathhouses are not among the "hot indoor workplaces" listed in Article 587 of the Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health (blast furnaces, glass melting, drying rooms and the like), so they do not, as a rule, fall under the once-every-half-month measurement of temperature and humidity in Article 607. On the other hand, the heat-illness measures effective 1 June 2025 (Article 612-2) require a reporting structure and worsening-prevention procedures for work at WBGT 28 or more, or 31 or more degrees, expected to continue an hour or more or exceed four hours a day. Check whether cleaning and inspection work entering the sauna room qualifies in light of the actual work. Whether a particular case qualifies must be checked for each site's work content.

**Q. Can a high-school part-timer help with the sauna's closing work?**
A. If under 18, they cannot in principle work from 10 p.m. under Article 61, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act. Being even a few minutes past 10 p.m. for closing work falls within "between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m." Unless it fits an exception in the text — such as a shift-work business with the administrative agency's permission, or a male aged 16 or over on shift — you cannot keep them in that band. Place the shift's end time before 10 p.m. as the default. And even with an Article 36 agreement, you cannot use it as a basis to make a minor do overtime or holiday work (Article 60, paragraph 1).

- Labor Standards Act, Article 57, paragraph 1 — keeping a family-register certificate proving the age of a person under 18; Article 60, paragraph 1 — Articles 32-2 to 32-5 and Article 36 etc. do not apply to those under 18; Article 61, paragraph 1 — "an employer must not use a person under 18 between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m.; provided that this does not apply to a male aged 16 or over used on shift work"; paragraph 3 — a shift-work business, with the administrative agency's permission, until 10:30 p.m. and from 5:30 a.m.; Article 89 — the duty to prepare and file work rules (10 or more regular workers)
