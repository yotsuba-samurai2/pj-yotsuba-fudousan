**In short:** A workplace that comes to regularly use **50 or more workers** must, all at once, appoint a **health officer (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 12), an industrial physician (Article 13), and set up a health committee (Article 18).** For all three, "regularly 50 or more" is the line in the Industrial Safety and Health Act Enforcement Order (Articles 4, 5, and 9), counted per establishment (the unit of the place where people work, not the company). The health officer and industrial physician are **appointed within 14 days** of the event that requires appointment, and the appointment report is filed without delay with the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations, Articles 7 and 13). The health committee is set up and held **at least once a month**, its proceedings made known, and records kept for 3 years (Article 23). This page sets out how to count 50, the number and qualification of health officers, the appointment and duties of the industrial physician, the committee's composition, and the destination and deadline of the filings.

This page is for general-affairs and HR staff of a workplace whose headcount grows toward, or reaches, regularly 50. It sets out the safety-health structure that arises on reaching 50, from a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint. Medical judgment and interview guidance are the domain of the industrial physician and physicians; what the company does is appointment, filing, and building the structure.

## How do you count "regularly 50," and when does the duty arise?

"Regularly 50 or more" is counted **not across the whole company but per establishment (the unit of each place of work — head office, branch, factory, store).** Regularly used workers include not only regular staff but part-timers and casual workers used as a matter of ordinary course.

| Point of counting | Thinking |
|---|---|
| Unit | Per establishment (by place), not per company |
| Who is included | Regular staff, plus part-timers etc. used as a matter of ordinary course |
| Time it arises | The day of reaching regularly 50 or more is "the day the event requiring appointment arises" |

The duty begins from the day you reach 50. At a workplace under 50, the health officer, industrial physician, and health committee are not legal duties; instead there is the "safety-health promoter etc." system of choosing someone to handle safety-health work. At a workplace under 50, a stress check is for now an effort duty, and the move to review that is covered in [From when is a stress check mandatory under 50](/en/labor/column/stress-check-50nin-miman-2028). Because counting dispatched workers and the like can need individual judgment, it is safest to prepare early as an increase approaches 50.

## How many health officers, with which qualification, and by when must you appoint them?

A health officer is **appointed at a workplace of regularly 50 or more, from among persons dedicated to that workplace** (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 12; Enforcement Order Article 4). The number rises with the scale of the workplace (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 7).

| Number of regularly used workers | Number of health officers |
|---|---|
| 50 to 200 | 1 or more |
| Over 200 to 500 | 2 or more |
| Over 500 to 1,000 | 3 or more |
| Over 1,000 to 2,000 | 4 or more |
| Over 2,000 to 3,000 | 5 or more |
| Over 3,000 | 6 or more |

The qualification is, in principle, a person holding a **health officer licence (Class 1 or Class 2), a physician, a dentist, a labour-health consultant, and the like.** In industries such as agriculture-livestock-fishery, mining, construction, manufacturing, transport, medical, and cleaning, a Class 1 health officer licence etc. is required; in other industries a Class 2 holder may also be appointed. Appointment is made **within 14 days** of the day the event requiring appointment arises (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 7, paragraph 1, item 1). The health officer patrols the workplace etc. at least once a week and manages the technical matters of health. If there is no licence-holder in-house, plan ahead to sit the licence exam or to use an outside licence-holder.

## To whom do you entrust the industrial physician, and what duties do they bear?

The industrial physician is **appointed at a workplace of regularly 50 or more**, from among physicians with the requirements set by Ministry ordinance (such as completing the prescribed training) (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 13; Enforcement Order Article 5; Regulations Article 14). You find one via the local medical association, a medical institution, or a body that can introduce an industrial physician. The company hires or entrusts the physician; introducing the qualified person itself is not this office's work.

| Main duties of the industrial physician | Content |
|---|---|
| Health checkups and after-measures, interview guidance | Opinions on employment measures from the results; interview guidance for long-hour workers etc. |
| Maintaining the work environment, work management | Checking the health state, investigating causes of health harm and preventing recurrence |
| Health education, health consultation and management | Measures to maintain and promote workers' health |
| Workplace patrol | In principle at least once a month (relaxable to once every 2 months under certain conditions) |

A dedicated industrial physician is required at a workplace of regularly 1,000 or more (500 or more for certain hazardous work). Most workplaces of 50 or more entrust a non-dedicated (part-time) industrial physician. Interview guidance for long-hour workers and high-stress persons is the physician's medical work; the company builds the structure to carry it out. The whole picture of the health-checkup duty is set out in [The duty to conduct periodic health checkups, costs, and time](/en/labor/column/teiki-kenko-shindan-jisshi-gimu-hiyo-jikan).

## Who do you make committee members of the health committee, and how many times a month do you hold it?

The health committee is **set up at a workplace of regularly 50 or more** (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 18; Enforcement Order Article 9) and held **at least once a month** (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 23). It is a place where labour and management investigate and deliberate on preventing workers' health harm and promoting health.

| Committee composition | Content |
|---|---|
| Chair | 1 person designated by the employer from the overall safety-health manager or a person who oversees the operation of the business |
| Health officer | Designated by the employer from among the appointed health officers |
| Industrial physician | Designated by the employer from among the appointed industrial physicians |
| Worker experienced in health | Designated from workers engaged in the actual work |

Half of the members other than the chair are **designated on the recommendation of the majority union (or, failing one, the majority representative)** (Article 18, paragraph 4). Each time it is held, the gist of the proceedings is made known to workers, and a record is made and kept for 3 years. Where a safety committee is also required by industry and scale, the safety committee and the health committee can be run together as a "safety and health committee." Rather than holding it as a formality, it should be made a place of substantive deliberation — long-hour-work measures, use of the group analysis of stress checks, and so on.

## Where and by when do you file the appointment report?

Once the health officer and industrial physician are appointed, you **file the appointment report without delay with the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office** (Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 7, paragraph 2; Article 13, paragraph 2). The form is the "Overall Safety-Health Manager / Safety Manager / Health Officer / Industrial Physician Appointment Report."

| Filing | Destination | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Health officer appointment report | Head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office | Without delay after appointment (within 14 days of the event) |
| Industrial physician appointment report | Head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office | Without delay after appointment |
| Health committee | No filing required (set-up, monthly holding, record-keeping are the duties) | At least once a month |

The health committee's set-up and operation are duties, but there is no filing of the set-up itself. The health officer and industrial physician, however, require an appointment report. The report needs prescribed attachments such as a copy of the licence. On reaching 50, it is realistic to proceed with appointment, entrustment, committee set-up, and filing in parallel. Note that the starting point of the deadline is "the day you reach regularly 50 or more."

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is "regularly 50" counted across the whole company, or per establishment?**
A. Per establishment (the unit of the place of work — head office, branch, factory, store). Even if the whole company is 50, if each establishment is under 50 the duties for a health officer, industrial physician, and health committee do not arise. Conversely, if one establishment is 50 or more, the duty arises for that establishment. Regularly used workers are judged to include part-timers and casual workers used as a matter of ordinary course.

**Q. Can the health officer be someone outside the company?**
A. A health officer is in principle appointed from among persons dedicated to that workplace. So someone outside who holds concurrent posts at other companies, or who is not stationed there, cannot in principle be appointed. The qualification is a health officer licence (Class 1 required in some industries), physician, labour-health consultant, and the like. If there is no licence-holder in-house, plan ahead to sit the licence exam or review the assignment. Appointment is made within 14 days of the event.

**Q. Does the industrial physician have to come every month?**
A. The industrial physician's workplace patrol is in principle at least once a month, but where the employer provides prescribed information to the physician and the physician agrees, it can be relaxed to once every 2 months. Most workplaces of 50 or more entrust a non-dedicated (part-time) industrial physician; a dedicated one is required at a workplace of regularly 1,000 or more (500 or more for certain hazardous work). You find one via the local medical association or a medical institution.

**Q. What happens if we do not hold the health committee?**
A. Setting up the health committee, holding it at least once a month, and keeping records for 3 years are duties under the Industrial Safety and Health Act (Article 18; Industrial Safety and Health Regulations Article 23). Not holding it is a violation and can draw a corrective recommendation or guidance. The gist of the proceedings must be made known to workers. It should be run as a place to substantively deliberate on long-hour-work measures and the like, not as a formal holding.
