**In short:** One-off and day-labor staff at a live house or event are covered by workers' accident compensation insurance if they are workers under the employer's direction, and their working conditions must be stated. People hired day by day or for short terms of 30 days or less can be "day-labor insured persons" under employment insurance if they meet the requirements. If load-in, the show, and strike run into late night, a late-night premium applies for 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. Do not assume "it is a contract-for-work, so labor law does not apply" — judge by the reality.

Live houses, small theaters, and event operations often gather sound, lighting, merchandise, reception, and load-in/strike staff on a one-off or short-term basis only for the show day. It is tempting to skip labor-law procedures "because it is only one day" or "because it is a contract for work," but if the reality is that of a worker, the rules on workers' accident insurance, working-conditions disclosure, and premium wages reach even one-off work. Labor for spot work in general is in [labor management for spot work (gap-time part-time)](/en/labor/column/spot-work-sukima-baito-roumu-kanri); this article focuses on one-off staff specific to shows and events (day-labor employment insurance, the late-night premium, and time management of load-in and strike).

## How are one-off and day-labor staff treated under employment and accident insurance?

First, workers' accident compensation insurance applies to any business that uses even one worker, regardless of employment form or term. Even a one-off staffer for a single day is covered if they are a worker under the employer's direction, and injuries during work or commuting are treated as covered. Premiums are borne entirely by the employer, and a worker can receive benefits even if the coverage procedure has not been done.

Employment insurance splits at the entrance by whether the work is regular or day labor.

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Day laborer | A person hired day by day, or hired for a fixed term of 30 days or less (Employment Insurance Act, Article 42) |
| Day-labor insured person | A day laborer who meets requirements such as the applicable area. Eligible for the day-labor job-seeker benefit (Article 43) |
| Switch to regular | Where, for example, employed 18 or more days in each of the preceding two months by the same employer, switches to a general insured person from the next month (Article 42 proviso and Article 43) |

A day-labor insured person is issued a "day-labor insured person's handbook" (Article 44); for each day of hiring, the employer affixes an employment-insurance stamp to the handbook and cancels it, paying the premium as a day-labor insured person. Using the same person repeatedly beyond a certain number of days switches them to regular treatment, so the procedure changes with how you use them. Which category applies, and the grades and amounts of stamp premiums, are revised, so confirm the latest with the governing Hello Work.

## How are premium wages and working time calculated when load-in, the show, and strike run into late night?

Shows and events have long daily hold — load-in by day, the show at night, strike after the curtain — and tend to run into late night. Any time under the employer's direction, whether load-in, rehearsal, the show, or strike, is working time. You cannot treat post-show strike and clean-up as "outside the show" and leave it unpaid.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Statutory working hours | In principle 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week (Labor Standards Act, Article 32) |
| Breaks | At least 45 minutes for over 6 hours, at least 1 hour for over 8 hours (Article 34) |
| Overtime premium | 25%+ on time beyond 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week (Article 37; an Article 36 agreement is a premise) |
| Late-night premium | 25%+ for work in 10 p.m.–5 a.m. (Article 37(4)) |
| Holiday premium | 35%+ for work on a statutory holiday (Article 37) |

If actual work up to late-night strike exceeds 8 hours and also falls after 10 p.m., the overtime premium (25%+) and the late-night premium (25%+) stack. Standby and rehearsal time is also working time if the person is bound and cannot use it freely. Even for one-off work, recording start and end times, breaks, and the late-night band, and reflecting them in wage calculation, prevents unpaid-wage trouble.

## Is working-conditions disclosure (the 2024 items) needed even for one-off work, and where is the line with a contract for work?

Even for a one-off, single-day labor contract, Article 15 of the Labor Standards Act requires disclosure of working conditions at each contract. From April 2024, additional items must be disclosed (Labor Standards Act Enforcement Regulations, Article 5).

| 2024 April additions (per worker) | Content |
|---|---|
| Scope of change to workplace and duties | Disclosed to all workers at contract conclusion (and at renewal for fixed-term) |
| Presence and content of a renewal cap | Disclosed where a fixed-term contract sets a cap on total term or number of renewals |
| Matters on indefinite-term conversion | At the renewal when the conversion right arises, disclose the opportunity to apply and the conditions after conversion |

Basic items such as wages, working hours, workplace, duties, and contract term are disclosed in writing even for one-off work. Even when calling the same person repeatedly, make the working conditions clear each time.

Be careful with the framing "it is a contract for work, so labor law does not apply." Even if the contract is named a contract for work, if the reality is that the person cannot refuse requests, is directed on time, place, and method, and is paid for time, they are judged a worker, and the rules on accident insurance, working time, premium wages, and working-conditions disclosure reach them. The line between outsourcing and employment is in [the line between outsourcing and employment — how worker status is judged](/en/labor/column/gaichu-koyo-sakaime-roudoushasei), and the full set of disclosure items in [the working-conditions disclosure rules and the 2024 changes](/en/labor/column/roudou-joken-meiji-2024-henko-han-i).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does workers' accident insurance apply even to a staffer for a single day's event?**
A. Yes. Workers' accident insurance applies to any business using workers, regardless of employment form or term. Even a one-off staffer for a single day is covered for injuries during work or commuting if they are a worker under the employer's direction. Premiums are borne entirely by the employer, and a worker can receive benefits regardless of whether the procedure was done. Neglecting the procedure can lead to cost recovery, so complete the labor-insurance establishment procedure before using staff.

**Q. How do day-labor staff enroll in employment insurance?**
A. People hired day by day or for short terms of 30 days or less become "day-labor insured persons" if they meet requirements such as the applicable area (Employment Insurance Act, Articles 42 and 43). A day-labor insured person's handbook is issued, and for each day of hiring the employer affixes and cancels an employment-insurance stamp on the handbook and pays the premium. But in certain cases — such as 18 or more days in each of the preceding two months with the same employer — they switch to a general insured person from the next month. Stamp grades and amounts are revised, so confirm with the governing Hello Work.

**Q. Do you have to pay wages for post-show strike and clean-up time?**
A. Strike and clean-up under the employer's direction is working time, so you pay wages. You cannot treat post-show work as "outside the show" and leave it unpaid. For actual work beyond 8 hours a day, an overtime premium (25%+; an Article 36 agreement is a premise) applies, and for the 10 p.m.–5 a.m. portion a late-night premium (25%+), and where both apply they stack. Recording start/end times, breaks, and the late-night band prevents unpaid-wage trouble.

**Q. If you contract as a "contract for work," are labor-law procedures unnecessary?**
A. Even if the contract is named a contract for work, it is judged by the reality. If the person cannot refuse work, is directed on time, place, and method, and is paid for time, they are judged a worker, and the rules on accident insurance, working time, premium wages, and working-conditions disclosure reach them. You cannot escape labor-law application by the name alone, so arranging contracts and procedures that match the reality is safe.
