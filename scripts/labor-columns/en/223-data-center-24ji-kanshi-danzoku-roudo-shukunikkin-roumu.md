**Bottom line first**: Data-centre 24-hour monitors are covered, in principle, by the ordinary rules on working hours, breaks, holidays and premium wages. To lift those rules as monitoring or intermittent duty you need permission from the head of the Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Article 41(iii), Enforcement Rules Article 34, Form 14); to treat an overnight stay as a sleeping/day shift you need a separate permission (Rules Article 23, Form 10). Neither permission waives the late-night premium (Article 37(4)). Equipment work belongs to holders of the relevant national qualifications, construction and property to real-estate agents and architects, authorisations to a Gyoseishoshi, tax to a tax accountant and disputes to an attorney, each contracted separately as an independent business entity. No referral fees.

This page focuses on the labour side a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles: whether monitoring time is working time, the line between the intermittent-duty exemption and the sleeping-shift permission, and premium wages. The equipment work and the building use are handled by others.

## Do the ordinary working-hour rules apply as they are to 24-hour data-centre monitoring?

In principle, yes. Even for monitors, the statutory hours (8 hours a day, 40 a week, Labour Standards Act Article 32), breaks (Article 34), holidays (Article 35) and premium wages (Article 37) apply as the starting point. "Little actual work" or "mostly standby" does not by itself lift the rules; lifting them requires the permission described below.

| Assumption | Reality |
|---|---|
| Rules do not apply because monitoring has little actual work | Wrong. Ordinary rules apply unless permission is obtained |
| An overnight stay is automatically a sleeping shift | Wrong. Treating it as a sleeping shift needs the inspection office head's permission |
| A fixed wage for 24-hour duty removes the premium | Wrong. The late-night premium remains even with permission |

The key point is that lifting the rules needs a **permission**, not just an actual state of affairs. There are two different permissions, and which one applies changes both the form and the effect.

## On what conditions is the "monitoring or intermittent duty" exemption granted?

For monitoring or intermittent duty with little actual work and much standby, if the employer obtains permission from the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office, the rules on working hours, breaks and holidays (Chapter 4) are disapplied (Article 41(iii)). The permission is obtained by submitting Form 14 to the inspection office head (Enforcement Rules Article 34).

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Basis | Labour Standards Act Article 41(iii) (monitoring or intermittent duty for which the employer has obtained administrative permission) |
| Form and procedure | Submit Form 14 to the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office head (Rules Article 34) |
| Rules lifted | Working hours (Article 32), breaks (Article 34), holidays (Article 35), overtime/holiday premium (part of Article 37) |
| Rules that remain | Late-night premium (Article 37(4)), annual leave, minimum wage, etc. |
| Harder to qualify | Shifts with frequent fault response, enquiry handling or log-monitoring decisions, where actual work is heavy and standby is short |

Whether data-centre monitoring qualifies depends on how much actual work continues. Which shifts qualify is a matter for the inspection office's review, so we do not reach an individual conclusion here (**unverified**).

## How do the sleeping-shift and day-shift permissions differ, and how do they relate to premium wages?

The sleeping/day-shift permission is a separate system from the intermittent-duty permission above. For an overnight stay (night duty) or a daytime holiday shift (day duty) after full release from ordinary working hours, where normally almost no work is required, the employer obtains permission from the inspection office head (Enforcement Rules Article 23). The application uses Form 10.

| Item | Intermittent-duty permission | Sleeping/day-shift permission |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Article 41(iii) | Same item, Rules Article 23 |
| Form | Form 14 | Form 10 |
| Assumed duty | Monitoring/intermittent work as part of ordinary duty | Standby overnight or on a holiday after release from ordinary duty |
| Scope | Monitoring/intermittent work normally requiring almost no labour | Periodic rounds, receipt of urgent documents/calls, standby for emergencies; continuation of ordinary work is not allowed |

Under the permission standards (general standard = Notification Hatsu-ki No. 17 of 13 September 1947; allowance and frequency = Notification Kihatsu No. 150 of 14 March 1988), the night-duty allowance must be at least one-third of the average daily wage of comparable workers (including the late-night premium); night duty is, as a rule, once a week and day duty once a month; and adequate sleeping facilities must be provided. If, during a permitted sleeping shift, the worker performs work of the same nature as ordinary duty (such as fault response), that time is working time and ordinary wages (including any premium) are due on top of the shift allowance. The difference between night work and night duty is set out in [how night shifts and night duty differ at group homes](/en/labor/column/group-home-yakin-shukuchoku-roudou-chigai), and the medical/care application in [standards for sleeping-shift permission in medicine and care](/en/labor/column/iryo-kaigo-shukujitchoku-kyoka-kijun).

## Is nap time working time (how does it differ from standby time)?

Even if no actual work is done during a nap, if staying in the nap room is required and there is a duty to respond to alarms or take calls, and release from work is not guaranteed, the nap time is working time as a whole. This is the view from the Oboshi Building Management case (Supreme Court First Petty Bench, 28 February 2002), which held such nap time to be working time under Article 32.

| How the time is placed | Working time? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Staying in the nap room is required; duty to answer alarms/calls | Yes | Release from work is not guaranteed |
| Free to leave the premises with no call-out; a complete break | No | Release from work is guaranteed (Article 34 break) |
| Standby waiting for alerts or visitors | Yes | Cannot leave the employer's direction |

Whether it is called a "nap" or a "break", the substance governs. See also [is on-call allowance in home nursing working time](/en/labor/column/homon-kango-oncall-teate-jokin-kansan) and [nap and intermittent duty for security guards](/en/labor/column/keibigyo-kamin-dansoku-roudoujikan-roumu).

## When you run rotating shifts, how do the 36 agreement and the late-night premium work?

For ordinary monitoring without permission, you calculate premium wages for overtime beyond statutory hours, for statutory-holiday work and for late-night work; where they overlap, the rates add up (a late-night overtime hour is 25% + 25% = 50% or more). Rotating a 24-hour operation presupposes the overtime cap (Article 36) and a 36 agreement filed in advance.

| Category | Basis | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Article 37(1) | 25% or more |
| Overtime over 60 hours a month | Same paragraph (applied to SMEs from 1 April 2023) | 50% or more |
| Statutory-holiday work | Article 37(1) | 35% or more |
| Late-night work (22:00–05:00) | Article 37(4) | 25% or more |

The key point is that **neither the intermittent-duty permission nor the sleeping-shift permission waives the late-night premium**. Article 41 does not disapply the night-work provisions, so the late-night premium is due even with permission (administrative interpretation = Notification Kihatsu No. 1506 of 14 October 1948). ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If the night monitoring has little actual work, is overtime pay unnecessary?**
A. No. Unless you obtain the inspection office head's permission (the intermittent-duty exemption under Article 41(iii), or the sleeping-shift permission under Rules Article 23), the ordinary working-hour rules apply and overtime, holiday and late-night premiums are due. The substance alone does not lift the rules, and which shifts qualify is decided by the inspection office's review.

**Q. Can a 24-hour resident overnight shift be treated as night duty?**
A. If it is an overnight stay after complete release from ordinary working hours, with almost no work normally required, it may fall under the sleeping-shift permission (Rules Article 23, Form 10). If instead you continue monitoring and responding as ordinary duty through the night, it is not night duty; it becomes a matter of the intermittent-duty permission or the ordinary rules. Time spent on ordinary work during the shift is working time, paid separately.

**Q. Do the exemption or the sleeping-shift permission also remove the late-night premium?**
A. No. Article 41 does not disapply the night-work provisions, so the late-night premium (22:00–05:00) is due whether you hold the intermittent-duty permission or the sleeping-shift permission (administrative interpretation = Notification Kihatsu No. 1506 of 14 October 1948).
