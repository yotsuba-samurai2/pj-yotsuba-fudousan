**Bottom line first**: An employer hiring a foreign cook with the "Skilled Labor" residence status must give written notice of working conditions (Article 15, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act and Article 5 of its Enforcement Ordinance), enroll them in social and employment insurance as the eligibility requires, pay at or above the regional minimum wage, and provide equal treatment without discrimination by nationality (Article 3 of the Labor Standards Act). These are the same labor laws as for Japanese and do not relax by nationality. Whether the "Skilled Labor" status applies, and its application and renewal, are the realm of the administrative scrivener.

## What kind of person is a cook with the "Skilled Labor" status?

"Skilled Labor" is a residence status for a foreign national engaging in work that requires mature skills belonging to a special field of industry (Appended Table I-2 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act). A cook of Chinese or Taiwanese cuisine can fall within this category, but not just anyone qualifies: the landing-criteria ministerial ordinance (the Ministerial Ordinance Setting the Criteria under Article 7, paragraph 1, item 2 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act) sets the years of practical experience.

For a cook of foreign cuisine, item 1 of the "Skilled Labor" provision of that ordinance requires, in principle, 10 years or more of practical experience (including the period of majoring in that cuisine at an educational institution abroad) in the skill of cooking cuisine that was devised abroad and requires special treatment in Japan. For some cuisines, such as Thai, a separate standard such as 5 years or more is set under an Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA). The criteria also include receiving remuneration at or above what a Japanese would receive for the same work.

## Must the notice of working conditions for a foreign worker be in their native language?

Written notice is mandatory regardless of nationality. Delivery in the native language is not a legal duty, but an administrative guideline says it is desirable.

Article 15, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act and Article 5 of its Enforcement Ordinance require, at the conclusion of the labor contract, notice of the contract term, place of work and duties, start and end times, wages, matters on retirement and so on, with the mandatory items given by written delivery (by email and the like if the worker so wishes). This applies equally to Japanese and foreign workers.

| Way of giving notice | Standing | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Written notice (including email and the like) | Mandatory (regardless of nationality) | Labor Standards Act Article 15, paragraph 1; Enforcement Ordinance Article 5 |
| A method the person can understand, such as the native language or plain Japanese | Desirable (the aim of the duty to make efforts) | The Guideline for employers to deal appropriately with the improvement of employment management of foreign workers |

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare publishes model notices of working conditions in several languages. If the person does not understand the contract terms, it causes later trouble over wages and working hours. The practice of giving notice of working conditions to foreign workers is set out in [notice of working conditions for foreign workers and native-language delivery](/en/labor/column/gaikokujin-roudou-joken-meiji-bogoko-2024).

## From when do you enroll them in social and employment insurance?

Nationality is irrelevant to the enrollment requirements. They become insured from the point they are employed at a covered workplace and meet the eligibility requirements.

For health insurance and employees' pension insurance, a person regularly employed at a covered workplace becomes insured, and eligibility is acquired on the day the employment relationship begins. Where the weekly scheduled hours and monthly scheduled days are three-quarters or more of a regular worker's, they enroll just like full-timers; even below three-quarters, short-time workers meeting certain requirements such as the number of employees fall within the coverage expansion. For employment insurance, a person is insured if the weekly scheduled hours are 20 or more and employment is expected for 31 days or more.

| Insurance | Rough eligibility | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance and employees' pension insurance | At a covered workplace, scheduled hours/days three-quarters or more of a regular worker's (short-time: the coverage-expansion requirements) | Health Insurance Act; Employees' Pension Insurance Act |
| Employment insurance | 20 or more weekly hours and employment expected for 31 days or more | Employment Insurance Act, Article 6 |
| Workers' accident compensation insurance | Every worker employed (including part-timers and foreign nationals) | Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act |

In addition, when you hire or separate from a foreign national, a notification of the employment status of the foreign national to the Hello Work office is required (Article 28 of the Act on Comprehensively Advancing Labor Measures, and Stabilizing the Employment of Workers, and Enriching Workers' Vocational Lives). The line for social insurance in short-time work is set out in [social insurance for short-time workers (the three-quarters standard and coverage expansion)](/en/labor/column/tanjikan-koyo-shakaihoken-4bunno3).

## How do you meet equal pay for equal work (the same wage as Japanese)?

There are two axes: the prohibition of discrimination by nationality, and the prohibition of unreasonable differences in treatment between regular employees and part-time/fixed-term workers.

Article 3 of the Labor Standards Act provides that an employer must not engage in discriminatory treatment regarding wages, working hours or other working conditions by reason of a worker's nationality, creed or social status. You cannot lower wages for the reason that someone is foreign. The regional minimum wage also applies regardless of nationality, and wages cannot fall below it. The FY2026 regional minimum wage has a national weighted average of 1,177 yen, but the amount differs by prefecture and the effective dates vary, so check the minimum wage of the workplace's location (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare recommendation, referenced 25 November 2026).

Furthermore, setting unreasonable differences in treatment between part-time/fixed-term workers and regular (ordinary) workers within the same company is prohibited by Article 8 (balanced treatment) and Article 9 (equal treatment) of the Act on Improvement of Employment Management of Part-Time and Fixed-Term Workers. Where you hire a foreign cook as a part-time or fixed-term worker, you must be able to explain that the differences in base pay, allowances, bonuses and so on are not unreasonable in light of the content of the duties and the scope of changes in assignment. The practice of explaining and proving differences in treatment is set out in [equal pay for equal work for foreign workers and explaining differences in treatment](/en/labor/column/gaikokujin-doto-chingin-rissho-roudou-joken-tsuchi).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How many years of experience does a Chinese-cuisine cook with "Skilled Labor" need?**
A. For a cook of foreign cuisine, the landing-criteria ministerial ordinance (the Ministerial Ordinance Setting the Criteria under Article 7, paragraph 1, item 2 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act) requires, in principle, 10 years or more of practical experience (including the period of majoring in that cuisine at an educational institution abroad) in the skill of cooking cuisine devised abroad that requires special treatment in Japan. For cuisines such as Thai, a separate standard such as 5 years or more is set under an Economic Partnership Agreement. Proving the years of experience and the qualification judgment are the realm of the administrative scrivener; confirm before hiring.

**Q. Is it illegal if we do not give a foreign cook's working conditions in Chinese?**
A. Delivery in the native language itself is not a legal duty. However, written notice of working conditions is mandatory regardless of nationality under Article 15, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act and Article 5 of its Enforcement Ordinance. In addition, the administrative guideline on the employment management of foreign workers says notice by a method the person can understand is desirable, and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare publishes model notices of working conditions in several languages. If the person does not understand the contract terms, it leads to later trouble over wages and working hours.

**Q. May we set a slightly lower wage for the reason that someone is foreign?**
A. No. Article 3 of the Labor Standards Act prohibits discriminatory treatment regarding wages and other working conditions by reason of nationality. The regional minimum wage also applies regardless of nationality and cannot be undercut. Further, setting unreasonable differences in treatment between part-time/fixed-term workers and regular employees within the same company is prohibited by Articles 8 and 9 of the Act on Improvement of Employment Management of Part-Time and Fixed-Term Workers. Wages must be in a form reasonably explainable in light of the content of the duties and the like.

**Q. For social insurance, do we enroll them just like a Japanese even with the "Skilled Labor" status?**
A. Yes, the same. For health insurance and employees' pension, where a person is employed at a covered workplace with scheduled hours/days three-quarters or more of a regular worker's (short-time: the coverage-expansion requirements), they become insured regardless of nationality, and eligibility is acquired on the day the employment relationship begins. For employment insurance they are insured at 20 or more weekly hours and employment expected for 31 days or more, and workers' accident compensation insurance applies to every worker employed. In addition, a notification of the employment status of the foreign national to the Hello Work office is required on hiring and separation.
