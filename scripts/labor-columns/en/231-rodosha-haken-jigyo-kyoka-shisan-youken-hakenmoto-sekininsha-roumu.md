**Bottom line first**: To run a worker dispatch business you must obtain the license of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Article 5, paragraph 1 of the Act on Securing the Proper Operation of Worker Dispatching Businesses and Protecting Dispatched Workers). The license criteria (Article 7, paragraph 1) require not dispatching solely to a specific party, the ability to manage employment properly, proper management of personal information, and the ability to carry out the business accurately; of these, the asset requirements and the appointment of a responsible person at the dispatching agency are specified in the administrative guidelines. Per workplace, the base assets must be at least 20 million yen, cash and deposits at least 15 million yen, and the base assets at least one-seventh of total liabilities. Preparing and submitting the license application is the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant, not of the administrative scrivener (Appended Table I, item 20-11 of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant Act).

## What requirements does a worker dispatch license have?

Since the 2015 amendment removed the former distinction between the "general" (licensed) and "specified" (notification) categories, a worker dispatch business is now entirely under a licensing system. There is no form of the business for which mere notification suffices.

Article 5, paragraph 1 of the Act on Securing the Proper Operation of Worker Dispatching Businesses and Protecting Dispatched Workers (Act No. 88 of 1985; the "Worker Dispatch Act") provides that a person who intends to run a worker dispatch business must obtain the license of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. The license criteria are set out in Article 7, paragraph 1, and the license is not granted unless all of them are met. In addition, the applicant must not fall under the grounds for disqualification in Article 6 (such as a person for whom a set period has not elapsed since an imprisonment sentence or a fine for violating labor-related laws).

| License criterion (Worker Dispatch Act Article 7, paragraph 1) | Outline |
|---|---|
| Item 1 | The business is not aimed at dispatching workers solely to a specific party (prohibition of exclusive dispatch) |
| Item 2 | The applicant has the ability to manage the employment of dispatched workers properly |
| Item 3 | Personal information is managed properly and measures are taken to protect the secrets of dispatched workers and others |
| Item 4 | In addition to the above, the applicant has the ability to carry out the business accurately (the asset requirements, the responsible person, and the workplace requirements specify this item through the administrative guidelines) |

What specifies item 4 of these criteria is the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Guidelines for Handling Work Related to Worker Dispatching Businesses." The asset requirements, the responsible person, career support, and the workplace floor area are not the text of the statute itself; this guideline sets out the base amounts and the practice (referenced 26 November 2026). The license is valid in principle for 3 years at first and 5 years after renewal.

## How much do the asset requirements (base assets, cash/deposits, liability ratio) need to be?

You must meet all three figures. Missing even one means no license. Where there are several workplaces, the amounts increase by the number of workplaces.

The asset requirements set out in the "Guidelines for Handling Work Related to Worker Dispatching Businesses" are the three below. "Base assets" means the total assets minus deferred assets and goodwill, less the total liabilities.

| Requirement | Base amount | How it is counted |
|---|---|---|
| Base assets | At least 20 million yen × number of workplaces | Judged from the balance sheet of the most recent fiscal year |
| Cash and deposits | At least 15 million yen × number of workplaces | Cash and deposits in the business's own name as business funds; judged on the balance sheet |
| Ratio of base assets to liabilities | Base assets at least one-seventh of total liabilities | Same as above |

For example, with one workplace the rough target is base assets of at least 20 million yen and cash/deposits of at least 15 million yen; with two workplaces, base assets of at least 40 million yen and cash/deposits of at least 30 million yen. If the most recent settlement of accounts falls short, you can raise capital and show it through an audit certificate (interim or monthly settlement) by a certified public accountant or an audit corporation. Preparing the financial statements and certifying the capital increase are the realm of the tax accountant and certified public accountant; where these cross business entities, each is contracted separately as an independent entity, and there is no referral fee. The view on the timing of forming a company before getting the license is set out in [the timing of forming a company and enrolling in social insurance](/en/labor/column/kaisha-setsuritsu-shakaihoken-roudouhoken-kigen).

## What do the responsible person and career support need to have in place?

The criteria look not only at assets but at "people" and "mechanisms": the appointment of a responsible person at the dispatching agency, and a plan of training that supports the career formation of dispatched workers.

The responsible person at the dispatching agency is the person responsible for the proper employment management of dispatched workers, appointed for each workplace. The appointment requires a certain amount of employment-management experience and the completion of the training course for responsible persons (within roughly 3 years of completion), among others. The number required also rises with the number of dispatched workers. In addition, Article 30-2 of the Worker Dispatch Act requires the dispatching agency to provide dispatched workers with staged and systematic training and to offer career counseling to those who wish it; at the licensing stage too, having this training plan (career support system) is included in the criteria.

| What to have in place | Standing | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment of a responsible person at the dispatching agency | Appointed per workplace; employment-management experience and completion of the responsible-person course required | Worker Dispatch Act Article 36; administrative guidelines |
| Staged and systematic training | Having a training plan for the career advancement of dispatched workers | Worker Dispatch Act Article 30-2; license criteria |
| Career counseling for those who wish it | Setting up a point of contact and a person in charge | Worker Dispatch Act Article 30-2 |

The years of employment-management experience the responsible person needs, and the concrete hours and content of training, are set out in the administrative guidelines and may be revised in operation, so confirm the latest handling with the Prefectural Labour Bureau with jurisdiction before applying. Building the work rules and the employment-management system is the realm of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant; the duty to prepare and file work rules at a workplace that regularly employs 10 or more workers is set out in [the duty to prepare work rules (10 or more regular employees)](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## What do you have to disclose under the margin-rate disclosure duty?

A duty that continues even after you get the license is the disclosure of information such as the margin rate. It is a system to give dispatched workers material for choosing a dispatching agency.

Article 23, paragraph 5 of the Worker Dispatch Act requires the dispatching agency to provide information such as the number of dispatched workers, the number of client companies, the average amount of the dispatch fee, the average wage of dispatched workers, the margin rate, matters on training, and whether a labor-management agreement has been concluded. The margin rate is the figure obtained by dividing, by the average dispatch fee, the amount of the average dispatch fee minus the average wage of dispatched workers. Because the margin includes not only the agency's profit but also social-insurance premiums and training costs, a lower rate is not necessarily better. From April 2021, providing this information at all times over the internet has been the principle.

| Information provided | Content |
|---|---|
| Number of dispatched workers / number of client companies | Results per fiscal year |
| Average dispatch fee / average wage of dispatched workers | Average amounts per, for example, 8 hours a day |
| Margin rate | (Average dispatch fee − average wage of dispatched workers) ÷ average dispatch fee |
| Matters on training / whether a labor-management agreement exists | Content of career support; whether under an agreement |

Failing to provide this information or presenting false content can be subject to guidance, advice, or even revocation of the license. The management of the clash date and the client management ledger that the client (receiving side) prepares is set out in [the clash date and management ledger for the side receiving dispatched workers](/en/labor/column/rodosha-haken-ukeire-teishokuhi-kanri-daicho-37gokokuji-roumu). The line with "disguised contracting," where the form is a contract for work or services but the reality amounts to dispatch, is covered in [disguised contracting and worker status in contract development and SES](/en/labor/column/ses-jutaku-kaihatsu-gisou-ukeoi-roudoushasei-roumu).

## Who may you lawfully ask to handle the license application?

Preparing and submitting the application for a dispatch license is the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant. It is not the work of the administrative scrivener. Getting this wrong can leave you with documents the person you asked cannot lawfully handle.

Article 2, paragraph 1, items 1 and 1-2 of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant Act make the preparation of application documents to be submitted to administrative agencies under the labor and social insurance laws listed in Appended Table I of that Act, and the agency for the procedure of submitting them, the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant. The Worker Dispatch Act is listed in item 20-11 of that Appended Table I. And Article 27 of that Act prohibits a person who is not a Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant or such a corporation from performing those affairs as a business for remuneration (except where another law provides otherwise, and so on). An administrative scrivener may not prepare, as a business, documents whose preparation is restricted by another law (Article 1-2, paragraph 2 of the Administrative Scrivener Act).

| Procedure / judgment | Lawful party in charge |
|---|---|
| Preparation and submission of the dispatch license application and business plan | Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant |
| Preparing work rules, dispatched workers' employment contracts, and the training plan | Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant |
| Preparing the company's articles of incorporation and the company formation | Administrative scrivener (articles) / judicial scrivener (registration) |
| Certifying the settlement and capital increase for the asset requirements | Tax accountant / certified public accountant |
| Disputes such as contesting a revocation of the license | Lawyer |

Where these cross business entities, each is contracted separately as an independent entity, and there is no referral fee. Because forming the company (articles and registration) and building up the assets (settlement and capital increase) are premises of the license requirements, having a prospect of the license from the formation stage avoids ending up unable to get the license on the asset requirements after formation. What you can entrust under an advisory contract is set out in [what the social insurance and labor consultant's advisory fee pays for](/en/labor/column/sharoushi-komonryo-nan-no-taika).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does a worker dispatch business need a license rather than a notification?**
A. Yes. The 2015 amendment removed the former distinction between the "specified worker dispatch business (notification)" and the "general worker dispatch business (license)," and a worker dispatch business is now entirely under the license of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Worker Dispatch Act Article 5, paragraph 1). The license criteria are in Article 7, paragraph 1, and you must meet the asset requirements, the responsible person, personal-information management, and so on. Preparing and submitting the license application is the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant.

**Q. How much should I have for the asset requirements?**
A. Under the criteria in the "Guidelines for Handling Work Related to Worker Dispatching Businesses," per workplace the base assets (total assets minus deferred assets and goodwill, less total liabilities) must be at least 20 million yen, the cash and deposits in the business's own name as business funds at least 15 million yen, and the base assets at least one-seventh of total liabilities. With several workplaces, the amounts rise by that number. If the most recent settlement falls short, you can raise capital and show it through a CPA's audit certificate. Because the base amounts and the examination method can be revised, confirm with the Labour Bureau with jurisdiction before applying.

**Q. Must I disclose the margin rate?**
A. Yes. Under Article 23, paragraph 5 of the Worker Dispatch Act, a dispatching agency must provide the number of dispatched workers, the number of client companies, the average dispatch fee, the average wage of dispatched workers, the margin rate, matters on training, and so on. From April 2021, providing these at all times over the internet has been the principle. Because the margin also includes social-insurance premiums and training costs, a lower rate is not necessarily better, and it is to be judged together with the other information.

**Q. May I ask an administrative scrivener to handle the dispatch license application?**
A. The Worker Dispatch Act is listed in Appended Table I (item 20-11) of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant Act, so preparing and submitting the license application under that Act is the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant. A non-consultant performing this as a business for remuneration is prohibited by Article 27 of that Act. An administrative scrivener may not prepare, as a business, documents restricted by another law (Administrative Scrivener Act Article 1-2, paragraph 2). The articles of incorporation are for the administrative scrivener, the registration for the judicial scrivener, and the settlement and capital-increase certification for the tax accountant — the surrounding procedures are divided.
