**Bottom line first**: A worker dispatch business generally requires a licence from Japan’s Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Worker Dispatch Act, Article 5(1)). The standard financial criteria are ¥20 million in adjusted net assets and ¥15 million in cash and deposits held in the applicant’s name, each multiplied by the number of workplaces, plus adjusted net assets of at least one-seventh of total liabilities. The review also covers responsible personnel and career development support. Employers may apply themselves; paid preparation and filing on their behalf are generally handled by a certified social insurance and labour consultant or firm, subject to statutory exceptions.

## What requirements does a worker dispatch license have?

The 2015 reform abolished notification for the former ‘specified worker dispatch business’. Ordinary dispatch businesses require a licence. An exception under Article 18(1) of the special regional development act allows a qualifying business cooperative certified by the prefectural governor to dispatch employees hired indefinitely after filing a notification.

Generally, a licence from the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare is required before operating a worker dispatch business (Worker Dispatch Act, Article 5(1)). Applicants must satisfy all the criteria in Article 7(1) and must not be disqualified under Article 6. Disqualification may apply, for example, during a prescribed period after completion of a custodial or more severe sentence, or a fine for an offence specified in Article 6.

| License criterion (Worker Dispatch Act Article 7, paragraph 1) | Outline |
|---|---|
| Item 1 | The business generally must not be intended to dispatch workers exclusively to a particular client; exceptions are set by ministerial ordinance |
| Item 2 | Capacity to manage the employment of dispatched workers properly, including responsible personnel, training and career development support |
| Item 3 | Personal information is managed properly and measures are taken to protect the secrets of dispatched workers and others |
| Item 4 | Capacity to operate the business properly, including finances, organisation and premises |

The ministry’s licensing guidance explains how the employment-management and business-capacity criteria are assessed. It sets out the relevant rules for finances, responsible personnel, training and premises. A new licence lasts three years; a renewed licence lasts five years.

## How much do the asset requirements (base assets, cash/deposits, liability ratio) need to be?

A standard new application must meet all three criteria below. Only the adjusted net assets and cash-and-deposits thresholds are multiplied by the workplace count. The liability ratio is assessed for the applicant as a whole.

The asset requirements set out in the "Guidelines for Handling Work Related to Worker Dispatching Businesses" are the three below. "Base assets" means the total assets minus deferred assets and goodwill, less the total liabilities.

| Requirement | Base amount | How it is counted |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted net assets | At least ¥20 million × number of workplaces | Normally the latest year-end balance sheet; a new company before its first closing may use its opening balance sheet |
| Cash and deposits | At least ¥15 million × number of workplaces | Cash and deposits held in the applicant’s own name as business funds |
| Ratio of base assets to liabilities | Base assets at least one-seventh of total liabilities | Same as above |

Normally, the latest annual financial statements are used. Newly formed companies that have not yet closed their first accounts and newly established sole traders may need different documents; confirm with the relevant Labour Bureau. Where an asset or funding shortfall is corrected after year-end, interim or monthly accounts accompanied by a CPA’s or audit firm’s audit certificate may be accepted. The transitional relief for small operators applies only to qualifying existing operators seeking renewal, not generally to new applications. See also [when to incorporate and enrol in social insurance](/en/labor/column/kaisha-setsuritsu-shakaihoken-roudouhoken-kigen).

## What do the responsible person and career support need to have in place?

The criteria look not only at assets but at "people" and "mechanisms": the appointment of a responsible person at the dispatching agency, and a plan of training that supports the career formation of dispatched workers.

Each workplace appoints a person responsible for managing the employment of dispatched workers. A new licence generally requires specified experience and training for that role completed within the three years before the application was received. Confirm the number required based on staffing and other factors. Article 30-2 also requires staged, systematic training and access to career counselling for workers who request it.

| What to have in place | Standing | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment of a responsible person at the dispatching agency | Appointed per workplace; employment-management experience and completion of the responsible-person course required | Worker Dispatch Act Article 36; administrative guidelines |
| Staged and systematic training | Having a training plan for the career advancement of dispatched workers | Worker Dispatch Act Article 30-2; license criteria |
| Career counseling for those who wish it | Setting up a point of contact and a person in charge | Worker Dispatch Act Article 30-2 |

The years of employment-management experience the responsible person needs, and the concrete hours and content of training, are set out in the administrative guidelines and may be revised in operation, so confirm the latest handling with the Prefectural Labour Bureau with jurisdiction before applying. Building the work rules and the employment-management system is the realm of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant; the duty to prepare and file work rules at a workplace that regularly employs 10 or more workers is set out in [the duty to prepare work rules (10 or more regular employees)](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## What do you have to disclose under the margin-rate disclosure duty?

A duty that continues even after you get the license is the disclosure of information such as the margin rate. It is a system to give dispatched workers material for choosing a dispatching agency.

Article 23, paragraph 5 of the Worker Dispatch Act requires the dispatching agency to provide information such as the number of dispatched workers, the number of client companies, the average amount of the dispatch fee, the average wage of dispatched workers, the margin rate, matters on training, and whether a labor-management agreement has been concluded. The margin rate is the figure obtained by dividing, by the average dispatch fee, the amount of the average dispatch fee minus the average wage of dispatched workers. Because the margin includes not only the agency's profit but also social-insurance premiums and training costs, a lower rate is not necessarily better. From April 2021, providing this information at all times over the internet has been the principle.

| Information provided | Content |
|---|---|
| Number of dispatched workers / number of client companies | Results per fiscal year |
| Average dispatch fee / average wage of dispatched workers | Average amounts per, for example, 8 hours a day |
| Margin rate | (Average dispatch fee − average wage of dispatched workers) ÷ average dispatch fee |
| Matters on training / whether a labor-management agreement exists | Content of career support; whether under an agreement |

Failing to provide this information or presenting false content can be subject to guidance, advice, or even revocation of the license. The management of the clash date and the client management ledger that the client (receiving side) prepares is set out in [the clash date and management ledger for the side receiving dispatched workers](/en/labor/column/rodosha-haken-ukeire-teishokuhi-kanri-daicho-37gokokuji-roumu). The line with "disguised contracting," where the form is a contract for work or services but the reality amounts to dispatch, is covered in [disguised contracting and worker status in contract development and SES](/en/labor/column/ses-jutaku-kaihatsu-gisou-ukeoi-roudoushasei-roumu).

## Who may you lawfully ask to handle the license application?

An employer may apply directly. Preparing and filing someone else’s application on a paid business basis is generally reserved for certified social insurance and labour consultants and their firms, subject to statutory exceptions.

Article 2, paragraph 1, items 1 and 1-2 of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant Act make the preparation of application documents to be submitted to administrative agencies under the labor and social insurance laws listed in Appended Table I of that Act, and the agency for the procedure of submitting them, the work of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant. The Worker Dispatch Act is listed in item 20-11 of that Appended Table I. And Article 27 of that Act prohibits a person who is not a Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant or such a corporation from performing those affairs as a business for remuneration (except where another law provides otherwise, and so on). An administrative scrivener may not prepare, as a business, documents whose preparation is restricted by another law (Article 1-3, paragraph 2 of the Administrative Scrivener Act).

| Procedure / judgment | Lawful party in charge |
|---|---|
| Preparation and submission of the dispatch license application and business plan | Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant |
| Preparing work rules, dispatched workers' employment contracts, and the training plan | Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant |
| Preparing the company's articles of incorporation and the company formation | Administrative scrivener (articles) / judicial scrivener (registration) |
| Preparation of financial records and tax matters | Tax accountant or other appropriate professional |
| Audit certification of interim or monthly accounts for licensing | Certified public accountant or audit firm |
| Disputes such as contesting a revocation of the license | Lawyer |

Separate engagements apply if different firms are involved. No referral fee is charged. If operating through a company, plan incorporation and licensing together; if operating as a sole trader, prepare the documents for an individual applicant. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does a worker dispatch business need a license rather than a notification?**
A. Ordinary worker dispatch businesses require a licence from the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. The original notification scheme for specified dispatch was abolished in 2015. A separate statutory exception allows a certified regional development cooperative to dispatch staff with indefinite employment contracts after notification.

**Q. How much should I have for the asset requirements?**
A. Normally, multiply ¥20 million in adjusted net assets and ¥15 million in cash and deposits in the applicant’s name by the number of workplaces. Adjusted net assets must also be at least one-seventh of total liabilities. Some specific businesses or qualifying renewals may be subject to exceptions or transitional rules; ask the relevant Labour Bureau which criteria and evidence apply.

**Q. Must I disclose the margin rate?**
A. Yes. Under Article 23, paragraph 5 of the Worker Dispatch Act, a dispatching agency must provide the number of dispatched workers, the number of client companies, the average dispatch fee, the average wage of dispatched workers, the margin rate, matters on training, and so on. From April 2021, providing these at all times over the internet has been the principle. Because the margin also includes social-insurance premiums and training costs, a lower rate is not necessarily better, and it is to be judged together with the other information.

**Q. May I ask an administrative scrivener to handle the dispatch license application?**
A. The employer can apply directly. Preparing and filing the application on someone else’s behalf as a paid business is generally reserved for certified social insurance and labour consultants or their firms, subject to statutory exceptions (Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant Act, Articles 2(1)(i), 2(1)(i-2) and 27, and Schedule I, item 20-11). An administrative scrivener’s qualification alone does not generally authorise paid preparation of this application (Administrative Scrivener Act, Article 1-3(2)).
