**Bottom line first**: Under the amended Child Care and Family Care Leave Act effective 1 April 2025, the duty to publish male workers' child-care-leave uptake expanded from employers with more than 1,000 regularly employed workers to those with more than 300 (Article 22-2). What is published is the male child-care-leave uptake rate, within roughly three months after the end of the preceding business year, by a method such as the internet that the general public can view.

## From what company size does the duty to publish male child-care-leave uptake apply?

Article 22-2 of the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act (Act No. 76 of 1991) provides that "an employer with more than 300 regularly employed workers must, as provided by Ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, publish at least once a year the status of its workers' child-care-leave uptake as prescribed by Ordinance." The employer is covered if its number of "regularly employed workers," the basis figure, exceeds 300.

| Period | Employers covered | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| From 1 April 2023 | More than 1,000 regularly employed workers | Amendment by Act No. 58 of 2021 |
| From 1 April 2025 | More than 300 regularly employed workers (newly adding over 300 up to 1,000) | Amendment by Act No. 42 of 2024 |

"Regularly employed workers" include not only those employed without a fixed term, but also part-time and fixed-term workers who have been continuously employed for at least a year (or are expected to be employed for over a year from hiring). Note that this is judged per employer (not per workplace). The publication duty is one part of the overall amendment of the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act. For the whole picture by effective date, see [what changed in the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act in 2025](/en/labor/column/ikuji-kaigo-kyugyo-ho-2025-kaisei).

## What changed in April 2025 (from over 1,000 to over 300)?

Previously the publication of child-care-leave uptake was a duty on employers with more than 1,000 regularly employed workers (effective 1 April 2023). By the amendment through Act No. 42 of 2024 (promulgated 31 May 2024), from 1 April 2025 this threshold was lowered to over 300. This newly obliged employers with over 300 up to 1,000 regularly employed workers to publish every year.

What is published did not change. The employer chooses and publishes one of the following.

| Rate published | Numerator | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Child-care-leave uptake rate | Number of male workers who took child-care leave in the preceding business year | Number of male workers whose spouse gave birth in the preceding business year |
| (2) Rate of child-care leave plus childcare-purpose leave | Male workers who took qualifying parental leave or employer-provided childcare-purpose leave in the preceding business year; do not count the same worker twice for the same child | Number of male workers whose spouse gave birth in the preceding business year |

The "preceding business year" means the business year immediately before the one in which the publication date falls. Whether (1) or (2) was used must be stated together with the rate. Only the scope widened; the approach to calculation and the method of publication are the same framework as for employers over 1,000.

## For what period and by what formula do you calculate the uptake rate?

Use the business year immediately before the publication year. For both methods, the denominator is the number of male workers whose spouses gave birth that year. For method (1), the numerator is male workers who took qualifying parental leave that year. Method (2) also includes users of qualifying childcare-purpose leave. If the same male worker used both types of leave for the same child, count him once, not twice. Count split parental leave for the same worker and child once as well.

Qualifying parental leave includes post-birth paternity leave and equivalent leave under Article 23, paragraph 2 or Article 24, paragraph 1 of the Act. For method (2), childcare-purpose leave means employer-provided leave for raising a child before elementary school, with the childcare purpose stated in the work rules or an equivalent document. Do not include statutory parental leave or equivalent leave, child nursing and related leave, work-and-childcare support leave, or statutory annual paid leave in this separate category. Leave used only before, and not on or after, the expected date of birth does not count.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Calculation period | Preceding business year (the one immediately before the business year in which publication falls) |
| Denominator | Number of male workers whose spouse gave birth in the preceding business year |
| Numerator (1) | Number of male workers who took child-care leave in the preceding business year |
| Numerator (2) | Count both categories for the same business year, counting the same man for the same child only once |
| Same child | Count the same male worker once if he took split parental leave for the same child or used both parental leave and childcare-purpose leave for that child |
| Takings across years | Count continuous parental leave in the business year in which it began. If leave is split across business years, count only the first period |

Where the number of male workers whose spouses gave birth (the denominator) is zero, the guidance is to show the rate as "–." Truncate the percentage after one decimal place and state alongside it which formula ((1) or (2)) was used and the period of the preceding business year used for calculation. On the uptake of child-care leave itself (including post-birth child-care leave), see [the 2025 scheme of the post-birth leave support benefit](/en/labor/column/shussei-go-kyugyo-shien-kyufu-2025). Grasping those in scope, the premise of the calculation, depends on having day-to-day labor management that records births and child-care leave.

## Where and by when should you publish?

Publication is done by the internet or another method so that the general public can view it. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare recommends publishing on the company's own website or on "Ryouritsu-shien no Hiroba," which gathers information on companies working to balance work with childcare and family care.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Frequency | At least once a year |
| Timing | Within roughly three months after the end of the preceding business year |
| Method | The internet, etc. — a method the general public can view (company website, Ryouritsu-shien no Hiroba, etc.) |
| Matters stated alongside | The period of the preceding business year used, and which of (1) or (2) was used |

"Within roughly three months" calls for prompt publication. Starting from the end of the business year, work backward from publishing the figure to prepare the steps: tally the takers and the spouses who gave birth, calculate the rate, and post it. You may also, optionally, publish the average days of child-care leave taken, but the object of the duty is the male child-care-leave uptake rate, etc.

## What problems arise if you fail to publish or publish figures at odds with reality?

If the publication duty is not performed, first the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (the power delegated to the head of the Prefectural Labour Bureau) may require a report, or give advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56). Where an employer who received a recommendation does not follow it, the Minister may publish that fact (Article 56-2). Further, a person who fails to make the report under Article 56, or makes a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66).

| Stage | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Advice, guidance, recommendation | The Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) seeks correction | Article 56 |
| Publication | If the recommendation is not followed, the fact can be published | Article 56-2 |
| Civil fine | A person who fails to report, or reports falsely, up to 200,000 yen | Article 66 |

If a published figure or the calculation method is wrong, check the underlying records, recalculate and correct the published figure. A counting error can undermine confidence in the figures, but is distinct from giving a false response to a request for a report under Article 66. Legal assessment of published figures and disputes over calculation belong to a lawyer, and tax judgments to a tax accountant; each is separate from the labor-and-social-security attorney's work and is contracted separately as an independent business. There is no referral fee. The expansion to 300 of the environment for public officials' child-care leave is a separate framework from the private "publication" duty; see [the expansion to 300 of environment-building for public officials' child-care leave](/en/labor/column/ikukyu-shutoku-koumu-2025-300nin-kakudai).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is a company with exactly 300 employees covered by the publication duty?**
A. Article 22-2 covers "an employer with more than 300 regularly employed workers." So exactly 300 does not count as "more than," and is outside the scope. It is covered at 301 or more (over 300). Regularly employed workers are counted per employer, including those employed without a fixed term and part-time and fixed-term workers who have been continuously employed for at least a year (or are expected to be for at least a year from hiring). Whether your headcount exceeds the threshold must be judged after checking the range of workers to include.

**Q. Do we publish the overall uptake rate including women?**
A. The duty applies to male workers’ uptake. Publish either (1) qualifying parental-leave uptake or (2) combined uptake of qualifying parental leave and employer-provided childcare-purpose leave. Under method (2), count a worker only once if he used both types for the same child. For both rates, the denominator is the number of male workers whose spouses gave birth in the preceding business year. Female workers’ uptake is outside this publication duty.

**Q. For what period's uptake rate, and by when, do we publish?**
A. The calculation period is the "preceding business year," meaning the business year immediately before the one in which the publication date falls. The timing of publication is within roughly three months after the end of the preceding business year. You publish at least once a year by a method such as the internet that the general public can view. The Ministry recommends publishing on "Ryouritsu-shien no Hiroba." State alongside the rate the period of the preceding business year used and which of (1) or (2) was used.

**Q. If we do not publish, is there an immediate fine?**
A. There is no immediate penalty. First the Minister (Prefectural Labour Bureau head) requires a report, or gives advice, guidance or a recommendation (Article 56), and if the recommendation is not followed, can publish that fact (Article 56-2). However, failing to make the report under Article 56, or making a false report, is subject to a civil fine of up to 200,000 yen (Article 66). The basic course is to put publication in order promptly in response to a request for correction. Whether a particular case qualifies and how to proceed with calculation must be judged after checking the company's own situation.
