**In short:** The education-training leave benefit lets an in-service general insured person under employment insurance who takes unpaid leave to reskill receive an amount equivalent to the basic allowance. It was created on October 1, 2025 (Reiwa 7) (amendment to the Employment Insurance Act under Article 2 of Act No. 26 of 2024). The number of days paid is 90, 120, or 150 depending on the base period, and individual eligibility is decided by Hello Work. This article only introduces the framework.

For owners and HR staff at small and medium-sized companies who want to allow employee reskilling through unpaid leave, this article organizes the framework of the education-training leave benefit newly created in October 2025 and what employers should prepare in work rules and procedures. Individual eligibility and the benefit amount are matters Hello Work decides; this article only introduces the system. Among the 2025 employment-insurance reforms, the easing of the post-separation benefit restriction and the raise of the education-training benefit are organized in [the 2025 employment insurance reform: how did the benefit restriction for voluntary resignation and the education-training benefit change](/en/labor/column/koyohoken-2025-jikotsugou-kyufu-seigen-kyoiku-kunren).

## What is the education-training leave benefit, and when was it created?

The education-training leave benefit is a system that pays an amount equivalent to the basic allowance (unemployment benefit) when an in-service general insured person takes unpaid leave to receive training. Its distinctive feature is supporting the living expenses of a person who reskills without leaving their job. The basis is the amendment to the Employment Insurance Act under Article 2 of the Act Partially Amending the Employment Insurance Act and Other Acts (Act No. 26 of 2024), which took effect October 1, 2025 (Reiwa 7). The details are set out in the Ministerial Order Partially Amending the Enforcement Regulations of the Employment Insurance Act and others.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Creation | October 1, 2025 (Reiwa 7) |
| Basis | Amendment to the Employment Insurance Act under Article 2 of Act No. 26 of 2024 |
| Who is covered | An in-service general insured person under employment insurance (a person who has not separated) |
| Premise leave | Unpaid education-training leave (taken while remaining in service) |
| Nature of the benefit | An amount equivalent to the basic allowance (the same idea as unemployment benefit at separation) |

Until now, the employment-insurance benefit related to training has centered on the education-training benefit (Article 60-2 of the Employment Insurance Act), which subsidizes part of the course cost. The education-training leave benefit is a separate benefit, differing in that it supports "living expenses during the leave" rather than "course costs." To avoid confusing the two, you must explain them separately when fielding a consultation.

## What kind of leave and which employees are covered?

To be covered, both the requirements of the leave itself and the requirements on the employee's insured period must be met. The published requirements are as follows (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare guidance, referenced October 2026; confirm the details with the latest information).

| Category | Main requirements |
|---|---|
| The employee's status | An in-service general insured person under employment insurance |
| Insured period | A deemed insured period of 12 months or more in total in the 2 years before the leave begins, and a base period of 5 years or more |
| Nature of the leave | Unpaid leave the employee takes voluntarily based on a labor agreement or the work rules |
| Days of leave | 30 days or more |
| Employer's involvement | The employer has approved taking the leave, and there is no plan to dismiss during the period |
| Income | The person earns no wages or other income during the leave period |

The key is that this leave is "unpaid" and that the employee "takes it voluntarily." If the company has the employee take training by a work order (wages are paid), it is not the education-training leave meant here. The structure is that the employee themselves takes unpaid leave to reskill, and employment insurance supports their living expenses during that time. Because the insured-period requirement branches finely by rules such as aggregation where there is a separation period, confirm individual eligibility with Hello Work.

## How are the benefit amount and payment period determined?

The amount paid per day is calculated on the same idea as the basic allowance received at separation. The number of days paid (the prescribed number of benefit days) is determined by the base period (the employment-insurance enrollment period).

| Base period | Days paid |
|---|---|
| 5 years to under 10 years | 90 days |
| 10 years to under 20 years | 120 days |
| 20 years or more | 150 days |

- **Amount per day:** as with the basic allowance at separation, it is calculated, in principle, by multiplying the wage daily amount based on the wages for the 6 months before the leave begins by a benefit rate (roughly 45–80%). The wage daily amount and the basic-allowance daily amount have caps by age bracket, and the specific figure changes by year and age, so confirm it with the latest information from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and Hello Work before receiving (referenced October 2026).
- **Payment period:** the period during which the benefit can be received is, in principle, up to 1 year. If the leave is interrupted for pregnancy, childbirth, childcare, or the like, there is treatment allowing the period to be extended within a certain range.
- **The reference date:** the base period on which the days paid are based is judged as of the day before the leave begins.

The fact that the days paid fall in the same brackets as the prescribed number of benefit days of the basic allowance (for an ordinary separatee) shows the nature of the system. The idea is to extend equivalent living-expense protection to a person who reskills while remaining in service, instead of separating and receiving unemployment benefit. Because a simulation of the specific amount and days is shown on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare site, in a consultation it is safe to explain the "rough framework" and have the fixed amount confirmed at Hello Work.

## What should the employer prepare in work rules and procedures?

The education-training leave benefit is a benefit the employee themselves receives, but the unpaid education-training leave that is its premise is a company scheme. So there are preparations the employer side should arrange.

- **Stating it in the work rules / labor-management agreement:** set the education-training leave (unpaid) scheme in a labor agreement or the work rules. Make clear who is eligible, the days that can be taken, the application procedure, and that it is unpaid.
- **Certification regarding wages:** when the employee applies to Hello Work, there are documents the company prepares and submits, such as a certification of the monthly wage (a wage certification equivalent to the separation certificate).
- **Confirming there is no plan to dismiss:** because it is a requirement that there be no plan to dismiss during the leave period, the employer needs to confirm and certify that point.

Even if an employee says "I want to reskill" while the work rules have no provision for education-training leave, the premise of the benefit is not in place. A company that introduces the scheme should, as a practical point, first complete the arrangement of the work rules. Drafting and amending the work rules is an area a Shakai Hoken Roumushi can support. Whether to make the leave unpaid or paid, and how to design the number of days, is a matter of the company's scheme design and must be decided consistently with the benefit requirements (unpaid, 30 days or more).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. When is the education-training leave benefit a benefit you can receive?**
A. It is a benefit an in-service general insured person under employment insurance can receive—an amount equivalent to the basic allowance—when they take unpaid education-training leave to reskill. It was created on October 1, 2025 (Reiwa 7). It is a separate system from the education-training benefit, which subsidizes course costs; this one supports living expenses during the leave. Individual eligibility is decided by Hello Work.

**Q. Is everyone covered?**
A. It is required that the person be an in-service general insured person with a deemed insured period of 12 months or more in total in the 2 years before the leave begins and a base period of 5 years or more. In addition, it must be voluntary unpaid leave based on a labor agreement or the work rules, 30 days or more, with the employer's approval and no plan to dismiss. Because fine eligibility branches by things such as whether there is a separation period, confirm with Hello Work.

**Q. About how much is the amount and the days paid?**
A. The amount per day is calculated on the same idea as the basic allowance at separation—in principle, the wage daily amount based on the wages for the 6 months before the leave begins multiplied by a benefit rate (roughly 45–80%). The days paid are, by base period, 90 days for 5 to under 10 years, 120 days for 10 to under 20 years, and 150 days for 20 years or more. The wage daily amount has a cap by age bracket, and the figure changes by year and age, so confirm it with the latest information from Hello Work before receiving.

**Q. What should the company prepare?**
A. Set the premise unpaid education-training leave in a labor agreement or the work rules, making clear who is eligible, the days that can be taken, and that it is unpaid. When the employee applies, there are documents the company prepares and submits, such as a certification of the monthly wage, and it is also necessary to confirm there is no plan to dismiss during the leave.
