**In short:** An employee-director (a director who also holds an employee post such as department or branch manager) **has worker status for the employee part of their duties, and the Labor Standards Act can apply to that part.** So unless they are a manager/supervisor (Labor Standards Act, Article 41, item 2), overtime pay (premium wages) matching the working hours of the employee part can be required. The position of director itself is a mandate relationship with the company (Companies Act, Article 330; Civil Code, Article 644) and is not that of a worker. For employment insurance, an employee-director with a strong worker character and a recognized employment relationship can be insured, and to confirm this the "employee-director employment-reality certificate" is filed with Hello Work. For social insurance (Health Insurance and Employees' Pension), the standard remuneration is set on the combined total of the director's remuneration and the employee wage. This page sets out worker status, overtime pay, employment and social insurance, and the division of roles.

This page is for managers and labour staff of family companies and SMEs where directors also do frontline work, and companies that made a relative a director through business succession. It sets out the labour management of employee-directors from a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint. The final judgment of worker status and insured status is made on the reality by the Labour Standards Inspection Office, Hello Work, and the insurer.

## What is an employee-director, and how does it differ from a director, an executive officer, and a worker?

An employee-director is a state in which **the same person holds both the position of a director and an employee (staff) post such as department, branch, or factory manager.** Its feature is that managerial duties as a director and labour duties as an employee coexist in one person.

| Position | Relationship with the company | Worker? |
|---|---|---|
| Director (no concurrent post) | Mandate (Companies Act, Article 330; Civil Code, Article 644) | Not a worker |
| Executive officer (not a director) | Depends on the contract (often employment-type) | A worker if employment-type |
| Employee-director | Director part is mandate; employee part is employment | Worker status in the employee part |
| Ordinary employee | Employment (labour contract) | A worker |

The director position is a mandate relationship, so there is no Labor Standards Act protection for duties as a director. Duties as an employee, however, are performed under direction and so worker status arises there. **The error that easily occurs with an employee-director is to treat them uniformly as "a director, so labour law is entirely irrelevant."**

## Does the Labor Standards Act (overtime, hours, leave) apply to an employee-director?

**The Labor Standards Act can apply to the employee part of the duties.** The "worker" of Labor Standards Act Article 9 means a person who is used in a business and paid wages; where the employee part of an employee-director fits this, the provisions on working hours, premium wages, and annual paid leave reach it.

However, if the person is a **manager/supervisor** under Labor Standards Act Article 41, item 2 (a person in a supervisory or managing position regardless of the type of business), the provisions on working hours, breaks, and holidays do not apply, and premium wages for overtime and holiday work are not required. Still, a manager/supervisor is judged on the reality — the duties, responsibility and authority, work mode, and treatment — and **a job title alone does not make someone a manager/supervisor.** The "manager in name only" issue is covered in [Manager in name only and premium / late-night pay](/en/labor/column/kanri-kantokusha-nabakari-warimashi-shinya).

| Point | Treatment for an employee-director |
|---|---|
| Overtime pay (overtime / holiday premium) | Required if the employee part has worker status and they are not a manager/supervisor |
| Late-night premium | Required even for a manager/supervisor (Labor Standards Act, Article 37, paragraph 4) |
| Annual paid leave | Subject to grant if they are a worker as an employee |
| Article 36 agreement | If the employee part is a worker, an Article 36 agreement is needed for overtime |

If the employee part is not a manager/supervisor, overtime requires a concluded and filed Article 36 agreement and payment of premium wages. For the basics of the Article 36 agreement, see [How far overtime goes under an Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). In practice, the premise is to have a system (attendance records, wage ledger) that can grasp the employee working hours.

## Can they be an employment-insurance insured person (what does the employee-director employment-reality certificate check)?

A director is in principle not an employment-insurance insured person, but **an employee-director with a strong worker character and a recognized employment relationship can be insured in their capacity as an employee.**

To confirm this, the **"employee-director employment-reality certificate"** is filed with Hello Work together with the notification of acquiring insured status, and worker status is judged. What is looked at is roughly the following reality.

| What is checked | Purpose |
|---|---|
| The amount and ratio of director remuneration and wage (employee salary) | Whether a wage as the consideration for labour is paid |
| The reality of work (attendance, direction, application of work rules) | Whether there is a reality of working as an employee |
| The degree of the director's business-execution and decision power | Whether the managerial character is not too strong |

As supporting documents, a wage ledger, attendance record, labour contract, articles of incorporation, and certificate of registered matters may be required. If the wage part is extremely small relative to the director remuneration — so the character is of a manager rather than a worker — the person may not be recognized as insured. **The final decision on insured status is made on the reality by Hello Work.**

## How is the social-insurance remuneration set (the director-remuneration part and the salary part)?

Under Health Insurance and Employees' Pension, a director used by a corporation and receiving remuneration is an insured person. For an employee-director, the standard monthly remuneration is set using **the combined total of the director remuneration and the employee salary.** Unlike employment insurance, it is not cut out as "the employee part only"; it is seen on the combined total.

| System | Treatment for an employee-director |
|---|---|
| Health Insurance / Employees' Pension | Standard remuneration set on the combined director remuneration + employee salary |
| Employment insurance | Insured based on the employee wage part, if worker status is recognized |
| Workers' compensation | Covered for labour as an employee (the director's business-execution part is in principle not covered) |

For points such as combining remuneration when holding directorships at several establishments, see [Social insurance for directors working at multiple establishments](/en/labor/column/nii-ijo-jigyosho-kinmu-todoke-yakuin-tanjikan-shaho). The tax treatment of the amount of director remuneration and matters such as fixed periodic remuneration are the domain of a tax accountant, with a different purpose from setting the social-insurance standard remuneration.

## When making a relative an employee-director through business succession, what to watch for?

In business succession, making a successor relative a director while keeping them in frontline duties can create an employee-director. What to watch here is that **worker status is judged on the reality.**

- Make it so that the existence, as a reality, of the employee duties, working hours, and wages can be backed up by work rules, the wage ledger, and attendance records
- Manage the director remuneration and employee salary separately, so they can be explained in the employee-director employment-reality certificate
- Because a live-in relative may be separately asked for a "live-in relative" worker-status check in employment insurance, where both the employee-director and the relative points overlap, confirm with the competent Hello Work

For the change of representative and the social insurance of directors in succession, see [Change of representative and directors' social insurance in business succession](/en/labor/column/jigyo-shokei-daihyo-kotai-yakuin-shakaihoken-tetsuzuki). For the size of workplace at which work rules become mandatory, see [How many employees make work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo). **The director registration itself is the work of a judicial scrivener, and the tax of director remuneration that of a tax accountant**, handled separately from the labour design.
