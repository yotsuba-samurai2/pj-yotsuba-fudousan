**Bottom line first**: A condominium manager's "standby time" is working time even without actual work, unless release from the employer's direction is guaranteed. To remove the working-hours, break and holiday rules as monitoring or intermittent duty requires permission from the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Article 41(iii)), and even with permission the late-night premium (Article 37(4)) is not waived. The permission is obtained with an application in Form No. 14 and reviewed against circular criteria such as standby time being at least as long as actual-work time. The application and work rules belong to a Shakai Hoken Roumushi, unpaid-overtime claims and litigation to an attorney, and drafting or interpreting the management-entrustment contract to an attorney or a Gyoseishoshi, each contracted separately as an independent business entity.

This page focuses on the labour side a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles: whether standby time is working time, the exemption permission for monitoring or intermittent duty, and premium wages. It is aimed at condominium and building management companies and at rental-property owners who employ a manager.

## Is a condominium manager's "standby time" working time?

Standby time spent at the reception or waiting, ready for visitors, phone calls or emergencies, differs from a break and is in principle working time. Standby time is not guaranteed release from the employer's direction and keeps you ready to respond at once. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Guidelines on measures employers should take for the proper grasp of working hours" also treats working time as time under the employer's direction and includes standby time.

| Type of time | Working time? | View |
|---|---|---|
| Break time | No | Time with a guaranteed right to be released from work (Labour Standards Act Article 34) |
| Standby time | Yes | Waiting for visitors, calls or emergencies, unable to leave the employer's direction |
| Time of private acts | No | Time for acts unrelated to work and not incidental to performing it |

As to how a resident manager spends time outside scheduled hours, direction does not reach private acts unrelated to work and not incidental to performing it, while time on standby — not released from work because residents may need a response — counts as working time (the general view from the Obayashi Facilities case, Supreme Court Second Petty Bench, 19 October 2007, and the Tokyo High Court judgment after remand, 9 September 2008). Because which the reality amounts to turns on the specific arrangement of the duty, we do not conclude on the individual case here (**unverified**). The idea of on-call/standby allowances and working time also relates to [Is a home-visit nursing on-call allowance working time](/en/labor/column/homon-kango-oncall-teate-jokin-kansan).

## What does the exemption permission for monitoring/intermittent duty waive?

For monitoring or intermittent duty with little actual work and much standby time, such as a watchman or manager, if the employer obtains permission from the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office, the provisions on working hours, breaks and holidays (Chapter 4 of the Labour Standards Act) are excluded (Article 41(iii)). The late-night premium, however, is not removed.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Basis | Labour Standards Act Article 41(iii) (a person in monitoring or intermittent duty for whom the employer has obtained administrative permission) |
| Rules removed by permission | Working hours (Art. 32), breaks (Art. 34), holidays (Art. 35), the overtime/holiday part of premium wages (Art. 37) |
| Rules that remain even with permission | The late-night (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) premium (Art. 37(4); Art. 41 does not exclude the night-work provisions) |
| Annual paid leave not removed | Annual paid leave (Art. 39) is not in Chapter 4 and is not exempted |

The idea that permission lowers all labour costs is wrong; the late-night premium remains (administrative interpretation, Hatsumoto No. 1506 of 14 October 1948). This follows the same reasoning as a manager still needing a late-night premium; the relationship between a nominal title and premium wages is in [Nominal managers and the late-night premium](/en/labor/column/kanri-kantokusha-nabakari-warimashi-shinya). The same issue of naps and standby in the security business is in [Naps and intermittent duty in the security business](/en/labor/column/keibigyo-kamin-dansoku-roudoujikan-roumu), and the working-hour rules for building cleaning in [Working-hour rules for building maintenance and cleaning](/en/labor/column/biru-maintenance-seisou-tsusan-roudoujikan-tekiyo-kakudai-roumu).

## On what criteria and documents does the Inspection Office grant permission?

Permission is obtained by submitting an application in Form No. 14 to the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Enforcement Regulations Article 34). Whether duty is granted as intermittent is reviewed on the reality of the work, following the criteria shown in administrative circulars (Hatsumoto No. 17 of 13 September 1947; Hatsumoto No. 150 of 14 March 1988).

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Application | Form No. 14 (application for exemption permission for a person in monitoring or intermittent duty) to the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office |
| Guide for intermittent duty | Actual work is intermittent and standby time is much; the circulars give as a guide that standby time is at least as long as actual-work time and the total actual-work time does not exceed eight hours a day |
| Reality reviewed | How actual work and standby are allocated across a day's duty, the frequency of night response, and whether resident or commuting |
| Hard to be granted | Duty with much actual work — visits, cleaning, inspection, resident response — i.e. little standby time |

Duty with ongoing actual work or judgement is hard to bring within the permission for monitoring or intermittent duty, and whether a duty can be granted turns on the reality of the work. Because the specific application of which manager's duty qualifies depends on the Inspection Office's review, we do not conclude on the individual case here (**unverified**). The permission criteria for overnight/day duty are in [Permission criteria for overnight/day duty in medicine and care](/en/labor/column/iryo-kaigo-shukujitchoku-kyoka-kijun), and the duty design of a facility with night shifts in [Staffing and night-shift labour of a fee-charging home for the elderly](/en/labor/column/tokutei-shisetsu-yuryo-home-jinin-haichi-yakin-roumu).

## What are the risks of operating without permission?

If you operate on the premise that "a manager is intermittent duty so no overtime pay is needed" without obtaining permission, the ordinary working-hours rules apply as they are. If you recalculate standby time as working time, unpaid premium wages arise for the part exceeding statutory hours (8 hours a day / 40 a week, Labour Standards Act Article 32) and may be claimed retroactively.

| Category | Basis | Premium rate |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) | 25% or more |
| Overtime beyond 60 hours a month | Same (applied to SMEs too from 1 April 2023) | 50% or more |
| Statutory-holiday work | Article 37(1) | 35% or more |
| Late-night work (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) | Article 37(4) | 25% or more |

To avoid unpaid wages, you first distinguish standby time from break time on the reality, obtain permission properly where the duty qualifies, and calculate premium wages correctly where it does not. For long duty, the overtime cap and concluding/filing a 36 Agreement are the premise ([How far can a 36 Agreement allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made)). Choosing a variable working-hours system to even out peaks is in [Choosing variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata), and the duty to prepare work rules in [From how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a manager's standby time at the reception be unpaid as a break?**
A. In principle no. Standby time ready for visitors, calls or emergencies, not guaranteed release from the employer's direction, is working time even without actual work (the Ministry's guidelines on the proper grasp of working hours also include standby time as working time). An unpaid break is limited to time with a guaranteed right to be released from work (Labour Standards Act Article 34).

**Q. If we get permission for intermittent duty, is overtime pay no longer needed at all?**
A. No. With permission from the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Article 41(iii)), the rules on working hours, breaks and holidays and the overtime/holiday premium are removed, but the late-night (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) premium is not (administrative interpretation, Hatsumoto No. 1506 of 14 October 1948). Also, duty with much actual work and little standby is hard to be granted in the first place.

**Q. Is all of a resident manager's time in their quarters working time?**
A. Not all. Direction does not reach private acts unrelated to work and not incidental to performing it, while time on standby — not released from work because residents may need a response — counts as working time (the general view from the Obayashi Facilities case, Supreme Court Second Petty Bench, 19 October 2007, and the Tokyo High Court judgment after remand, 9 September 2008). Which the reality amounts to turns on the specific arrangement of the duty.

**Q. Can a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handle the design of a manager's working hours?**
A. The working-time status of standby, the permission for monitoring or intermittent duty (Form No. 14), premium wages, and work rules and working-hour management are the labour area of a Shakai Hoken Roumushi.
