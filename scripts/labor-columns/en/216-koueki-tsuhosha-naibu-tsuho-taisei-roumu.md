**In short:** Building a structure for employees' internal reports is **a duty for an employer that regularly uses 301 or more workers**, and an effort duty for 300 or fewer (Whistleblower Protection Act Article 11, paragraphs 2 and 3). There are two things to do: first, **designate whistleblowing-response officers** (Article 11, paragraph 1); second, **put in place a structure for receipt, investigation, and correction.** The officer bears a duty of confidentiality, and leaking information that identifies the whistleblower without just cause draws a fine of up to 300,000 yen (Articles 12 and 21). Dismissal because of whistleblowing is void (Article 3), and demotion, pay cuts, and other disadvantageous treatment are prohibited (Article 5). This article sets out which companies owe what, from the statute and the guideline.

This page is for the HR, general affairs, and management of small and mid-sized companies that want to set up an internal-reporting system. From a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint, it sets out the structure required by the amended Whistleblower Protection Act (2020 amendment, in force 1 June 2022). The legal evaluation of whether a reported matter amounts to a violation, and the rights and wrongs of how to investigate, are the attorney's domain; what the company builds is the mechanism — rules, a window, and officers.

## Which companies owe the duty to build an internal-reporting structure?

Whistleblower Protection Act Article 11 requires the employer to **designate officers** (paragraph 1) and to **put a structure in place and take other necessary measures** (paragraph 2). On top of that, for an employer that regularly uses 300 or fewer workers, these are read as "must endeavor to" (paragraph 3). So it is **a duty for 301 or more, and an effort duty for 300 or fewer.**

| Number of regularly used workers | Officer designation / structure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 301 or more | Duty | Article 11, paragraphs 1 and 2 |
| 300 or fewer | Effort duty | Article 11, paragraph 3 |

"Regularly used workers" are counted to include not only regular staff but part-timers and casual workers used as a matter of ordinary course. Even a company under the effort duty does well to build the structure, and the guideline set by the Prime Minister (Cabinet Office Notice No. 118 of 20 August 2021) is a reference regardless of scale. Because a harassment consultation window and a whistleblowing window differ in purpose, decide whether to design them separately or to have one serve both with roles sorted out. ## How do you build the flow of window, receipt, investigation, and correction?

The guideline requires, as a structure to respond appropriately to internal reports, the stages of **receipt, investigation, and correction**, and the **independence and exclusion of conflicts of interest** of those who carry them out. The company sets the flow from receiving a report to correction as internal rules.

| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| Receipt | Set an internal reporting window and receive reports by letter, email, phone, etc. An in-house window and an outside window (law firm, etc.) can both be provided |
| Investigation | Investigate the report as needed and hear from those involved. Manage information so the whistleblower is not identified |
| Correction | If a violation is confirmed, take corrective measures and prevent recurrence. Inform the whistleblower of the status of the response |
| Independence / conflict exclusion | Exclude those with an interest in the subject of the report from those involved in the response |

So that whistleblowers can report with confidence, the window clearly states the receipt method and the confidentiality rules. On receiving a report, decide who is involved as an officer, and record the scope and method of investigation. Whether the reported matter amounts to a violation, and how far to investigate, is a legal evaluation to be pursued by consulting an attorney as needed. The company's side puts in place the mechanism — window, receipt, investigation, correction — and the rules that govern its operation.

## When you designate a whistleblowing-response officer, what duty of confidentiality arises?

The company must designate those who engage in the work of receiving public-interest reports, investigating, and taking corrective measures (whistleblowing-response work) as "**whistleblowing-response officers**" (Article 11, paragraph 1). A person designated as an officer bears a **statutory duty of confidentiality.**

An officer (or a former officer) must not, without just cause, leak information learned in the course of whistleblowing-response work **that identifies the whistleblower** (Article 12). A person who leaks in violation of this is punished by a **fine of up to 300,000 yen** (Article 21). A feature is that the duty covers "information identifying the whistleblower," not "the whole content of the report" — its focus is protecting who reported. The company should make it clear to the person that they are designated as an officer (such as in writing) and set in the rules the scope of officers and the handover procedure. Without designated officers, this confidentiality mechanism does not work, so it is the core of building the structure.

## How far is disadvantageous treatment because of a report prohibited?

The Whistleblower Protection Act broadly voids or prohibits a company's responses because of whistleblowing.

| Prohibited treatment | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Void dismissal | Dismissal because of whistleblowing is void | Article 3 |
| Void termination of worker-dispatch contract | Termination of a worker-dispatch contract because of a dispatched worker's report is void | Article 4 |
| Prohibition of other disadvantageous treatment | Prohibition of demotion, pay cuts, non-payment of retirement allowance, and other disadvantageous treatment | Article 5 |

The "whistleblowing" here means one that meets requirements such as the reportable fact and where it is reported. For a report that meets the requirements, dismissal because of it is void, and treatment such as demotion, pay cuts, and non-payment of retirement allowance is prohibited. In practice, so that a reassignment or appraisal of the reporting employee is not taken as "because of the report," it is important to keep the response to the report and personnel measures separate and recorded.

## What changes with the December 2026 amendment?

The Whistleblower Protection Act was **amended in 2025 and takes effect on 1 December 2026** (Act No. 62 of 2025; the effective date is set by Cabinet Order No. 408 of 2025). Building on the structure in force since 2022, it is a review in the direction of **strengthening protection and effectiveness.**

| Direction of amendment | Outline |
|---|---|
| Criminal penalty for disadvantageous treatment | A criminal penalty is newly set for dismissal, discipline, etc. because of a report |
| Shift of the burden of proof | A treatment introduced whereby a dismissal etc. within a set period after a report is presumed to be because of the report |
| Administrative measures for breach of the structure duty | A mechanism of administrative recommendation and order is set, with a penalty reaching a breach of the order |
| Wider protection / prohibited acts | Prohibition of obstructing or searching out a report, and a review of the scope of protected persons |

As of November 2026, when this article is published, it is still before the effective date. Confirm the specific penalty content and the requirements of the presumption against the materials the Consumer Affairs Agency publishes toward the effective date and the amended statute. For a company, it is realistic to build the current structure and then review the rules to match the effective date.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Even with 300 or fewer employees, must we set up an internal-reporting window?**
A. For an employer that regularly uses 300 or fewer workers, designating officers and building the structure is an effort duty (Whistleblower Protection Act Article 11, paragraph 3). It is not a duty, but the guideline set by the Prime Minister is a reference regardless of scale, and building the structure is itself regarded as desirable. An employer of 301 or more owes the duty. If a future increase is expected to reach 301, preparing the rules and window early makes the transition smooth.

**Q. Must the internal-reporting window be placed outside the company?**
A. The law does not go so far as to require the window to be placed outside. You can set it inside, or also provide an outside window such as a law firm. What matters is managing information so the whistleblower is not identified, and not involving anyone with an interest in the response (independence / conflict exclusion). Set a combination of in-house and outside windows in the rules according to your scale and structure.

**Q. What if an employee designated as an officer accidentally tells a colleague the whistleblower's name?**
A. If an officer (or a former officer), without just cause, leaks information identifying the whistleblower learned in whistleblowing-response work, it draws a fine of up to 300,000 yen (Articles 12 and 21). The duty covers "information identifying the whistleblower," not the whole content of the report. The company should make clear who the officers are and set in the rules how information is handled and handed over, so that no leak occurs.

**Q. Can we transfer a reporting employee for a reason unrelated to the report?**
A. Dismissal "because of" whistleblowing is void, and demotion, pay cuts, non-payment of retirement allowance, and other disadvantageous treatment are prohibited (Articles 3 and 5). A personnel measure for a legitimate reason unrelated to the report is not uniformly barred, but so that it is not taken as "because of the report," it is important to keep the response to the report and the personnel measure separate and to record the basis of the decision. The amendment effective December 2026 plans a treatment presuming a dismissal etc. within a set period after a report to be because of the report, calling for more careful operation.
