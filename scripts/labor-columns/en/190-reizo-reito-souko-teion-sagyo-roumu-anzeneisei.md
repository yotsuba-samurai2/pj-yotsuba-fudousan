**Bottom line first**: Work inside refrigerated and frozen warehouses counts as "work handling large amounts of low-temperature objects and work in markedly cold places" (Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, (ro) of the Ordinance for Enforcement of the Industrial Safety and Health Act), triggering the special-work-category health checkup on reassignment and every six months (Article 45) and a break facility placed outside a markedly cold workplace (Article 614). As work especially harmful to health, cold work caps overtime at 2 hours a day even with an Article 36 agreement (Article 36, paragraph 6, item 1 of the Labor Standards Act; Article 18 of the Enforcement Ordinance). Designing busy-season overtime and variable working hours as if for an ambient-temperature EC warehouse hits this ceiling. The authoritative record lives in the work rules, the labor-management agreement and the safety-and-health framework.

## What health risks should you grasp first, in labor terms, for cold work at a refrigerated or frozen warehouse?

Work inside refrigerated (roughly 10°C or below) and frozen (below freezing) warehouses makes cold itself a cause of health harm. Article 22, item 2 of the Industrial Safety and Health Act requires the employer to take measures to prevent "health harm from radiation, high temperature, low temperature, ultrasound, noise, vibration, abnormal air pressure, etc.", with low temperature spelled out as such harm. Blood-pressure swings from sudden temperature differences, mistakes or falls from numb fingers, and frostbite or hypothermia from long exposure are real on-site risks.

The labor starting point is recognizing that the work counts as a "special work category" in law. Work inside a refrigerated or frozen warehouse falls under Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, (ro) of the Ordinance for Enforcement of the Industrial Safety and Health Act: "work handling large amounts of low-temperature objects and work in markedly cold places." Once in this category, the special-work-category health checkup (every six months) and the overtime ceiling (2 hours a day) pile on—rules an ambient-temperature warehouse does not have. The design of a variable working-hour system and short-time part-timers for an ambient-temperature EC fulfillment center is set out in [labor management for logistics warehouses and EC fulfillment centers](/en/labor/column/butsuryu-souko-ec-center-hendo-henkei-roudoujikan-tanjikan-roumu), but a cold site needs safety and health added on top.

Note that the industrial-safety-and-health laws set no uniform numeric standard for warehouse internal temperature (no "below X°C is prohibited"). The Ordinance on Health Standards in the Office, which sets an effort target for office temperature (18°C or above, 28°C or below), does not apply to the work floor inside a refrigerated or frozen warehouse. That is precisely why the design protects workers not by raising the temperature but by managing exposure time, cold-weather gear, breaks and health management.

## How should you design breaks and re-warming the body in a cold environment?

Break time itself follows the ordinary standard of Article 34 of the Labor Standards Act, even for cold work. Where working hours exceed six, give at least 45 minutes; where they exceed eight, at least one hour, during the working hours (Article 34, paragraph 1). Breaks are in principle given all at once (paragraph 2) and must be used freely (paragraph 3). What matters for cold work is "where" the break is taken.

There are two provisions on break facilities, of differing force.

| Provision | Content | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinance Article 613 | Endeavor to provide a break facility that workers can use effectively | Duty of effort |
| Ordinance Article 614 | At markedly hot, cold or humid workplaces and the like, provide a break facility **outside** the workplace (except where there is an unavoidable reason, such as underground) | Mandatory |

Because the inside of a frozen warehouse is a "markedly cold workplace," placing a break facility inside is not enough; Article 614 requires a separate one in a warm place outside. Write into the work rules and work procedures the practice that, when workers feel numb fingers or dulled movement, they re-warm their bodies at the outside break facility. There is no uniform legal standard for the frequency or length of re-warming, so set it in practice with the advice of the occupational physician and the health officer, given the internal temperature, the work and individual differences. In addition, hot, cold or humid indoor workplaces that pose a risk of harm require temperature-and-humidity adjustment measures such as cooling, heating and ventilation (Ordinance Article 606) and measurement of temperature and humidity every half month (Article 607). Since warming the inside of a freezer is hard to assume, in reality you adjust the temperature and humidity of the anteroom and sorting area and respond by breaking up exposure time.

## Where should you watch out, between shift work, the night premium, and the overtime ceiling for cold work?

Cold chains are often sites that run 24 hours, where shift work and night work are unavoidable. Night work (10 p.m. to 5 a.m.) carries a separate night premium of 25% or more of the ordinary wage (Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act). Because this arises separately from the overtime premium, state the night premium rate and calculation method in the wage rules. The design of shift work itself (rest intervals between shifts, consecutive working days, how shifts are built) is set out in [shift work, variable working hours and night work in manufacturing](/en/labor/column/seizogyo-kotai-kinmu-henkei-roudoujikan-shinya-roumu).

What is most easily overlooked in cold work is the overtime ceiling. Article 36, paragraph 6, item 1 of the Labor Standards Act caps the time an Article 36 agreement may extend to 2 hours a day for "underground work and other work especially harmful to health prescribed by Ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare." Article 18 of the Enforcement Ordinance, which prescribes that "work especially harmful to health," includes "work handling large amounts of low-temperature objects and work in markedly cold places."

| Item | Ambient-temperature warehouse work | Cold (refrigerated/frozen) internal work |
|---|---|---|
| Daily overtime ceiling | Within the Article 36 agreement (the ordinary upper-limit rule) | Up to 2 hours a day (Article 36, paragraph 6, item 1; Enforcement Ordinance Article 18) |
| Basis | Labor Standards Act, Article 36 | Same Act, Article 36, paragraph 6, item 1; Enforcement Ordinance Article 18 |

So, busy season or not, you cannot have cold internal work exceed 2 hours of overtime a day. This applies equally to the "overtime" portion beyond the scheduled hours fixed in advance, even where you adopt a variable working-hour system. The crux of this article is that long shifts that an ambient-temperature EC warehouse could build within the ordinary upper-limit rule of an Article 36 agreement hit the 2-hour-a-day frame in cold work. For the basics of how far overtime can go under an Article 36 agreement, see [how far overtime can go under an Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). On the heat side (heatstroke), a reporting framework and the like have separately been made mandatory with penalties ([the mandatory heatstroke measures](/en/labor/column/necchusho-taisaku-gimuka-2025-06-genba-roumu)).

## What must the employer do on cold-weather gear, health checkups and safety-and-health education?

The safety and health of cold work is built up from individual measures. Here they are with their legal bases.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Supply of cold-weather gear etc. | Measures needed to prevent health harm from low temperature (cold-weather clothing, gloves, cold-weather boots, etc.); replace wet gloves promptly | Industrial Safety and Health Act, Article 22, item 2 |
| Special-work-category health checkup | A health checkup on reassignment and once every six months | Ordinance Article 45 (work under Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, (ro)) |
| General health checkup | Health checkup at hiring and periodically (once a year or less) | Industrial Safety and Health Act, Article 66 |
| Safety-and-health education | At hiring and on change of work content, educate on cold hazards, cold-weather protection and emergency response | Industrial Safety and Health Act, Article 59 |
| Break facility | Provide a warm break facility outside the workplace | Ordinance Article 614 |

Especially weighty is the special-work-category health checkup. Because cold work falls under Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, (ro) of the Ordinance, in addition to the general periodic health checkup (once a year) a health checkup is needed on reassignment and once every six months (Article 45). Part-timers too fall within scope if they are regularly employed and engaged in this special work. Since health checkups and social-insurance coverage tests for short-time workers vary with the actual work, see also [the duty to conduct periodic health checkups](/en/labor/column/teiki-kenko-shindan-jisshi-gimu-hiyo-jikan). In safety-and-health education, as a guard against being locked in (a door closing inside and leaving a worker unable to get out), informing workers of emergency unlocking devices and ways to escape from inside is indispensable. This is a point on which a fatal accident has occurred in the past.

## How do you set up busy-season long hours, the Article 36 agreement and the variable working-hour system, and who is in charge?

Using a variable working-hour system for the swings of cold work is possible in itself, but the order of design differs from an ambient-temperature warehouse.

| Document | What to write |
|---|---|
| Work rules | The cold-work category, start/end times and breaks of each shift (including outside breaks), shift and night work, supply of cold-weather gear, the health-checkup category |
| Labor-management agreement (variable working hours) | Covered workers, the target period, the hours of each working day. The yearly system is filed with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office |
| Article 36 agreement | The limits on overtime and holiday work. **Reflect the special 2-hour-a-day ceiling for cold work in the agreement** (Enforcement Ordinance Article 18) |
| Wage rules | The premium rates and calculation methods for overtime, holiday and night work |

The key is to build the 2-hour-a-day ceiling for cold work into the Article 36 agreement when you conclude it. Diverting the ambient-temperature template as is makes you illegal the moment you have a worker do more than 2 hours of overtime on site, even if the agreement itself is valid. For choosing a variable working-hour system, see [choosing between variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata); for the duty to prepare work rules, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

The division of roles splits by the governing law. Preparing and filing the work rules, wage rules, labor-management agreement and Article 36 agreement, the safety-and-health framework (designing the special-work health checkups and education), and the variable working-hour system are handled by a Shakai Hoken Roumushi. Registration of a warehousing business under the Warehousing Business Act, notification of refrigeration equipment under the High Pressure Gas Safety Act, and registration as a Class I fluorocarbon charging and recovery operator under the Act on Rational Use and Proper Management of Fluorocarbons—applications to public agencies—are handled by a Gyoseishoshi. Construction and safety of the refrigeration equipment itself are handled by licensed equipment and electrical contractors (High Pressure Gas Production Safety Manager, electrician, etc.). Suitability of the warehouse premises—zoning, floor load, power and the like—is surveyed and brokered by a real estate transaction agent. Each is contracted separately as an independent business entity, and there is no referral fee. Negotiation or litigation over unpaid premium wages or a breach of the duty of care for safety in an individual case is not handled by a Shakai Hoken Roumushi; we refer you to a lawyer (Bengoshi).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. May we have workers inside a frozen warehouse do 3 hours of overtime a day in the busy season?**
A. No. Work inside a frozen warehouse counts as work especially harmful to health—"work handling large amounts of low-temperature objects and work in markedly cold places" (Enforcement Ordinance Article 18). For this work, even with an Article 36 agreement, the overtime that may be extended in a day is capped at 2 hours (Article 36, paragraph 6, item 1 of the Labor Standards Act). The 2-hour frame applies even to the portion beyond the scheduled hours fixed in advance under a variable working-hour system. How to count time switched to other work, such as ambient-temperature sorting, varies with the actual work, so individual confirmation is needed.

**Q. If we put heaters and a stove inside the warehouse, does that satisfy the break-facility duty?**
A. At a markedly cold workplace, providing a break facility "outside" the workplace is the duty under Ordinance Article 614. Placing heaters inside alone does not meet this "outside the workplace" requirement. Since the internal temperature must be kept low for freezing, you provide a warm break facility in an outside anteroom or separate room and have workers re-warm their bodies there. Break time itself follows Article 34 of the Labor Standards Act: at least 45 minutes for over six hours and one hour for over eight, given during the working hours.

**Q. Do cold-work part-timers also need the six-monthly health checkup?**
A. If they are regularly employed and engaged in work inside a refrigerated or frozen warehouse (the special work under Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, (ro)), a special-work-category health checkup is needed on reassignment and once every six months (Ordinance Article 45). This turns not on employment form but on the work engaged in. Together with the scope of workers subject to the general periodic health checkup (once a year), short-time workers are judged individually by actual working hours and continuity of use.

**Q. Can the labor matters of a frozen warehouse and the licensing of refrigeration equipment and the warehousing business be entrusted to the same office?**
A. The professionals in charge differ. Preparing and filing the work rules, Article 36 agreement, wage rules and labor-management agreement, and designing the safety-and-health framework and variable working-hour system for cold work, are the work of a Shakai Hoken Roumushi. Registration of a warehousing business under the Warehousing Business Act and notification/registration under the High Pressure Gas Safety Act and the Act on Rational Use and Proper Management of Fluorocarbons are the work of a Gyoseishoshi; a Shakai Hoken Roumushi cannot prepare these documents submitted to public agencies. Construction and safety of refrigeration equipment are handled by licensed equipment and electrical contractors, and survey and brokerage of warehouse premises by a real estate transaction agent. Each is contracted separately as an independent business entity, and there is no referral fee.
