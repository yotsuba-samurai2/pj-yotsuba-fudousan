**In short:** What a company does for a pregnant employee falls into three parts. First, **maternity leave** — before birth, 6 weeks before the due date (14 weeks for a multiple pregnancy) on the employee's request; after birth, 8 weeks regardless of request (Labour Standards Act Article 65). Second, **work restrictions and maternal health management** — on the request of an expectant or nursing mother (pregnant, or within one year of birth), limits on overtime and night work (Article 66), and securing time for health guidance and checkups (Equal Employment Opportunity Act Article 17). Third, **premium exemption and benefits during leave** — during maternity leave, health insurance and employees' pension premiums are exempted for both the insured and the employer (Health Insurance Act Article 159-3; Employees' Pension Insurance Act Article 81-2-2), and the maternity allowance is paid (Health Insurance Act Article 102). This article sets out the basis and procedure for each.

This page is for the HR and management of small and mid-sized companies with pregnant and birthing employees. From a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint, it sets out what the company should do from notice of pregnancy through the return after birth. The medical judgment written on the maternal health management guidance card is the attending physician's domain; what the company does is operate leave and work restrictions, and the procedures for premium exemption and benefits.

## From when and for how long can maternity leave be taken?

Maternity leave is set by Labour Standards Act Article 65, and **its nature differs before and after birth.** Before birth it is "on request"; after birth it is, in principle, "regardless of request."

| Category | Period | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-birth leave | 6 weeks before the due date (14 weeks for a multiple pregnancy) | When the employee requests leave (Article 65, paragraph 1) |
| Post-birth leave (principle) | 8 weeks after the day of birth | Work prohibited regardless of request (Article 65, paragraph 2, main clause) |
| After 6 weeks post-birth | Weeks 6 to 8 after birth | Work allowed for duties the employee requests and a physician finds no problem with (Article 65, paragraph 2, proviso) |

Pre-birth leave is counted from the due date, and any delay beyond the due date is also included in pre-birth leave. Of the 8 weeks after birth, the first 6 weeks are **compulsory leave** during which the employee cannot be made to work even if she wishes; the remaining 2 weeks allow work only for duties the employee requests and a physician approves. Note that the Labour Standards Act does not require wages to be paid for the maternity leave period. What supports this unpaid period is the maternity allowance and premium exemption below. Benefits for continuing to childcare leave after birth are covered in [How much is the post-birth childcare leave (postnatal paternity leave) benefit](/en/labor/column/shussei-go-kyugyo-shien-kyufu-2025) and [What companies must do under the 2025 amendment to the Childcare and Family Care Leave Act](/en/labor/column/ikuji-kaigo-kyugyo-ho-2025-kaisei).

## What work restrictions and accommodations does a company make for a pregnant or post-birth employee?

For an expectant or nursing mother (a pregnant woman and a woman within one year of birth), there are **work restrictions on the employee's request** and restrictions on **dangerous and harmful work** that must be observed without waiting for a request.

| Measure | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer to lighter duties | On a pregnant woman's request, transfer her to other lighter duties | Labour Standards Act Article 65, paragraph 3 |
| Limit on overtime and holiday work | On an expectant or nursing mother's request, no overtime or holiday work | Labour Standards Act Article 66, paragraph 2 |
| Limit on night work | On an expectant or nursing mother's request, no night work | Labour Standards Act Article 66, paragraph 3 |
| Limit on variable working hours | On request, not made to work beyond statutory hours even under a variable-hours system | Labour Standards Act Article 66, paragraph 1 |
| Restriction on dangerous/harmful work | Not assigned to handling heavy objects, work in places emitting harmful gas, etc. | Labour Standards Act Article 64-3; Rules on Labour Standards for Women |

Limits on overtime and night work, and transfer to lighter duties, **become the company's duty only once the employee requests them.** Conversely, the restriction on dangerous and harmful work must be observed regardless of request. Exactly which duties are restricted is set by the Rules on Labour Standards for Women, and some, such as handling heavy objects, draw the line differently depending on whether the woman is pregnant. In addition, dismissal or other disadvantageous treatment because of pregnancy, birth, or requesting or taking maternity leave is prohibited (Equal Employment Act Article 9, paragraph 3), and dismissal of a woman who is pregnant or within one year of birth is void unless the company proves it is for another reason (same Article, paragraph 4).

## When a maternal health management guidance card is issued, what does the company do?

The Equal Employment Opportunity Act imposes two measures on the company for health management during and after pregnancy: **securing time to receive health guidance and checkups** (Article 17) and **work measures so the guidance can be followed** (Article 18).

| Timing | Frequency of time to be secured |
|---|---|
| Up to 23 weeks of pregnancy | Once every 4 weeks |
| From 24 to 35 weeks | Once every 2 weeks |
| From 36 weeks until birth | Once a week |
| After birth (within one year) | The time needed, following the physician's instruction |

So that an employee who receives guidance from a physician or midwife at a checkup can follow it, the company takes measures such as changing working hours (staggered commuting etc.), reducing duties, or leave (Article 18). The tool used here is the **maternal health management guidance card** — a form the attending physician fills in with the necessary measures and the employee passes to the company; the company then acts on its content. The medical judgment of the symptoms and necessary measures written on the card is the physician's; the company receives it and makes the labour-side adjustment of duties. The point of the system is to reflect the physician's guidance in work, not to rely on the employee's self-report alone.

## How do you handle the premium exemption and benefits during leave?

Maternity leave is usually unpaid, but **exemption from social insurance premiums** and **health insurance benefits** provide support. Each has fixed requirements and destinations.

| System | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Premium exemption | From the month in which maternity leave begins to the month before the one containing the day after it ends, premiums are not collected (exempted for both insured and employer) | Health Insurance Act Article 159-3; Employees' Pension Insurance Act Article 81-2-2 |
| Maternity allowance | Paid for the period not worked, from 42 days before the day of birth (98 days for a multiple pregnancy; if birth is after the due date, from before the due date) to 56 days after the day of birth | Health Insurance Act Article 102 |
| Lump-sum birth allowance | A sum set by Cabinet Order (currently 500,000 yen) paid on giving birth | Health Insurance Act Article 101 |

The premium exemption is obtained when the **employer files the "Notice of Taking Maternity Leave" with the pension office (the insurer).** Even while exempted, insured status continues, and in the pension calculation the period is treated as one for which premiums were paid. The maternity allowance is roughly two-thirds of the standard daily remuneration (the average of the standard monthly remuneration over a fixed period before the start of payment, divided by 30). Because the amount of the lump-sum birth allowance and any add-on can change with reform, confirm it against the latest guidance from the health insurance association just before the procedure (as of November 2026, 500,000 yen in principle). These are health insurance and pension procedures that a shakai hoken roumushi can handle.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Must pre-birth leave of 6 weeks always be given?**
A. Pre-birth leave is leave during which the employee cannot be made to work when she requests it (Labour Standards Act Article 65, paragraph 1). If she does not request it, working right up to just before the due date is not itself barred. By contrast, the 8 weeks after birth (the first 6 in particular) cannot be worked regardless of request — this differs from pre-birth leave. For a multiple pregnancy, pre-birth leave extends to 14 weeks. In practice, deciding early when pre-birth leave begins, while checking the employee's condition and wishes, makes the procedure smooth.

**Q. A pregnant employee asked not to be made to work overtime. Must we comply?**
A. When an expectant or nursing mother (pregnant, or within one year of birth) requests it, she cannot be made to do overtime or holiday work (Article 66, paragraph 2), nor night work (Article 66, paragraph 3). Because these require the employee's request, once there is a request the company must comply. Even under a variable-hours system, on request she cannot be made to work beyond the daily and weekly statutory hours (Article 66, paragraph 1). Keep a record of the scope and period of the request, and set the procedure in the work rules for stable operation.

**Q. Does the company prepare the form for the maternal health management guidance card?**
A. The card is a form shown by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare; the attending physician fills in the necessary measures and the employee submits it to the company. The company responds according to the measures written on the card (change of working hours, reduction of duties, leave, etc.) (Equal Employment Act Article 18). Rather than the company judging the rightness of the card's medical content, its role is to adjust work in line with the entries. Even without a card, securing time for checkups (Article 17) is the company's duty.

**Q. During maternity leave, is the employee's own share of social insurance premiums also exempted?**
A. Yes. During the maternity leave period, both the insured's (the employee's) share and the employer's share of health insurance and employees' pension premiums are exempted (Health Insurance Act Article 159-3; Employees' Pension Insurance Act Article 81-2-2). The exemption is obtained when the employer files the notice of taking maternity leave with the pension office. The exemption period runs from the month in which leave begins to the month before the one containing the day after it ends. Even while exempted, insured status continues, and the period is treated in the pension calculation as one for which premiums were paid.
