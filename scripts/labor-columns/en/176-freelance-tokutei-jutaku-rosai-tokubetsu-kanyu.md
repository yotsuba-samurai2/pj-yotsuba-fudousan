**In short:** Freelancers (specified entrusted businesses) can, **from November 1, 2024, join workers' accident insurance by special enrollment regardless of industry**. Accident insurance is originally a system for workers, but the injuries of people who take on work on commission without employing workers can be made a target of coverage through the special-enrollment system (Workers' Accident Compensation Insurance Act, Articles 33 to 37). What was newly established that day is the "**specified freelance business (the business carried on by a specified entrusted business)**" under Type 2 special enrollment (sole proprietors and the like, Article 35), so special enrollment of sole proprietors and the like, formerly limited to certain industries such as construction and transport, spread to freelancers generally. Enrollment is **applied for by the person through a special-enrollment association**, choosing a daily benefit base (in the range of 3,500 to 25,000 yen), which the prefectural labour bureau director decides. Freelancers who work on commission could not, as a rule, receive accident-insurance benefits when injured at work, because they are not workers. The November 1, 2024 amendment fills this gap. This page is for both the freelancer and companies that order work from freelancers, setting out the key points of the special-enrollment system from the viewpoint of a shakai hoken roumushi.

## From November 1, 2024, how far can freelancers (specified entrusted businesses) join accident insurance?

Workers' accident insurance is originally a system for workers. Freelancers who work without employing workers become a target of the **special-enrollment system** (Workers' Accident Compensation Insurance Act, Articles 33 to 37, Chapter 4-2), a voluntary enrollment on the person's own application where it is appropriate to protect them in a way similar to workers given the nature of their business.

On November 1, 2024, the "**specified freelance business (the business carried on by a specified entrusted business)**" was newly established under Type 2 special enrollment (sole proprietors and the like, Article 35). This made special enrollment possible **regardless of industry**, for the business a specified entrusted business carries on by taking work on commission. Formerly, special enrollment of sole proprietors and the like was limited to listed industries such as construction, transport and fishing, and other freelancers (IT, design, interpreting, lecturing, sales agency and so on) were outside it, but this expansion opened the way widely.

Here, a "specified entrusted business" follows the Freelance Act's definition — a business that takes on work on commission and **does not employ workers** (an individual, or a company with only one representative). Injuries in the course of taking on the same kind of work from consumers (so-called B-to-C) are also included in the coverage.

## What are the target, procedure and premium (daily benefit base) of special enrollment?

Special enrollment is applied for not directly by the person but **through a special-enrollment association approved by the labour bureau director**. The key points of procedure and cost are as follows.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Who may enroll | A specified entrusted business that carries on a business by taking work on commission without employing workers (specified freelance business) |
| Where to apply | The special-enrollment association for the specified freelance business (the person applies through the association to the prefectural labour bureau) |
| Daily benefit base | Applied for from a step within the range of 3,500 to 25,000 yen, and decided by the prefectural labour bureau director (the basis for both benefits and premium) |
| Premium | Daily benefit base × 365 (premium-calculation base) × the premium rate for the specified freelance business |
| Scope of coverage | Employment injury, multiple-employer-factor injury, and commuting injury. Medical, absence, disability, survivors' and other benefits |

The daily benefit base is both the basis of benefit amounts such as absence compensation and the basis for calculating the premium. Set it higher and both the benefits and the premium grow. After enrollment, the premium is paid through the special-enrollment association. Check specific amounts and eligibility with the association and the latest guidance. This differs from the Type 1 special enrollment of a small-business owner who uses workers (Article 34) in both the window and the requirements (the difference is covered in [an executive's accident-insurance special enrollment](/en/labor/column/shacho-rosai-tokubetsu-kanyu-hitori), and the construction sole-proprietor example in [social insurance in construction and a sole proprietor's accident-insurance special enrollment](/en/labor/column/kensetsugyo-shakaihoken-hitorioyakata-rosai-tokubetsu)).

## What are the ordering side's duties under the Freelance Act, and how do they differ?

The Freelance Act, effective the same November 1, 2024 (the Act on Optimising Transactions Related to Specified Entrusted Businesses, Act No. 25 of 2023), is a **completely separate system** from accident-insurance special enrollment. Whereas special enrollment is "a scheme the freelancer joins voluntarily," the Act is "**a law that imposes transaction optimisation on the side that orders work from freelancers**."

The main duties imposed on the ordering side (specified business-committing businesses and the like) are as follows.

| Duty of the ordering side | Outline | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure of transaction terms | Disclose work content, fee amount, payment date and the like in writing (Article 3) | All business commissions |
| Fee payment date | As a rule, pay within as short a period as possible within 60 days from the day the deliverable is received (Article 4) | All business commissions |
| Prohibited acts | Prohibits seven types such as refusal of receipt, fee reduction, returns and undervaluing (Article 5) | Commissions of 1 month or more |
| Accurate display of recruitment information | Display recruitment information accurately in advertisements and the like (Article 12) | Where recruiting |
| Consideration for childcare / care and harassment measures | Consideration for requests (Article 13); a consultation system (Article 14) | Article 13 is 6 months or more |
| Advance notice of mid-term termination | As a rule, notice 30 days in advance (Article 16) | Commissions of 6 months or more |

The Act aims at transaction optimisation and does not impose on the orderer a duty to enroll freelancers in accident insurance. The ordering side's duties are set out in detail in [the Freelance Act and what ordering companies must check](/en/labor/column/freelancelaw-hatchu-kigyo-kakunin).

## How should ordering companies approach special enrollment?

Special enrollment is a system the **freelancer joins voluntarily**. Ordering companies have no duty to enroll them and cannot do the procedure on the person's behalf. That said, if the substance of the commission contract is strong in worker-ness — subject to direction and supervision, bound as to time and place — then even if named a commission, the person is treated as a worker and becomes a target of accident and social insurance. In ordering, the starting point is first to discern **whether the substance is a commission or employment**.

On that basis, ordering companies must correctly fulfil the Freelance Act's duties such as disclosure of transaction terms and the fee payment date. Given that special enrollment is the person's voluntary enrollment, making the roles clear in the contract for safety and in the event of an accident helps avoid later trouble. The final judgment of worker-ness is made against the actual situation by a qualified professional.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. From when, and in which industries, can freelancers join accident insurance?**
A. From November 1, 2024. On that day, the "specified freelance business (the business carried on by a specified entrusted business)" was newly established under Type 2 special enrollment (sole proprietors and the like, Workers' Accident Compensation Insurance Act, Article 35), so freelancers who work on commission without employing workers can join by special enrollment regardless of industry. Formerly it was limited to certain industries such as construction and transport, but it now covers IT, design, interpreting, lecturing, sales agency and more widely. Injuries in taking the same kind of work from consumers are also covered.

**Q. Where is special enrollment applied for, and how is the premium decided?**
A. The person applies through a special-enrollment association for the specified freelance business, approved by the prefectural labour bureau director. A daily benefit base is applied for from a step within the range of 3,500 to 25,000 yen, and the prefectural labour bureau director decides it. The premium is calculated as "daily benefit base × 365 (premium-calculation base) × the premium rate for the specified freelance business" and paid through the association. Check specific amounts and eligibility with the association and the latest guidance.

**Q. Are the Freelance Act and accident-insurance special enrollment the same thing?**
A. No, they are separate systems. Special enrollment is a scheme the freelancer joins voluntarily in accident insurance. The Freelance Act (the Act on Optimising Transactions Related to Specified Entrusted Businesses, Act No. 25 of 2023, effective November 1, 2024) is a law that imposes on the side ordering work from freelancers duties such as disclosure of transaction terms (Article 3) and the fee payment date (Article 4); it does not impose on the orderer a duty to enroll freelancers in accident insurance. They merely share an effective date, with different aims.

**Q. Does an ordering company have a duty to enroll freelancers in accident insurance?**
A. No. Special enrollment is a system the person joins voluntarily, and the ordering company has neither a duty to enroll nor the ability to do the procedure on the person's behalf. However, even if named a commission, if the substance is employment — subject to direction and supervision, bound as to time and place — the person is treated as a worker and becomes a target of accident and social insurance. In ordering, the starting point is first to discern whether the substance is a commission or employment. The final judgment of worker-ness is made against the actual situation by a qualified professional.
