**Bottom line first**: Early-morning prep carries no premium by itself. A premium applies to the night band from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. (Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act, 25% or more) and to overtime beyond 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week (Article 37, paragraph 1, 25% or more). Absorb peaks and troughs with a monthly (Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hour system; fixed overtime pay is valid only where it can be distinguished from the ordinary wage. The authoritative record lives in the work rules, wage rules and labor-management agreement.

## From what time does early-morning prep become premium pay?

"Early start" does not mean "premium." Whether a premium applies is decided not by the clock time itself but by two things: whether it falls in the night band, and whether it exceeds statutory working hours.

Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act requires a night premium of 25% or more of the ordinary wage for work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. So if prep starts at 4 a.m., the hour from 4 a.m. to 5 a.m. is in the night band and needs a 25%-or-more night premium, but from 5 a.m. onward that alone is not subject to a premium. Early-morning work from 5 a.m. becomes premium only when that day's hours exceed 8, or the week exceeds 40, and overtime arises (Article 37, paragraph 1).

| Band / category | Basis | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. (night band) | Article 37, paragraph 4 | 25% or more |
| Overtime beyond 8 hours a day / 40 hours a week | Article 37, paragraph 1 | 25% or more |
| Overtime beyond 60 hours in a month | Article 37, paragraph 1, proviso | 50% or more (applied to SMEs too from 1 April 2023) |
| Statutory-holiday work | Article 37, paragraph 1 | 35% or more |
| Overtime and night | Article 37, paragraphs 1 and 4 | 50% or more (25% + 25%) |

Whether to pay an "early-morning allowance" is up to the company; it is not a legal duty. Note that the portion in the night band needs the night premium separately from any early-morning allowance. The thinking on the night premium is also set out in the shift-based [night work and variable hours in manufacturing](/en/labor/column/seizogyo-kotai-kinmu-henkei-roudoujikan-shinya-roumu).

## How do you use a variable working-hour system for a shop with large swings in busyness?

A bakery or pastry shop peaks on weekends and at Christmas and Valentine's and troughs on weekdays. If you staff for the peak under the plain principle of 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week (Article 32), you have idle hands in the lull and overtime swells in the peak. A variable working-hour system levels this out.

| System | Basis | Framework | Suited to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly variable working-hour system | Article 32-2 | Average within 40 hours a week over up to one month; set by work rules or a labor-management agreement | Shops with within-month swings by day of week |
| Yearly variable working-hour system | Article 32-4 | Average within 40 hours a week over the target period (over one month, up to one year); a labor-management agreement is mandatory | Shops with large seasonal swings such as Christmas |

The yearly system has ceilings: 10 hours a day and 52 hours a week; where the target period exceeds three months, working days are capped at 280 a year, and consecutive working days are in principle capped at 6 (Article 12-4 of the Enforcement Ordinance). The point to watch is that a variable system changes the day-by-day distribution while leaving the total untouched; it does not reduce overtime. Anything beyond the pre-set frame is treated as overtime as usual, needing a Article 36 agreement and premium pay. For choosing the system, see [choosing between variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata).

## How far is fixed overtime pay (deemed overtime) allowed?

Fixed overtime pay is conditional on the ordinary wage and the premium-wage portion being distinguishable. If they cannot be distinguished, what you paid as fixed overtime is not recognized as payment of premium wages.

In the Medical Corporation Koshinkai case (Supreme Court, Second Petty Bench, judgment of 7 July 2017), the Court held that even where there is an agreement to include premium wages in the salary, unless the portion that is the ordinary-hours wage and the portion that is the premium wage can be distinguished, premium wages cannot be said to have been paid. To use fixed overtime pay, you must show in the wage rules and the notice of working conditions how many hours of overtime (and night and holiday work) the fixed sum corresponds to, and pay the difference in any month where the actual premium wage exceeds the fixed sum.

| Point fixed overtime pay must meet | Content |
|---|---|
| Distinguishability | Separate the ordinary wage such as base pay from the fixed overtime pay (the premium-wage portion) in amounts |
| Stating the corresponding hours | Show in the work rules, wage rules and contract how many hours of premium the fixed sum corresponds to |
| Settling the difference | Pay the difference in any month where the actual premium wage exceeds the fixed sum |
| Night and holiday are separate | Do not assume that the night and holiday premiums are automatically included in a fixed sum meant for overtime |

The design "we pay a fixed sum, so no overtime pay is needed" is especially fragile in manufacturing-and-retail with much early-morning and night work. The valid requirements and difference settlement for fixed overtime pay are set out in [the valid requirements for fixed overtime pay and difference settlement](/en/labor/column/kotei-zangyodai-tekiho-youken-sagaku-seisan); the point that even a "manager" needs the night premium is in [the "manager in name only" and the night premium](/en/labor/column/kanri-kantokusha-nabakari-warimashi-shinya).

## When production and sales are on separate shifts, what about the Article 36 agreement?

A Article 36 agreement is concluded per workplace (establishment). Even if you split shifts between production (prep) and over-the-counter sales, if it is one workplace — the same store — one Article 36 agreement suffices in principle. But unless you conclude a Article 36 agreement and file it with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office, you cannot have anyone work overtime beyond statutory hours or on a statutory holiday at all.

Under the overtime cap that took effect on 1 April 2019 (1 April 2020 for SMEs), the overtime a Article 36 agreement can extend is in principle limited to 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year (Article 36, paragraph 4). Even with a special clause for temporary special circumstances, you cannot exceed the following (Article 36, paragraphs 5 and 6).

| Limit | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Principle | 45 hours a month, 360 hours a year | Article 36, paragraph 4 |
| Special clause (yearly) | Within 720 hours a year | Article 36, paragraph 5 |
| Special clause (single month) | Overtime plus holiday work under 100 hours a month | Article 36, paragraph 6, item 2 |
| Special clause (multi-month) | Overtime plus holiday work averaging 80 hours or less over 2 to 6 months | Article 36, paragraph 6, item 3 |
| Special clause (count) | May exceed 45 hours a month up to 6 times a year | Article 36, paragraph 5 |

Even where production is early morning and sales is daytime to evening on separate shifts, if one worker does both, that person's hours are managed on a combined basis. For the limits and filing of a Article 36 agreement, see [how far overtime can go under a Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made); for the duty to prepare and file work rules, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo). Labor design involving night and early-morning shifts and fixed overtime pay shares much with [the labor of opening a restaurant — night shifts and deemed overtime](/en/labor/column/inshokuten-kaigyo-roumu-shinya-shift-minashi), so check them together.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If prep starts at 4 a.m. before opening, is all of it premium?**
A. Not all of it. A premium applies first to the night band from 4 a.m. to 5 a.m. (part of 10 p.m. to 5 a.m.), which needs the 25%-or-more night premium under Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act. Early-morning work from 5 a.m. is not premium by itself. An overtime premium under Article 37, paragraph 1 applies only when that day's hours exceed 8 or the week exceeds 40 and overtime arises. It is judged by the night band or exceeding statutory hours, not by "early morning means a flat premium."

**Q. I want to work people 10 hours a day in the busy season — if I adopt a variable working-hour system, is overtime pay unnecessary?**
A. It does not become unnecessary. A variable working-hour system lets you set daily hours above 8 within a pre-set frame, and within that frame no overtime premium arises, but anything beyond the frame needs a Article 36 agreement and premium pay as usual. The yearly system caps at 10 hours a day and 52 hours a week, with consecutive work in principle up to 6 days (Article 12-4 of the Enforcement Ordinance). If the frame-setting or the agreement/work-rule provisions are inadequate, the variable system itself is void and the whole period is recalculated at the plain 8 hours and 40 hours.

**Q. If we pay 30,000 yen a month in fixed overtime pay, can we skip calculating overtime?**
A. No. Fixed overtime pay requires that the ordinary wage and the premium-wage portion be distinguishable, that you state how many hours of premium it corresponds to, and that you pay the difference in any month where the actual premium wage exceeds the fixed sum. The Supreme Court (Medical Corporation Koshinkai case, 7 July 2017) held that including a premium in the wage in a way that cannot be distinguished is not recognized as payment of premium wages. It presupposes tallying the actual overtime, night and holiday work each month and comparing it against the fixed sum.

**Q. If we split shifts between production and sales staff, do we need separate Article 36 agreements?**
A. In principle, no. Because a Article 36 agreement is concluded per workplace, one agreement suffices for the same store as one workplace even if production and sales are on separate shifts. However, the overtime cap (in principle 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year, within 720 hours a year even with a special clause) applies per worker, so where one person does both production and sales, that person's hours are managed on a combined basis. Where workplaces are physically separate, in principle an agreement and filing are needed per workplace.
