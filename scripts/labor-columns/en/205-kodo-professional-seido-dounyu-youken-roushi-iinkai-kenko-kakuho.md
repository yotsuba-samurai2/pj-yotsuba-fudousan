**Bottom line first**: The high-level professional (kodo professional) system removes the working-hours, break, holiday and late-night premium rules for the target worker only once you have a resolution by four-fifths or more of the labour-management committee, the worker's written consent, and the filing of the resolution with the head of the Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2). The target is limited to five duties set by ordinance, such as financial-product development, and the annual income requirement is a level substantially above three times the benchmark average annual wage, namely 10.75 million yen or more. Without at least 104 days off a year and four days per four weeks, grasping health-management time, and a selective measure, the exemption does not hold. The resolution, work rules and filing belong to a Shakai Hoken Roumushi; disputes over validity or unpaid wages belong to an attorney, each contracted separately as an independent business entity.

This page focuses on the labour side a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles: the requirements and operating procedures of the high-level professional system (kodo pro) under Labour Standards Act Article 41-2. Individual court disputes over whether an application is valid or void are the area of an attorney, and this article focuses on system design.

## How does the high-level professional system differ from the discretionary-work system?

The discretionary-work system is a "deemed working time" system that treats the worker as having worked the hours set by resolution or agreement regardless of actual hours, and the premium-wage rules for overtime, late-night and holiday work still apply. The high-level professional system, by contrast, does not apply the working-hours, break, holiday and late-night premium rules themselves to the target worker (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1). The decisive difference is that it removes the worker from the regulatory frame rather than deeming hours.

| Item | Discretionary-work system (professional / planning) | High-level professional system |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Labour Standards Act Articles 38-3 / 38-4 | Labour Standards Act Article 41-2 |
| Effect | Deems the set hours as worked | Does not apply the working-hours, break, holiday and late-night premium rules |
| Late-night premium | Required | Not required (the rule itself does not apply) |
| Income requirement | None | 10.75 million yen or more |
| Worker consent | Required for the planning type | Required (in writing); a withdrawal procedure is also required |

The shift of the professional discretionary-work type to worker consent is set out in [Worker consent for the professional discretionary-work system](/en/labor/column/sairyo-roudo-honnin-doui-2024). Kodo pro is not a "no overtime pay at all" system; in exchange for removing the rules (including the late-night premium), the measures for days off and health are strictly required. The difference from paying by fixed overtime is in [Requirements for lawful fixed overtime pay](/en/labor/column/kotei-zangyodai-tekiho-youken-sagaku-seisan).

## Where are the target duties and the income requirement fixed?

The target duties are limited to those set by ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare as requiring high-level expertise, where the link between the time spent and the results obtained is usually not high (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1, item 1; Enforcement Regulations Article 34-2). The current ordinance fixes the following five duties, and no others may use the system.

| # | Target duty (ordinance) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Development of financial products using knowledge such as financial engineering |
| 2 | Asset management (including instructions) and dealing/trading of securities (fund manager, trader, dealer) |
| 3 | Analysis and evaluation of securities-market trends and securities value, and investment advice (analyst) |
| 4 | Investigation, analysis, planning and advice on important matters of a client's business operations (consultant) |
| 5 | Research and development of new technology, products or services |

The income requirement, in addition to the job scope being clearly set by agreement with the employer (item 2(a)), is that the wage expected to be paid in one year is a "level substantially above three times the benchmark average annual wage" set by ordinance, namely 10.75 million yen or more (item 2(b)). Even if the income reaches 10.75 million yen, a worker cannot be a target unless the duty falls within the five types above. Not receiving concrete instructions from the employer about how the time on the duty is spent is also a premise. The difference from "nominal" manager operation is in [Nominal managers and the late-night premium](/en/labor/column/kanri-kantokusha-nabakari-warimashi-shinya).

## How do you arrange the committee resolution and the worker's consent?

Kodo pro takes effect only once a labour-management committee set up at the workplace resolves the required matters by a vote of four-fifths or more of the members, and the resolution is filed with the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1). In addition, the target worker must have consented — for example, by signing a document making clear the content of the system and the covered period.

| Procedure | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Set up the committee | Members from the employer and worker sides; Article 38-4 is applied mutatis mutandis | Article 41-2(3) |
| Resolution | Vote of four-fifths or more. Sets the target duty, scope of target workers, days off, health-management time, selective measure, consent withdrawal, complaint handling, prohibition of disadvantageous treatment | Article 41-2(1) items |
| Filing | File the resolution with the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office (an effect-triggering requirement) | Article 41-2(1) |
| Worker consent | Signing a document etc. making clear the system's content; disadvantageous treatment such as dismissal of a worker who did not consent is prohibited | Article 41-2(1) item 9 |
| Reporting | Report the implementation of days off, health-management time and health measures periodically to the authority | Article 41-2(2) |

The resolution must also set a procedure to withdraw consent, and treating a worker disadvantageously for not consenting or for withdrawing is prohibited. The difference in nature between a labour-management agreement and a committee resolution, and the filing practice, relate to [From how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo); the overtime cap and arranging the 36 Agreement are in [How far can a 36 Agreement allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made).

## What happens if you neglect the 104 days off and the health measures?

The kodo pro exemption holds only as a set with the requirements of securing days off, grasping health-management time, and a selective measure. If the employer does not take these measures, the proviso to Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1 means the exemption effect does not arise at all. In other words, if you neglect the measures, the working-hours and premium-wage rules apply as usual to the worker you intended as a target, leading straight to an unpaid-premium-wage problem.

| Requirement | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Secure days off | At least 104 days a year and four days per four weeks | Article 41-2(1) item 4 |
| Grasp health-management time | Grasp the sum of time in and outside the workplace by an objective method such as time cards or PC-use time | Article 41-2(1) item 3 |
| Selective measure (one of) | ① A rest of a set time or more per 24 hours from start (interval of 11 hours or more) + late-night work within four times a month ② A cap on health-management time (the part over 40 hours a week within 100 hours a month / 240 hours in three months) ③ At least one continuous two-week period off a year ④ A special medical check-up | Article 41-2(1) item 5 |
| Medical interview | A worker whose health-management time exceeds a set time (the part over 40 hours a week exceeding 100 hours a month) must have a physician's interview guidance (a duty with penalties) | Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 66-8-4 |

The physician's interview guidance for a worker whose health-management time over 40 hours a week exceeds 100 hours a month is a duty regardless of whether the worker applies, and a penalty-backed measure under the Industrial Safety and Health Act. The overtime cap and the health idea behind a physician's overtime cap also connect to [A physician's overtime cap and health measures](/en/labor/column/isha-jikangai-jougen-2024-suijun-kenko-kakuho). Levelling the burden by taking annual paid leave without using the system is in [The duty to take five days of annual paid leave](/en/labor/column/nenji-yukyu-5nichi-torikirenai); the variable working-hours system to even out peaks is in [Choosing variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If the annual income is 10.75 million yen or more, can any occupation use kodo pro?**
A. No. Even if the income requirement (a level substantially above three times the benchmark average annual wage, namely 10.75 million yen or more) is met, the target duties are limited to the five set in Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1, item 1 and Enforcement Regulations Article 34-2 (financial-product development, asset management/trading, securities analysis/advice, client-business investigation/advice, research and development). No other occupation may use it. Not receiving concrete instructions from the employer about how the time on the duty is spent is also a premise.

**Q. If we file the committee resolution, is the worker's consent unnecessary?**
A. It is necessary. Kodo pro requires, in addition to a resolution by four-fifths or more of the members and filing with the head of the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office, that the target worker has consented — for example, by signing a document making clear the system's content and covered period (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1). The resolution must also set a procedure to withdraw consent, and dismissal or other disadvantageous treatment for not consenting or for withdrawing is prohibited.

**Q. If we use kodo pro, is no late-night premium needed even for late-night work?**
A. While the requirements are met, the working-hours, break, holiday and late-night premium rules do not apply to the target worker (Labour Standards Act Article 41-2, paragraph 1). However, if you do not take at least 104 days off and four days per four weeks, grasp health-management time, or take a selective measure, the proviso to that paragraph means the exemption effect does not arise and the premium-wage rules apply as usual. The physician's interview guidance for a worker whose health-management time exceeds 100 hours a month is also a penalty-backed duty.
