**In short:** A company that receives dispatched workers (the dispatch recipient) has duties under the Worker Dispatch Act separate from those of the dispatching agency. **The same establishment may in principle receive dispatch for up to 3 years (establishment unit, Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-2), and the same person may in principle be received in the same organizational unit for up to 3 years (individual unit, Article 40-3)**, and the day this limit is reached is the "conflict date." The establishment unit can be extended by up to 3 years after hearing the opinion of the majority union (or, failing one, the majority representative); the individual unit has no extension procedure. During receipt, a **dispatch-recipient management ledger** is made for each dispatched worker (Article 42), recording the reality of work, stored for 3 years, and the agency is notified at least once a month. Even if you call it a contract for work or a business consignment, where your company directs the workers it can be judged "disguised contracting," with the reality treated as worker dispatch (Notice No. 37 of the Ministry of Labour, 1986). This page sets out conflict-date control, opinion-hearing, the ledger, the disguised-contracting line, and the division of roles.

This page is for HR and site managers of dispatch-recipient companies that receive dispatched workers, or that are unsure where a contract for work / business consignment ends and dispatch begins. It sets out the labour duties on the recipient from a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint. ## How do the conflict dates the recipient must keep differ between the individual unit and the establishment unit?

The "conflict date" is **the day the upper limit of the dispatchable period is reached, from the next day of which dispatch can no longer be received.** The period limits of the Worker Dispatch Act come in two kinds — establishment unit and individual unit — and they run separately.

| Period limit | Basis | Limit | How it is counted | Extension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Establishment unit | Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-2 | In principle 3 years | Totaled from the day the establishment began receiving dispatch | Extendable up to 3 years via opinion-hearing |
| Individual unit | Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-3 | In principle 3 years | The period the same worker is received in the same organizational unit | No extension procedure |

The establishment unit looks at "how many years this establishment has been receiving dispatch." Even if the dispatched workers change, the establishment's receipt period is totaled. The individual unit looks at "how many years the same person is received in the same organizational unit (a unit such as a section or team, judged by the grouping of work and the chain of direction)." **A dispatched worker on an indefinite-term contract, or aged 60 or over, and certain other dispatch, is outside the period limit.** Even if you receive the same person for 3 years, changing the organizational unit does not hit the individual-unit limit — but the establishment-unit conflict date advances separately. Not confusing the two clocks is the starting point.

The dispatching side also has a restriction not to dispatch the same worker to the same organizational unit beyond 3 years (Worker Dispatch Act Article 35-3). The receiving side (Article 40-3) and the sending side (Article 35-3) bear the same 3-year limit under different articles.

## To extend the establishment-unit period, whose opinion do you hear and how?

To receive beyond the establishment unit's 3 years, you must **hear the opinion of the majority union (or, failing one, the majority representative) by one month before the conflict date** (opinion-hearing, Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-2, paragraph 4).

| Procedure | Content |
|---|---|
| Party whose opinion is heard | The majority union; failing one, a majority representative chosen by a proper procedure |
| Timing | From the start of the dispatchable period until one month before the conflict date |
| Prior information | Notify in writing the establishment and the period you intend to extend |
| If an objection is raised | By the day before the conflict date, explain the extension period, its reasons, and your response |

The majority representative must not be a manager/supervisor and must be chosen by a democratic method such as a vote or show of hands; **it is improper for the company to designate them.** Receiving beyond 3 years without the opinion-hearing is a period-limit violation and can fall under the labour-contract-offer deemed system (Article 40-6). The individual-unit 3 years has no such extension procedure; to continue, consider changing the organizational unit or switching to direct employment. Keep the record of the opinion-hearing in writing and make it known.

## What must be recorded in the dispatch-recipient management ledger?

The recipient must make a **dispatch-recipient management ledger** for each dispatched worker and record the reality of work (Worker Dispatch Act Article 42). It is a different thing from the dispatch-agency management ledger the agency makes (Article 37) — the party that makes it and the focus of the record both differ.

| Main items recorded | Example |
|---|---|
| Name of the dispatched worker; name and establishment of the dispatching agency | Whom you receive, and from where |
| Days worked on dispatch, start/finish times, break times | The actual reality of work |
| Type of work engaged in; name and location of the establishment worked at | Where and what |
| Whether indefinite- or fixed-term; whether subject to the period limit | The premise for conflict-date control |
| State of complaint handling; education/training and safety-health status | Performance of the measures the recipient must take |

The ledger is **stored for 3 years** (Worker Dispatch Act Enforcement Regulations Article 37), and some items are **notified to the agency at least once a month on a set date** (Enforcement Regulations Article 38). The measures the recipient must take — the dispatched workers' safety-health, complaint handling, prevention of sexual and power harassment, and so on — are organized in the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Guidelines on measures the dispatch recipient must take." These cannot be left to the agency; they are the duty of the receiving company itself.

## When does a contract for work or business consignment become "disguised contracting"?

Even if the form is a "contract for work" or "business consignment," **where in reality the orderer directs the other party's workers, it is judged worker dispatch.** This is disguised contracting, and the standard for judging it is Notice No. 37 of the Ministry of Labour of 1986 (Standard for distinguishing worker-dispatch business from business carried out by contract for work).

To be recognized as a contract for work, the Notice requires **meeting all of** roughly the following two pillars.

| Pillar | What must be met (gist) |
|---|---|
| ① Directly using one's own workers' labour | The contractor itself directs and manages the work method, working hours, and the decision and change of assignment |
| ② Processing the work independently | Procuring and bearing the funds itself, bearing legal responsibility, and processing with its own equipment, machinery, or specialist skill (not a mere supply of labour) |

Where the orderer's staff directly instruct the consignee's workers on the work, directly instruct start/finish or overtime, or decide assignments, pillar ① is not met and disguised contracting is more readily found. Found disguised contracting can, like a period-limit violation, fall under the labour-contract-offer deemed system. The general line between a contract for work / business consignment and worker status is also covered in [Where business consignment and worker status part](/en/labor/column/gaichu-koyo-sakaime-roudoushasei). When in doubt, returning to "who is directing" is the practical axis.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If the dispatched workers change, is the establishment unit's 3 years reset?**
A. No. The establishment-unit period limit (Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-2) looks at "the period that establishment has been receiving dispatch" on a totaled basis, so it is not reset when dispatched workers change. To receive beyond 3 years, you must hear the opinion of the majority union (or the majority representative) by one month before the conflict date. Treating it as if it resets when people change is an easily made error.

**Q. Is there a way to receive the same dispatched worker beyond 3 years?**
A. The individual-unit period limit (Worker Dispatch Act Article 40-3) has no extension procedure like the establishment unit. In the same organizational unit, 3 years is in principle the limit. Moving the worker to a different organizational unit with a different grouping of work, or switching to direct employment, are options — but note the establishment-unit conflict date is advancing separately. A dispatched worker on an indefinite-term contract and certain other dispatch are outside the period limit.

**Q. If we have a contract for work, can it not be disguised contracting?**
A. Even if the contract is nominally a contract for work or business consignment, where in reality the orderer directs the other party's workers, it can be judged worker dispatch (Notice No. 37 of 1986). Where the orderer's staff directly give work instructions, start/finish or overtime instructions, or decide assignments, disguised contracting is more readily found. The axis of judgment is "who is directing."

**Q. How long must the dispatch-recipient management ledger be kept?**
A. The dispatch-recipient management ledger is made for each dispatched worker, records the reality of work (days worked, start/finish times, type of work, etc.), and is kept for 3 years (Worker Dispatch Act Article 42; Enforcement Regulations Article 37). Some items are notified to the agency at least once a month on a set date (Enforcement Regulations Article 38). It is a different thing from the dispatch-agency management ledger the agency makes, and is the duty of the receiving company itself.
