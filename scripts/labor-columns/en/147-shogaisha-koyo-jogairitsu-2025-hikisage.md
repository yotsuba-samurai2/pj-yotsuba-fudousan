**In short:** Industry exclusion rates fell by ten percentage points on April 1, 2025. The private-sector statutory employment rate has been 2.7% since July 2026. Recalculate each establishment's deduction, but the required whole-number headcount does not always rise.

This guide focuses on industry classification and exclusion-rate calculations for HR staff in construction, transport, medical services and other covered industries. **System check and content update: October 10, 2026.** For general employee and disabled-worker counting, see [disability employment at 2.7%: how to count your workforce](/en/labor/column/shogaisha-koyoritsu-2.7-kakunin-jiko).

## How do exclusion rates work, and which legislation sets them?

障害者の雇用の促進等に関する法律 (Act on Employment Promotion, etc. of Persons with Disabilities), Article 43 provides for employers' disability employment duties. An exclusion rate deducts a specified number from the regular-worker headcount used as the calculation base in designated industries.

The system was abolished in the main statutory provisions by the 2002 reform but remains temporarily under transitional provisions. The designated industries and current exclusion rates are set by a ministerial ordinance: **障害者の雇用の促進等に関する法律施行規則 (Enforcement Regulations), supplementary provisions Article 1-3 and Appended Table 4**. Distinguish these provisions from the statutory employment rate itself.

Apply exclusion rates establishment by establishment. Do not apply one rate uniformly to a headquarters, factory and other establishments in different industries. Identify their industry classifications and workforce counts before calculating the company total.

## How did industry rates change from April 2025?

After reductions in April 2004 and July 2010, a further uniform ten-percentage-point reduction took effect on April 1, 2025. Industries previously at 10% or less left the system and receive no exclusion deduction. Examples of the remaining rates are:

| Industry examples from April 1, 2025 | Exclusion rate |
|---|---|
| Primary nonferrous metal smelting/refining; freight forwarding excluding collection/delivery utilization transport | 5% |
| Construction; iron and steel; road freight transport; postal services including correspondence delivery | 10% |
| Port transport; security services | 15% |
| Railways; medical services; geriatric health services facilities | 20% |
| Forestry excluding hunting | 25% |
| Metal mining; child welfare services | 30% |
| Road passenger transport | 45% |
| Ship operation and related businesses by seafarers and others | 70% |

Check MHLW's full list for the precise industry scope. If classification is unclear, organize the establishment's actual activities and ask Hello Work or the relevant authority.

## Does a lower exclusion rate always increase the required number?

A smaller deduction increases the calculation base, but rounding means the required whole-number headcount can remain unchanged. Industries leaving the exclusion system do not necessarily face a larger increase than other industries.

The following is **a hypothetical calculation holding the employment rate at 2.7% and changing only the exclusion rate from 20% to 10%**. The actual employment rate in April 2025 was 2.5%; this is not a direct comparison of historical and current obligations.

| Assumption | Regular workers | Exclusion rate | Base after deduction | Required disabled-worker count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At 20% | 1,000 | 20% | 800 | 21 (800 × 2.7% = 21.6, rounded down) |
| At 10% | 1,000 | 10% | 900 | 24 (900 × 2.7% = 24.3, rounded down) |

This example increases the requirement by three. Actual calculations must reflect working-time equivalents, disability category and severity, establishment deductions, and company-wide aggregation. Here the deduction is a whole number; consult the latest reporting instructions when a deduction produces a fraction.

## How should current calculations and earlier reports be separated?

The private-sector statutory employment rate **became 2.7% on July 1, 2026**. The report of employment as of June 1, 2026 uses the then-applicable 2.5%. Do not retrospectively substitute 2.7% for that reference date.

Check, in order: each establishment's industry and exclusion rate; the employment rate applicable to the reference date; deductions, rounding and worker equivalents; and the gap against employees already counted. General recruitment, counting and levy guidance is in [the 2.7% workforce-counting guide](/en/labor/column/shogaisha-koyoritsu-2.7-kakunin-jiko).

Employment-status reports go through Hello Work to the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. Levy declarations and payments go to the Japan Organization for Employment of the Elderly, Persons with Disabilities and Job Seekers (JEED). ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. What changed in exclusion rates in 2025?**
A. Each designated industry received a ten-percentage-point reduction from April 1, 2025. Industries previously at 10% or less left the system, so their establishments calculate without an exclusion deduction.

**Q. Does a lower rate always increase the required headcount?**
A. The calculation base rises, but rounding can leave the required whole-number count unchanged. In the hypothetical 1,000-worker example at a fixed 2.7%, changing the exclusion rate from 20% to 10% changes the requirement from 21 to 24.

**Q. Where can I check covered industries and rates?**
A. The Enforcement Regulations, supplementary provisions Article 1-3 and Appended Table 4, and MHLW's list. Apply them by establishment; do not use one uniform rate across all establishments. Ask Hello Work about unclear classifications with the actual activities documented.

**Q. Does the June 1, 2026 report also use 2.7%?**
A. No. The private-sector rate on that date was 2.5%; 2.7% took effect on July 1, 2026. Follow the period or reference-date instructions for each report and levy calculation.
