**Bottom line first**: If the Labor Standards Inspection Office comes to inspect (an on-site inspection), the basic flow is to show the ledgers and documents requested, and if you receive a correction recommendation or guidance note, correct the matter by the deadline and submit a correction report. The on-site inspection and the demand to produce ledgers are powers of the labor standards inspector under Article 101 of the Labor Standards Act, and refusal or false entry is subject to the penalty in Article 120. Keeping the statutory three ledgers, the Article 36 agreement and the work rules in order at all times is the best preparation for not being caught out in an inspection.

## What kinds of inspection does the Labor Standards Inspection Office carry out, and why do they come?

An inspection by the Labor Standards Inspection Office is called an on-site inspection, in which a labor standards inspector enters the workplace to check ledgers and the actual working conditions. The basis is Article 101 of the Labor Standards Act: the inspector may make an on-site inspection of the workplace, dormitory and other attached buildings, demand the production of ledgers and documents, and question the employer or workers (carrying a certificate proving their status). By the operation of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and the Prefectural Labour Bureaus, inspections are roughly divided as follows.

| Type | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Periodic inspection | A routine inspection, choosing targets based on an annual plan |
| Complaint inspection | An inspection on a worker's report (Article 104) |
| Accident inspection | An inspection when a work accident occurs, to find the cause and prevent recurrence |
| Re-inspection | An inspection to confirm the response to a past correction recommendation |

A worker may report a fact of violation of the Act at the workplace to the administrative agency or the labor standards inspector, and the employer must not dismiss or otherwise treat the worker adversely on the ground of the report (Article 104). In other words, "why they came" may be a planned periodic inspection, or may be triggered by a worker's report or a work accident (the classification of inspections is by the operation of the Ministry and the Bureaus, viewed 12 November 2026).

## On the day, what is examined, and what ledgers and documents are demanded?

In an on-site inspection, the actual conditions of working hours, wages and health and safety are checked against the ledgers. At the center are the documents whose preparation and retention are required by law.

| Document | Basis |
|---|---|
| Workers' roster | Labor Standards Act Article 107 |
| Wage ledger | Article 108 |
| Attendance records such as time cards | Records of working hours (the basis of wage calculation under Article 108) |
| Agreement on overtime and holiday work (Article 36 agreement) | Article 36 |
| Work rules (workplaces with 10 or more regular workers) | Article 89 |
| Health-check records and health-and-safety documents | Industrial Safety and Health Act etc. |

The workers' roster, wage ledger and attendance records are the so-called statutory three ledgers, whose preparation and retention for a set period are mandatory (see [preparing and retaining the statutory three ledgers](/en/labor/column/hotei-san-choubo-seibi-hozon)). Article 91 of the Industrial Safety and Health Act also gives the inspector power to enter, question and inspect objects, so the health-and-safety side may be checked too. If you refuse to produce the demanded ledgers and documents, or produce ledgers and documents with false entries, you are subject to a fine up to 300,000 yen under Article 120, item 4 of the Labor Standards Act. On the day, the basic approach is to promptly show the documents demanded and to answer later on points you cannot confirm.

## How do a "correction recommendation" and a "guidance note" differ, and what happens if you leave them?

The documents issued as a result of an inspection are mainly the correction recommendation and the guidance note. Neither is a legally defined "disposition"; both are documents issued as administrative guidance.

| Document | When issued | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Correction recommendation | When a violation of law is found | Administrative guidance stating the violation, the correction content and the correction deadline |
| Guidance note | When there is no outright violation but improvement is desirable | Administrative guidance stating the items to improve |

Both the correction recommendation and the guidance note are administrative guidance and are not in themselves administrative dispositions subject to an appeal. But if left, a re-inspection may follow, and in malicious or serious cases it may lead to the referral for prosecution or disclosure of the company name below. Under the Industrial Safety and Health Act, an order to stop using machinery or equipment (a use-stop order) may be issued where the danger of a work accident is imminent; this is an administrative disposition. Once issued, the starting point is to check the cited provisions and the correction deadline and organize what to correct by when.

## By when and how do you write the correction report? What if unpaid overtime is found?

Once you receive a correction recommendation or guidance note, you correct the matter by the given deadline and submit a correction (improvement) report. In the report, for each item pointed out, you write concretely what you corrected and how (or the correction plan and timing).

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| Organize the points | List the items, cited provisions and correction deadlines |
| Check the facts | Check the actual working hours and wages against the ledgers |
| Carry out the correction | Revise provisions, review operation, pay shortfalls, etc. |
| Write the report | State the correction content and timing for each item and submit by the deadline |

A typical point raised is unpaid premium wages (overtime pay). If the working-hour records and the wage ledger do not match, you may be required to pay the shortfall retroactively. If you use a fixed overtime allowance (deemed overtime pay), whether it meets the requirements and whether you separately pay the excess is checked (see [the requirements for a lawful fixed overtime allowance and settling the difference](/en/labor/column/kotei-zangyodai-tekiho-youken-sagaku-seisan)). If correcting by the deadline is hard, do not leave it — present the correction plan and expected timing and consult. Supporting the drafting of the correction report and organizing the actual working-hour and wage conditions is the labor attorney's field. Fees are in the [fee schedule](/en/labor/ryokin).

## In what cases does it reach referral for prosecution or disclosure of the company name?

Most cases end with a correction report, but malicious or serious cases may proceed to criminal process. A labor standards inspector performs the duties of a judicial police officer under the Code of Criminal Procedure for crimes of violation of the Labor Standards Act (Article 102). That is, in certain cases the inspector has the power to investigate and refer the case to the prosecutor (referral for prosecution).

| Stage | Outline |
|---|---|
| Correction recommendation / guidance | Administrative guidance; usually ends with correction by the deadline |
| Re-inspection | Confirms the correction status; repeated if not corrected |
| Referral for prosecution | For malicious or serious violations, referral to the prosecutor under Article 102 |
| Company name disclosure | For illegal long working hours or referred cases, the company name may be disclosed by the operation of the Ministry |

What reaches referral or disclosure is mainly malicious or serious cases — repeatedly ignoring correction recommendations, hiding a serious work accident, or producing false ledgers (company name disclosure is based on the operation of the Ministry, viewed 12 November 2026). The criminal process after referral, legal disputes over responses to administrative guidance or dispositions, and lawsuits over unpaid wages are the lawyer's field. Where the labor attorney ends and the lawyer begins can also be seen from the division in [is dismissal a matter for a labor attorney or a lawyer](/en/labor/column/kaiko-sharoushi-bengoshi-dochira). These are engaged separately from the labor attorney's office as independent bodies. There is no referral fee.

## What should you keep in order at all times so an inspection is no trouble?

The best preparation against an inspection is everyday labor management. If you keep the documents central to an inspection in order at ordinary times, you can respond calmly even when an inspection comes.

| What to prepare | Point |
|---|---|
| Statutory three ledgers | Prepare the workers' roster, wage ledger and attendance records; keep the retention period |
| Working-hour records | Grasp objectively with time cards etc. and match them to wage calculation |
| Article 36 agreement | Conclude and file before overtime or holiday work; operate within the upper-limit regulation |
| Work rules | Prepare and file if 10 or more regular workers; match them to actual conditions |
| Premium wages | Check the overtime-pay calculation; for a fixed overtime allowance, check the requirements and settling the difference |

Concluding and filing the Article 36 agreement and operating within the upper-limit regulation are set out in [how far the Article 36 agreement can be left to a labor attorney](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made), and the duty to prepare the work rules and what they contain in [the duty to prepare work rules and what they contain](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo). Keeping these in order in ordinary times and keeping records is the best preparation for an inspection. Preparing the ledgers, agreement and work rules, and supporting the correction report when an inspection comes, is the labor attorney's field. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can we refuse an inspection by the Labor Standards Inspection Office?**
A. You cannot refuse without just cause. Article 101 of the Labor Standards Act provides that a labor standards inspector may make an on-site inspection of the workplace, demand the production of ledgers and documents, and question the employer and workers. Refusing, obstructing or evading the on-site inspection, not stating in response to questioning, not producing the ledgers and documents, or producing ledgers and documents with false entries is subject to a fine up to 300,000 yen under Article 120, item 4. You can discuss scheduling, but refusing the inspection itself is not appropriate.

**Q. Is a correction recommendation an administrative disposition? Can we dispute it if we object?**
A. Both the correction recommendation and the guidance note are not legally defined administrative dispositions but documents issued as administrative guidance. So they are not themselves a target to dispute as a revocation of a disposition. But if you doubt the basis or the fact-finding, you check the cited provisions and facts and then explain and discuss with the Inspection Office. The stage of legal disputes such as referral for prosecution, administrative dispositions, or lawsuits over unpaid wages is the lawyer's field.

**Q. If unpaid overtime is found in an inspection, how far back do we pay?**
A. You are generally required to settle the shortfall of premium wages. The range to go back and the calculation method are checked based on the working-hour records and the wage ledger. If you use a fixed overtime allowance, whether it meets the requirements and whether you separately pay the excess is checked. Because the amount and individual judgments on the limitation period differ by case, organize the actual working-hour and wage conditions against the ledgers and consult a labor attorney as needed. If it develops into a legal dispute such as a lawsuit over unpaid wages, that is the lawyer's field.

**Q. Is a tax office audit the same as an inspection by the Labor Standards Inspection Office?**
A. They are different. The inspection by the Labor Standards Inspection Office (on-site inspection) confirms compliance with labor-related laws such as the Labor Standards Act and the Industrial Safety and Health Act, covering working hours, wages and health and safety. A tax audit confirming the filing and payment of taxes is conducted by the tax office and is the tax accountant's field. Because their governing laws and responsible agencies differ, consult each separately as an independent body or agency.
