**In short:** A nursery-kindergarten combined centre (yoho-renkei-gata ninteikodomoen) mixes, in one building, Type 1 children who receive education only and Type 2 and 3 children in long-hours childcare. Type 1 is the **standard education time** (4 hours a day as the norm); Type 2 and 3 are the **childcare standard time** (up to 11 hours a day) or **short childcare time** (up to 8 hours a day), so hoiku-kyoyu (childcare teacher) shifts are built by overlapping these long and short spans. A hoiku-kyoyu is in principle required to hold **both a kindergarten-teacher licence and childcare-worker registration** (Ninteikodomoen Act, Article 15), but a special measure under which either one alone qualifies you has been extended, by the 2024 amendment, to the **end of FY2029 (March 2030)**. For short-hours staff, social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) applies if they work **three-quarters or more** of a regular worker's hours, and otherwise if they meet 20-plus hours a week, a prospect of employment over two months, not being a student, and the employer's size (over 51 insured persons until September 2027) — the 88,000-yen monthly wage requirement was abolished on October 1, 2026. This page organises opening hours and shifts, the hoiku-kyoyu qualification, short-hours staff's social insurance, and the labour side of the processing-improvement addition from a shakai hoken roumushi's viewpoint, sending applications for approval and certification to a gyoseishoshi, accounting and tax to a tax accountant, and premises to a real-estate agent — each contracted separately as an independent business entity.

A yoho-renkei-gata ninteikodomoen combines the functions of a kindergarten and a nursery, so opening hours, staff qualifications and the line on social insurance all become problems at once. This page is for directors and office staff who are opening or running a ninteikodomoen. The final judgment on whether a particular centre meets the standards or the addition requirements must be checked against the approving/certifying authority (prefecture or municipality) and the Children and Families Agency's guidelines.

## How do a ninteikodomoen's opening hours change across Type 1, 2 and 3 certification?

Opening hours are set by the certification category of the children admitted. Under the Child and Childcare Support Act, a child aged 3 or over who does not fall under a ground requiring childcare is **Type 1 (standard education time)**; a child aged 3 or over who does fall under such a ground is **Type 2**; and a child under 3 who falls under such a ground is **Type 3**. Type 1 follows the standard education time (4 hours a day as the norm), while Type 2 and 3 split, according to the amount of childcare needed, into the **childcare standard time (up to 11 hours a day)** or **short childcare time (up to 8 hours a day)**.

So in the same centre there are Type 1 children who arrive in the morning and go home in the early afternoon, alongside Type 2 and 3 children who stay 11 hours from morning to evening. Hoiku-kyoyu shifts must staff the education time generously while running early, late and extended childcare without gaps, so the busy-and-slack swing and the skew by time of day are large. This early-late-extended shift design is the same issue in nursery labour; [Labour when opening a nursery](/en/labor/column/hoikuen-kaisetsu-roumu-haichi-kijun-shogu) sets out the staffing standards and how to build a variable working-hours system.

| Certification category | Who | Time category |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | Age 3+, no ground for childcare | Standard education time (4 hours/day as the norm) |
| Type 2 | Age 3+, with a ground for childcare | Childcare standard time (up to 11h) / short childcare time (up to 8h) |
| Type 3 | Under 3, with a ground for childcare | Childcare standard time (up to 11h) / short childcare time (up to 8h) |

Actual opening hours and extended-childcare settings differ by centre, so working hours are designed against the centre's operating rules and the expected mix of certification categories.

## How does the hoiku-kyoyu qualification bear on shift design?

The chief hoiku-kyoyu, instructional hoiku-kyoyu, hoiku-kyoyu and others placed in a yoho-renkei-gata ninteikodomoen must in principle **hold a kindergarten-teacher licence and be registered as a childcare worker** (Ninteikodomoen Act, Article 15). This flows from the centre's character, where the same staff handle both education (Type 1 children) and childcare (Type 2 and 3 children).

Because holding both at once is hard, a **special measure under which either one qualifies you** as a hoiku-kyoyu is in place and, by the 2024 amendment, was extended to the **end of FY2029 (March 2030)**. The separate measure allowing a chief or instructional hoiku-kyoyu to serve with only one of the two has a shorter extension, to the **end of FY2026 (March 2027)**. As the deadlines approach, staff who hold only one side will need to acquire the other, so in building shifts it is essential to track who must satisfy the dual qualification by when.

| Category | Principle | Deadline of the measure |
|---|---|---|
| Hoiku-kyoyu etc. (dual-holding measure) | Kindergarten-teacher licence + childcare registration | Either one accepted until end of FY2029 (March 2030) |
| Chief / instructional hoiku-kyoyu (one-side-only measure) | Same | Until end of FY2026 (March 2027) |

The scope of the measure and the credits needed to acquire a licence should be checked against the latest guidance of MEXT and the Children and Families Agency and the prefecture's handling. The final check on whether the dual qualification is met is made by a qualified professional.

## By what standard are short-hours staff enrolled in social insurance?

A ninteikodomoen often supports early, late and extended childcare with short-hours part-time staff, so the social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) enrolment test is essential. The test has two stages. First, if a person's weekly scheduled hours and monthly scheduled working days are **three-quarters or more** of those of a regular worker in the same work, they are insured (this three-quarters rule is covered in detail in [Part-timers' social insurance and the three-quarters rule](/en/labor/column/tanjikan-koyo-shakaihoken-4bunno3)).

Short-hours staff below three-quarters are still insured if they meet all of: ① 20 or more scheduled hours a week; ② a prospect of employment over two months; ③ not being a student; and ④ the employer's number of employees' pension insured persons being at or above a threshold (over 51 until September 2027; expanding to over 36 from October 2027). There used to be a "monthly scheduled wage of 88,000 yen or more" wage requirement, but it was **abolished on October 1, 2026 under the 2025 pension-system reform act**, so the test now runs on 20-plus hours and the rest regardless of wage amount. The full picture of this phased expansion of the size requirement is set out in [Expansion of social insurance coverage (from 2027)](/en/labor/column/shakaihoken-tekiyo-kakudai-2027).

| Test | Content |
|---|---|
| Three-quarters rule | Weekly scheduled hours / monthly scheduled days are three-quarters or more of a regular worker's → insured |
| Short-hours worker requirements | 20+ hours/week / prospect over 2 months / not a student / size (over 51 until Sept 2027, over 36 from Oct 2027) |
| Wage requirement (88,000 yen/month) | Abolished on October 1, 2026 |

When the enrolment prospect changes, the premium and standard-remuneration filings change too. The final judgment on whether a particular staff member is insured is made by a qualified professional in light of the employer's size and the actual way of working.

## When reflecting the processing-improvement addition in wages, what do you put in order on the labour side?

The processing-improvement addition (shogu-kaizen-to-kasan) is added on top of the facility-type benefit on the premise of raising staff wages, so **how the addition received is distributed to which staff** must be written into the work rules and wage rules. The methods of deciding, calculating and paying wages, and matters on pay raises, are absolute mandatory items of the work rules (Labor Standards Act, Article 89), and a centre that regularly uses 10 or more persons is obliged to draw up and file work rules. Writing the allowances and distribution rules that correspond to the addition into the rules, so there is no gap with actual payment, is the foundation for explanation at audits and performance reports.

| What to put in order | Basis / aim |
|---|---|
| Wage rules (allowances, distribution rules) | Mandatory items under LSA Art. 89; make the basis for distributing the addition explicit |
| Drawing up / filing work rules | Obligatory where 10 or more are regularly used (LSA Art. 89) |
| Wage ledger, attendance records | Consistency between the addition's performance report and actual payment |

The calculation of the addition amount, its accounting and tax treatment, and the performance-report figures themselves are the field of a tax accountant and the local guidelines. What labour handles is the rules and the attendance/wage-ledger management needed to deliver the addition to staff as wages. The final judgment on whether the distribution is appropriate is made by a qualified professional against the centre's reality and the local guidelines.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do a ninteikodomoen's opening hours differ between Type 1 and Type 2/3?**
A. Yes. Type 1 is the standard education time (4 hours a day as the norm), while Type 2 and 3 split, by the amount of childcare needed, into the childcare standard time (up to 11 hours a day) or short childcare time (up to 8 hours a day). Because short-hours Type 1 and long-hours Type 2/3 children mix in the same centre, hoiku-kyoyu shifts must overlap the education time with early, late and extended childcare. Actual opening hours differ by centre.

**Q. Does a hoiku-kyoyu need both a kindergarten-teacher licence and a childcare-worker qualification?**
A. Dual-holding is the principle (Ninteikodomoen Act, Article 15). However, a special measure lets either one qualify you, and by the 2024 amendment it was extended to the end of FY2029 (March 2030). The measure for a chief or instructional hoiku-kyoyu to serve with only one side runs only to the end of FY2026 (March 2027), so you must track who satisfies the dual qualification by when.

**Q. Do short-hours part-time hoiku-kyoyu join social insurance?**
A. If they work three-quarters or more of a regular worker's hours, they are insured. Even below three-quarters, they are insured if they meet 20-plus hours a week, a prospect of employment over two months, not being a student, and the employer's number of employees' pension insured persons being at a threshold (over 51 until September 2027, over 36 from October). The 88,000-yen monthly wage requirement was abolished on October 1, 2026. The final judgment is made by a qualified professional against the actual way of working.

**Q. Can a shakai hoken roumushi do the procedures and accounting for the processing-improvement addition?**
A. A shakai hoken roumushi handles the work and wage rules and the wage-ledger/attendance management needed to deliver the addition to staff as wages. The calculation of the addition amount, accounting and tax filing, and the performance-report figures themselves are the field of a tax accountant and the local guidelines. The approval/certification application is a gyoseishoshi's; each is contracted separately as an independent business entity.
