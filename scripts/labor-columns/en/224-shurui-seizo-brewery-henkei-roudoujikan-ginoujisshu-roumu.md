**Bottom line first**: At a craft-beer or sake brewery you may use the one-year variable working-hours system (Labour Standards Act Article 32-4) for the brewing peak, but the labour-management agreement must keep each day within 10 hours and each week within 52, cap working days at 280 a year where the target period exceeds three months, and keep consecutive working days to six as a rule (Enforcement Rules Article 12-4). The production floor needs a health officer (50+ workers) or a safety and health promoter (10-49 in manufacturing) by headcount (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 12 etc.). For foreign workers, brewing alcohol is **outside** the Specified Skilled Worker "food and beverage manufacturing" field (MAFF FAQ, March 2026 update). The brewing licence, technical-intern plan approval and residence status belong to a Gyoseishoshi, liquor tax to a tax accountant, construction and zoning to real-estate agents and architects, and disputes to an attorney, each contracted separately as an independent business entity. No referral fees.

This page focuses on the labour a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles: peak-season hours, the safety and health structure on a food-production floor, and the labour structure for receiving foreign workers. The brewing licence and residence-status applications are the area of a Gyoseishoshi; liquor tax is a tax accountant's.

## How do you use a variable working-hours system for the brewing peak?

Where the busy stretch (mashing, wort-making, fermentation management, bottling) and the slack stretch differ sharply across the year, the one-year variable working-hours system (Article 32-4) is an option. By labour-management agreement you set daily and weekly hours so that the average weekly hours over a target period of more than one month and up to one year stay within 40; you can place longer scheduled hours in the peak by shortening the slack.

| Shape of the fluctuation | Suited system |
|---|---|
| Peak and slack clearly split across the year | One-year variable system (Article 32-4) |
| Busy and quiet weeks within a month | One-month variable system (Article 32-2) |
| Let workers choose start and finish times | Flextime (Article 32-3) |

See [choosing variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata) and, for shift use in manufacturing, [rotating shifts and variable hours in manufacturing](/en/labor/column/seizogyo-kotai-kinmu-henkei-roudoujikan-shinya-roumu).

## What is the procedure, and what are the limits, for the one-year variable system?

You need a labour-management agreement (concluded, filed) and a provision in the work rules. The agreement sets the scope of covered workers, the target period (over one month, up to one year), the specified (busy) period, the working days and daily hours over the target period, and the agreement's term, and is filed with the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office head. The limits are set in detail in Enforcement Rules Article 12-4.

| Item | Limit / content |
|---|---|
| Daily hours | Up to 10 hours |
| Weekly hours | Up to 52 hours |
| Working days (where target period exceeds three months) | Up to 280 a year |
| Consecutive working days | Up to 6 as a rule |
| Consecutive days in the specified (busy) period | Within a range leaving one day off a week (up to 12 in a row) |

Because the target-period hours are fixed in advance, you build the calendar around the brewing plan. Hours beyond the target-period schedule are overtime, requiring premium wages (Article 37) and a 36 agreement (Article 36). See [how far a 36 agreement can allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made) and [from how many employees are work rules required](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## On the production floor, what do you appoint and what do you prepare for?

A brewery is a food-production floor, and you build the safety and health structure by the number of workers regularly employed.

| Regularly employed workers | Appoint | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 10 to under 50 | Safety and health promoter (manufacturing is a designated industry) | Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 12-2 |
| 50 or more | Health officer and industrial physician | Article 12 (health officer), Article 13 (industrial physician) |
| 100 or more in manufacturing | General safety and health manager | Article 10, Enforcement Order Article 2 |

On top of this you need safety and health education at hire and on change of duties (Article 59), and measures for hot kettles and fermentation tanks, slips on wet floors, work at height and other site hazards. If you use boilers or pressure vessels, appointing a boiler-handling operations chief or requiring boiler-engineer qualifications may apply depending on scale, but which threshold triggers what varies by equipment, so we reach no individual conclusion here (**unverified**). Safety and health education for foreign workers must be given in a way they understand; see [safety and health education for foreign workers and preventing accidents](/en/labor/column/gaikokujin-anzeneisei-kyoiku-bogoku-roudo-saigai-boshi).

## When receiving foreign workers, how do you set up residence status and the labour structure?

This is where misunderstanding is common. Front-line work in alcohol brewing is **outside** the Specified Skilled Worker "food and beverage manufacturing" field. MAFF's FAQ for that field (March 2026 update) states alcohol brewing is excluded, and a business whose main product is sake, beer, wine or shochu cannot receive SSW workers in this field. Non-alcoholic beverages (soft drinks, tea, coffee) are included.

| Work you want to fill | Residence-status view |
|---|---|
| Front-line brewing/production of alcohol (beer, sake, etc.) | Outside SSW "food and beverage manufacturing". Residence status such as Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services may be considered, but simple production-line work raises its eligibility individually, so no conclusion is reached (**unverified**) |
| Intake via technical intern training | Whether it fits a target occupation/task is checked against OTIT's list of transferable occupations (additions and scope change; check individually, **unverified**) |
| Food service at an attached taproom | May fall under SSW "food service" (food preparation, customer service, store management) |

What a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles is the labour after intake: condition notices, treatment at least equal to Japanese workers, social and labour insurance, time management, and safety and health education. Which residence status fits your work, and the technical-intern plan approval and residence-status applications themselves, are the area of a Gyoseishoshi. ## What are the working conditions for taproom (attached food-service) staff?

If you attach a taproom or restaurant, the production workers and the food-service (hall, kitchen) workers differ in hours and in the shape of the busy period. Food service leans to nights and holidays, and where it reaches late night (22:00-05:00) the late-night premium (Article 37(4)) is due.

| Category | Main points |
|---|---|
| Production (brewing) workers | Peak-season variable hours, safety and health, overtime premium |
| Food-service (taproom) workers | Night/holiday shifts, late-night premium, fixed overtime design, granting breaks |

Even employed at the same establishment, conditions and premiums differ between production and food service, so you build the work and wage rules to match the substance. See [restaurant-opening labour (night shifts, deemed overtime)](/en/labor/column/inshokuten-kaigyo-roumu-shinya-shift-minashi).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. I want longer hours only in the brewing peak; how many hours can the one-year variable system allow?**
A. The daily hours set by agreement cap at 10 and the weekly at 52 (Article 32-4, Enforcement Rules Article 12-4). Where the target period exceeds three months, working days cap at 280 a year and consecutive working days at six as a rule (even in a specified busy period, within a range leaving one day off a week). Hours beyond that are overtime, requiring premium wages and a 36 agreement.

**Q. Can I hire brewing staff under Specified Skilled Worker "food and beverage manufacturing"?**
A. Front-line alcohol brewing is outside the "food and beverage manufacturing" field. MAFF's FAQ (March 2026 update) excludes alcohol brewing, so a business whose main product is sake, beer and the like cannot receive workers in this field. Non-alcoholic beverages are included. Residence status such as Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services may be considered for brewing, but eligibility is individual, and the application is a Gyoseishoshi's area.

**Q. Can taproom staff be on the same work rules as production workers?**
A. Food service leans to nights and holidays, so the late-night premium and the way breaks are granted differ from production. Even at the same establishment, conditions and premiums differ between production (brewing) and food service (hall, kitchen), so it is safer to build work and wage rules to match the substance.

**Q. From how many workers are safety and health managers required?**
A. A workplace regularly employing 50 or more appoints a health officer and industrial physician (Articles 12, 13); manufacturing with 100 or more appoints a general safety and health manager (Article 10); manufacturing with 10 to under 50 appoints a safety and health promoter (Article 12-2). Safety and health education at hire etc. (Article 59) is also required. Qualification thresholds for equipment such as boilers vary by scale and are checked individually.
