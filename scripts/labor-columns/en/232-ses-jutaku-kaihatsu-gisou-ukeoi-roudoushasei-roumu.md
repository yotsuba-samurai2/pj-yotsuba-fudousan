**Bottom line first**: In contract development or system engineering services (SES), an arrangement is in substance worker dispatch if the client directly instructs and supervises engineers employed by the contractor, regardless of whether the contract is labelled ‘contract for work’ or ‘quasi-mandate’. Disguised contracting and unlicensed dispatch are distinct issues that may overlap. The test under Japan’s Notice No. 37 looks at who directs the work and manages working hours, and whether the contractor operates independently.

## How do quasi-mandate, contracts for work and worker dispatch differ in SES?

Even in the same form of "an engineer working at a client's site," the legal framework splits into three. The difference is "who gives the orders" and "what the party is responsible for."

SES is a service label, not a legal term that necessarily means quasi-mandate. Under Japan’s Civil Code, a quasi-mandate is for carrying out a service, while a contract for work is for completing specified work (Articles 656 and 643; Article 632). With either arrangement, the contractor directs its own employees. In worker dispatch, the agency remains the employer and the assigned worker works under the receiving company’s direction (Worker Dispatch Act, Article 2(i)).

| Arrangement | Who directs the work | Main legal basis | Dispatch business licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quasi-mandate | Contractor (employer) | Civil Code, Articles 656 and 643 | Not required for a genuine quasi-mandate |
| Contract for work | Contractor (employer) | Civil Code, Article 632 | Not required for a genuine contract for work |
| Worker dispatch | The client (dispatch destination) | Worker Dispatch Act Article 2, item 1 | The agency needs a license |

For the client to give orders lawfully as a worker dispatch, the dispatching agency must hold the license of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare. Those license requirements are set out in [getting a worker dispatch license (asset requirements, responsible person, disclosure)](/en/labor/column/rodosha-haken-jigyo-kyoka-shisan-youken-hakenmoto-sekininsha-roumu). The problem of "worker status," where a worker in general outsourcing is treated as the client's worker, is covered in [the line between outsourcing and employment (worker status)](/en/labor/column/gaichu-koyo-sakaime-roudoushasei).

## How far must the chain of command reach at the client's site before it is disguised contracting?

Being stationed at a client's site is not in itself illegal. The problem is whether the client at the destination directly instructs the engineer on how to do the work and on working hours.

If the client directly allocates tasks, dictates how they are done, or sets hours or overtime for the contractor’s employees, those are signs of worker dispatch. Merely forwarding the client’s instructions through a contractor supervisor is not enough. Check who actually decides how instructions are implemented and manages work and attendance.

| Sign | Operation leaning to disguised contracting | Operation that stands as quasi-mandate / contract |
|---|---|---|
| Work instructions | The client dictates how the engineers carry out tasks | The contractor considers client requests, then determines and directs the work itself |
| Working hours | The client decides start/end times and overtime | The contractor manages working hours |
| Staffing / assignment | The client decides the assignment of people | The contractor decides staffing and assignment |
| Equipment or expertise | A loan of equipment alone does not determine the classification | The contractor can independently carry out the work with its own equipment, or through its own planning, expertise or experience |

Working at a client’s site is not decisive. Where the client contracts directly with an individual, assess separately whether that individual is a worker in relation to the client under Japan’s Labor Standards Act, rather than assuming dispatch (which presupposes employment by a dispatching agency). Relevant factors include freedom to turn down work, supervision, restrictions on working hours and the nature of remuneration (Article 9).

## How do you apply Notice No. 37 (the distinction criteria) to SES?

The line between worker dispatch and a contract for work is set by Ministry of Labour Notice No. 37 of 1986, "Criteria for Distinguishing a Worker Dispatching Business from a Business Conducted by Contract for Work." You apply this notice directly to SES and contract development.

Article 2 of that notice provides that even a business operator that has its own employed workers engage in work under a contract in the form of a contract for work is treated as running a worker dispatch business unless it falls under both 1 and 2 below. For SES to say it is "a contract for work or quasi-mandate," the contractor must meet both of these.

| Requirement of Article 2 of Notice No. 37 | Details (applied to SES) |
|---|---|
| 1 Directly using the labor of its own employed workers itself | (i) the contractor itself directs and evaluates how the work is conducted; (ii) it itself directs and manages start/end times, breaks, days off, and overtime; (iii) it itself directs and manages matters such as maintaining order in the enterprise |
| 2 Processing the contracted work independently as its own business | (i) procuring and paying the funds for processing the work at its own responsibility; (ii) bearing the responsibilities of a business operator under the Civil Code and the Commercial Code; (iii) not merely providing physical labor (procuring the machinery, equipment, and materials at its own responsibility and expense, or processing the work based on its own planning or its own expert skills or experience) |

Article 3 of Notice No. 37 addresses forms deliberately used to evade the law where the true business is dispatch. By contrast, in agile development, exchanging information, advice and suggestions as equals, with the contractor’s developers making their own working decisions, does not by itself amount to direct supervision (MHLW Q&A, Volume 3).

## What should the client and the contractor each put in place?

The key to avoiding disguised contracting is to keep the command and the working-hours management fully on the contractor's side. The points to put in place differ between the client and the contractor.

The contractor should define the scope and deliverables and manage its own work and attendance. A designated contact is one approach; even in agile development, that person is not invariably required to be on site or attend every meeting. The client may coordinate requests with the contractor rather than dictate hours or methods. If direct supervision is needed, check the agency’s licence and the applicable legal steps before accepting a dispatch.

| Party | What to put in place |
|---|---|
| Contractor (SES / development) | Clear communication channels; in-house supervision and attendance management; defined scope, deliverables and acceptance criteria |
| Client (receiving side) | Running things without direct instructions to the engineer / routing requests through the contractor's responsible person / receiving as a licensed dispatch if direct command is needed |
| Text of the contract | Preparing the business consignment contract or quasi-mandate contract is the realm of the lawyer and administrative scrivener |

## What happens if you are flagged for disguised contracting?

Holding a dispatch licence does not cure disguised contracting. The Labour Bureau may give corrective guidance and the receiving company may owe obligations to dispatched workers.

Article 40-6(1)(ii) of Japan’s Worker Dispatch Act covers accepting workers from an unlicensed agency. Article 40-6(1)(v) separately covers receiving workers under a contract labelled as something other than dispatch to evade the rules, without setting the mandatory dispatch terms. Where the rule applies, the recipient is deemed to make a job offer on the same terms when the unlawful acceptance occurs. The rule does not apply if the recipient was unaware and not negligent. Employment is not automatic: the worker must accept the offer within one year of the end of the unlawful acceptance. The unlicensed agency may separately face penalties (Article 59(ii)).

| What can happen if flagged | Basis |
|---|---|
| Corrective guidance by the Labour Bureau (both client and contractor) | Guidance and advice under the Worker Dispatch Act |
| Penalties for operating a dispatch business without a licence | Worker Dispatch Act, Article 59(ii) |
| An offer of employment on the same terms may be deemed to have been made | Worker Dispatch Act, Article 40-6 |

Corrective guidance itself is the Labour Bureau's authority, and individual disputes (such as damages or confirmation of status) are the realm of the lawyer. This article is general information, and whether a particular case amounts to disguised contracting must be confirmed against the reality of the operation at the site. Because judgment turns on the operation rather than the form of the contract, it helps to design the flow of command and working-hours management before concluding the contract.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If I name the contract a "quasi-mandate," is it not disguised contracting even with site stationing?**
A. The written label and location are not decisive. Where the client gives direct, concrete orders to the contractor’s employees, the arrangement is in substance worker dispatch. Notice No. 37 looks at whether the contractor directs its staff, manages working hours and carries out the work independently.

**Q. What is the problem if the client gives direct instructions to the engineer?**
A. Direct instructions from the client on work methods or hours are evidence of supervision. Sharing information or making suggestions as equals is not alone dispatch. Having the contractor’s supervisor pass along orders unchanged does not cure the problem. If direct supervision is needed, consider a properly licensed and compliant dispatch arrangement.

**Q. What effect does a finding of disguised contracting have on the client?**
A. Corrective guidance is possible. If Article 40-6 applies, the recipient is deemed to have offered employment on the same terms; it does not apply if the recipient was unaware and not negligent. The worker must accept within one year after the unlawful arrangement ends for employment to arise. The agency operating without a licence may separately face penalties.

A. The roles are divided. Preparing the text of the business consignment contract or quasi-mandate contract itself is the realm of the lawyer and administrative scrivener. Building the work rules, working-hours management, and employment-management system is the realm of the Certified Social Insurance and Labor Consultant. Corrective guidance by the Labour Bureau is an administrative power, and individual disputes are handled by the lawyer.
