**In short:** When a Specified Skilled Worker changes employer, insured status for social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) and employment insurance is **lost at the former company and acquired again at the new one**, because insurance attaches to the **employment relationship**, not the residence status (Health Insurance Act, Articles 35 and 36; Employees' Pension Insurance Act, Article 27; Employment Insurance Act, Article 7). The former company files the loss — within 5 days for social insurance and 10 days for employment insurance from the day after the leaving date — and the new company files the acquisition on the same deadlines. A job change itself also requires a **fresh application to change the residence status** (unlike a technical intern, this is not a free transfer), plus a notification from the accepting organisation. From a shakai hoken roumushi's labour-management viewpoint, this page organises the loss and acquisition of social and employment insurance and the steps to avoid any uninsured gap, leaving the application to change the residence status and the support plan to a gyoseishoshi or a registered support organisation, and disputes over termination to an attorney, under separate engagements as independent business entities.

A Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo) may change jobs within a certain range, but not "just by filing a notice." The immigration procedure and the social- and employment-insurance procedures move at the same time, and getting the order or the deadlines wrong can create an uninsured gap or make the stay unstable. This page is for the HR staff of small and medium-sized enterprises that accept or release Specified Skilled Workers.

## Can a Specified Skilled Worker change jobs freely?

Unlike a technical intern, who in principle cannot freely change training sites, a Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo) can change jobs of their own accord. However, a job change requires a **fresh application to change the residence status**; the worker cannot move to the next company merely by filing a notice. A change within the same specified industrial field and work category is the most common, while moving to a different field requires meeting the skills test and Japanese-language test requirements anew. Starting work at the new company before permission is granted can amount to unauthorised activity, so the start date is set after permission.

Where the worker moves from a technical intern to a Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo) but **stays at the same company**, no loss or acquisition of insured status arises. The details of the in-house transition are covered in [Transition from technical intern to Specified Skilled Worker and social insurance](/en/labor/column/ginou-jisshu-tokutei-ginou-ikou-gaishoku-shakaihoken). The loss and acquisition below arise only when the company changes.

| | Technical intern | Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo) |
|---|---|---|
| Job change / transfer | In principle not free | Possible at the worker's will |
| Immigration procedure needed | (On transition) change of residence status | Change of residence status at each job change |
| Social and employment insurance | Attaches to the employment relationship | Attaches to the employment relationship (loss/acquisition when the company changes) |

## How do the former and the new company handle social and employment insurance?

Social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) and employment insurance attach to the employment relationship with the company, not to the residence status. So when the company changes, insured status is lost at the former company and acquired again at the new one. The procedures and deadlines do not change with nationality or residence status — they are the same as for a Japanese person joining or leaving mid-career.

| Procedure | Who / where to | Deadline | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health insurance & employees' pension — loss notice (former) | Employer → Pension Office (or health-insurance society) | Within 5 days of the day after leaving | Health Insurance Act, Art. 36; Employees' Pension Insurance Act, Art. 27 |
| Health insurance & employees' pension — acquisition notice (new) | Employer → Pension Office (or health-insurance society) | Within 5 days of the joining date | Health Insurance Act, Art. 35; Employees' Pension Insurance Act, Art. 27 |
| Employment insurance — loss notice (former) | Employer → Hello Work | Within 10 days of the day after ceasing to be insured | Employment Insurance Act, Art. 7 |
| Employment insurance — acquisition notice (new) | Employer → Hello Work | By the 10th of the month after becoming insured | Employment Insurance Act, Art. 7 |

The date of losing insured status is not the leaving date itself but the **day after the leaving date** (Health Insurance Act, Article 36). If the former company hands the worker the employment-insurance separation slip and a document confirming loss of health insurance (such as the certificate of loss of insured status) at the time of leaving, the new company's acquisition procedure goes smoothly. Because a Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo)'s pay must be equal to or above that of a Japanese worker, a pay rise on the job change may require a revision of the standard monthly remuneration (monthly-change notice) at the new company.

## Who arranges the change of residence status and the employment contract?

The application to change the residence status and its drafting and filing on the applicant's behalf are the work of the applicant or an **accredited gyoseishoshi or a registered support organisation**. A shakai hoken roumushi cannot file residence-status applications on behalf of the applicant. In the Specified Skilled Worker scheme, the new accepting organisation draws up the support plan for the ichigo worker and either provides the support in-house or entrusts it to a registered support organisation. These are the field of a gyoseishoshi or a registered support organisation, and are contracted separately from shakai hoken roumushi work, as an independent business entity.

On the other hand, the working conditions of the employment contract itself (working hours, wages, holidays, application of social insurance) and the drawing-up of work rules and wage rules are handled by a shakai hoken roumushi. The labour side of the accepting organisation's support system (in-house or entrusted) is in [The in-house support system for Specified Skilled Workers and labour management](/en/labor/column/tokutei-ginou-jisha-shien-taisei-roumu), and matching the residence period with the contract period is in [Residence period and employment-contract period](/en/labor/column/zairyu-kigen-koyo-keiyaku-kikan). The accepting organisation also has a duty to notify the Immigration Services Agency when the employment contract is changed or ended (ad-hoc notification, in principle within 14 days of the day the event arises). This notification is an immigration procedure done by a gyoseishoshi, a registered support organisation or the person concerned, separate from the loss and acquisition of social and employment insurance.

## How do you prevent gaps (uninsured periods) in the procedures?

The most common problem is a gap between the leaving date and the joining date, which creates an **uninsured, no-pension period**. If the worker can join the new company on the day after leaving the former one, no gap arises; but if a gap opens up — for instance while waiting for permission to change the residence status — the worker must enrol in National Health Insurance and the National Pension at the municipality for that period. Seeing a doctor while health insurance has lapsed means paying the full medical cost up front.

The order that avoids an uninsured period is as follows. First, **align the prospect of permission to change the residence status with the start date of work**. Second, the former company should not delay the loss notice and the issue of the separation slip and certificate of loss of insured status at the time of leaving. Third, the new company should file the acquisition notice on the joining date so that the health-insurance card (or the insurance information for the My Number card) can be used quickly. Fourth, where a gap is unavoidable, guide the worker on switching to National Health Insurance and the National Pension for that period. Gaps in the procedures affect not only the worker's life but also the review at the time of renewing the period of stay (the record of fulfilling public obligations).

| Common gap | Result | How to prevent |
|---|---|---|
| Gap between leaving and joining | Uninsured, no-pension period | Guide the switch to National Health Insurance / National Pension; adjust the joining date |
| Former company's loss notice is late | New company's acquisition stalls | Issue the certificate of loss of insured status and the separation slip on leaving |
| Pay rose but left unaddressed | Standard monthly remuneration out of step with reality | File a revision (monthly-change notice) at the new company |
| Work started before permission | Risk of unauthorised activity | Start work after permission to change the residence status |

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Can a Specified Skilled Worker move to the next company just by filing a notice?**
A. No. A Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo) can change jobs of their own accord, but each job change requires an application to change the residence status. Starting work at the new company before permission is granted can amount to unauthorised activity, so the start date is set after permission. The application and its filing on the applicant's behalf are the work of the applicant or an accredited gyoseishoshi or a registered support organisation; a shakai hoken roumushi cannot file residence-status applications.

**Q. What happens to social and employment insurance when the company changes?**
A. Insured status is lost at the former company and acquired again at the new one, because insurance attaches to the employment relationship, not the residence status (Health Insurance Act, Articles 35 and 36; Employees' Pension Insurance Act, Article 27; Employment Insurance Act, Article 7). The former company files the loss within 5 days (social insurance) or 10 days (employment insurance) from the day after leaving, and the new company files the acquisition on the same deadlines.

**Q. What happens to insurance if there is a gap between leaving and joining?**
A. That period is uninsured with no pension, so the worker must enrol in National Health Insurance and the National Pension at the municipality. Seeing a doctor while health insurance has lapsed means paying the full medical cost up front. If the schedule can be arranged so that the worker joins on the day after leaving, no gap arises.

**Q. The pay rose on the job change. Is any procedure needed?**
A. A Specified Skilled Worker (ichigo)'s pay must be equal to or above that of a Japanese worker, and if the pay rise meets requirements such as the standard monthly remuneration changing by two or more grades, a revision (monthly-change notice) may be needed at the new company. Whether it actually applies depends on the make-up of the pay and the size of the change, so a shakai hoken roumushi checks it.
