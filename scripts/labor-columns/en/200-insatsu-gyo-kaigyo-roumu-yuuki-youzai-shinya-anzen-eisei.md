**Bottom line first**: The first things to set up when opening a printing company are a safety and health management structure scaled to your size, and the work chief, environment measurement and special health examinations for processes that handle organic solvents. Where offset-printing inks and cleaning agents contain organic solvents, you must appoint an organic-solvent work chief (Article 19 of the Ordinance on Prevention of Organic Solvent Poisoning), measure the work environment once every six months (Article 28) and give the organic-solvent health examination on hiring, on reassignment and once every six months (Article 29). Night and shift work carries the night premium under Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act, and the authoritative record lives in the work rules, the Article 36 agreement and the wage rules.

## What labor structure do you set up first when opening a printing business?

The Industrial Safety and Health Act requires a safety and health management structure scaled to the industry and size of the workplace. Printing is manufacturing (including processing of goods), so it is among the industries that must appoint a safety manager. The people you must place change with the size at opening.

| Number of regular workers | What to place | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 10 to under 50 | Safety and health promoter | Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 12-2; Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health Article 12-2 |
| 50 or more | Safety manager, health manager, industrial physician, health committee | Articles 11, 12, 13 and 18 of the Act |
| 300 or more | General safety and health manager | Article 10 of the Act; Article 2, item 2 of the Enforcement Order |

Printing (manufacturing) differs from a workplace that is only retail or clerical in that it is among the industries that must appoint a safety manager and a safety and health promoter. At an opening scale of 10 to under 50, you appoint one safety and health promoter to cover both safety and health. At 50 or more, you add the appointment of a safety manager, a health manager and an industrial physician and the setting up of a health committee. A general safety and health manager is needed at 300 or more regular workers for manufacturing (forestry, mining, construction, transport and cleaning are from 100, so do not confuse the industry category).

For the basic duty to prepare work rules, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo). Acquiring or leasing a factory or warehouse, and the building, fire-service and environmental permits for installing printing machinery, are not the labor and social-security attorney's field: the premises belong to a licensed real-estate agent and the permits to an administrative scrivener (Gyoseishoshi), each contracted separately as an independent business.

## What health and safety is needed for processes handling organic solvents or specified chemicals?

Offset-printing inks, the additives in dampening water, and the cleaning agents for rollers and blankets can contain organic solvents such as toluene. An indoor workplace that handles organic solvents above a certain level is subject to the Ordinance on Prevention of Organic Solvent Poisoning (the Organic Solvent Ordinance), and you put three pillars in place.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Appointing a work chief | Appoint an organic-solvent work chief from those who have completed the organic-solvent work chief skill training, to direct the work, inspect the local exhaust ventilation, and monitor the use of protective equipment | Organic Solvent Ordinance Articles 19 and 19-2 (Enforcement Order Article 6, item 22) |
| Work environment measurement | For the prescribed indoor workplaces where organic-solvent work is done, measure the organic-solvent concentration once every six months and keep the records for three years | Organic Solvent Ordinance Article 28 |
| Organic-solvent health examination | For workers constantly engaged in the covered organic-solvent work, give a physician's examination on hiring, on reassignment to that work, and once every six months thereafter | Organic Solvent Ordinance Article 29 |

The work chief is chosen from those who have completed the organic-solvent work chief skill training (Organic Solvent Ordinance, Article 19, paragraph 2). You evaluate the measured work environment to judge the control class and, where needed, move to measures such as installing or improving local exhaust ventilation (Article 28-2). To do the measurement in-house, at a designated workplace under the Working Environment Measurement Act the measurement must be done by a working environment measurement expert or a measurement institution, and it is often outsourced to an external institution.

Where a substance handled in a printing process is a specified chemical, the Ordinance on Prevention of Hazards Due to Specified Chemical Substances (the Specified Chemicals Ordinance) applies separately, requiring the appointment of a specified-chemicals work chief (Article 27), work environment measurement once every six months for Class 1 and Class 2 substances (Article 36) and a health examination on hiring, on reassignment and once every six months (Article 39). Which substance falls under which ordinance, and the design of individual chemical management based on the Safety Data Sheet (SDS), are set out across the whole autonomous chemical-management system in [autonomous chemical management, SDS and risk assessment](/en/labor/column/kagaku-busshitsu-jiritsuteki-kanri-sdsra). Reading the SDS for each brand and the detail of proper management belong to a chemical-management specialist.

## How do you handle premium wages and health for night and shift work?

Commercial printing and the printing of newspapers and flyers tends to be designed to run in the night band to meet deadlines, or to run the machines on a shift system. Work in the night band carries a statutory premium wage.

Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act provides that where work is done between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m., a premium of 25% or more of the ordinary wage must be paid. This is required even for scheduled work, and it is a statutory duty separate from whether the company voluntarily sets a "night allowance" or "shift allowance." If a shift allowance is to cover the night premium, the wage rules must show distinctly that the allowance includes the equivalent of the night premium and that it does not fall below the premium for the actual night-work hours. Where overtime extends into the night, overtime (25% or more) and night (25% or more) overlap to make 50% or more. For the variable working-hour system when running machines non-stop on two or three shifts, and how the premiums overlap, see [shift work in manufacturing: variable working hours and the night premium](/en/labor/column/seizogyo-kotai-kinmu-henkei-roudoujikan-shinya-roumu).

On the health side, a worker who constantly performs work that includes night work is, as a person engaged in specified work, subject to health examinations more frequent than the ordinary once-a-year periodic examination. Article 45, paragraph 1 of the Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health requires an examination on assignment and once every six months for a worker constantly performing the work in Article 13, paragraph 1, item 3, and item ヌ of that provision lists "work that includes night work." For the basic duty and cost of periodic health examinations, see [the duty, cost and time of periodic health examinations](/en/labor/column/teiki-kenko-shindan-jisshi-gimu-hiyo-jikan). A night worker who also handles organic solvents may be subject to both the organic-solvent health examination (every six months) and the specified-work examination for night work (every six months), so build them into the annual examination plan from the outset.

## What should you cover in the work rules and the Article 36 agreement?

A workplace that regularly employs 10 or more workers must prepare work rules and file them with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office (Article 89 of the Labor Standards Act). In printing, you reflect the shift categories, the basis for the night and shift allowances, and matters on safety and health in the work rules and the wage rules.

To have workers do overtime or holiday work, you must conclude a labor-management agreement under Article 36 of the Labor Standards Act (the Article 36 agreement) and file it with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office. There is a ceiling on the overtime an Article 36 agreement can extend; the basics are in [how far overtime can go under an Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). To distribute working hours across peaks and troughs or continuous operation, you use a monthly (Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hour system with the required provisions and filing. For how to choose the variable system, see [choosing between variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata). Because how these documents are set is itself a requirement for the system's validity, an omission makes the variable system void and the excess is all recalculated as overtime.

## What procedures and rough costs are needed when hiring?

When you hire, you must state the working conditions. Under Article 15 of the Labor Standards Act, you state the wage, working hours, place of work, duties and so on in writing. The amendment effective 1 April 2024 added the scope of changes to the place of work and duties to the matters to be stated (Article 5 of the Enforcement Ordinance).

| Procedure | Content |
|---|---|
| Stating working conditions | Written notice of working conditions (Labor Standards Act Article 15; Enforcement Ordinance Article 5) |
| Joining labor insurance | Workers' accident and employment insurance (workers' accident insurance covers from even one employee) |
| Joining social insurance | Compulsory for a company; part-time and short-hour workers who meet the requirements also join |
| Safety and health education | Education on hiring and on a change of work content (Industrial Safety and Health Act Article 59, paragraph 1) |
| Health examination | Pre-employment examination and later periodic examinations; organic-solvent work and night work are subject to special and specified-work examinations |

The cost of health examinations is, as a rule, borne by the employer for the items the law requires the employer to carry out. The rough cost of joining social and labor insurance, and of putting the work rules, Article 36 agreement and wage rules in order, varies with the size of the workplace and the pattern of work. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. If the cleaning agent contains only a little organic solvent, do we still need a work chief and a special examination?**
A. Whether the Organic Solvent Ordinance applies turns on the class of the organic solvent (Class 1, 2 or 3), whether the work amounts to organic-solvent work at an indoor workplace, and whether the consumption reaches the level the ordinance sets. Even if a cleaning agent contains organic solvent, where the amount is very small so it is not organic-solvent work under the ordinance, or where an exemption has been granted, it may not be subject to the work chief or the special examination. First check the type and content of organic solvent on the product's SDS, and judge in light of the actual work and consumption. Whether a particular case qualifies must be checked for each site's work content.

**Q. If we pay a shift allowance to run the machines at night, do we still need a separate night premium?**
A. Yes. The night premium is a statutory premium wage under Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act, and work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. must be paid at 25% or more above the ordinary wage. A shift or night allowance is one the company sets voluntarily, and its name alone does not extinguish the night-premium duty. If a shift allowance is to cover the night premium, the wage rules must show distinctly that the allowance includes the equivalent of the night premium and that it does not fall below the premium for the actual night-work hours.

**Q. May we measure the work environment in-house?**
A. For an indoor workplace handling organic solvents or specified chemicals that is a designated workplace under the Working Environment Measurement Act, the measurement must be done by a working environment measurement expert or a measurement institution. If you have no qualified person in-house, it is common to outsource to an external measurement institution. Measure once every six months, judge the control class based on the evaluation of the results, and move to any needed improvement measures. Keep the records for three years.

**Q. If we have fewer than 10 employees, do we need no safety and health structure at all?**
A. Even with fewer than 10, if yours is a workplace handling organic solvents, the appointment of an organic-solvent work chief, the work environment measurement and the organic-solvent health examination are required regardless of size. These apply by the content of the work and the substances handled, not by the number of employees. On the other hand, appointing a safety and health promoter (10 to under 50) and the duty to prepare and file work rules (10 or more regular) are divided by number. It is a mistake to think that fewer than 10 employees means no safety and health duties at all.
