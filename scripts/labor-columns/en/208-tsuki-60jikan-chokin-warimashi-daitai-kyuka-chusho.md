**Bottom line first**: The part of monthly overtime exceeding 60 hours must be paid a premium of 50% or more of the ordinary wage (Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) proviso). This rise was once deferred for SMEs, but has applied to SMEs too since 1 April 2023. A "substitute leave" system can replace the extra premium with paid leave: by a labour-management agreement, the time matching the raised portion (the gap between 50% and 25%) of the over-60-hours part can be swapped for leave (Article 37(3)). Substitute leave is given in units of one day or half a day, within two months from the day after the month-end in which 60 hours were exceeded (Enforcement Regulations Article 19-2). Wage rules and the agreement belong to a Shakai Hoken Roumushi, and claims or disputes over unpaid premium wages to an attorney, each contracted separately as an independent business entity.

This page focuses on the wage and labour-management-agreement design a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles: the premium rate for over-60-hours monthly overtime and substitute leave. It is aimed at SME owners and labour staff with heavy overtime.

## When did the 50% premium for over-60-hours overtime apply to SMEs?

The rule setting the premium for the part of monthly overtime exceeding 60 hours at 50% or more applied to large firms from 1 April 2010, but a deferral "for the time being" was placed on SMEs. This deferral was abolished, and the 50%-or-more rate has applied to SMEs too since 1 April 2023 (Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) proviso).

| Category | Overtime up to 60 hours a month | Part exceeding 60 hours a month |
|---|---|---|
| Premium rate (from 1 April 2023, same for SMEs) | 25% or more | 50% or more |
| Start of application to large firms | As before | 1 April 2010 |
| Start of application to SMEs | As before | 1 April 2023 (deferral abolished) |

Whether a firm is an SME is judged by business type, by capital / total contribution or the number of workers regularly employed, and it is treated as an SME if it meets either one. Which a firm falls under turns on its own capital and headcount, so we do not give one conclusion here (**unverified**). That the overtime cap and concluding/filing a 36 Agreement are the premise is in [How far can a 36 Agreement allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made).

## How are the 60 hours counted, and how do they overlap with late-night and holiday premiums?

The 60 hours are counted by accumulating the hours extended as statutory overtime in one month (a one-month wage-calculation period with a set start day). Hours worked on a statutory holiday (one day a week or four in four weeks) are treated separately as holiday work and are not included in the 60 hours. The late-night (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) premium is for a time band and must be paid overlapping with the over-60-hours premium.

| Category | Basis | Premium rate |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime (part up to 60 hours a month) | Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) | 25% or more |
| Overtime (part exceeding 60 hours a month) | Article 37(1) proviso | 50% or more |
| Statutory-holiday work (not counted in the 60 hours) | Article 37(1) | 35% or more |
| Late-night work (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) | Article 37(4) | 25% or more |

Where over-60-hours overtime falls at late-night, it becomes 75% or more — the 50% for over-60-hours plus the 25% for late-night. The late-night premium must be paid to managers too; the relationship between a nominal title and the late-night premium is in [Nominal managers and the late-night premium](/en/labor/column/kanri-kantokusha-nabakari-warimashi-shinya). The requirements for valid fixed overtime pay, paid as a set amount, are in [Requirements for valid fixed overtime pay and settling the difference](/en/labor/column/kotei-zangyodai-tekiho-youken-sagaku-seisan).

## What is substitute leave, and how does it replace the premium?

Substitute leave is a system that, for over-60-hours monthly overtime, gives paid leave instead of paying the premium for the raised portion (the gap between 50% and 25%) (Labour Standards Act Article 37(3)). Only when a labour-management agreement is concluded and the worker actually takes the leave does the duty to pay the raised portion fall away. What is replaced is only the raised portion; the 25% premium must still be paid.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Replaceable range | Only the raised portion of over-60-hours work (the gap between 50% and 25%). The 25% premium is paid |
| How to convert | Hours exceeding 60 × (rate otherwise payable − rate when substitute leave is taken) = hours of substitute leave |
| Unit of leave | One day or half a day (Enforcement Regulations Article 19-2) |
| Period to give it | Within two months from the day after the month-end in which 60 hours were exceeded (same regulation) |
| Taking is voluntary | Whether to take substitute leave is the worker's choice. If not taken, the raised-portion premium is paid |

For example, at a workplace setting the rate at 50% and the take-substitute rate at 25%, if a month has 20 hours of over-60-hours overtime, the substitute-leave hours are 20 × (0.5 − 0.25) = 5 hours. Annual paid leave is excluded from this substitute leave (Labour Standards Act Article 37(3)) and is treated as a separate thing. The idea of leave units is also in [Introducing hourly paid leave and the labour-management agreement](/en/labor/column/jikantan-nenkyu-dounyu-roushi-kyotei-shugyokisoku).

## What must the substitute-leave labour-management agreement set out?

To introduce substitute leave you need a written labour-management agreement with the majority union (or, if none, a representative of the majority of workers) (Labour Standards Act Article 37(3); Enforcement Regulations Article 19-2). Even with this agreement, whether to take substitute leave rests on the worker's own choice.

| Item in the agreement | Content |
|---|---|
| How to calculate substitute-leave hours | Multiply the over-60-hours time by the conversion rate (rate otherwise payable − rate when taken) |
| Unit of substitute leave | One day or half a day |
| Period in which it can be given | Within two months from the day after the month-end in which 60 hours were exceeded |
| Payday for the premium | The wage-calculation period and payday for the raised portion if substitute leave is not taken |

These are the items set in Labour Standards Act Enforcement Regulations Article 19-2. Unlike a 36 Agreement, the substitute-leave agreement need not be filed with the Labour Standards Inspection Office, but it is usual to place a basis provision in the work rules too. The duty to prepare work rules is in [From how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## Where should you fix the work rules and wage rules?

To run the over-60-hours premium and substitute leave correctly, you must put the premium rates and calculation into the wage rules and the substitute-leave provisions into the work rules. You also need payroll operation that correctly counts the 60 hours and the overlap of each premium.

| Place to fix | Content |
|---|---|
| Wage rules | Up to 60 hours a month = 25% or more, over 60 hours = 50% or more, and calculating the overlap of late-night / holiday premiums |
| Work rules | Provisions where substitute leave is introduced (unit, period, taking procedure) |
| Agreement | Substitute-leave calculation, unit, period, premium payday (no filing needed) |
| Payroll operation | Accumulating the 60 hours, overlap with late-night, deducting the premium when substitute leave is taken |

Because a calculation error on the premium may be claimed retroactively as unpaid premium wages, the first step is to align the wage rules and agreement with the reality. Where fixed overtime pay is combined, you must design the range it covers and settlement of the difference, set out in [Requirements for valid fixed overtime pay and settling the difference](/en/labor/column/kotei-zangyodai-tekiho-youken-sagaku-seisan).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the 50% premium for over-60-hours overtime apply to SMEs?**
A. It does. A deferral was once placed on SMEs, but this was abolished, and from 1 April 2023 the 50%-or-more rate applies to SMEs too for monthly overtime exceeding 60 hours (Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) proviso).

**Q. If we introduce substitute leave, do we no longer pay any premium at all?**
A. No. What substitute leave replaces is only the raised portion of the over-60-hours part (the gap between 50% and 25%). The 25% premium must be paid. Also, whether to take substitute leave is the worker's choice; if not taken, the raised-portion premium is paid (Labour Standards Act Article 37(3)).

**Q. What is the rate if over-60-hours overtime falls at late-night?**
A. It becomes 75% or more — the 50% for over-60-hours plus the 25% for late-night. The late-night premium (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) is for a time band and must be paid overlapping with the over-60-hours premium (Labour Standards Act Article 37(1) proviso and (4)). Statutory-holiday work is treated separately as holiday work and is not counted in the 60 hours.

## Basis for this article

- Labour Standards Act (Act No. 49 of 1947) Article 37(1) — a premium of 25% or more (35% or more for holidays) is paid for overtime and holiday work; by the proviso, overtime exceeding 60 hours in a month must be paid a premium calculated at 50% or more
- Labour Standards Act Article 37(3) — where, by a written agreement with the majority union (or, if none, a representative of the majority of workers), it is provided to give leave for which the ordinary wage is paid (excluding annual paid leave under Article 39) to a worker made to work beyond the time in the Article 37(1) proviso, and the worker takes that leave, the premium under the proviso need not be paid for the hours set by Ordinance (substitute leave)
- Labour Standards Act Article 37(4) — a premium of 25% or more is paid for late-night work from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. (paid overlapping with the over-60-hours premium)
- Labour Standards Act Enforcement Regulations (Ordinance of the Ministry of Welfare No. 23 of 1947) Article 19-2 — items set in the substitute-leave agreement (how to calculate hours; unit = one day or half a day; period that can be given = within two months from the day after the month-end in which 60 hours were exceeded). The conversion rate is the rate when substitute leave is not taken (the agreed rate under the Article 37(1) proviso) minus the rate when taken (the agreed rate under the main text of that paragraph)
- Application of the over-60-hours premium to SMEs — applied to large firms from 1 April 2010; a deferral "for the time being" was placed on SMEs, which was abolished so that it applies to SMEs too from 1 April 2023 (confirmed against Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare materials on 14 November 2026)
- Confirmed on e-Gov on 14 November 2026: Labour Standards Act Article 37 and Enforcement Regulations Article 19-2. Whether a firm is an SME, and the conversion rate or premium rates set, turn on each workplace, and this article does not conclude on the individual case (**unverified**)
