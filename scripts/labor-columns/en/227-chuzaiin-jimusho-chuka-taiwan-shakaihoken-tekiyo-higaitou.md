**Bottom line first**: A representative office in Japan is an unregistered base for liaison and information gathering. Whereas a foreign company's Japanese branch or sales office is treated as a corporation and is compulsorily covered by employees' pension and health insurance, a representative office is treated as an individual-proprietor establishment: voluntary coverage if it regularly employs fewer than five, and compulsory coverage if it is a covered industry with five or more (Japan External Trade Organization [JETRO], "Japan's social security system," accessed 24 November 2026). The definition of a covered establishment is in Article 3(3) of the Health Insurance Act and Article 6(1) of the Employees' Pension Insurance Act: a corporation's establishment is covered regardless of headcount, while an individual establishment is compulsorily covered in a covered industry with five or more. Workers' accident insurance in principle applies to all workers (Article 3(1), Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act), and employment insurance makes a person insured at 20 or more scheduled weekly hours and a prospect of 31 or more days of employment (Article 6, Employment Insurance Act). Double pension enrollment for a worker dispatched from the home country can be avoided by the certificate of coverage under the Japan-China Social Security Agreement (in force 1 September 2019), but this does not work with Taiwan because there is no agreement. The final judgment on coverage is made by the implementing agencies such as the Pension Office; whether a representative office may be opened and the residence-status application are the field of a gyoseishoshi (administrative scrivener), the commercial registration when converting to a branch the field of a judicial scrivener, and withholding and tax treaties the field of a tax accountant, each contracted separately as an independent business entity where the matter crosses entities. There is no referral fee.

## Does a representative office become a "covered establishment" for social insurance?

First, pin down the nature of the base. A representative office is a base where the home-country corporation conducts information gathering, liaison, and market research in Japan; it cannot carry on sales or conclude contracts, and it is not commercially registered in Japan. A branch or sales office, by contrast, is registered and can transact continuously. The social-insurance treatment follows this difference directly.

The covered establishments for social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) are set out in Article 3(3) of the Health Insurance Act and Article 6(1) of the Employees' Pension Insurance Act. In summary:

| Category | Requirement for compulsory coverage | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A corporation's establishment | Covered regardless of headcount if it regularly employs staff | Health Insurance Act Art. 3(3) item 2 / Employees' Pension Insurance Act Art. 6(1) item 2 |
| An individual establishment | In a statutory covered industry, compulsory when it regularly employs five or more | Health Insurance Act Art. 3(3) item 1 / Employees' Pension Insurance Act Art. 6(1) item 1 |
| Other individual establishments | Voluntary coverage with the consent of at least half the employees and approval | Health Insurance Act Art. 31 / Employees' Pension Insurance Act Art. 6(3), etc. |

Which of these a representative office falls under is the practical fork. JETRO explains that a foreign company's Japanese branch or sales office is treated as a corporation and compulsorily covered, while a representative office is treated as an individual establishment. On that treatment, a representative office is under voluntary coverage if it regularly employs fewer than five (with the office representative as the business operator, who is not themselves an insured person), and compulsory coverage if it is a covered industry with five or more (JETRO, "Japan's social security system," accessed 24 November 2026). Because the representative is treated as an individual proprietor, they cannot in principle be an insured person of health insurance or employees' pension, but exceptionally this may be allowed if a document showing the employment relationship with the overseas head office is submitted, and the judgment is made case by case by the Pension Office with jurisdiction over the location. For the whole picture when a foreign company advances into Japan as a subsidiary or branch, see [the whole picture of social insurance costs when a Chinese company hires in Japan](/en/labor/column/chuka-kigyo-nihon-koyo-shakaihoken-cost-zentaizo).

## How does salary paid by the home country enter the calculation of social insurance premiums?

When a person becomes insured in Japan, premiums are calculated on the standard monthly remuneration. The "remuneration" that is its basis is defined in Article 3(5) of the Health Insurance Act and Article 3(1) item 3 of the Employees' Pension Insurance Act as "everything a worker receives as consideration for labor, whatever its name — wages, salary, allowances, bonuses, and the like," and it is not limited to what is paid in currency (excluding what is received temporarily and what is received at intervals exceeding three months).

| Point | Approach |
|---|---|
| Scope of remuneration | In principle, whatever is received as consideration for labor in Japan, regardless of name or payer |
| Payment from the home-country corporation | For an insured person used by the Japanese establishment, salary paid in the home country may also enter the basis if it is consideration for labor |
| In-kind, housing allowance, etc. | Non-currency in-kind benefits may also be included (converted at the value set by the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare) |
| Specific scope of inclusion | Varies with the actual payment and the contractual relationship; rests with the Pension Office's case-by-case judgment (no uniform conclusion here — unverified) |

The point is that "it is paid in the home country, so it is unrelated to Japan's premiums" is not necessarily true. For an insured person used by the Japanese establishment, even if payment is from the home-country corporation, the part that is consideration for labor in Japan is in principle included in remuneration. That said, how much of the home-country-paid portion is included, and at what exchange rate, varies with the actual payment and the contractual relationship, and the specific handling rests with the Pension Office with jurisdiction. For the organization when a posted worker keeps enrolling in the home country's social insurance, see also [social insurance for foreigners coming to Japan on an intra-company transfer or secondment](/en/labor/column/kigyonai-tenkin-shukko-gaikokujin-shakaihoken-nyu).

## Are workers' accident insurance and employment insurance required in Japan?

Social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) and labor insurance (workers' accident and employment insurance) follow different rules. Even a representative office needs labor-insurance procedures if it uses workers in Japan.

| Insurance | Approach to coverage | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Workers' accident insurance | In principle applies if even one worker is used; irrespective of nationality or residence status | Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act Art. 3(1) |
| Employment insurance | A person becomes insured at 20 or more scheduled weekly hours and a prospect of 31 or more days of employment | Employment Insurance Act Art. 6 |
| Office representative | If not a worker, labor insurance does not apply; for workers' accident insurance there is room for special enrollment | Special enrollment system of the Workers' Accident Insurance Act |

Workers' accident insurance in principle applies to all businesses that use workers under the Labor Standards Act—covering not only staff hired locally in Japan but also a worker dispatched from the home country if they actually work as a worker. Employment insurance is judged by the 20-hours/31-days requirement, and a person who has come on assignment from the home country and is enrolled in a system equivalent to employment insurance abroad may be excluded. Where the office representative is not a worker receiving direction and orders, they are not an insured person of labor insurance, but for workers' accident insurance there is room to enroll specially under certain requirements (JETRO, "Japan's social security system," accessed 24 November 2026). For how completely workers' accident insurance differs between a posting and a business trip, see [an overseas business trip and an overseas posting are completely different for workers' accident insurance](/en/labor/column/kaigai-shucho-haken-rosai-chigai).

## How do you avoid double enrollment with the social security agreement (there is one with China, none with Taiwan)?

If you station a posted worker in Japan while they remain enrolled in the home country's social insurance, pension premiums may arise in both countries where they also become insured in Japan—double enrollment. The mechanism that avoids this is the social security agreement, and whether it is available depends on the counterpart country.

| Country/region | Agreement | Mechanism to avoid double enrollment |
|---|---|---|
| China | Japan-China Social Security Agreement (in force 1 September 2019) | A worker temporarily dispatched from the home country enrolls in the home country's pension only via the certificate of coverage and is exempted from Japan's employees' pension (covers pension only) |
| Taiwan | No agreement | No equivalent exemption mechanism. If the person becomes insured in Japan, a double burden with the local system may arise |

A worker dispatched from China obtains the certificate of coverage from the home-country implementing agency and presents it on the Japanese side, thereby being exempted from enrolling in Japan's employees' pension. The agreement covers only public pension; it does not include medical, workers' accident, or employment insurance. Initially five years from the start of the posting is the target; extension when the posting continues past five years is organized in [when a posting runs past five years, what happens to social insurance — extending the certificate of coverage](/en/labor/column/chugoku-chuzai-tekiyo-shomeisho-encho-5nen-koe-shakaihosho), and the whole picture of double enrollment in [social insurance for a posting to China and Chinese employees — preventing double enrollment under the Japan-China Social Security Agreement](/en/labor/column/chugoku-chuzai-shakaihosho-kyotei-nijukanyu). With Taiwan, by contrast, there is no social security agreement, so this exemption cannot be used. For moving to or receiving from Taiwan, see [social insurance when you hire in Taiwan or second someone to Taiwan](/en/labor/column/taiwan-koyo-shukko-shakaihoken-kyotei-nashi).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Even an unregistered representative office — do we need social-insurance enrollment procedures?**
A. If you use employees in Japan, procedures may be required. A representative office is treated as an individual establishment: compulsory health-insurance and employees'-pension coverage if it is a covered industry with five or more, and voluntary coverage with the office representative as the business operator if fewer than five, is the practical organization (JETRO, "Japan's social security system," accessed 24 November 2026). Judge not by whether it is registered but by the reality of using workers and by headcount and industry. Workers' accident insurance in principle applies if even one worker is used; employment insurance is judged by the 20-hours/31-days requirement. Confirm the specific coverage with the Pension Office, Labor Standards Inspection Office, and Hello Work with jurisdiction.

**Q. Can the representative of the representative office themselves join health insurance and employees' pension?**
A. In principle, no. Because the representative is treated as an individual proprietor, they are in principle not an insured person of health insurance or employees' pension. However, by submitting a document showing the employment relationship with the overseas head office, they may exceptionally be recognized as an insured person, and the judgment is made case by case by the Pension Office with jurisdiction over the location (JETRO, "Japan's social security system," accessed 24 November 2026). Because the treatment can differ by Pension Office, it is safe to confirm with the jurisdiction before arrival. Whether an individual case qualifies is determined by a qualified professional.

**Q. If we pay the home-country salary and the Japan salary separately, which do we use to calculate premiums?**
A. If the person becomes insured in Japan, premiums are calculated on the standard monthly remuneration. Remuneration is "everything received as consideration for labor" (Article 3(5), Health Insurance Act; Article 3(1) item 3, Employees' Pension Insurance Act), and even where the payer is the home-country corporation, the part that is consideration for labor in Japan may in principle enter the basis. However, how much of the home-country-paid portion is included and at what exchange rate varies with the actual payment and the contractual relationship, and the specific handling rests with the Pension Office with jurisdiction. This article gives no uniform conclusion.

**Q. We will station a posted worker from a Taiwanese head office. Can we avoid double enrollment as with China?**
A. The same mechanism is not available. Because there is no social security agreement between Japan and Taiwan, there is no pension exemption by a certificate of coverage as with China. If the posted worker becomes insured in Japan, continuing to enroll in the Taiwanese system may create a double burden. Who becomes insured in Japan, and how they are treated on the Taiwanese side, varies with the employment relationship, the form of salary payment, and the reality of the work. Please consult about Japan's social insurance with a Shakai Hoken Roumushi, the Taiwanese system with a local professional, the residence status with a gyoseishoshi, and tax with a tax accountant, each as an independent business entity contracted separately.
