"I've decided to close with no successor, but how do I tell the staff and what do I process first?" and "cash flow is tight — I'm not sure I can even pay the last wages, let alone retirement pay" — these are questions we often hear from owners of small and medium enterprises that have decided to close or dissolve. This page organises the employee-side procedures at closure (dismissal notice, company-reason separation, the advance payment of unpaid wages, and social-insurance loss) as a labour design. The petition for bankruptcy or special liquidation itself, and the dissolution/liquidation registration, are outside this article's scope (the domain of an attorney and a judicial scrivener) and are touched on only in passing.

## How should you treat employees when you close? (dismissal notice, 30 days ahead)

Where you end employment contracts on closure, it is in principle a "dismissal." Labor Standards Act Article 20 provides that when an employer is to dismiss a worker, it must **give notice at least 30 days ahead,** or, failing notice, **pay 30 days' or more average wage (notice pay).**

| Approach | Content |
|---|---|
| Notice 30 days ahead | Give notice of the dismissal 30 or more days before the dismissal date (safer to record in writing) |
| Notice short of 30 days | Pay the shortfall in days as average wage (e.g. notice 20 days ahead → 10 days) |
| Immediate dismissal | Paying 30 days' or more average wage lets you dismiss without a notice period |

Note that **a closure due to management difficulty does not in principle amount to the "unavoidable cause" that would dispense with notice.** The proviso to Article 20, paragraph 1 lets you dispense with notice/notice pay — with the permission of the labour standards inspection office director — where continuing the business has become impossible due to a natural disaster or other unavoidable cause, or for a dismissal attributable to the worker; but a closure due to worsening cash flow or no successor is not usually the object of this certification. So even on closure, notice or notice pay is required. An individual dispute over a dismissal (contesting the dismissal's validity) is the work of an attorney; a Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles the procedure and labour design. ## How much is the notice pay, and how is it calculated?

Notice pay is calculated on "average wage." Average wage is, under Labor Standards Act Article 12, **in principle the total wages paid over the immediately preceding 3 months divided by the total days (calendar days) of that period.**

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Average wage | Total wages of the preceding 3 months ÷ total days of that period (Article 12) |
| Reckoning | From the day of the dismissal notice (from the last wage closing date where there is one) |
| Notice-pay amount | (30 days − notice days) × average wage. At least 30 days for immediate dismissal |
| Prescription | The extinctive prescription of a wage claim is 3 years for the time being (Article 115 and Supplementary Provisions) |

For example, if you give notice 10 days before the dismissal date, you pay 20 days' average wage (30 minus 10) as notice pay. The average-wage calculation is easy to get wrong over the wage closing date, commuting allowance, commission pay and absence deductions, so calculate with the wage ledger in order. For the thinking on average wage and wage calculation, see [how much does it cost to entrust wage calculation to a Shakai Hoken Roumushi](/en/labor/column/kyuyo-keisan-soba-sharoushi).

## Will the separation certificate be a company reason? How does the unemployment benefit change?

A separation due to closure (abolition of the establishment) ordinarily amounts to a **specified eligible recipient (so-called company reason)** as the employment-insurance separation reason. Correctly stating the separation reason on the certificate affects the employee's basic allowance (unemployment benefit).

| Aspect | Self-reason separation | Company-reason separation (specified eligible recipient) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | The worker's reasons (changing jobs, etc.) | Abolition of the establishment, insolvency, dismissal, etc. |
| Benefit restriction | In principle there is a restriction period | No benefit restriction |
| Prescribed benefit days | Relatively short | Can be more generous by age and insured period |
| Procedure | Correct classification of the separation reason on the certificate is a premise | Same |

When preparing the separation certificate on closure, classify the reason correctly as abolition of the establishment. Processing it wrongly as a self-reason separation risks depriving the employee of benefits they would otherwise receive. For the restriction mechanism, see [how does the benefit restriction for self-reason separation change in 2025 under employment insurance](/en/labor/column/koyohoken-2025-jikotsugou-kyufu-seigen-kyoiku-kunren). Preparing and filing the certificate is a procedure with Hello Work (the public employment security office), and a Shakai Hoken Roumushi can act on your behalf.

## When you cannot pay wages or retirement allowances, can you use the advance-payment system?

Where you cannot pay wages or retirement allowances at closure and it reaches insolvency, the **advance payment of unpaid wages** may be usable. Under Article 7 of the Act on Securing Payment of Wages (Act No. 34 of 1976), the Japan Organization of Occupational Health and Safety advances part of the unpaid wages.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Implementing body | Japan Organization of Occupational Health and Safety |
| Covered insolvency | Legal insolvency (decision to commence bankruptcy proceedings, etc.) / de facto insolvency (certification by the labour standards inspection office director for SMEs) |
| Covered workers | Those who left between the day 6 months before the petition date (for de facto insolvency, the certification-application date) and the following 2 years |
| Covered wages | Unpaid regular wages and retirement allowances whose due date fell between 6 months before the separation date and the day before the advance-payment claim. Total of 20,000 yen or more. Bonuses are excluded |
| Advance-payment amount | 80% of the total unpaid wages (subject to an age-based cap) |

The advance-payment cap is set by age on the separation date.

| Age on separation date | Cap on unpaid wages | Advance-payment cap (80%) |
|---|---|---|
| Under 30 | 1,100,000 yen | 880,000 yen |
| 30 to under 45 | 2,200,000 yen | 1,760,000 yen |
| 45 or over | 3,700,000 yen | 2,960,000 yen |

The certification of de facto insolvency is obtained by applying to the labour standards inspection office director (for SMEs). The advance-payment claim is made to the Organization. Note that the advance payment is the state system covering part of the wages; it does not fully compensate the unpaid wages. ## What is the order of loss procedures for social and labour insurance?

On employees' separation, loss procedures for social insurance (health insurance, employees' pension) and labour insurance (employment insurance, workers' accident insurance) are needed. On closure, beyond each employee's loss of eligibility, you proceed to the procedure for abolishing the establishment itself (full loss).

| Procedure | Submit to | Rough timing |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance / employees' pension loss-of-insured-status notification | Pension office (or health-insurance society) | Within 5 days of the fact of separation |
| Employment insurance loss notification / separation certificate | Hello Work | Within 10 days from the day after separation |
| Health insurance / employees' pension full-loss notification for the establishment | Pension office | Promptly after abolishing the establishment |
| Labour insurance — declaration and settlement of the fixed premium | Labour standards inspection office / labour bureau | Within 50 days of closing the business |

In order, you first carry out each employee's loss of eligibility (health insurance, employees' pension, employment insurance) and issue the separation certificate, and lastly proceed to the establishment's full loss and the labour-insurance fixed settlement. For the whole picture of the social-insurance full-loss procedure, see [how do you proceed with the social-insurance full-loss procedure when you wind up a company](/en/labor/column/kaisha-tatamu-shakaihoken-zenso-tetsuzuki). The timing is set by the system, and delay hampers the employee's switch to National Health Insurance / National Pension and the receipt of unemployment benefit, so proceed on a plan.

## FAQ

**Q. On closure, do we not need to give a dismissal notice?**
A. In principle you do. A closure due to management difficulty or no successor is not usually the "impossibility of continuing the business due to a natural disaster or other unavoidable cause" that would dispense with notice (Article 20, paragraph 1 proviso). Notice at least 30 days ahead, or average wage for the shortfall in days (notice pay), is required.

**Q. How is the notice pay calculated?**
A. On average wage. Average wage is the total wages of the preceding 3 months divided by the total days of that period (Article 12), and the notice pay is (30 days − notice days). Giving notice 30 days ahead means no notice pay; immediate dismissal means paying 30 days' or more.

**Q. If we reach insolvency unable to pay wages or retirement pay, do employees receive nothing?**
A. The advance payment of unpaid wages may be usable. The Japan Organization of Occupational Health and Safety advances 80% of unpaid regular wages and retirement allowances whose due date fell between 6 months before the separation date and the day before the claim (total of 20,000 yen or more; bonuses excluded), within an age-based cap on the separation date (Act on Securing Payment of Wages, Article 7). It does not compensate the full amount.

**Q. Can the separation certificate be a company reason?**
A. A separation due to abolition of the establishment ordinarily amounts to a specified eligible recipient (so-called company reason). No benefit restriction applies, and the prescribed benefit days can be more generous. Processing it wrongly as a self-reason separation risks depriving the employee of benefits they would otherwise receive, so classify it accurately.
