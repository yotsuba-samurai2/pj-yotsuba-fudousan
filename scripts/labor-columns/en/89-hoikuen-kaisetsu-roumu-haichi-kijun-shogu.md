**In short:** Labour for opening a nursery starts from a **staffing plan that meets the nursery-teacher ratios**. The ratios are set in Article 33(2) of the standards for the facilities and operation of child-welfare institutions (Ordinance No. 63 of 1948): roughly 1 nursery teacher per 3 infants, 1 per 6 for ages 1 to under 3, 1 per 15 for ages 3 to under 4, and 1 per 25 for age 4 and over (at least 2 per nursery). On top of this sit variable-hours shifts that cover early, late and extended care, a wage regime matching the treatment-improvement add-ons, and the social-insurance test for part-time nursery teachers. This article sets out how the ratios feed the staffing plan, how to build shifts, the wage regime, the social-insurance test, and who handles the setup application and the premises — from a shakai hoken roumushi's labour-management viewpoint.

When you open a nursery (licensed, small-scale licensed, or an employer-led one), the securing of qualified staff, shifts, wages and social-insurance procedures all start at once. This page is for operators about to open a nursery, and for managers who want to tidy up labour at an existing one. The final decision on whether a given nursery meets the ratios or the add-on requirements is made by the licensing/confirming authority (prefecture or municipality) and against the Children and Families Agency's guidelines, so check with them.

## How do the nursery-teacher ratios feed the staffing plan?

The ratios are not simply "how many to hire"; they are a rostering question of **how many you can keep placed in each time band**. They turn on the number of children by age.

| Category | Nursery teachers (roughly) |
|---|---|
| Infants (under 1) | 1 per 3 |
| Age 1 to under 3 | 1 per 6 |
| Age 3 to under 4 | 1 per 15 (was 1 per 20 before the reform) |
| Age 4 and over | 1 per 25 (was 1 per 30 before the reform) |

In addition, a nursery may not go below 2 nursery teachers (proviso to Article 33(2)). The figures for age 3-to-under-4 (1 per 15) and age 4-and-over (1 per 25) were revised in April 2024 (Reiwa 6). However, a transitional measure allows operation under the former ratios (1 per 20 and 1 per 30) for the time being. The deadline and handling of the transitional measure may change, so confirm with your authority and the Children and Families Agency (referenced September 2026).

From the labour side, the ratios must be **met throughout every hour the nursery is open**. The longer the opening hours, the more total head-count is needed, and you run it with part-timers alongside full-timers. How you count them as full-time equivalents follows the same logic as [How to count full-time equivalents](/en/labor/column/shogai-fukushi-kaigo-jokin-kansan-keisan-jinin).

## How do you build early/late/extended-care shifts with variable working hours?

A nursery is often open around 11 hours a day, and extended care makes it longer. Since no one works straight through, you combine early, middle and late shifts. The tool for this is the variable-hours system.

| System | Basis | Main feature |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly variable-hours system | Labor Standards Act Art. 32-2 | Average to within 40 hours a week over up to a month; set by work rules or labour-management agreement |
| Yearly variable-hours system | Labor Standards Act Art. 32-4 | Average to within 40 hours a week over up to a year; needs a labour-management agreement and filing; caps such as 10 hours a day / 52 hours a week |

If you want daily scheduled hours to be longer on some days and shorter on others, set the variable-hours system in the work rules and labour-management agreement. Without it, hours over 8 a day are overtime requiring premium pay. On choosing a system, see [Choosing between a variable-hours system and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata); on the annual-leave 5-day duty, see [When you cannot use up the 5 days of annual leave](/en/labor/column/nenji-yukyu-5nichi-torikirenai).

## How do you build a wage regime matching the treatment-improvement add-ons?

Nursery wages are inseparable from the Children and Families Agency's treatment-improvement add-ons. To receive them, the wage regime and work rules must be in place and the wage improvement must actually be carried out.

| Add-on | Aim | What to arrange in labour |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment-improvement add-on I | Wage improvement across staff (career-path requirement) | State working conditions and a wage structure by rank, responsibility and duties in the work rules, and make them known |
| Treatment-improvement add-on II | Improvement by skill and experience | Appoint deputy chief nursery teachers / duty-area leaders and set corresponding allowances in the wage regime |
| Treatment-improvement add-on III | Raising income (wage improvement) | Reliably improve the wages of eligible staff and keep records for reporting |

An add-on is not "receive and done": the amount received is distributed to staff as personnel cost and checked in a results report. If the wage regime conflicts with the requirements, the report stumbles. Names and requirements are revised repeatedly, so confirm against the latest implementation guidelines (referenced September 2026). The design thinking resembles [Building a wage regime for the care-worker treatment-improvement add-on](/en/labor/column/kaigo-shogu-kaizen-kasan-chingin-kitei) (a different sector). How many add-on tiers you can obtain is decided finally by the authority and the Children and Families Agency.

## How do you judge social insurance for part-time and short-hours nursery teachers?

Nurseries have many part-time nursery teachers, so the line for social insurance (health insurance and employees' pension) is a core issue. There are two yardsticks.

| Yardstick | Content |
|---|---|
| Three-quarters test | Applies if weekly scheduled hours and monthly scheduled days are three-quarters or more of an ordinary worker's at the same office |
| Coverage expansion for short-hours workers | At a specified applicable office (101 or more, and from October 2024, 51 or more regular employees), part-timers meeting 20+ weekly hours, monthly wage of ¥88,000+, and not being a student are also covered |

First check the three-quarters test; even if it does not fit, depending on the office's size and the worker's conditions, they may fall under coverage expansion. See [Social insurance when you hire for short hours](/en/labor/column/tanjikan-koyo-shakaihoken-4bunno3). A nursery using 10 or more people must also draw up and file work rules ([What you need for work rules at 10 employees](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How did the nursery-teacher ratios change in the 2024 reform?**
A. Age 3-to-under-4 went from 1 per 20 to 1 per 15, and age 4-and-over from 1 per 30 to 1 per 25 (Article 33(2) of the standards for child-welfare institutions). However, a transitional measure allows operation under the former ratios for the time being. The deadline and handling may change, so confirm with your authority and the Children and Families Agency (referenced September 2026).

**Q. Do I always need a variable-hours system to build early/late shifts?**
A. If you build shifts within 8 hours a day / 40 a week, you can do so without one. But if you want daily scheduled hours longer on some days and shorter on others to cover opening hours, set a monthly (Art. 32-2) or yearly (Art. 32-4) variable-hours system in the work rules and labour-management agreement. Without it, hours over 8 a day become overtime.

**Q. How do I set up the wage regime to receive the treatment-improvement add-ons?**
A. Add-on I requires stating working conditions and a wage structure by rank, responsibility and duties in the work rules as a career-path requirement; add-on II requires appointing deputy chief nursery teachers, etc., with corresponding allowances; add-on III requires reliably improving eligible staff's wages. Distribute the amount received as personnel cost and keep records for reporting. Requirements are revised repeatedly, so confirm against the latest implementation guidelines (referenced September 2026).

**Q. Must short-hours part-time nursery teachers join social insurance?**
A. First, if they meet the three-quarters test (weekly hours and monthly days at three-quarters or more of an ordinary worker's), they are covered. Even if not, at a specified applicable office (51 or more regular employees from October 2024), they may fall under coverage expansion with 20+ weekly hours, a monthly wage of ¥88,000+, and not being a student. It turns on the office's size and the worker's conditions.
