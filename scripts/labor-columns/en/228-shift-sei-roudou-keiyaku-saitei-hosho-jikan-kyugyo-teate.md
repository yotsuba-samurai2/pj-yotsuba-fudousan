**Bottom line first**: Even when you hire on a shift basis, you must state the start and end times, whether work beyond scheduled hours may be required, breaks, and holidays as working conditions (Article 15(1) of the Labor Standards Act; Article 5(1) item 2 of the Labor Standards Act Enforcement Regulations). Writing only "by shift" on the notice of working conditions is insufficient; for days whose times are confirmed at the time of contracting you must state the times, and for days not yet confirmed you must make clear how the times will be decided or the standard time band (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare [MHLW], "Points to note for appropriately managing the employment of so-called 'shift-system' workers"; the version accessed on 24 November 2026 is dated 19 June 2026). If you do not set a minimum guaranteed number of workdays or hours, disputes such as "I am barely put on any shifts" arise more easily. When you reduce a once-confirmed shift for the employer's convenience, an allowance for absence from work of at least 60 percent of average wages may be required for the period of absence (Article 26, Labor Standards Act). Drafting work rules and labor-management agreements and designing working conditions are the field of a Shakai Hoken Roumushi; individual disputes over unpaid wages the field of an attorney; and applications for subsidies the field of a Shakai Hoken Roumushi, each contracted separately as an independent business entity. There is no referral fee.

## What working conditions must be stated even under a shift system?

A shift system is a way of working in which the workdays and working hours are not fixed at the time the labor contract is concluded, and become specific only through a work shift prepared each fixed period (one week, one month, etc.). A form in which the pattern is fixed by the work rules, like a three-shift rotation, is not included in a "shift system" here (MHLW, "Shift-system points to note"; the version accessed on 24 November 2026 is dated 19 June 2026).

Even under a shift system, the duty to state working conditions is not avoided. The employer must state working conditions when concluding a labor contract (Article 15(1), Labor Standards Act), and the items to state are set out in Article 5 of the Enforcement Regulations. What especially becomes an issue under a shift system is the following.

| Item to state | Basis | Note under a shift system |
|---|---|---|
| Start and end times, whether work beyond scheduled hours may be required, breaks, holidays, leave | Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(1) item 2 (written statement required, Art. 5(4)) | "By shift" alone is not enough. State times for confirmed days; show how times are decided for unconfirmed days |
| Workplace and duties (including the scope of changes) | Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(1) item 1-3 | The "scope of changes" was added by the amendment in force 1 April 2024 |
| Criteria for renewing a fixed-term contract (including any renewal cap) | Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(1) item 1-2 | Shift-system part-timers are often on fixed terms; stating a renewal cap may be required |
| The opportunity to apply for conversion to open-ended, and the conditions after conversion | Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(5) | Stated at renewal of a fixed-term contract exceeding five years in total |

Start and end times and holidays are items that must be stated in writing (Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(4)). For days whose times are set at the time of contracting, state the times; for days not set, make clear how the times are decided or the standard time band. The items added by the amendment to the Enforcement Regulations in force 1 April 2024 are organized in [what changed in the working-conditions notice rules from April 2024](/en/labor/column/roudou-joken-meiji-2024-henko-han-i). For the difference from one-off, short-time work, see also [labor management of spot work (one-off part-time jobs)](/en/labor/column/spot-work-sukima-baito-roumu-kanri).

## What are the risks of not setting a minimum guaranteed number of hours?

A shift system has the advantage of flexible working for both sides, but it is also a form of work where disputes arise easily—hardly any workdays are set, or more days than wished are assigned. The MHLW points-to-note illustrate matters that are good to decide in advance to prevent such trouble.

| Matter good to decide in advance | Examples of content |
|---|---|
| Rules for preparing the shift | Hear the worker's wishes when preparing the shift / decide the deadline and method of notice |
| Rules for change and cancellation | The deadline and procedure for changing or canceling a once-confirmed shift |
| A guide to the number of workdays/hours | ① Maximum workdays/hours, ② guide workdays/hours, ③ minimum workdays/hours |

If you do not set a minimum number of workdays or hours, dissatisfaction arises—"I am barely put on shifts and my income is unstable"—and the worker's own life planning also collapses. Setting a guide and a minimum helps prevent disputes. Also, changing once-confirmed workdays or working hours basically amounts to a change in working conditions and in principle requires the agreement of both the employer and the worker (MHLW, "Shift-system points to note," accessed 24 November 2026). The content of a labor contract is in principle changed by the agreement of the employer and the worker (Article 8, Labor Contract Act). These rules for preparation and change can be agreed individually with labor and management, or set uniformly in the work rules.

## If you cut shifts for the employer's convenience, is an absence allowance required?

When you reduce (cancel) a once-confirmed shift for the employer's convenience, an absence allowance can be at issue. When the employer causes a worker to be absent for a reason attributable to the employer, an allowance of at least 60 percent of average wages must be paid during the absence (Article 26, Labor Standards Act).

| Situation | Approach to the absence allowance |
|---|---|
| A confirmed shift was canceled for the employer's convenience | As absence for a reason attributable to the employer, an allowance of at least 60 percent of average wages may be required |
| The worker was not put on a day whose shift was not yet confirmed | Whether it is "absence" depends on whether there is a minimum guarantee, the nature of the shift, and the content of the labor-management agreement (case-by-case) |
| The worker took leave for the worker's own reason | Not for a reason attributable to the employer, so not subject to the absence allowance |

The line here is difficult. If you reduce a confirmed shift on the employer's side, that part can be absence for "a reason attributable to the employer" and subject to the absence allowance. On the other hand, merely not putting a worker on a day not yet confirmed does not immediately become an absence-allowance issue; the judgment divides on circumstances such as whether a minimum guarantee is set and how definite the shift was. Average wages are in principle calculated by dividing the total wages of the latest three months by the total number of days (Article 12, Labor Standards Act). The calculation of the absence allowance itself and the line for force majeure are organized in detail in [how much is the allowance when you place someone on absence — the 60 percent of Article 26](/en/labor/column/kyugyo-teate-26jo-heikin-chingin-roumu). Cutting shifts itself can also amount to a disadvantageous change in working conditions, and individual disputes over unpaid wages are the field of an attorney.

## How do you build shift-system work rules and agreements that avoid trouble?

Most shift-system disputes arise because the rules for preparation and change are not decided and the statement of working conditions is insufficient. Setting these in writing in advance is prevention.

- **Set them in the work rules**: An establishment that regularly uses 10 or more workers has a duty to set start and end times, holidays, and so on in the work rules and file them with the Labor Standards Inspection Office (Article 89, Labor Standards Act). Setting the rules for preparing, changing, and notifying shifts uniformly in the work rules stabilizes operation. The duty to prepare work rules is organized in [work rules are mandatory from 10 workers — what is required](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).
- **Match the notice of working conditions to reality**: State times for confirmed days, and show how times are decided for unconfirmed days. The point is not to finish with the single phrase "by shift."
- **Watch the treatment gap for part-time and fixed-term**: You are required not to set an unreasonable gap in treatment between shift-system part-time or fixed-term workers and regular employees. The duty to explain the treatment gap is covered in [the treatment explanation for part-time and fixed-term from October 2026](/en/labor/column/part-yuki-2026-10-meiji-taigu-setsumei).
- **Check the night-time and deemed-hours treatment**: For the premium and deemed-hours treatment when shifts are set for night hours at a restaurant and the like, see [labor for opening a restaurant — night premiums, shifts, and deemed hours](/en/labor/column/inshokuten-kaigyo-roumu-shinya-shift-minashi).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is it enough to write only "working hours are by shift" on the notice of working conditions?**
A. It is not enough. Start and end times and holidays are working conditions that must be stated in writing (Article 15(1), Labor Standards Act; Article 5(1) item 2 and (4), Enforcement Regulations), and for days whose times are set at the time of contracting you must state the times. For days not set, you are also required to make clear how the times are decided or the standard time band (MHLW, "Shift-system points to note," accessed 24 November 2026). The single phrase "by shift" does not count as having stated them. Also setting the rules for preparing, notifying, and changing shifts prevents later trouble.

**Q. The busy season is over, so from next month I want to cut shifts sharply. Is there a problem?**
A. When you reduce a once-confirmed shift for the employer's convenience, an absence allowance of at least 60 percent of average wages may be required for that absence (Article 26, Labor Standards Act). Unilaterally changing a confirmed shift basically amounts to a change in working conditions and in principle requires the worker's agreement (Article 8, Labor Contract Act). On the other hand, merely not putting a worker on a day not yet confirmed is treated differently depending on whether a minimum guarantee is set and the nature of the shift. Because the assessment changes with the reason, amount, and procedure of the cut, we recommend checking case by case against the work rules and the shift rules.

**Q. Do shift-system part-timers also need a renewal cap and conversion statement?**
A. If they are on a fixed-term contract, it may become necessary. By the amendment to the Enforcement Regulations in force 1 April 2024, a fixed-term contract requires a statement of the renewal cap (the cap on total contract period or number of renewals) (Enforcement Reg. Art. 5(1) item 1-2), and at a renewal where conversion to open-ended can be applied for after exceeding five years in total, a statement of the opportunity to apply for conversion and the conditions after conversion is required (same Art., (5)). Because shift-system part-timers are often hired on fixed terms, check at each renewal whether these statements are needed.

**Q. How many hours is the minimum working time for a shift system set at by law?**
A. The law does not set a uniform minimum working time. The MHLW points-to-note illustrate setting maximum, guide, and minimum numbers of workdays and hours to prevent disputes, but the specific hours are decided by labor and management (accessed 24 November 2026). Whether to set a minimum guarantee and how many hours to make it are designed in light of the busyness of the work and the worker's wishes. Setting one helps both income stability and dispute prevention. The appropriateness of an individual design is determined by a qualified professional after a consultation.
