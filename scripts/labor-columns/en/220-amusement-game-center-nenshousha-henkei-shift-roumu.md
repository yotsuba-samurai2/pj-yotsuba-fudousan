**Bottom line first**: At a game center (Category 5 business under Article 2, paragraph 1, item 5 of the Amusement Business Act), labor management must meet two separate sets of rules on minors at once. One is the Labor Standards Act: those under 18 cannot in principle work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. (Article 61), and the Article 36 agreement and the variable working-hour system cannot be used (Article 60). The other is the Amusement Business Act: having a person under 18 serve customers between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day, or letting them enter as customers, is prohibited (Article 22). Student-centered shifts are built on the monthly or yearly variable working-hour system (Articles 32-2 and 32-4).

## How late can those under 18 or under 16 work at a game center?

The limit on working them as employees is set by the Labor Standards Act. Article 61, paragraph 1 provides that a person under 18 must not be used between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. It is not enough to pay the night premium; employing them at all in that band is prohibited.

| Division | Basis | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Ban on night work | Article 61, paragraph 1 | In principle no work 10 p.m.–5 a.m.; but a male aged 16 or over on shift work is excluded |
| Non-application of the variable system and the Article 36 agreement | Article 60, paragraph 1 | Overtime and holiday work under an Article 36 agreement and the variable working-hour systems in principle cannot be used |
| Ban on hazardous work | Article 62 | Cannot be put to dangerous or harmful work prescribed by Ordinance |
| Keeping proof-of-age documents | Article 57, paragraph 1 | Keep a family-register certificate proving age at the workplace |
| Minimum age | Article 56, paragraph 1 | In principle cannot be used until the first 31 March after reaching age 15 |

A child who has not passed the first 31 March after reaching age 15 (the fiscal year-end of lower-secondary graduation) in principle cannot be employed. Most high-school part-timers are under 18 and past that fiscal year-end, so Article 61's ban on night work applies directly. Even being a few minutes past 10 p.m. for closing work means you cannot keep them in that band. The full picture of the limits on minors' hours, night work and hazardous work is in [the night and overtime limits for part-timers under 18](/en/labor/column/nenshousha-18sai-miman-shinya-jikangai-seigen-roumu).

## How do the Amusement Business Act's rules on minors differ from the Labor Standards Act's?

This is the easy-to-confuse part. A game center is subject, separately from the Labor Standards Act, to the rules on minors under the Act on Control and Improvement of Amusement Business, etc. (the Amusement Business Act). The two differ in purpose and in the structure of their provisions.

| | Labor Standards Act | Amusement Business Act |
|---|---|---|
| Who is protected | Minors working as employees | Mainly minors as customers, and minors serving customers at night |
| Night band | 10 p.m.–5 a.m. (Article 61) | 10 p.m.–6 a.m. next day (Article 22, paragraph 1, items 4 and 5) |
| Main content | Ban on night work, overtime and hazardous work; minimum age | Ban on having under-18s serve customers, restriction on their entering as customers, ban on serving them alcohol/tobacco |

Article 22, paragraph 1 of the Amusement Business Act lists, as prohibited acts of a person running an amusement business: item 3, having a person under 18 entertain customers at the premises; item 4, having a person under 18 engage in work serving customers at the premises between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day; item 5, letting a person under 18 enter the premises as a customer (for a Category 5 business — a game center — letting them enter as a customer between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day); and item 6, providing alcohol or tobacco to a person under 20.

Note the gap in the night band. The Labor Standards Act's ban on night work (Article 61) runs 10 p.m. to 5 a.m., but the Amusement Business Act's restriction on "work serving customers" (Article 22, paragraph 1, item 4) runs 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. the next day. When you have an under-18 employee work at the counter, you must meet both bands' rules at once. Further, Article 22, paragraph 2 allows prefectures by by-law to ban under-18s from entering a game center as customers in the band after 6 a.m. and before 10 p.m., or to require a guardian to accompany them. Entry hours for those under 16 and the like differ by local by-law, so you need to check the by-law where the premises are. For the requirements and flow of the license itself, see [the license for a game center (Category 5 under the Amusement Business Act)](/en/legal/column/fuzoku-eigyo-5go-game-center-amusement-kyoka-youken-nagare) (an administrative-scrivener article).

## How do you build student-centered shifts with the variable working-hour system?

Game centers swing widely in traffic across weekday evenings, weekends and holidays, and long school breaks, and tend to be centered on student part-timers. For adult employees, you distribute working days and hours to match the swings using a monthly (Labor Standards Act Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hour system.

| System | Basis | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly variable working-hour system | Article 32-2 | Averaged over a period of up to one month to 40 hours a week, you may work over 8 hours on specified weeks and days. File if by agreement |
| Yearly variable working-hour system | Article 32-4 | Averaged over a target period of over one month and up to one year to 40 hours a week. Set the scope of workers, target period and hours per working day in an agreement and file it |

For how to choose, see [choosing between variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata). But these variable systems in principle cannot be used for those under 18 (Article 60, paragraph 1). Minors are, as a rule, worked within the statutory limit of 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week. For a person under 18 past the year-end after age 15, Article 60, paragraph 3 allows adjusting the distribution of hours by day — shortening one day to within 4 hours and extending another to up to 10 hours — without moving the weekly total of 40 hours, but this is not a release of overtime. Writing the treatment of minors into the work rules reduces hesitation in shift design on site. For the basic duty to prepare work rules, see [from how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## How do you calculate premium wages for peak periods and at night?

When adult employees do overtime or night work, statutory premium wages apply.

| Kind of work | Premium | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime (over statutory hours) | 25% or more | Labor Standards Act Article 37, paragraph 1 (Cabinet Order) |
| Night (10 p.m.–5 a.m.) | 25% or more | Article 37, paragraph 4 |
| Overtime extending into the night | 50% or more combined | Article 37, paragraphs 1 and 4 |
| The part of monthly overtime over 60 hours | 50% or more | Article 37, paragraph 1 proviso |

At a store open past midnight, adult employees' night-band work carries the night premium under Article 37, paragraph 4. To have workers do overtime you must conclude an Article 36 agreement and file it; its ceiling is in [how far overtime can go under an Article 36 agreement](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). Minors cannot be made to do overtime or holiday work on the basis of an Article 36 agreement, and they cannot do night work after 10 p.m. in the first place, so a minor's shift is divided by time band before it is a matter of calculating premiums. The regional minimum wage, the floor for wages, applies regardless of the time band (Minimum Wage Act), and the night premium is added on top of the minimum-wage amount.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. The Amusement Business Act allows under-18s to enter until 10 p.m., so can we work a high-schooler until 10 p.m.?**
A. No. The Amusement Business Act's entry restriction (Article 22, paragraph 1, item 5 and paragraph 2) is about entering as a customer, which is separate from working as an employee. For employing them, Article 61, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act applies, and a person under 18 cannot in principle work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. Moreover, Article 22, paragraph 1, item 4 of the Amusement Business Act bans having a person under 18 engage in work serving customers between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day. You must meet both separate rules at once, so place the high-schooler's shift end before 10 p.m. as the default.

**Q. Can we have a high-schooler do overtime within an Article 36 agreement during peak periods?**
A. No. Article 60, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act provides that Article 36 (the overtime and holiday-work agreement) does not apply to those under 18. Even with an Article 36 agreement, you cannot use it as a basis to make a minor do overtime or holiday work. For a person past the year-end after age 15, there is room under paragraph 3 to adjust the distribution of hours by day without moving the weekly total, but this is not a release of overtime.

**Q. We are open until midnight. How do we calculate premiums for adult employees?**
A. Work between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. carries the night premium (25% or more) under Article 37, paragraph 4 of the Labor Standards Act. If that work is also overtime over the statutory hours, the overtime premium (25% or more) and the night premium overlap to make 50% or more combined. The part of monthly overtime over 60 hours is 50% or more. To have workers do overtime, you must conclude an Article 36 agreement and file it with the head of the competent Labor Standards Inspection Office.

**Q. As a "shift-work" business, can a game center work a high-school boy at night?**
A. The shift-work proviso in Article 61, paragraph 1 of the Labor Standards Act applies only to males aged 16 or over, and with a shift-work business's permission from the administrative agency there is room to work them until 10:30 p.m. and from 5:30 a.m. (paragraph 3). But whether this permission is needed or applies is a judgment for each business's actual situation, and Article 22, paragraph 1, item 4 of the Amusement Business Act — banning work serving customers between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. — applies separately. Do not glibly treat "shift work means night is fine"; whether a particular case qualifies is judged by a qualified professional.
