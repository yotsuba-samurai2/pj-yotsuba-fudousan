**In short:** to broker the sale or lease of real estate as a business in Japan, you must obtain a licence from the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism or from a prefectural governor, under Article 3, paragraph 1 of the Real Estate Transaction Business Act. If your offices are only within one prefecture, it is a governor's licence; if they span two or more prefectures, a minister's licence. The licence is valid for five years, and to continue you must renew it (Article 3, paragraphs 2 and 3). You must place, at each office, a full-time licensed real estate transaction agent who is an adult, in a ratio of at least one for every five persons engaged in the business (Article 31-3, paragraph 1; the ratio is set at one-fifth by Article 15-5-3 of the Enforcement Regulation). Before you start trading you must also arrange a business guarantee deposit (10 million yen for the main office and 5 million yen for each additional office; joining the Real Estate Transaction Business Guarantee Association can replace the deposit) (Articles 25 and 64-9). ## When is a real estate transaction business licence required? (The difference between a governor's and a minister's licence)

The starting point is "what you do as a business." Article 2, item 2 of the Real Estate Transaction Business Act defines the real estate transaction business as the sale or exchange of land or buildings, or acting as agent or broker in the sale, exchange or lease of land or buildings, carried on as a business (repeatedly and continuously with an indefinite number of counterparties). To do these you need a licence under Article 3, paragraph 1. Note that leasing out property you yourself own ("leasing as the lessor yourself") is, as a rule, not a real estate transaction business, because it is not agency or brokerage. This line can shift with the individual facts, so confirming with the competent authority at the planning stage is essential.

The category of licence turns on where your offices are.

| Item | Governor's licence | Minister's licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Real Estate Transaction Business Act, Article 3, paragraph 1 | Same paragraph |
| Criterion | Offices only within one prefecture | Offices span two or more prefectures |
| Filed with | That prefectural governor | The Minister (via the prefecture where the main office is located) |
| Validity | 5 years (renewal licence required; Article 3, paragraphs 2 and 3) | 5 years (same) |

The licence is valid for five years from the date of the licence, and to continue after expiry you must obtain a renewal licence (Article 3, paragraphs 2 and 3). Many prefectures require the renewal application between 90 and 30 days before expiry. The grounds for refusal of a licence are set out in Article 5, paragraph 1, under which past dispositions and the situation of officers are examined.

## How many "full-time licensed real estate transaction agents" are required, and who qualifies?

Article 31-3, paragraph 1 of the Real Estate Transaction Business Act requires that, at each office and at other places prescribed by the Ministry's regulation, you place a number of adult full-time licensed real estate transaction agents such that their ratio to the number of persons engaged in the business is at least the number prescribed by the regulation. That ratio is set at one-fifth by Article 15-5-3 of the Enforcement Regulation, so in practice it is explained as "at least one full-time licensed real estate transaction agent for every five persons engaged in the business."

| Item | Content (basis) |
| --- | --- |
| Place of placement | Each office (separately for a sales venue etc. that handles contracts) |
| Number required | At least one per five persons engaged (Article 31-3, paragraph 1; Enforcement Regulation Article 15-5-3) |
| Qualification | An adult full-time licensed real estate transaction agent (who holds the agent's card) |
| Meaning of "full-time" | Working full-time at that office and engaged exclusively in the real estate transaction business; concurrent posts at other companies or non-full-time work are, as a rule, not allowed |

"Full-time" means being constantly present at that office and engaged exclusively in that business. Where the person concurrently serves as an officer of another company, holds another occupation, or lives too far to commute, they are, as a rule, not recognised as full-time. If the number falls below the standard, there is a duty to take the necessary measures within two weeks (Article 31-3, paragraph 3). Whether the requirement is met is a judgment under the Real Estate Transaction Business Act, not something an administrative scrivener decides on your behalf as an exclusive service.

## What are the requirements for premises accepted as an office? (Independence and continuity)

An office is both the premise for obtaining a licence and the unit that determines the business guarantee deposit and the number of full-time agents. The Ministry's "Interpretation and Operation of the Real Estate Transaction Business Act" and each prefecture's guide state that an office must be a facility where business can be carried on continuously and that is physically independent of the living space of any other corporation or individual.

| Point examined | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Independence | Clearly partitioned from other companies or living space by walls and the like, with an independent entrance |
| Continuity | A form in which business can be carried on continuously, not a temporary structure |
| Right to use | A right to use the place as an office, by ownership or a lease agreement |
| Full-time agent on site | A full-time licensed real estate transaction agent working full-time at that office |

If you make one room of your home the office, whether it can be clearly separated from the living space and whether the entrance and traffic line are independent are examined. For a rented property, whether the lease permits use as an office, and whether it conflicts with the rules of the sectional ownership, must also be checked. Because the fine acceptability of premises is judged by the competent authority, consulting before you sign makes it easier to avoid a bounce-back just before filing. ## A business guarantee deposit or the guarantee association (the deposit contribution) — which do you choose?

Even with a licence, you cannot start trading on that alone. As a financial safeguard to protect the counterparty, you must either lodge a business guarantee deposit or join the Real Estate Transaction Business Guarantee Association.

| Item | Business guarantee deposit (Article 25) | Joining the guarantee association (Article 64-9) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main office | 10 million yen (Enforcement Order Article 2-4) | Deposit contribution of 600,000 yen |
| Each additional office | 5 million yen | 300,000 yen |
| Paid/lodged with | Lodged at the deposit office nearest the main office | Paid in money to the association |
| Timing | Lodge and notify the licensing authority before starting (Article 25, paragraphs 4 and 5) | Pay by the day you intend to join (Article 64-9, paragraph 1) |

You cannot begin business until you have lodged the business guarantee deposit and notified the licensing authority (Article 25, paragraphs 4 and 5). If you do not notify of the lodging within three months of the date of the licence, the authority may demand it, and if there is still no notification it may revoke the licence (Article 25, paragraphs 6 and 7). If instead you join the Real Estate Transaction Business Guarantee Association, paying the deposit contribution in money (600,000 yen for the main office and 300,000 yen for each additional office) exempts you from lodging the business guarantee deposit (Article 64-9). Because it is easier on the initial outlay, many new entrants join the association. Which suits you depends on your funding plan and scale. Lodging the deposit or the procedure to join the association is done by you with the deposit office and the association; an administrative scrivener does not lodge or pay on your behalf.

## FAQ

**Q. Even if I only lease out an apartment I own myself, do I need a real estate transaction business licence?**
A. As a rule, no. The real estate transaction business (Article 2, item 2 of the Act) means the sale or exchange, or the agency or brokerage of sale, exchange or lease, carried on as a business; leasing out your own property as the lessor yourself is not agency or brokerage, so it is, as a rule, not the business. However, repeated sales and the like may require a licence, and the line shifts with the individual facts. Confirm with the competent authority at the planning stage.

**Q. Is a minister's licence "higher ranked" than a governor's? Does the area in which I can operate differ?**
A. It is not a difference in rank but a category based on where your offices are. If your offices are only within one prefecture it is a governor's licence; if they span two or more prefectures, a minister's licence (Article 3, paragraph 1). Even with a governor's licence, the location of the properties you can handle is not limited to that prefecture — you can handle properties in other prefectures too. The validity is five years for both.

**Q. May a full-time licensed real estate transaction agent also serve as an officer of another company?**
A. As a rule, no. "Full-time" means being constantly present at that office and engaged exclusively in the real estate transaction business. Serving as a full-time officer of another company, holding another occupation, or living where you cannot commute may negate the full-time status. When the ratio of at least one per five persons engaged (Article 31-3, paragraph 1; Enforcement Regulation Article 15-5-3) falls short, there is a duty to take the necessary measures within two weeks.

**Q. I cannot raise the 10 million yen business guarantee deposit. Must I give up opening?**
A. There is the option of joining the Real Estate Transaction Business Guarantee Association. On joining, paying a deposit contribution of 600,000 yen for the main office and 300,000 yen for each additional office exempts you from lodging the business guarantee deposit (Article 64-9). Because it is easier on the initial outlay, it is often chosen by new entrants. Which suits you depends on your funding plan and scale, so confirm the joining procedure with the association.
