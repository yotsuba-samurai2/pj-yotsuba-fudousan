**In short:** where an ordinary-form will such as a holographic or notarial will cannot be made in time, there is room to use the "special-form wills" the Civil Code provides. There are four special forms: wills in imminent danger used when death is imminent (general imminent-danger will = Civil Code Article 976, shipwreck imminent-danger will = Civil Code Article 979) and wills in isolated places used where traffic is cut off (person in quarantine for an infectious disease = Civil Code Article 977, person aboard a ship = Civil Code Article 978). This article is an overview that lays the four forms side by side: in which situations each can be used, who the witnesses and attesting officials are, and whether the family court's confirmation is needed. The detailed requirements of the general imminent-danger will (Article 976) — three witnesses, oral statement (kuju), writing, and confirmation within 20 days — are **delegated to [The will of a person in imminent danger of death and the family court's confirmation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kikyuji-yuigon-shibo-kikyusha-minpo-976jo-kakunin)**, and this article centres on the shipwreck imminent-danger will (Article 979), the wills in isolated places (Articles 977, 978), and a cross-cutting comparison of the four. A will in imminent danger (Articles 976, 979) does not take effect merely on being made: it does not take effect without obtaining the family court's "confirmation of the will"; a will in an isolated place (Articles 977, 978) does not require this confirmation. Further, if the testator survives for six months from the time they become able to make a will in an ordinary form, any special-form will loses effect (Civil Code Article 983). This article is general information and does not reach the final judgement of whether a particular will is valid. Drafting the will and arranging witnesses are supported by Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office (administrative scrivener); but the petition to the family court for confirmation (preparing and acting on documents submitted to a court) is outside an administrative scrivener's scope and is done by the person or an attorney (Administrative Scriveners Act Articles 1-2, 1-3). ## What kinds of special-form wills are there?

Wills have an "ordinary form" usable at any time in normal circumstances, and a "special form" usable only in special situations. The ordinary forms are the holographic will (Civil Code Article 968) and the notarial will (Article 969) and the like; the special form is an exceptional method allowed only in emergency or isolated situations where the ordinary form cannot be made in time. The special forms further divide into "wills in imminent danger" for situations of imminent death and "wills in isolated places" for places where traffic is cut off — four forms in all.

| Class | Form | Basis | Situation | Attendance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Imminent-danger will | General imminent-danger will | Civil Code Art. 976 | Imminent death from illness etc. | Three or more witnesses |
| Imminent-danger will | Shipwreck imminent-danger will | Civil Code Art. 979 | Imminent death aboard a wrecked ship | Two or more witnesses |
| Isolated-place will | Quarantine will | Civil Code Art. 977 | Traffic cut off by administrative order | One police officer + one or more witnesses |
| Isolated-place will | On-board will | Civil Code Art. 978 | Aboard a ship | One captain or clerk + two or more witnesses |

You cannot use a special form where the ordinary form is available. It is strictly an emergency means limited to emergency or isolated situations where the ordinary form cannot be made in time. The form that most often raises problems is the general imminent-danger will (Article 976); its detailed requirements — three witnesses, oral statement, writing, reading aloud — and how to proceed with the 20-day confirmation are covered in [The will of a person in imminent danger of death and the family court's confirmation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kikyuji-yuigon-shibo-kikyusha-minpo-976jo-kakunin). This article goes on to the remaining shipwreck imminent-danger will (Article 979), the wills in isolated places (Articles 977, 978), and the cross-cutting comparison of the four. The difference between the ordinary holographic and notarial wills is set out in [The difference between a holographic will and a notarial will](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/jihitsu-kosei-yuigon).

## How does a shipwreck imminent-danger will (Art. 979) differ from the general type?

A shipwreck imminent-danger will is the form made, when a ship has been wrecked, by a person in imminent danger of death aboard it. Such a person may make a will orally before two or more witnesses (Civil Code Article 979(1)); a witness writes down its tenor and signs and seals it (Article 979(3)). Whereas the general imminent-danger will (Article 976) requires three or more witnesses, an oral statement (kuju) to one of them, and reading-aloud with each witness's approval of the writing, the shipwreck type allows two or more witnesses and an oral will — the requirements are somewhat looser. It helps to understand this as simplifying the steps for the extreme situation of a shipwreck. For the detailed steps of witnesses, oral statement, and writing of the general imminent-danger will, see [The will of a person in imminent danger of death and the family court's confirmation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kikyuji-yuigon-shibo-kikyusha-minpo-976jo-kakunin).

| Requirement | General imminent-danger (Art. 976) | Shipwreck imminent-danger (Art. 979) |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | Imminent death from illness or another cause | Imminent death aboard a wrecked ship |
| Number of witnesses | Three or more | Two or more |
| How conveyed | Oral statement to one | Orally stated |
| Writing | Witness who received the statement writes, reads aloud, approves | A witness writes down the tenor |
| Time to request confirmation | Within 20 days of the day of the will | Without delay |

For the shipwreck will too, witness disqualifications (minors, presumptive heirs and legatees and their spouses and lineal relatives, etc.) apply as in the ordinary form (Civil Code Articles 982, 974). Loose as it is, whether the two witnesses are not disqualified, and whether the tenor stated orally rests on the testator's own intention, are assessed at the family court's confirmation that follows, or at trial if disputed.

## What are the wills in isolated places (quarantine / on-board)?

A will in an isolated place is the form made by a person who, without being in imminent danger of death, is in a place where traffic is cut off and cannot follow an ordinary form. Unlike a will in imminent danger, it is marked by the presence of a public attesting official. A quarantine will may be made, by a person in a place where traffic is cut off by administrative order for an infectious disease, before one police officer and one or more witnesses (Civil Code Article 977). An on-board will may be made, by a person aboard a ship, before one captain or clerk and two or more witnesses (Civil Code Article 978). In each, the testator, the writer, the attesting official, and the witnesses each sign and seal the will (Civil Code Article 980; special rules for persons unable to speak etc. in Article 981).

| Form | Basis | Situation | Attendance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarantine will | Civil Code Art. 977 | Traffic cut off by administrative order for an infectious disease | One police officer + one or more witnesses |
| On-board will | Civil Code Art. 978 | Aboard a ship (not wrecked) | One captain or clerk + two or more witnesses |

What matters in practice here is that **a will in an isolated place does not require the family court's confirmation (the 20-day rule etc.) of the kind imminent-danger wills need.** Whereas wills in imminent danger (Articles 976, 979) are made in the pressing circumstance of imminent death and so put in place a mechanism for the family court to confirm the true intention, wills in isolated places are made before a public attesting official who produces the will document itself, and so no confirmation procedure is provided. That said, the point that they lapse if the testator survives six months after an ordinary form becomes possible (Civil Code Article 983) applies equally to wills in isolated places.

## How do the need for confirmation and the six-month lapse differ by form?

Viewing the four forms across "is the family court's confirmation needed," "by when must it be requested," and "does it lapse at six months" gives the whole picture of how they are used. A will in imminent danger does not take effect on being made and takes effect only on obtaining the family court's "confirmation of the will." A general imminent-danger will, within 20 days of the day of the will, and a shipwreck imminent-danger will, without delay, do not take effect unless one of the witnesses or an interested party requests the family court's confirmation (Civil Code Articles 976(4), 979(3)). The family court cannot confirm unless it is satisfied that the will issued from the testator's true intention (Civil Code Article 976(5), applied by Article 979(4)). This "confirmation of a will" is an adjudication case listed in Appended Table I of the Domestic Relations Case Procedure Act (item 102, "confirmation of a will"; its objects are Civil Code Article 976(4) and Article 979(3)) — a procedure separate from "probate" (Civil Code Article 1004), which prevents forgery.

| Form | Family court's confirmation | Time to request | Lapse at six months (Art. 983) |
|---|---|---|---|
| General imminent-danger (Art. 976) | Needed | Within 20 days of the day of the will | Applies |
| Shipwreck imminent-danger (Art. 979) | Needed | Without delay | Applies |
| Quarantine will (Art. 977) | Not needed | — | Applies |
| On-board will (Art. 978) | Not needed | — | Applies |

Exactly how to proceed with the general imminent-danger will's 20-day confirmation (who petitions, what is examined) is covered in detail in [The will of a person in imminent danger of death and the family court's confirmation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kikyuji-yuigon-shibo-kikyusha-minpo-976jo-kakunin). Confirmation looks at "whether it is the true intention" and does not finally guarantee that the will's content is valid. And common to the four forms, a will does not take effect if the testator survives for six months from the time they become able to make a will in an ordinary form (Civil Code Article 983). Once health recovers, or the isolating circumstance is resolved, so that a holographic or notarial will can be made, and six months pass in that state, the earlier special-form will lapses. So, once the situation is escaped, it is safer to redo the will in a reliable form such as a notarial will.

## Whom should you ask for a special-form will?

A special-form will divides the professionals who can help between drafting and the family-court procedure. Note the demarcation here: a request to the family court for confirmation is a petition to a court (an adjudication procedure), and preparing and acting on documents submitted to a court is outside an administrative scrivener's scope (Administrative Scriveners Act Articles 1-2, 1-3). This part is done by the person or an attorney. What an administrative scrivener can handle is support for drafting the will (organising the oral statement or the orally stated tenor), arranging witnesses, and confirming requirements.

## FAQ

**Q. How does a shipwreck imminent-danger will (Art. 979) differ from a general imminent-danger will (Art. 976)?**
A. A general imminent-danger will (Civil Code Article 976) is made by a person in imminent danger of death from illness etc. before three or more witnesses, by orally stating the tenor to one, and confirmation of the family court is obtained within 20 days of the day of the will. A shipwreck imminent-danger will (Civil Code Article 979) is made orally before two or more witnesses aboard a wrecked ship; a witness writes it down, signs and seals it, and confirmation of the family court is obtained without delay. The number of witnesses, the way of conveying, and the time to request confirmation differ. For the detailed steps of Article 976's witnesses, oral statement, and writing, see [The will of a person in imminent danger of death and the family court's confirmation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kikyuji-yuigon-shibo-kikyusha-minpo-976jo-kakunin).

**Q. Do wills in isolated places (quarantine / on-board) also need the family court's confirmation?**
A. No. The family court's confirmation is required for wills in imminent danger (Civil Code Articles 976, 979). The quarantine will (Article 977) is made before one police officer and one or more witnesses, and the on-board will (Article 978) before one captain or clerk and two or more witnesses, producing a will document; the family-court confirmation of the imminent-danger wills is not provided for them. Still, all lapse if the testator survives six months after an ordinary form becomes possible (Article 983).

**Q. What happens to a special-form will if my health recovers?**
A. If the testator survives for six months from the time they become able to make a will in an ordinary form (holographic, notarial, etc.), the special-form will loses effect (Civil Code Article 983). This is common to all four forms, because it is strictly an emergency/isolation means. Once the situation is escaped, it is safer to redo the will in a reliable form such as a notarial will.

**Q. Can I ask an administrative scrivener for the confirmation petition?**
A. The petition to the family court for confirmation amounts to preparing and acting on documents submitted to a court and is outside an administrative scrivener's scope (Administrative Scriveners Act Articles 1-2, 1-3). This part is done by the person or by an attorney. What an administrative scrivener can handle is support for drafting the will, arranging witnesses, and confirming requirements, each as an independent business under a separate contract.
