**In short:** a "low-light" restaurant (a dimly lit bar, etc.) kept at 10 lux or below, or a restaurant with narrow, hard-to-see-into seats (booth or semi-private partitioned seats), counts as an amusement business even without hostess-style service, and you need a licence from the prefectural public safety commission for each premises. The basis is the Act on Control and Improvement of Amusement Business (the Entertainment Business Act): a low-light restaurant is Article 2, paragraph 1, item 2, and a partitioned-seat restaurant is Article 2, paragraph 1, item 3, with the licence under Article 3, paragraph 1. Unlike a notification, a licence is granted only when you satisfy all of: the location requirement (land-use zoning and distance from protected facilities, i.e. restricted areas), the structural requirement (illumination, sight lines, guest seats) and the personal requirement (the grounds for disqualification in Article 4, paragraph 1). If you do not confirm before renting that the location and structure meet the requirements, you may find after signing the lease that the licence cannot be granted. This article is general information organising the type-2 and type-3 requirements and the flow of the application; it does not judge the outcome of any individual licence.

## What are the type-2 and type-3 businesses under the Act?

Even a restaurant that does not provide hostess-style service is regulated as an "amusement business" if the interior is kept dark or the seats are made hard to see into. Two categories apply.

- **Low-light restaurant (type 2):** Article 2, paragraph 1, item 2 defines "a café, bar or other business, with equipment provided, that serves food and drink to customers and operates with the illumination inside the premises, measured as prescribed by National Public Safety Commission rules, at 10 lux or below." A bar operating at 10 lux or below counts, even without entertainment.
- **Partitioned-seat restaurant (type 3):** Article 2, paragraph 1, item 3 defines "a café, bar or other business, with equipment provided, that serves food and drink and operates with seating that is hard to see into from outside and that is no more than 5 square metres in area." A shop with hard-to-see-into seats of 5 square metres or less (booth or semi-private) counts.

Both fall within the "entertaining food-and-drink business" in Article 2, paragraph 4, and under Article 3, paragraph 1 you must obtain the licence of the prefectural public safety commission with jurisdiction over the premises, for each premises (the so-called type-2 or type-3 licence).

| Category | Example of applicable shop | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low-light restaurant (type 2) | A dim bar at 10 lux or below | Act Art. 2(1)(ii), Art. 3(1) |
| Partitioned-seat restaurant (type 3) | A shop with hard-to-see-into seats of 5 m² or less | Act Art. 2(1)(iii), Art. 3(1) |

## How are they distinguished from type 1 (hostess service) and the specified-amusement restaurant?

These are often confused, but the required procedure depends on the content of the business.

| Business content | Required procedure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entertaining customers and serving food (snack bar, club, etc.) | Amusement business (type 1) licence | Act Art. 2(1)(i), Art. 3(1) |
| No entertainment; low light at 10 lux or below | Amusement business (type 2) licence | Act Art. 2(1)(ii), Art. 3(1) |
| No entertainment; hard-to-see seats of 5 m² or less | Amusement business (type 3) licence | Act Art. 2(1)(iii), Art. 3(1) |
| Letting customers enjoy amusement and serving alcohol late at night (after midnight) | Specified-amusement restaurant business licence | Act Art. 31-22 |
| Serving alcohol as the main offering late at night (no entertainment, amusement, low light or partitioning) | Late-night alcohol-serving restaurant business notification | Act Art. 33(1) |

If there is "entertainment" (Article 2, paragraph 3: "treating customers in a way that creates a mood of pleasure and amusement"), it is type 1; without it, illumination or seat structure can make it type 2 or type 3. Letting customers enjoy amusement and serving alcohol late at night needs the [Specified-amusement restaurant business licence](https://luck428.com/legal/column/tokutei-yukyo-inshokuten-eigyo-kyoka-youken); merely serving alcohol late at night with no entertainment, amusement, low light or partitioning needs only the notification in [Serving alcohol after midnight: what notification do you need?](https://luck428.com/legal/column/shinya-shurui-teikyo-todokede-yoken). Note that type 2 and type 3 are amusement businesses, so under Article 13, paragraph 1 they may not, in principle, operate late at night (from midnight to 6 a.m.); there is an exception, limited to areas and days set by prefectural ordinance, to operate until a time set by ordinance. If you plan "a low-light bar open late," a type-2 licence can run into the operating-hours limit, so decide early which category you will open under. For the case with hostess-style service, see [A restaurant with hostess-style service (Entertainment Business Act, type 1)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/fuzoku-eigyo-1go-settai-inshoku-kyoka-bukken-kyori).

## How far must you meet the structural requirements of illumination, sight lines and seating?

The structural requirement is the "technical standard" under Article 4, paragraph 2, item 1, made concrete by National Public Safety Commission rules (Article 7 of the Enforcement Regulation) and prefectural ordinances. The typical thinking for types 2 and 3 is as follows.

| Item | Approach to the standard |
| --- | --- |
| Illumination inside the premises | A type-2 business must have structure and equipment that keep illumination from falling to 5 lux or below (in practice, operating roughly in the 5–10 lux band). A type-3 business must keep it from falling to 10 lux or below |
| Sight lines | A structure in which the guest room cannot easily be seen from outside the premises. A type-2 business must also not place equipment that blocks the line of sight inside the guest room |
| Prohibited equipment | No equipment liable to harm public morals (equipment specified by the Enforcement Order) |
| Guest-room floor area, partitions, equipment height | Set by the Enforcement Regulation and prefectural ordinances; figures differ by ordinance |

For a partitioned-seat (type-3) business, the seat size (5 square metres or less) is itself the defining element, but the floor area of each guest room and the treatment of the height of seat partitions differ by prefectural ordinance. If the drawings (area-calculation drawing and floor plan) and the actual state differ, you will be flagged in the on-site inspection, so it is safer to build the ordinance and police standards for the location into the interior design stage. Preparing the area-calculation drawing and floor plan and the interior design are the field of architects, surveyors and interior contractors, with whom you contract separately as an independent business from our office.

## Where can the property be (land-use zone and distance from protected facilities)?

Even for types 2 and 3, the "location requirement" is what most affects property choice. Article 4, paragraph 2, item 2 provides that a licence will not be granted in areas designated by prefectural ordinance (restricted areas) in accordance with standards set by cabinet order to preserve a good public-morals environment. That cabinet order is Article 6 of the Enforcement Order, which sets a framework allowing ordinances to designate as restricted areas (1) areas where many dwellings are concentrated (residential-cluster areas) and (2) the areas around protected facilities designated by prefectural ordinance, such as schools and hospitals, with the area around a protected facility limited to roughly a 100-metre zone around the site.

Which facilities count as protected facilities, and how many metres the distance is, differ by prefectural ordinance. Even if the property is in a commercial zone, a nearby protected facility such as a school or hospital can make the licence unobtainable due to the distance restriction. Land-use zones are fixed by city planning under the Building Standards Act and change block by block even along the same street. Before deciding on a property, always check both the land-use zone and the distance from protected facilities against the ordinance for the property's location and the guidance of the police station with jurisdiction. This article refrains from giving specific distance figures as a particular municipality's example (they can change with ordinance amendments, so checking the latest ordinance directly is the reliable course).

## What are the risks of unlicensed operation and lending your name?

Operating a type-2 or type-3 business without a licence can be subject, as a breach of Article 3, paragraph 1, to the penalty in Article 49, item 1 (imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of up to 10 million yen, or both). Imprisonment without distinction came into force on 1 June 2025 through amendment of the Penal Code, unifying the former imprisonment with and without work.

Also, "lending your name" — a licensee letting another person run an amusement business under the licensee's own name — is prohibited by Article 11, and a breach is subject to the penalty in Article 49, item 3. There is a dual-liability provision (Article 57) under which, if a company's representative or employee commits a violation in connection with the business, the company as well as the actor can be fined. Running the business yourself after getting a licence "in an acquaintance's name" can amount to lending your name. Unlicensed operation and lending your name are not only criminally punishable but can also affect future licensing as a ground for disqualification (Article 4, paragraph 1), so getting a proper licence from the start is in the end the shortest route. Whether something actually applies, and the check on grounds for disqualification, are made through the applicant's own declaration and a police investigation; confirming a criminal record or history is within the jurisdiction of the applicant and the police.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a licence even without providing hostess-style service?**
A. Yes. Even without entertainment, a low-light restaurant keeping illumination at 10 lux or below (Article 2, paragraph 1, item 2) or a partitioned-seat restaurant with hard-to-see-into seats of 5 square metres or less (Article 2, paragraph 1, item 3) counts as an amusement business and needs the licence of the prefectural public safety commission under Article 3, paragraph 1. It is judged by whether the illumination or seat structure touches the standards.

**Q. Can a low-light bar operate late at night?**
A. A type-2 business is an amusement business, so under Article 13, paragraph 1 it may not, in principle, operate late at night (from midnight to 6 a.m.). There is an exception, limited to areas and days set by prefectural ordinance, to operate until a time set by ordinance, but it depends on the area. An ordinary restaurant whose main offering is serving alcohol late at night, with no entertainment and no low light, needs only the late-night alcohol-serving restaurant business notification under Article 33, paragraph 1. Because the operating-hours treatment changes with the category, checking before opening is essential.

**Q. If the property is in a commercial zone, is the distance restriction irrelevant?**
A. No. Even in a commercial zone, a nearby protected facility such as a school or hospital designated by prefectural ordinance can make the licence unobtainable under the restricted-area distance limit. Article 6 of the Enforcement Order sets a limit of roughly 100 metres around a protected facility, and the specific facilities and distances are set by ordinance. Check the ordinance for the property's location and the police station with jurisdiction before signing the lease.

**Q. May I obtain the licence in an acquaintance's name?**
A. Lending your name is prohibited by Article 11, and a breach is subject to the penalty in Article 49, item 3 (the main clause of Article 49 provides imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of up to 10 million yen, or both). There is also a dual-liability provision for companies (Article 57). As a rule, the person who actually runs the business should obtain the licence. Licensing and whether grounds for disqualification (Article 4, paragraph 1) apply are confirmed by a qualified professional after a meeting.
