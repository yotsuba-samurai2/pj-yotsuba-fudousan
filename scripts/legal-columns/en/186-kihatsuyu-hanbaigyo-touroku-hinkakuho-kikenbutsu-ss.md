**In short:** opening a gasoline stand (service station) stacks several licences. A business that sells gasoline (volatile oil) must obtain registration by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry under the Act on Securing the Quality of Gasoline and Other Fuels (the Fuel Quality Act), Article 3. In addition, because gasoline is a hazardous material (Class 4), setting up a service station requires a setup permit for a hazardous-materials facility under the Fire Service Act (Article 11 of the Fire Service Act), and the permit is, as a rule, granted by the municipal mayor and the like. At the facility you appoint a hazardous-materials safety supervisor (Article 13 of the Fire Service Act), and the handling of hazardous materials is done according to the qualification of a hazardous-materials engineer (Class A, Class B, Class C) (Article 13-2). This article is general information organising the framework; it does not make specific legal judgements such as whether a particular registration or permit will be granted.

## What licences overlap when opening a gasoline stand?

Opening a service station stacks procedures with different governing laws: "registration on the side that sells the fuel (gasoline)" and "a permit for the facility that handles hazardous materials". Organised, it is as follows.

| Procedure | Governing law | Main counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration as a gasoline sales business | Article 3 of the Fuel Quality Act | Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry (regional bureau) |
| Setup permit for a hazardous-materials facility (service station) | Article 11 of the Fire Service Act | Municipal mayor and the like (fire headquarters / fire station) |
| Appointment of a hazardous-materials safety supervisor | Article 13 of the Fire Service Act | Notification to the municipal mayor and the like |
| Qualification of a hazardous-materials engineer | Article 13-2 of the Fire Service Act | Prefectural governor (licence) |

Besides these, work such as installing underground tanks and leak inspection, acquiring the land and checking the zoning, formation registration, and tax proceed in parallel. Because you cannot open by arranging only one of them, the point is to grasp the whole picture first and separate the order and the party in charge of each procedure. Which procedures are actually needed changes with the oil types handled and the form of the facility, and the final application is judged by each counter after its review.

## Who obtains the gasoline sales-business registration (Fuel Quality Act), and where?

A person who intends to conduct a gasoline sales business must obtain registration by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry (Act on Securing the Quality of Gasoline and Other Fuels = the Fuel Quality Act, Article 3). This is a system to secure the quality of the gasoline supplied to consumers, and a registered gasoline sales business bears duties concerning quality.

The Fuel Quality Act provides for the appointment and duties of a quality manager by the gasoline sales business (Articles 14 and 15), and provides a duty of analysis to confirm the quality of the gasoline (Article 16). The analysis may be done by holding one's own analysis equipment, or may be entrusted to a registered analysis organisation. Note that the "registration of a gasoline specified-processing business" provided in Article 12-2 and following of the Fuel Quality Act is a different system for those who process (blend and the like) gasoline, and is distinguished from the gasoline sales-business registration (Article 3) that an ordinary service station obtains.

| Role | Basis | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration as a gasoline sales business | Article 3 of the Fuel Quality Act | Obtain registration by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry |
| Appointment and duties of a quality manager | Articles 14 and 15 of the Fuel Quality Act | Place a manager for securing quality |
| Duty of analysis (quality confirmation) | Article 16 of the Fuel Quality Act | Analyse with one's own equipment or a registered analysis organisation |

The validity period and renewal of the registration, and the handling of signs and displays, may be amended. Confirm the specifics—the registration counter (the regional Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry with jurisdiction), the forms, and the frequency of analysis—with the latest guide of the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy and the regional bureau (accessed 2026-11-22). This article does not assert unverified figures (such as the number of years of the validity period).

## Where do you apply for the setup permit for the hazardous-materials facility (service station)?

Gasoline is a hazardous material under the Fire Service Act (a flammable liquid of Class 4), and a service station that handles it is one kind of "handling facility" for hazardous materials (classified by the Cabinet Order on the Regulation of Hazardous Materials). A person who intends to set up a manufacturing facility, storage facility, or handling facility must obtain the permit of the municipal mayor and the like for its location, structure, and equipment (Article 11 of the Fire Service Act). The permit is granted by the mayor of a municipality that has fire headquarters and a fire station for that area, and by the prefectural governor for other areas.

The technical standards for the location, structure, and equipment of a service station (frontage and depth, fire walls, underground tanks, the layout of fixed dispensing equipment, and so on) are set in detail by the Cabinet Order and the regulations on the regulation of hazardous materials. Changing the location, structure, or equipment after setup also requires a permit as a rule. Before and after the permit, you go through pre-construction procedures (various notifications under the fire-service laws) and a completion inspection after completion (Article 11, paragraph 5 of the Fire Service Act) before starting use. The suitability of specific standards and the review of drawings are judged by the fire headquarters (prevention division) with jurisdiction.

## How many hazardous-materials engineers / safety supervisors, and which qualifications, are required?

At a manufacturing facility and the like set by the Cabinet Order, such as a service station, you must appoint a hazardous-materials safety supervisor (Article 13 of the Fire Service Act). The hazardous-materials safety supervisor is appointed from among those who are a Class A or Class B hazardous-materials engineer with practical experience of handling hazardous materials for six months or more, and when you appoint or dismiss one you notify the municipal mayor and the like. A service station is treated as a facility that requires the appointment of a hazardous-materials safety supervisor regardless of the quantity handled.

The hazardous-materials engineer licence comes in three kinds—Class A, Class B, and Class C—and is granted by the prefectural governor to those who pass the hazardous-materials engineer examination (Article 13-2 of the Fire Service Act). Class A can handle hazardous materials of all classes, Class B can handle the class designated on the licence (gasoline is Class 4), and Class C can handle designated hazardous materials such as gasoline, kerosene, diesel oil, and third petroleum class. For a person other than a hazardous-materials engineer to handle hazardous materials, the attendance of a Class A or Class B hazardous-materials engineer is required (Class C cannot attend). Because the number and placement of qualified persons change with the facility's operating structure, confirm with the fire headquarters with jurisdiction.

| Category | Basis | Point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hazardous-materials safety supervisor | Article 13 of the Fire Service Act | Appointed from Class A or B (six months or more of experience); notified |
| Hazardous-materials engineer licence | Article 13-2 of the Fire Service Act | Class A, B, C. Granted by the prefectural governor |
| Handling by an unqualified person | Article 13-2 of the Fire Service Act, etc. | Attendance of Class A or B is required (Class C cannot attend) |

## In business succession (inheritance / transfer), can the registration or permit be taken over?

When you succeed to an existing service station as a business, the Fuel Quality Act registration and the Fire Service Act permit each have their succession decided under a different system. Under the Fire Service Act, a person who has been transferred or had delivered to them a manufacturing facility and the like for which a permit was granted succeeds to the position of the setter and must notify the municipal mayor and the like without delay (Article 11, paragraph 6 of the Fire Service Act). For the handling of succession on grounds other than transfer or delivery, such as inheritance, merger, or split, confirm with the fire headquarters with jurisdiction (this article does not assert all types of succession and leaves the unverified parts to confirmation with the counter).

For the gasoline sales-business registration under the Fuel Quality Act too, provisions on succession to the position accompanying inheritance, merger, split, or business transfer are placed in Section 1 (Articles 3 to 12 of the Fuel Quality Act), and a person who has succeeded makes the prescribed notification. The scope of what can be succeeded and the documents required change with whether it is a transfer or an inheritance, and whether it is an individual or a corporation. Because whether you can take over the registration or permit or must obtain them anew differs with the form of succession and the handling of each counter, we recommend confirming with the regional Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry and the fire headquarters with jurisdiction at the planning stage of the succession.

## FAQ

**Q. What licences are needed to open a gasoline stand?**
A. There are broadly two lines. On the side that sells the fuel, the gasoline sales business obtains registration by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry (Article 3 of the Fuel Quality Act). On the side of the facility handling hazardous materials, setting up a service station requires a setup permit for a hazardous-materials facility under the Fire Service Act (Article 11), and at the facility you appoint a hazardous-materials safety supervisor (Article 13). Besides these, underground-tank work, checking the land and zoning, formation registration, and tax proceed in parallel. Because the procedures actually needed change with the oil types and the form of the facility, confirm with each counter.

**Q. Where do you submit the gasoline sales-business registration?**
A. A person who intends to conduct a gasoline sales business obtains registration by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry (Article 3 of the Fuel Quality Act). A registered gasoline sales business bears quality-securing duties such as the appointment of a quality manager (Articles 14 and 15) and the analysis of gasoline (Article 16). Confirm the counter (the regional Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry with jurisdiction), the forms, and the frequency of analysis with the guide of the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy and the regional bureau. Note that the gasoline specified-processing business registration in Article 12-2 and following is a separate system.

**Q. Which qualification of hazardous-materials engineer is required?**
A. The licence comes in three kinds—Class A, Class B, and Class C—granted by the prefectural governor to those who pass the examination (Article 13-2 of the Fire Service Act). Gasoline is a Class 4 hazardous material; Class A can handle all classes, Class B the designated classes including Class 4, and Class C designated hazardous materials such as gasoline, kerosene, and diesel oil. At a service station, you appoint a hazardous-materials safety supervisor from among those who are Class A or B with six months or more of practical experience (Article 13). Because the number and placement needed change with the operating structure, confirm with the fire headquarters with jurisdiction.

**Q. When taking over an existing service station, can the permit be used as it is?**
A. Under the Fire Service Act, a person who has been transferred or had delivered to them a manufacturing facility and the like for which a permit was granted succeeds to the position of the setter and notifies the municipal mayor and the like without delay (Article 11, paragraph 6 of the Fire Service Act). The gasoline sales-business registration under the Fuel Quality Act also has provisions on succession to the position accompanying inheritance, merger, split, or business transfer in Section 1. Because whether it can be succeeded or must be obtained anew differs with the form of succession and the handling of each counter, confirm with the regional Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry and the fire headquarters with jurisdiction at the planning stage.
