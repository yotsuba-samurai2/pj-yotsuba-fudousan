**In short:** a "place that performs sample testing as a business" — testing blood, pathology, genetic samples and the like on referral — generally needs **clinical-laboratory (eisei-kensajo) registration**. A person opening a clinical laboratory must register with the **prefectural governor** for its location (the mayor in a city with a public health centre, the ward head in a special ward) (Clinical Laboratory Technicians Act, Article 20-3(1)). The registering authority must not grant registration where the structure and equipment, the management organisation, the method of **ensuring accuracy** of sample testing and other matters fail to meet the standards set by Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare ordinance (Enforcement Regulations Article 12, etc.) (Article 20-3(2)). What must be met are: structure and equipment such as separating the testing room, its floor area and the testing machines and instruments; staffing such as an administrator, an accuracy-control manager and the heads of each testing department; and an accuracy-control framework such as internal accuracy control, taking part in external accuracy-control surveys, and keeping standard operating documents and work logs. The details, forms and standard processing period (two months in Mie Prefecture's example) differ by local government. This article is general information; the final judgement on whether registration is needed and on meeting the standards rests with each authority's (prefecture, public-health-centre city, special ward) screening and a qualified professional. ## What is a clinical laboratory, and how does it differ from a clinic's testing room or a hospital's testing department?

A clinical laboratory is a **place that performs sample testing as a business**, excluding hospitals, clinics, birth centres and places inside facilities designated by the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Clinical Laboratory Technicians Act, Article 20-3(1), parenthetical). Sample testing examines "samples" taken from the human body — blood, urine, tissue — and is distinguished from physiological tests (electrocardiogram, EEG) that examine the body directly.

The point is "for whom and where." A testing room where a hospital or clinic examines its own patients' samples within itself is treated as part of that hospital or clinic under the Medical Care Act and the like, and falls outside clinical-laboratory registration. By contrast, a testing centre that **receives sample testing on referral** from other medical institutions and performs it at an independent facility needs clinical-laboratory registration.

| Category | Nature | Clinical-laboratory registration |
|---|---|---|
| A testing room inside a hospital/clinic/birth centre | Part of that medical institution | Out of scope (handled under the Medical Care Act, etc.) |
| An independent facility receiving sample testing | A place performing sample testing as a business | Generally needed (Article 20-3) |
| Only physiological tests that examine the body directly | Not sample testing | Not a target for registration as sample testing |

Because the entry point splits between self-contained and referral testing, sorting out "which tests, on whose samples, and where" is the starting point. The final judgement on applicability rests with the screening of the prefecture, public-health-centre city or special ward of the location.

## Where do you apply, and what structure and equipment standards must you meet?

The registering authority is the **prefectural governor** for the clinical laboratory's location. However, where the location is in a city with a public health centre or a special ward, the mayor or ward head is the authority (Article 20-3(1)). The actual window is the medical-affairs section or public health centre of the prefecture, city or ward.

Registration standards require that the structure and equipment, the management organisation, the method of ensuring the accuracy of sample testing and other matters meet the standards set by ordinance (Article 20-3(2)); the details are in Article 12, etc., of the Enforcement Regulations of the Clinical Laboratory Technicians Act. As to structure and equipment, the testing room must be separated from other parts, the necessary testing machines and instruments must be held for each type of test performed, and the testing room's floor area must meet the standard (the area is set in the Regulations' appended tables by type of test).

| Item | Content (outline) |
|---|---|
| Registered with | Prefectural governor of the location (mayor in a public-health-centre city, ward head in a special ward) |
| Basis | Article 20-3(1)(2), Enforcement Regulations Article 12, etc. |
| Structure/equipment | Separation of the testing room, machines by type of test, testing-room area (appended table) |
| Management organisation | Administrator, accuracy-control manager, department heads, etc. |
| Ensuring accuracy | Internal accuracy control, external accuracy-control survey, standard operating documents and work logs |

Sample testing is generally classified into microbiological, immunological, haematological, pathological, biochemical, urine/faeces general, and gene-related/chromosome testing, among others, and the required machines and area change with the category performed. As the details, appended tables and forms are amended from time to time, confirm the type of application and the area/equipment standards directly in the location's local-government registration standards and guide just before applying.

## What are the staffing requirements (administrator, accuracy-control manager, department heads)?

A clinical laboratory must place certain qualified persons as its management organisation. Central to this are the **administrator** and the **accuracy-control manager**. The administrator must, in principle, be a physician or a clinical laboratory technician, and in certain cases is limited to a clinical laboratory technician meeting standards set by the Minister.

The accuracy-control manager is placed to ensure the accuracy of sample testing; local-government registration standards indicate requirements such as being a physician or a clinical laboratory technician (handled to include a sanitary laboratory technician), having a number of years of practical experience in testing work (a practice treating six or more years as an example) and some years of accuracy-control experience (three or more years as an example). A common view is that in a clinical laboratory, **the administrator and the accuracy-control manager may not be the same person**.

| Role | Likely qualification/requirement (local-government practice) |
|---|---|
| Administrator | Physician or clinical laboratory technician (in some cases a technician meeting the Minister's standards) |
| Accuracy-control manager | Physician or clinical laboratory technician, etc.; testing experience plus accuracy-control experience; not concurrent with the administrator |
| Head of each testing department | Placed for each test category performed; qualification/number depends on the test type |
| Physicians/clinical laboratory technicians | A necessary number placed according to the classification of sample testing performed |

The years of experience and the handling of full-time/part-time status (e.g. an example allowing a part-time accuracy-control manager who attends at least one day a week where testing volume is small) vary in the local-government standards. Confirm the concrete staffing requirements directly in the location's standards just before applying. Securing and employing the qualified persons themselves is not the administrative scrivener's work but the part done by the opener (the person, a medical corporation, etc.).

## What is required for accuracy control (internal and external), work logs and standard operating documents?

Under the 2017 amendment to the Medical Care Act and the Clinical Laboratory Technicians Act, matters on **ensuring the accuracy of sample testing** were positioned as statutory registration standards, enforced on 1 December 2018 — this is the general view. With it, a framework was set to place an accuracy-control manager and to treat the method of ensuring accuracy as a registered/filed matter. The details are left to cabinet/ministerial orders, notifications and guidance (such as the clinical-laboratory guidance guidelines).

The core of the required accuracy control is: carrying out internal accuracy control (managing within the facility whether daily testing is done correctly), taking part in external accuracy-control surveys (joining a survey by an outside body to verify the facility's accuracy), and maintaining standard operating documents (documents setting out procedures for sample receipt, transport and storage and for each test), work logs and registers. These also become items checked in an on-site inspection by the prefectural governor and the like.

| Pillar of accuracy control | Content (outline) |
|---|---|
| Internal accuracy control | Manage testing accuracy routinely within the facility |
| Taking external accuracy-control surveys | Join an outside body's survey to verify accuracy |
| Standard operating documents | Documents setting procedures for sample receipt/transport/storage and each test |
| Work logs/registers | Keep records of daily testing and management |

Procedural rules are also set, such as a filing being needed when the method of ensuring accuracy changes. As the details and forms are amended from time to time, confirm how to prepare the documents just before applying, against the local-government guide and the latest notifications. Building the accuracy-control framework itself is borne by qualified persons, while the administrative scrivener handles preparing and submitting the application documents.

## What is the standard flow to registration, and the change, renewal and discontinuance filings?

Registration generally proceeds in the order of prior consultation → facility preparation → preparing and submitting the application → document review and on-site inspection → registration. The standard processing period differs by local government; Mie Prefecture's example gives two months. The particulars, including any term or renewal of registration, follow the local-government guidance.

As to post-registration changes, a change registration must be obtained to change the content of the sample-testing business (Article 20-4(1)); and when there is a change to the name, structure and equipment, management organisation or method of ensuring accuracy, or when the clinical laboratory is discontinued or suspended or a suspended facility is resumed, a filing must be made **within 30 days** to the prefectural governor and the like of the location (Article 20-4(3)).

| Procedure | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| New registration | Apply and be registered before opening | Article 20-3(1) |
| Change registration | Change to the content of the sample-testing business | Article 20-4(1) |
| Change filing | Change to name, structure/equipment, management organisation, accuracy method, etc. | Article 20-4(3) |
| Discontinuance/suspension/resumption filing | File within 30 days | Article 20-4(3) |

Running the sample-testing business without registration, or failing to correct non-conformity with the standards, carries rules such as cancellation of registration and suspension of business (Article 20-7). Confirm the forms, attachments, operation of screening and standard processing period directly on the location's local-government official page just before writing.

## FAQ

**Q. Does a testing room inside a hospital also need clinical-laboratory registration?**
A. A clinical laboratory is a "place that performs sample testing as a business," and hospitals, clinics, birth centres and the like are excluded (Clinical Laboratory Technicians Act, Article 20-3(1)). A testing room that examines a hospital's own patients' samples within the hospital is treated as part of that medical institution and is out of scope. Registration is needed where sample testing is received on referral from other medical institutions and performed at an independent facility. Applicability rests with the location's local-government screening.

**Q. Where do you apply for registration?**
A. You apply to the prefectural governor for the clinical laboratory's location. However, if the location is in a city with a public health centre it is the mayor, and in a special ward the ward head (Article 20-3(1)). The actual window is the medical-affairs section or public health centre of the prefecture, city or ward. The standard processing period differs by local government; Mie Prefecture's example gives two months. Confirm the application forms and attachments in the local-government guide.

**Q. Can the administrator and the accuracy-control manager be the same person?**
A. In a clinical laboratory, the common view is that the administrator and the accuracy-control manager may not be concurrently held. The administrator is in principle a physician or a clinical laboratory technician, and the accuracy-control manager a physician or a clinical laboratory technician, etc., with practical experience in testing and accuracy control required. As the qualifications, years of experience and full-time/part-time handling vary in the local-government standards, confirm them in the location's standards just before applying.

**Q. What should you do if you change the name or equipment after registration?**
A. A change registration is needed to change the content of the sample-testing business (Article 20-4(1)). When there is a change to the name, structure/equipment, management organisation or method of ensuring accuracy, or a discontinuance, suspension or resumption, file within 30 days with the prefectural governor and the like of the location (Article 20-4(3)). There are also rules for correction where a filing is neglected, and for cancellation of registration and suspension of business where the standards are no longer met (Article 20-7).
