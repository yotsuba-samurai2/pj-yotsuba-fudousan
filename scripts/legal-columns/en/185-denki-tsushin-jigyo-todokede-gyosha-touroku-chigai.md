**In short:** when you launch a web service, app, ISP, or MVNO, if you operate a "telecommunications business" under the Telecommunications Business Act, you need either registration by the Minister for Internal Affairs and Communications (Article 9 of the Telecommunications Business Act) or a notification (Article 16). Which one applies turns on whether you yourself install telecommunications line facilities exceeding a certain scale: as a rule, a large-scale business that installs such facilities is registration, and other businesses subject to notification are notification. A "telecommunications service" means intermediating others' communications using telecommunications facilities and the like (Article 2, item 3), and a service that neither intermediates others' communications nor installs line facilities (a so-called category-3 business) is excluded and needs neither registration nor notification (Article 164). After opening, watch obligations such as the external transmission rules (Article 27-12, in force in 2023). This article is general information organising the framework; it does not make specific legal judgements on whether a particular service falls under notification or registration.

## Is my service a "telecommunications business"?

The starting point is whether your service is a "telecommunications business" under the Telecommunications Business Act. Article 2 of the Act defines a telecommunications service as "intermediating others' communications using telecommunications facilities, or otherwise providing telecommunications facilities for the communications of others" (item 3), and a telecommunications business as "a business that provides a telecommunications service to meet the demand of others" (item 4; a business for broadcasting-station facility supply services is excluded). A telecommunications carrier means a person who has obtained registration under Article 9 and a person who has made a notification under Article 16, paragraph 1 (item 5).

Conversely, providing something for your own demand—such as operating your own website or running an in-house system for your own use—does not amount to provision "to meet the demand of others" and is treated as not falling under a telecommunications business (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), "Entry Manual for the Telecommunications Business [Supplement]", accessed 2026-11-22). First separating "is it for others?", "does it intermediate others' communications?", and "does it install line facilities?" is the entrance to judging whether a procedure is required.

| The entrance question | Where it leads |
| --- | --- |
| Do you provide a service to meet the demand of others? | If not, it is not a telecommunications business |
| Do you intermediate others' communications / install line facilities? | If neither, it is excluded (category-3 business, Article 164) |
| Do you install line facilities exceeding a certain scale? | If yes, registration (Article 9); otherwise notification (Article 16) |

Which box your service finally falls into depends on the content of the service, the range of users, and the configuration of the facilities. The application is judged by the MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications) after its review.

## What is the difference between a business that only needs notification and one that requires registration?

A person who intends to operate a telecommunications business must, as a rule, obtain registration from the Minister for Internal Affairs and Communications (Article 9 of the Telecommunications Business Act). However, a person whose telecommunications line facilities and the range of the area in which they are installed do not exceed the standards set by MIC ordinance, and the like, is subject to notification rather than registration. Excluding those who must obtain registration, a person who intends to operate a telecommunications business notifies the Minister (Article 16, paragraph 1).

Broadly, a business that itself installs line facilities exceeding a certain standard—such as nationwide transmission-line facilities—is registration, and other businesses subject to notification are notification. This standard (the line between the installation areas of subscriber-line facilities and relay-line facilities, and so on) is set in detail by the Telecommunications Business Act Enforcement Regulations and the like, and the figures and area judgements may be amended. Confirm which one your company falls under with the MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications) and the Entry Manual once your facility configuration is fixed. This article does not assert the specific numerical thresholds for installation areas and leaves them to the latest regulations and MIC guidance (we do not write unverified figures).

| Category | Basis | Rough target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration | Article 9 of the Telecommunications Business Act | A business that itself installs line facilities exceeding a certain scale |
| Notification | Article 16, paragraph 1 | A telecommunications business excluding those who must register |
| Exclusion | Article 164 | A service that neither intermediates others' communications nor installs line facilities (category-3 business) |

## From what point does it "intermediate others' communications"?

To "intermediate others' communications" means to relay and establish communications between third parties. Fixed-line telephony, mobile telephony, the sending and receiving of e-mail, and internet access services are organised as examples that intermediate others' communications (MIC, "Entry Manual for the Telecommunications Business [Supplement]", accessed 2026-11-22).

On the other hand, services where you yourself are the sender or recipient of information—such as online search services, online provision of information, and SNS in forms that do not intermediate communications between users—are cited as examples that do not intermediate others' communications. A telecommunications service that neither intermediates others' communications nor installs telecommunications line facilities (a so-called category-3 business) is, under Article 164 of the Telecommunications Business Act, outside much of the Act and is treated as needing neither registration nor notification. Still, whether a particular service amounts to intermediation changes with the design of the function: even the same "messaging feature" may amount to intermediation if it is designed to relay exchanges between third parties. For the final judgement of applicability, it is safest to seek confirmation from the MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications).

## What documents are needed for notification/registration and how does the procedure with the MIC proceed?

For both notification and registration, the counter is, as a rule, the MIC's Regional Bureau of Telecommunications with jurisdiction over the location (for part of Tokyo, the Kanto Bureau of Telecommunications, and so on). For a notification, you submit a telecommunications business notification form stating the type of telecommunications service provided, the service area, and the outline of the telecommunications facilities, with the documents set by the Enforcement Regulations attached. For a registration, you state the outline of the telecommunications line facilities you will install in a registration application, and registration follows a review.

| Procedure | Basis | Main counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notification | Article 16, paragraph 1 | MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications) |
| Registration | Article 9 | MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications) |
| Change / abolition | Enforcement Regulations, etc. | Change / abolition notification to the MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications) |

The matters stated, the attached documents, and the handling of the submission counter change with amendments and form changes. Immediately before you submit, confirm directly with the MIC's "Entry Manual for the Telecommunications Business [Supplement]" and the guidance of the Regional Bureau of Telecommunications with jurisdiction (accessed 2026-11-22). This article does not judge the propriety of exactly what to write on which form.

## What obligations must be observed after opening, such as the external transmission rules (2023 amendment)?

After opening, you observe the protection of the secrecy of communications (Article 4 of the Telecommunications Business Act) and the rules on protecting users' interests. A recent focus is the external transmission rules on mechanisms that cause information to be sent from a user's terminal to the outside (so-called cookies and the like). These are set in Article 27-12 of the Telecommunications Business Act, were introduced by the amendment under Act No. 70 of 2022, and came into force on 16 June 2023 (MIC, "External Transmission Rules", accessed 2026-11-22).

An operator that provides a telecommunications service subject to the external transmission rules is required, when it directs the user's terminal to transmit information, to notify the user of the content of the information to be sent, the destination, and the purpose of use, or to place it in a state that is easy for the user to know (to publish it), and the like. The scope of the services covered and the exclusions (such as identification codes) are set by MIC ordinance, and guidelines and FAQs are published. Whether your service is covered, and which response (notification, publication, obtaining consent, opt-out, and so on) to take, changes with the design of the service, so this article stays with information on the framework and does not judge the propriety of a specific response.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a notification even just to start my own web service?**
A. Providing something for your own demand—such as operating your own website or running an in-house system—does not amount to provision "to meet the demand of others" and is treated as not falling under a telecommunications business (MIC's Entry Manual). Also, a service that neither intermediates others' communications nor installs line facilities (a category-3 business) needs neither registration nor notification under Article 164 of the Telecommunications Business Act. However, if it has a function that relays exchanges between third parties, it may amount to intermediation. The final judgement of applicability is made by the MIC (Regional Bureau of Telecommunications).

**Q. How is it decided whether it is notification or registration?**
A. A person who intends to operate a telecommunications business, as a rule, obtains registration (Article 9), but a person whose telecommunications line facilities and installation area do not exceed the standards set by MIC ordinance, and the like, is subject to notification (Article 16, paragraph 1). Broadly, a business that itself installs line facilities exceeding a certain scale is registration, and other businesses subject to notification are notification. Because the specific thresholds are set in the Enforcement Regulations and may be amended, confirm with the Regional Bureau of Telecommunications and the Entry Manual once the facility configuration is fixed.

**Q. What are the external transmission rules, and when did they start?**
A. They are rules requiring, for mechanisms that send information from a user's terminal to the outside (cookies and the like), that the content of the information sent, the destination, and the purpose of use be notified to the user or placed in a state easy to know, and the like, set in Article 27-12 of the Telecommunications Business Act. They were introduced by the amendment under Act No. 70 of 2022 and came into force on 16 June 2023. Because the scope of the covered services and the response methods are set by MIC ordinance and guidelines, confirm with the latest MIC guidance.

**Q. Where is the counter for notification and registration?**
A. As a rule, the MIC's Regional Bureau of Telecommunications with jurisdiction over the location is the counter. For a notification you state the type of service, the service area, and the outline of the facilities on a telecommunications business notification form; for a registration you state the outline of the line facilities on a registration application; and both are submitted with the documents set by the Enforcement Regulations. Because the forms, attached documents, and handling of the submission counter may be amended, confirm with the Entry Manual and the guidance of the Regional Bureau with jurisdiction immediately before you submit.
