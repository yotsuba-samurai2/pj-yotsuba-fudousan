**In short:** to undertake, for a fee and at another's request, the design or construction supervision of buildings as a business, you generally need to register an architect office with the prefectural governor under Article 23 of the Architects Act. This is a system separate from the construction-business licence (Construction Business Act). The registration's term is five years from the date of registration, and to continue after it expires you must obtain a renewal registration (Article 23, paragraphs 2 and 3). Each architect office must have a full-time managing architect (Article 24, paragraph 1); a person can become a managing architect only if, after engaging for three or more years in design or other work prescribed by Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism ordinance, they have completed the managing-architect training given by a registered training body (paragraph 2). Architects who belong to the office also have, separately, a duty to take periodic training (Article 22-2). This article is general information and does not judge or guarantee whether a given office meets the registration or managing-architect requirements. Support for preparing and submitting the registration and change-notification documents is handled by the administrative scrivener of the Yotsuba group as an independent business; design, construction supervision and building-confirmation applications themselves are the exclusive work of an architect; leasing of the office premises and the important-matters explanation are by a real-estate agent, a separate business; registration of company formation and officer changes is by a judicial scrivener; social insurance and labor matters are by a labor and social security attorney; and tax is for a tax accountant. You contract with each separately.

## What is the difference between an architect-office "registration" and a construction-business licence?

The starting point is "what you do as a business". Article 23, paragraph 1 of the Architects Act provides that a first-class, second-class or wooden-building architect, or a person employing such a person, who intends to undertake design, construction supervision and the like as a business, for a fee and at another's request, must define an architect office and obtain the registration of the prefectural governor. In other words, it is a registration focused on "repeatedly and continuously performing design and supervision for a fee". By contrast, the construction-business licence (Article 3 of the Construction Business Act) is a separate system focused on "undertaking construction work", needed when you undertake work whose contract price for a single job is a certain amount or more (15 million yen for general building work, 5 million yen for other work, both tax included).

| Item | Architect-office registration | Construction-business licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governing law | Article 23 of the Architects Act | Article 3 of the Construction Business Act |
| Focus | Performing design/supervision as a business for a fee | Undertaking construction work as a business |
| When needed | Repeatedly performing design, etc. for a fee at another's request | Undertaking work whose single-job contract price is a set amount (15M general / 5M other, tax incl.) or more |
| Registration/licence authority | Prefectural governor | Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, or prefectural governor |
| Term | 5 years (renewal registration required) | 5 years (renewal required) |

Simply having a design office does not require a construction-business licence, but if you also undertake the construction yourself, both may be relevant. Because the requirements for the construction-business licence itself are a different issue, please also see [the requirements for a construction-business licence (managing responsible person, full-time engineer, financial basis)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kensetsugyo-kyoka-yoken-keikan-sengi-zaisan). If you handle electrical work, [registration, deemed registration and notification for an electrical construction business](https://luck428.com/legal/column/denki-koji-gyo-touroku-eigyosho-shunin) is yet another procedure. Because which system applies depends on the content of the business, confirming with the authority (the designated office-registration body of the prefecture) at an early stage is essential.

## What requirements (full-time status, completion of training) does the managing architect need?

Article 24, paragraph 1 of the Architects Act requires the opener of an architect office to place, for each first-class, second-class or wooden-building architect office, a full-time first-class, second-class or wooden-building architect who manages that office (the managing architect). The managing architect is a premise of registration; without one, you cannot obtain registration (a ground for refusal under Article 23-4).

The requirements for who can be a managing architect are set in Article 24, paragraph 2. A person must, after engaging for three or more years in design or other work prescribed by Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism ordinance, have completed the course of the managing-architect training given by a registered training body.

| Item | Content (basis) |
| --- | --- |
| Placement duty | Place a full-time managing architect at each office (Article 24, paragraph 1) |
| Qualification | Three or more years of design or other prescribed work as an architect (Article 24, paragraph 2) |
| Training | Completion of the managing-architect training by a registered body thereafter (Article 24, paragraph 2) |
| Meaning of "full-time" | Working full-time at that office and devoted to its management; concurrent or part-time service at another office is, in principle, not allowed |

What to watch here is that the managing-architect training is the "training to become a managing architect", separate from the periodic training (Article 22-2) described below. Whether the years and content of practice meet the requirement is a judgment under the Architects Act; an administrative scrivener does not make that judgment on your behalf as an exclusive matter. A "name-lending" managing architect who merely lends a name without actually working full-time is subject to refusal or cancellation of registration.

## When and what do you submit for the term, renewal and change notification of a registration?

The term of a registration is five years from the date of registration (Article 23, paragraph 2 of the Architects Act). If you wish to continue the architect-office business after the term expires, you must obtain a renewal registration (paragraph 3). If you forget to renew and the term lapses, the registration loses effect and you cannot continue the design business without registration.

| Procedure | Content (basis/practice) |
| --- | --- |
| Term of registration | Five years (Article 23, paragraph 2) |
| Renewal registration | Required to continue after the term (Article 23, paragraph 3); many prefectures require applying by roughly 30 days before expiry |
| Notification of change to registered matters | When the name, location, managing architect, belonging architects, etc. change |
| Notification of discontinuance, etc. | When the architect office is abolished or suspended |

The registration procedure is set in Article 23-2 (matters to be stated in the application) and Article 23-3 (implementation of registration). Because the timing of the renewal application and the deadline and form of the change notification differ in operation by prefecture (the designated office-registration body), managing the expiry date and notifying promptly when a change arises is the key in practice. The point that "it was this form in another prefecture" does not always apply directly often arises in application practice.

## How far are the office's reality (exclusivity, facilities) required?

Registration is not something that "suffices if the documents are in order"; it presupposes that the reality of an office exists. In particular, whether the full-time managing architect actually works full-time and manages that office is at issue. Not placing a managing architect, or having only a name without substance, is a violation of Article 24, paragraph 1 of the Architects Act and a ground for refusal (Article 23-4) or cancellation (Article 26) of registration. There are actual cases where a prefecture cancelled a registration on the ground that no managing architect was placed.

| Point checked | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Full-time status of the managing architect | Working full-time at that office, not concurrently at another |
| Reality as an office | Independent workspace for business; retention of design documents and work records |
| Not name-lending | The managing architect and belonging architects actually exist and are actually involved |

The concrete operation regarding facilities and document retention (documents to keep, retention periods, submission of reports) is set by the Architects Act, ministry ordinance and each prefecture's guide, and the details of items and forms are judged by the authority. Consulting the prefecture (the designated office-registration body) in advance before opening makes it easier to avoid a return of documents just before submission.

## FAQ

**Q. Even if I work alone on design at home, do I need an architect-office registration?**
A. If you undertake design, construction supervision and the like as a business (repeatedly and continuously) for a fee at another's request, you need an architect-office registration regardless of scale (Article 23 of the Architects Act). Even alone, that person must meet the requirements of a full-time managing architect (three or more years of practice as an architect plus completion of the managing-architect training). Whether the reality of an office exists is also checked.

**Q. If I hold a construction-business licence, is the architect-office registration unnecessary?**
A. They are separate systems, so even with a construction-business licence you still need an architect-office registration to perform design and supervision as a business. The construction-business licence is needed for undertaking construction work (such as 15 million yen for general building work, 5 million yen for other, both tax included, or more), and does not cover performing design and supervision as a business. Judge by the content of the work you handle.

**Q. Are the managing architect's periodic training and the managing-architect training the same thing?**
A. They are different. The managing-architect training is the training completed to become a managing architect, after practice (Article 24, paragraph 2). The periodic training is the training that first-class, second-class and wooden-building architects belonging to an architect office keep taking; under Article 22-2 of the Architects Act, they must take it at intervals prescribed by ministry ordinance (the law sets a range of three to five years, and the enforcement regulations set it at every three fiscal years).

**Q. What happens if I forget to renew and the term lapses?**
A. The registration's term is five years, and continuing requires a renewal registration (Article 23, paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Architects Act). If you forget to renew, the registration lapses, and you cannot continue the design business without registration. Many prefectures require applying by roughly 30 days before expiry. Managing the expiry date and acting with time to spare is important.
