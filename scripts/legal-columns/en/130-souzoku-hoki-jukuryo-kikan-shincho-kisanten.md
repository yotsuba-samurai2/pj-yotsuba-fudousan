**In short:** the three-month deliberation period for renouncing an inheritance runs from the time you learn the inheritance has begun for you (Civil Code Article 915(1)). If you cannot decide in time, you may ask the family court to extend it, and even after it passes the start of the clock can exceptionally shift. Preparing the filings and petitions to the family court is outside an administrative scrivener's scope.

## When does the three-month "deliberation period" start?

Civil Code Article 915(1) provides that an heir must, within three months from the time they learn the inheritance has begun for them, make a simple acceptance, a qualified acceptance or a renunciation. These three months are the deliberation period. In practice, the key point is that the clock starts not from "the date of death" but from "the time the heir learns the inheritance has begun for them."

| The heir's situation | When the clock starts | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An ordinary heir | When they learn the inheritance has begun for them | Civil Code Article 915(1) |
| The heir dies without accepting or renouncing (re-transferred inheritance) | When that person's own heir learns the inheritance has begun for them | Civil Code Article 916 |
| The heir is a minor or an adult ward | When the legal representative learns of the inheritance | Civil Code Article 917 |

If the period passes and the heir has neither made a qualified acceptance nor renounced within it, they are deemed to have made a simple acceptance (statutory simple acceptance; Civil Code Article 921(ii)). A simple acceptance means succeeding to the decedent's rights and obligations without limit (Civil Code Article 920). Under Civil Code Article 915(2), an heir may investigate the estate before accepting or renouncing.

## If three months is not enough, can the period be extended?

In some cases, yes. The proviso to Civil Code Article 915(1) provides that, at the request of an interested party or a public prosecutor, the family court may extend the deliberation period. It is used where the estate spans many items and takes time to investigate, or where the full picture of debts cannot be grasped.

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Who may petition | Interested parties (including heirs) and the public prosecutor |
| Where to file | The family court for the decedent's last domicile |
| When to petition | In principle, within the three-month deliberation period |
| Basis | Proviso to Civil Code Article 915(1); Domestic Relations Case Procedure Act Article 201 |

The extension petition is, in principle, made before the deliberation period expires. Whether an extension is granted and by how long is for the family court to decide. An immediate appeal may be filed against a ruling that dismisses the extension petition (Domestic Relations Case Procedure Act Article 201(9)). Confirm the procedural details in the court's guidance.

## If debts come to light after the period passes, can you no longer renounce?

Not necessarily. The Supreme Court judgment of 27 April 1984 set out an exception on when the clock starts. Where an heir believed the decedent had no estate at all and had reasonable grounds for so believing, the deliberation period runs from the time they recognised the existence of all or part of the estate, or the time they could normally have recognised it.

On this view, even where more than three months have passed since the death, if the heir learns of a debt afterward, the start of the clock may have shifted later, leaving room to renounce. But this is an assessment for each case, and one cannot categorically assert either that "you can always renounce once three months have passed" or that "you never can." Whether it applies depends on the facts — when the heir learned of the debt, how the investigation proceeded, and so on. For the outlook where a large debt comes to light later, consult a judicial scrivener or an attorney.

Note that once a renunciation is made, it cannot be withdrawn even within the deliberation period (Civil Code Article 919(1)). Rescission on grounds such as mistake or fraud is allowed separately, but the right to rescind is extinguished by prescription six months from when ratification becomes possible, or ten years from the renunciation (Civil Code Article 919(3)).

## Who may prepare the renunciation filing or the extension petition?

A renunciation is made by filing a declaration with the family court (Civil Code Article 938). Once the filing is accepted, the person is deemed never to have been an heir with respect to that inheritance (Civil Code Article 939). An extension of the deliberation period is likewise made by petition to the family court.

The point to note here is the difference in who may act. Preparing documents submitted to the family court — the declaration of renunciation and the extension petition — is outside an administrative scrivener's scope. These are handled by a judicial scrivener or an attorney. An administrative scrivener handles investigating the estate and debts, assisting with the estate inventory, and preparing the estate-division agreement.

| Document / procedure | Who prepares / represents |
| --- | --- |
| Declaration of renunciation | Judicial scrivener / attorney |
| Petition to extend the deliberation period | Judicial scrivener / attorney |
| Negotiation with creditors; inheritance disputes | Attorney |
| Investigating the estate and debts; assisting with the estate inventory | Administrative scrivener |

## FAQ

**Q. Do the three months run from the date of death?**
A. No. They run from the time you learn the inheritance has begun for you (Civil Code Article 915(1)). The start can vary with when you learned of the death or learned you had become an heir. A re-transferred inheritance (Article 916) and a minor or adult ward (Article 917) have separate rules on when the clock starts.

**Q. What do I do if I cannot decide within three months?**
A. You may petition the family court for the decedent's last domicile to extend the deliberation period (proviso to Civil Code Article 915(1); Domestic Relations Case Procedure Act Article 201). In principle you petition within the three-month period. Whether it is granted, and by how long, is for the family court to decide.

**Q. Once three months pass, can I no longer renounce?**
A. Not categorically. The Supreme Court judgment of 27 April 1984 held that the clock shifts where you believed there was no estate at all and had reasonable grounds for so believing. Whether it applies is case-specific, so consult a judicial scrivener or an attorney for the outlook.

**Q. Can an administrative scrivener prepare the renunciation documents?**
A. Preparing the declaration and petition submitted to the family court is outside an administrative scrivener's scope and is handled by a judicial scrivener or an attorney. An administrative scrivener investigates the estate and debts and assists with the estate inventory and the estate-division agreement.
