**In short:** when a Chinese or Taiwanese company winds up its Japanese subsidiary, it proceeds in order from the shareholders' resolution to dissolve, through liquidation by a liquidator, public notice to creditors in the Official Gazette and individual demands, confirmation and distribution of the residual assets, to the conclusion of liquidation (Articles 471, 475, 499 and 507 of the Companies Act). A manager who came to Japan on a Business Manager residence status loses the basis for that status through the company's liquidation, so immigration reviews a change to another status or a switch to departure preparation. Remitting the residual assets to the home country involves tax and a report under the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act. This article is general information organising the flow of withdrawal and the division of roles among professionals; it does not make specific legal judgements.

## What do you start with when a Chinese or Taiwanese company winds up its Japanese subsidiary?

When winding up a Japanese company (a stock company), you start by determining how to make the company disappear (ordinary liquidation, or special liquidation or bankruptcy if insolvent). If the assets exceed the liabilities and there is a prospect of paying all debts in full, the ordinary-liquidation procedure of the Companies Act applies. The main items to organise before you begin are as follows.

| To confirm before you begin | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Method of liquidation | Ordinary liquidation, or special liquidation or bankruptcy if insolvent (judging whether debts can be paid) |
| Officers and employees | Retirement of the representative and directors; dismissal notice to employees and loss of social-insurance qualification |
| Licences | Whether a notification of abolition or surrender is needed if the business holds a licence |
| Residence status | Treatment of officers staying on a status such as Business Manager |
| Assets | Assets such as real estate, deposits and receivables, and liabilities such as payables and borrowings |

Because dissolution and liquidation move registration, tax, labour, residence status and overseas remittance in parallel, deciding the overall order before you begin is the key. The final judgement of which method should apply is made by a qualified professional such as a lawyer or judicial scrivener according to the state of the debts.

## In what order do the dissolution/liquidation registration and the Official Gazette notice proceed?

The grounds for dissolution of a stock company are set out in Article 471 of the Companies Act, and dissolution by a shareholders' resolution (a special resolution under Article 309, paragraph 2 of the Companies Act) is common in practice. Once dissolved, the company must be liquidated (Article 475 of the Companies Act), and the liquidator completes current affairs, collects claims, pays debts, and distributes the residual assets. The skeleton of the order is as follows.

1. Resolve to dissolve at a shareholders' meeting and appoint a liquidator (Article 471 of the Companies Act)
2. Register the dissolution and register the liquidator (judicial scrivener)
3. Give public notice in the Official Gazette that creditors should state their claims within a certain period, and separately demand of each known creditor. This period may not be shorter than two months (Article 499 of the Companies Act)
4. After the period for stating claims passes, pay the debts and distribute the remaining assets (residual assets) to the shareholders
5. When liquidation affairs are finished, prepare a settlement report and obtain the approval of the shareholders' meeting (Article 507 of the Companies Act). With approval, liquidation concludes, and the conclusion of liquidation is registered (judicial scrivener)

The Official Gazette notice under Article 499 of the Companies Act is a procedure to protect creditors, and the period for stating claims must be two months or more. Because of this period, it takes at least several months from deciding on dissolution to the conclusion of liquidation, even when things go smoothly. Registration (dissolution, liquidator, conclusion of liquidation) is for a judicial scrivener.

## What happens to the residence status of a manager who came to Japan on a Business Manager status after withdrawal?

The Business Manager residence status presumes activities of managing or administering a business in Japan. When you wind up the company and stop the business, the basis for those activities is lost, so maintaining the residence status as it is becomes impossible in principle. The options after withdrawal are divided as follows by the individual review of immigration (the Immigration Services Agency).

- If you continue another business or employment in Japan, apply for a change to the residence status that corresponds to it
- If you leave Japan, switch to the residence status for departure preparation (Designated Activities) while liquidation procedures or preparations for departure remain, and then leave

The period of the Designated Activities for departure preparation, and whether a change is permitted, are judged by immigration according to the progress of liquidation, whether residual work remains, and the individual circumstances. When the residence period is approaching but liquidation is not finished, or when there are several officers whose treatment differs, early confirmation is needed. Preparation of the application for a change of residence status or a switch to Designated Activities is handled by an administrative scrivener, and if the business is licensed, submission of the notification of abolition is also the administrative scrivener's role. The final grant or refusal is judged by immigration after its review.

## What are the tax and foreign-exchange points when remitting the residual assets to the home country?

In liquidation, a tax return such as corporation tax is required for the business year up to the day of dissolution, each business year during liquidation thereafter, and the business year in which the residual assets are confirmed (the Corporation Tax Act). When distributing and remitting the residual assets to the shareholders (such as the home-country parent company), a tax organisation is essential, including whether withholding (taxation of deemed dividends, etc.) is required. The calculation of the tax and the filing are the role of a tax accountant.

For overseas remittance, under Article 55 of the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act (report of payments, etc.), when a resident or the like makes a certain payment toward a foreign country, a report is required except in cases prescribed by Cabinet Order. In practice, a report of payments routed through a bank or the like is made via the Bank of Japan, and payments exceeding a certain amount (payments exceeding the equivalent of 30 million yen) are treated as subject to the report (accessed 2026-10-30). Because the amount standard and the submission deadline are set by Cabinet Order and notification and the operation also changes, confirm the specific necessity at the time of remittance with the latest guidance of the financial institution and the Bank of Japan.

## FAQ

**Q. What do we start with to wind up our Japanese subsidiary?**
A. First, determine whether all debts can be paid in full (ordinary liquidation, or special liquidation or bankruptcy if insolvent). For ordinary liquidation, start with the shareholders' resolution to dissolve and the appointment of a liquidator (Article 471 of the Companies Act), then proceed to registration of dissolution, public notice to creditors in the Official Gazette and individual demands (Article 499 of the Companies Act), payment of debts and distribution of residual assets, and approval of the settlement report and conclusion of liquidation (Article 507 of the Companies Act). Because registration, tax, labour, residence status and remittance move at once, it is important to decide the order before you begin.

**Q. How long must the Official Gazette notice period be?**
A. A liquidating stock company must give public notice in the Official Gazette that creditors should state their claims within a certain period, and separately demand of each known creditor. This period may not be shorter than two months (Article 499 of the Companies Act). Because of this period, it takes at least several months from dissolution to the conclusion of liquidation, even when things go smoothly. The notice adds that creditors who do not state their claims within the period are excluded from the liquidation.

**Q. Does the Business Manager residence status lapse when we wind up the company?**
A. Because the Business Manager status presumes activities of managing or administering a business, winding up the company loses its basis, and maintaining it as it is becomes impossible in principle. If you continue another activity in Japan, immigration reviews a change to the corresponding residence status; if you go home, a switch to Designated Activities for departure preparation. Because the residence period and the grant or refusal depend on the progress of liquidation and the individual circumstances, confirm early with an administrative scrivener and immigration.

**Q. What should we watch for when remitting the residual assets to the home country?**
A. A tax return such as corporation tax is required for each business year during liquidation and the business year in which the residual assets are confirmed, and distribution involves whether withholding (taxation of deemed dividends, etc.) is required (the Corporation Tax Act). The calculation and filing of the tax are the role of a tax accountant. Overseas remittance may be subject to the report of payments under Article 55 of the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act, and payments exceeding a certain amount are treated as subject to it. Confirm the specific necessity at the time of remittance with the latest guidance of the financial institution and the Bank of Japan.
