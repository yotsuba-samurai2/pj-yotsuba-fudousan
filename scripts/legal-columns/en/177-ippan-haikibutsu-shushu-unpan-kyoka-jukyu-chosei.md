**In short:** to carry on, as a business, the collection and transport of night soil, septic-tank sludge, or business general waste, you need the licence of the mayor of the municipality with jurisdiction over that area (Waste Management Act Article 7(1)). This differs from industrial-waste collection and transport, which is the prefectural governor's licence (Article 14(1)): the licensing authority is different. The core of why a general-waste collection and transport licence is said to be hard to obtain lies in the criteria. The mayor must not grant the licence unless the application conforms to all of the criteria, including that "the collection or transport of general waste by the municipality is difficult" and that "the content of the application conforms to the general-waste management plan" (Article 7(5)). In other words, in an area where the municipality itself or the existing framework suffices, a new licence is hard to recognise. The Supreme Court judgment of 28 January 2014 (Minshu Vol. 68 No. 1 p. 49) also held that an operator already licensed holds standing to sue to revoke a licence granted to another in the same area, showing that the existing operator's business interest can be individually protected. ## How do the licensing schemes for general waste and industrial waste differ?

Waste is broadly divided into "general waste" and "industrial waste." Industrial waste is waste arising from business activity of the types specified by law (cinders, sludge, waste oil, waste plastics, etc.); everything else is general waste. Household refuse, night soil, sludge arising from cleaning septic tanks, and business general waste such as wastepaper from offices and shops are general waste. Which category applies entirely changes both the recipient of the licence and the governing article.

The decisive difference is the licensing authority. A person who intends to carry on the collection or transport of general waste as a business must obtain the licence of the mayor of the municipality with jurisdiction over that area (Waste Management Act Article 7(1)). By contrast, a person who intends to carry on the collection or transport of industrial waste as a business obtains the prefectural governor's licence (Article 14(1)). The municipality bears responsibility for processing the general waste within its area (Article 6-2) and sets a plan for that processing (Article 6). The general-waste licence is operated on the premise of this municipal responsibility and plan, which is fundamentally different from industrial waste.

| Category | Licensing authority | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| General-waste collection and transport | Mayor of the municipality | Waste Management Act Article 7(1) |
| Industrial-waste collection and transport | Prefectural governor | Waste Management Act Article 14(1) |
| Responsibility for general-waste processing | The municipality | Waste Management Act Article 6-2 |
| The processing plan | The municipality (general-waste management plan) | Waste Management Act Article 6 |

Preparing the application (documents submitted to a public agency) is an administrative scrivener's work. However, the final judgement on whether a given waste is general or industrial, and on whether a particular disposal method is lawful, is made by the municipality or local government as the licensing authority. The industrial-waste procedures are set out in [How to obtain an industrial-waste collection and transport licence](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/sanpai-shushu-unpan-kyoka-torikata) and [Industrial-waste disposal and intermediate treatment: the licence and facilities](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/sanpai-shobun-gyo-chukan-shori-kyoka-shisetsu).

## Why is a general-waste collection and transport licence hard to obtain as a new entrant (supply-demand adjustment)?

The reason lies in the licensing criteria themselves. The mayor must not grant the collection and transport licence unless the application conforms to all of the following items (Waste Management Act Article 7(5)). Item 1 is that "the collection or transport of general waste by the municipality is difficult"; item 2 is that "the content of the application conforms to the general-waste management plan"; item 3 is that the facilities and the applicant's ability conform to the standards set by ordinance of the Ministry of the Environment; and item 4 is that there is no ground for disqualification. Items 1 and 2 work as the so-called supply-demand adjustment.

Where the municipality itself, or existing licensed operators, can properly collect and transport the area's general waste, a new application is hard to recognise as either one where "the municipality's collection and transport is difficult" or one that "conforms to the processing plan." The Supreme Court judgment of 28 January 2014 (Minshu Vol. 68 No. 1 p. 49) held that an operator already licensed by the mayor for general-waste collection and transport or disposal in a given area, or granted a renewal, holds standing to sue to revoke a licence or renewal granted to another for that area. Where the processing plan is made on the premise of proper processing by existing operators, the mayor may refuse another's application, and the Act is understood to include a purpose of protecting the existing operators' business interest as an individual interest as well (accessed 2026-11-17).

| Criterion (Article 7(5)) | Content |
|---|---|
| Item 1 | The collection or transport of general waste by the municipality is difficult (supply-demand) |
| Item 2 | The content of the application conforms to the general-waste management plan (supply-demand / plan) |
| Item 3 | Facilities and ability conform to the standards set by Ministry of the Environment ordinance |
| Item 4 | No ground for disqualification (imprisonment or heavier; organised-crime-group members, etc.) |

In other words, the structure differs from the industrial-waste licence, which in principle can be obtained once the requirements are met. Whether a new entrant may enter depends heavily on the municipality's processing plan and the state of supply and demand. A request for review or administrative litigation contesting the propriety of a refusal is an attorney's domain, handled as an independent business under a separate contract.

## What licence, vehicles, and equipment are needed for collecting and transporting septic-tank sludge and night soil?

Night soil, and septic-tank sludge arising from cleaning septic tanks, are general waste. A business that collects and transports these by vacuum trucks and the like to a treatment plant is subject to the general-waste collection and transport licence (Waste Management Act Article 7(1)). As equipment, you need transport vehicles and containers that prevent the waste from scattering or leaking and prevent odour from escaping during collection and transport, and the facilities and the applicant's ability must conform to the standards set by ordinance of the Ministry of the Environment (Article 7(5), item 3). Because the specific standards for vehicles, containers, and storage are set by Ministry ordinance and each municipality's guidelines, confirmation at the window before applying is required.

Note that "cleaning a septic tank" itself is a separate licence. To carry on the cleaning of septic tanks as a business, you need the mayor's septic-tank cleaning licence under the Septic Tank Act (Septic Tank Act Article 35(1)), separately. The part of transporting the sludge produced by cleaning is general-waste collection and transport, and the cleaning work itself is the septic-tank cleaning business. To do both, you need each licence. Which licence is needed is determined by the content of the work actually performed.

| Content of the business | Licence needed | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Collection and transport of night soil / septic-tank sludge | General-waste collection and transport licence | Waste Management Act Article 7(1) |
| Cleaning of septic tanks | Septic-tank cleaning licence | Septic Tank Act Article 35(1) |
| Transport vehicles / containers | Structure preventing scattering, leaking, and odour (Ministry ordinance / municipal guidelines) | Waste Management Act Article 7(5), item 3 |

A general-waste collection and transport operator must not entrust the collection, transport, or disposal to others (Waste Management Act Article 7(14)), and there is a cap on the fees that may be received: they must not exceed the amount equivalent to the fee set by the municipality's ordinance (Article 7(12)). The licence may specify the area in which collection may be carried out and may attach conditions necessary to preserve the living environment (Article 7(11)).

## What about the municipal general-waste management plan, and how does the application proceed?

The starting point is the general-waste management plan set by the municipality (Waste Management Act Article 6). This plan sets the forecast of the amount of general waste generated and processed in the area, measures to restrain discharge, the types and classifications collected separately, basic matters on proper processing and those who carry it out, and the development of processing facilities. Whether a new licence is recognised is strongly tied to whether the content of the application conforms to this plan (Article 7(5), item 2) and whether the municipality's collection and transport is difficult (item 1). Before applying, therefore, confirming the municipality's processing plan and whether it is actually recruiting licensed operators (whether there is an implementation plan or public solicitation) is essential.

The general flow is as follows. First, confirm the general-waste management plan and implementation plan of the municipality for the area you wish to enter, and the licensing policy for collection and transport (whether new applications are accepted, whether by public solicitation). Second, confirm the collection and transport framework such as vehicles and containers, and that you do not fall under a ground for disqualification. Third, prepare the application in the form the municipality's window requires and apply. The licence lapses unless renewed every period set by Cabinet Order of not less than one year (Article 7(2)). Because acceptance, forms, and fees differ by municipality, generalisation is not possible. For points you cannot substantiate, confirm at the window rather than filling them in by guesswork.

| Step | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| 1 | The municipality's general-waste management plan / implementation plan and policy on new licences (whether by public solicitation) |
| 2 | Collection and transport vehicles, containers, and framework; no ground for disqualification |
| 3 | Preparation and submission of the application in the municipality's designated form |
| 4 | After licensing, renewal every period set by Cabinet Order (Article 7(2)) |

## FAQ

**Q. If I have an industrial-waste collection and transport licence, can I transport general waste too?**
A. No. An industrial-waste collection and transport licence is the prefectural governor's (Waste Management Act Article 14(1)); a general-waste collection and transport licence is the mayor's (Article 7(1)); they are separate licences. To transport general waste such as night soil, septic-tank sludge, or business general waste, you need the mayor's licence for that area separately. To do both, obtain each licence.

**Q. If I meet the requirements, will I always get a general-waste collection and transport licence?**
A. Not necessarily. The mayor must not grant the licence unless all criteria are met, including that the municipality's collection and transport is difficult and that the application conforms to the general-waste management plan (Waste Management Act Article 7(5)). In an area where the municipality or existing operators suffice, a new licence is hard to recognise. First confirm the municipality's processing plan and recruitment status.

**Q. Is transporting septic-tank sludge the same licence as cleaning septic tanks?**
A. They are separate licences. Collecting and transporting the sludge produced by cleaning is the general-waste collection and transport licence (Waste Management Act Article 7(1)); cleaning the septic tank itself is the septic-tank cleaning licence under the Septic Tank Act (Septic Tank Act Article 35(1)). To do both, you need each licence. The licence needed is determined by the content of the work actually performed.

**Q. What should I do if I am refused?**
A. If you are dissatisfied with a refusal, you can contest it by a request for review under the Administrative Complaint Review Act or a revocation action under the Administrative Case Litigation Act. The Supreme Court judgment of 28 January 2014 recognised an existing operator's standing to contest a licence granted to another in the same area. Negotiation, a request for review, and litigation contesting the propriety of a disposition are an attorney's domain, handled as an independent business under a separate contract. Preparing the application is supported by an administrative scrivener.
