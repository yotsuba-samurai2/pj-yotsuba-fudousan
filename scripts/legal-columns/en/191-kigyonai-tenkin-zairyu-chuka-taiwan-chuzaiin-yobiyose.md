**In short:** When you receive a transferee into a Japanese base from a parent or affiliated company in China or Taiwan, "Intra-company Transferee" is the status under which an employee of an overseas office transfers for a fixed period to the Japanese office to perform Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services work (Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Appended Table I-2). The criteria are: at least one continuous year of such work at the overseas office immediately before the transfer, and pay at least equal to what a Japanese national would receive (the landing-criteria ordinance). The pre-entry step is applying for a Certificate of Eligibility.

## Who qualifies for the Intra-company Transferee status?

The Intra-company Transferee item in Appended Table I-2 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act defines the activity as: activities listed under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, carried out at a Japanese office by an employee of an overseas office of an organization (public or private) that has a head office, branch or other office in Japan, who transfers to that Japanese office for a fixed period. In short: (1) within the same corporate group (an organization with a head office, branch or other office in Japan); (2) an employee based at the overseas office; (3) transfers for a fixed period to the Japanese office; and (4) performs Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services work.

Further, the Intra-company Transferee item of the landing-criteria ordinance (the ordinance setting the criteria under Article 7(1), item 2 of the Act) requires both of the following. First, that immediately before the transfer, where the applicant has been engaged in Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services work at an overseas head office, branch or other office, that period (adding, where applicable, any period worked at the organization's Japanese office under the Intra-company Transferee status) is at least one continuous year. Second, that the applicant receives pay at least equal to what a Japanese national would receive.

| Criterion | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Form of transfer | Transfer for a fixed period from the same organization's overseas office to its Japanese office | Immigration Act, App. Table I-2 |
| Job content | Work falling under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services | Immigration Act, App. Table I-2 |
| Work history | At least one continuous year at the overseas office immediately before (a Japanese Intra-company Transferee period may be added) | Landing-criteria ordinance |
| Pay | At least equal to a Japanese national's | Landing-criteria ordinance |

## How do you show the capital relationship among parent, subsidiary and affiliate?

What the provision assumes is a transfer among the head office, branches and offices of a single organization (public or private) that has a head office, branch or other office in Japan. Whether a move between a parent, subsidiary or affiliate can fall under Intra-company Transferee turns on a case-by-case review in light of the capital relationship and the reality of the offices. In practice, you prepare documents showing the capital or organizational link between the two companies (certificate of registered matters, documents showing the investment relationship, an organization chart) together with documents showing employment and duties at the overseas office, and have them ready for review.

How far the same-organization or affiliate scope is recognized is not settled by a general explanation alone, and the final decision rests with the Immigration Services Agency. Because structuring the proof of a capital relationship is highly fact-specific, it is safer to have an administrative scrivener who has filed the notification of application agency handle the immigration procedure, and to leave the specific eligibility decision to the Agency's review. ## Should you apply under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services instead?

Intra-company Transferee and Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (the "technical/humanities" status) overlap in the nature of the work, but their requirements are framed differently. The technical/humanities status generally requires meeting criteria such as a relevant academic major or a set number of years of practical experience. Intra-company Transferee, by contrast, pivots not on education or experience as such, but on being "a transfer from the same organization's overseas office to its Japanese office" and on having "at least one continuous year of work history immediately before."

So the basic split is: if you transfer an employee of an overseas group company to Japan for a fixed period, Intra-company Transferee; if you newly hire on the Japanese side (not a within-group transfer), the technical/humanities status. Either status assumes pay at least equal to a Japanese national's. Which to apply under is determined by the reality of the employment or transfer and by the documents you can produce, so decide the approach after organizing the transfer order, the employment contract and documents showing job content. The entry points to each status are set out at [status of residence and visa services](https://luck428.com/en/legal/services/visa).

## What documents does the Certificate of Eligibility application need?

If you newly wish to enter Japan under Intra-company Transferee, you apply for a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) (Immigration Act, Article 7-2). The documents required differ by category (Categories 1–4) depending on the scale of the receiving organization. In the Immigration Services Agency guidance, Category 1 covers listed companies, incorporated administrative agencies, certain public-interest corporations and the like; Category 2 covers organizations and individuals whose withholding tax in the statutory record summary of withholding slips for salary income for the prior year is at least 10 million yen; the higher the category, the simpler the documents.

Generally, you prepare documents showing the activity (transfer order, employment contract), documents showing the one-year-plus work history, duties and pay, and documents showing the relationship between the receiving organization and the overseas office and the business content (certificate of registered matters, etc.). Documents prepared in a foreign language need a Japanese translation. Note that the Immigration Services Agency put a change to the Intra-company Transferee submission documents into operation from 1 April 2026, so the specifics may change; confirm with the Agency's latest guidance before applying (checked 25 November 2026).

| Application type | Main occasion | Basis / procedure |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of Eligibility application | Before entry (new) | Immigration Act, Art. 7-2 |
| Change of status application | Change from another status | Immigration Act, Art. 20 |
| Extension of period application | Extension while in Japan | Immigration Act, Art. 21 |

Submission on another's behalf by a duly notified administrative scrivener or lawyer is subject to requirements (Immigration Act, Article 7-2(2)). For checking the reason for, and reapplying after, a refusal, see [if your Certificate of Eligibility is refused](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/zairyu-shikaku-nintei-shomeisho-fukoufu-saishinsei-chuka-taiwan).

## Who handles social insurance and tax after arrival?

Agency submission for the status is within the administrative scrivener's scope, but post-arrival work divides among different professionals. Coverage under health insurance, employees' pension and labor insurance, as well as work rules and labor management, are for a labor and social security attorney; establishing or changing the registration of a Japanese corporation or branch is for a judicial scrivener; and tax — including short-term-visitor exemption and the application of tax treaties — is for a tax accountant. Adjusting double coverage under a social security agreement (such as the Japan–China social security agreement) also involves the judgment of a labor and social security attorney and a tax accountant.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I bring an employee just hired overseas on Intra-company Transferee right away?**
A. The landing-criteria ordinance requires at least one continuous year of Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services work at the overseas office immediately before the transfer. If the work history is under a year because the person was just hired, the criterion may not be met. The eligibility decision is made by Immigration on the documents.

**Q. Does a move from a subsidiary to the parent count as Intra-company Transferee?**
A. The provision assumes a transfer within a single organization that has a head office, branch or other office. Whether a move among parent, subsidiary or affiliate can fall under it turns on a case-by-case review in light of the capital relationship and the reality of the offices. Prepare documents showing the capital relationship and seek the Immigration Services Agency's decision.

**Q. Should I apply under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services instead?**
A. The basic split is: transferring an overseas group employee for a fixed period, Intra-company Transferee; newly hiring on the Japanese side, the technical/humanities status. Because it depends on the reality of employment or transfer and the supporting documents, decide after organizing the transfer order and employment contract.

**Q. Can I hire one window for the immigration application and for social insurance and tax?**
A. No. Agency submission for the status is for an administrative scrivener, social insurance and labor for a labor and social security attorney, registration for a judicial scrivener, and tax for a tax accountant — each taking the matter as an independent business under a separate engagement, a divided-engagement arrangement.
