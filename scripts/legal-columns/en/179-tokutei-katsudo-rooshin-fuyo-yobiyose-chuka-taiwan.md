**In short:** if you work or run a business in Japan and come from mainland China or Taiwan, and you wish to bring an elderly parent remaining in your home country to support them, the "Dependent" status cannot be used. The family the Dependent status (Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, Appended Table I(4)) admits is limited to a spouse and children receiving support; parents are not included. Bringing a parent may be considered under "Designated Activities" (Appended Table I(5) = activities the Minister of Justice specifically designates for each foreign national; Article 2-2), specifically the "non-notified Designated Activities (support of elderly parents)" not set out in a Ministry of Justice notification. This is not a type with notified requirements but a discretionary case-by-case judgement by immigration; circumstances such as there being no relative in the home country able to support, the parent being elderly and alone, and the Japan side having the means to support are said to be considered comprehensively, but these are not published approval criteria. The bar for proof is high, and refusals are common. This article is general information and does not reach the judgement of whether a particular residence is permitted. ## Why can't the Dependent status be used to bring an elderly parent?

Statuses of residence are set out in the Act's appended tables, and a person may carry out the activities in the lower column corresponding to the status in the upper column (Article 2-2). The "Dependent" status used to bring a spouse or children is a status listed in Appended Table I(4), and its content is the daily activities of a spouse or child receiving support. In other words, what the Dependent status contemplates is a spouse and children, not a parent.

So, even if someone residing under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services or Business Manager wishes to bring an elderly parent from the home country to live together, the Dependent status does not grant a status of residence. To consider bringing a parent, one explores a different framework—Designated Activities (support of elderly parents). Bringing a spouse or children, and the activity-outside-status (28 hours a week) under the Dependent status, are set out in [The Dependent status, activity outside status, and bringing family](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kazoku-taizai-zairyu-shikakugai-katsudo-28jikan-yobiyose).

| Status | Family covered | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Dependent | Spouse / children receiving support | Act, Appended Table I(4) |
| Designated Activities (support of elderly parents) | Parent (non-notified, discretionary) | Act, Appended Table I(5), Article 2-2 |

Grasping the premise that a parent is outside the Dependent status is the starting point.

## When is Designated Activities (support of elderly parents) considered?

Designated Activities is a status whose content is "activities the Minister of Justice specifically designates for each foreign national" (Appended Table I(5)). This Designated Activities has "notified Designated Activities" whose requirements are set in advance by Ministry of Justice notification, and "non-notified Designated Activities" not set out in a notification. Support of elderly parents is not listed in a notification and is, as non-notified Designated Activities, something immigration may exceptionally permit by comprehensively considering the individual circumstances. Because it is not a type with criteria set out in a notification, it is not "if you meet these requirements it will always be granted."

As circumstances said to be generally considered, practitioners cite the parent being elderly, there being no other relative (person bound to support) in the home country able to care for the parent, the parent being alone in the home country, the Japan side having ample means to support the parent, and the purpose of coming to Japan being support. However, these are guideline figures spoken of as practitioners' experience, not approval criteria published by immigration. If siblings or other relatives able to support are alive in the home country, the likelihood of approval is said to fall.

| Circumstance considered (guideline) | Content |
|---|---|
| Parent's age / situation | Elderly and alone |
| Supporter in home country | No other relative able to support in the home country |
| Japan-side means | Stable income/assets able to support the parent |
| Purpose of coming to Japan | For the purpose of support |

These are material for the discretionary judgement of a "non-notified" case; meeting them does not guarantee approval. The final judgement of whether residence is permitted rests with immigration, and this article does not assert it.

## What documents show the necessity of support and the means?

In the review of non-notified Designated Activities, both "being unable to support the parent in the home country (necessity of support)" and "being able to support the parent on the Japan side (means)" must be shown with objective documents. The necessity of support is backed up with the home country's family register/public certificates and family-relationship documents showing the parent is alone and elderly and there is no other person bound to support in the home country. The means is shown with the Japan-side supporter's certificate of employment, income certificates (tax/payment certificates), and bank balances.

These home-country documents are usually accompanied by a Japanese translation. Translation certification and support in obtaining the notarisation/authentication of home-country documents are within an administrative scrivener's work. On the other hand, the outlook on what to submit for approval in a particular case hangs on immigration's discretion and cannot be guaranteed by this article. ## How do you obtain authentication of home-country documents for China and for Taiwan?

When submitting public documents issued in the home country to Japanese immigration, the authentication showing the document is genuine is an issue. The frameworks differ greatly between mainland China and Taiwan.

For mainland China, the Hague Convention (abolishing the requirement of legalisation for foreign public documents = the Apostille Convention) took effect between Japan and China on 7 November 2023. An apostille granted by China's competent authority can therefore replace the former consular legalisation (notarisation at a notary office → authentication by China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs etc. → consular legalisation at the Japanese embassy in China). The flow of preparing Chinese documents with an apostille is covered in detail in [Mainland China's shift to apostille and inheritance/company documents](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/chuka-tairiku-apostille-ikou-souzoku-kaisha-shorui).

Taiwan is not a party to the Hague Convention, so an apostille cannot be used. Taiwanese documents generally follow a flow of being notarised in Taiwan and then authenticated by the Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association (Taipei Office / Kaohsiung Office), the Japan-side window. The translation is likewise notarised/authenticated. For both, the latest handling may change at each window, so proceed with the date of reference confirmed.

| Home country | Authentication framework | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Mainland China | Apostille (in effect Japan-China 2023-11-07) | China's competent authority |
| Taiwan | Notarisation + Exchange Association authentication (not a Hague party) | Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association (Taipei/Kaohsiung) |

## What kinds of cases are prone to refusal?

Because support of elderly parents is a non-notified discretionary judgement, it is a type prone to refusal where the proof is weak. Typically unfavourable is a case where another relative (such as a sibling) able to support the parent is alive in the home country. If there is someone able to support in the home country yet the necessity of bringing them to Japan cannot be sufficiently shown, the "necessity of support" is hard to recognise. Likewise, where it cannot be objectively shown that the Japan-side supporter's income/assets suffice to support the parent, the proof of means is apt to be assessed as weak.

Further, non-notified Designated Activities is in principle not the subject of a certificate-of-eligibility application (bringing from abroad); it tends to be considered by a change-of-status application after entering on a short-term stay—a procedural constraint. This depends on immigration's operation, so using the latest guidance with the date of reference confirmed is indispensable. Whether a particular case is approved is immigration's judgement, and this article does not guarantee that outcome.

| Unfavourable circumstance | Reason |
|---|---|
| A relative able to support is alive in the home country | Necessity of support hard to show |
| Japan-side means insufficient | Proof of means weak |
| Purpose of support unclear | Weak showing of purpose of entry |
| Procedural constraint | Non-notified is in principle outside the certificate of eligibility |

## FAQ

**Q. Can I bring a parent under the Dependent status?**
A. No. The family the Dependent status (Immigration Control Act, Appended Table I(4)) admits is limited to a spouse and children receiving support; parents are not included. To consider bringing a parent, one explores a different framework—Designated Activities (support of elderly parents). This is a non-notified discretionary judgement and is not necessarily granted.

**Q. Does the Designated Activities for support of elderly parents have fixed approval requirements?**
A. No clear approval criterion is published. Support of elderly parents is "non-notified Designated Activities" not set out in a Ministry of Justice notification; immigration may exceptionally permit residence by comprehensively considering the individual circumstances. Factors such as the parent being elderly and alone, there being no relative able to support in the home country, and the Japan side having the means to support are said to be considered, but these are practitioners' experience and meeting them does not guarantee approval.

**Q. How does authentication of home-country documents differ for China and Taiwan?**
