**In short:** a shop sign may require a permit to display an outdoor advertisement, depending on its location and size. Outdoor-advertisement regulation is framed by the Outdoor Advertisement Act (Act No. 189 of 1949), while the actual prohibited areas, permit areas, size and height standards, and permit validity are set by the outdoor-advertisement ordinances of each prefecture, ordinance-designated city and so on. Article 3 of the Act lets a prefecture prohibit display and installation in designated areas or on designated objects by ordinance for good landscape/scenic preservation or to prevent public danger; Article 4 lets it require the prefectural governor's permit and impose other necessary restrictions by ordinance; and Article 5 lets it set, by ordinance, standards for the shape, area, colour, design and other methods of display. The sign-installing advertising business is subject to the registration of the outdoor advertising business set up by ordinance under Articles 9 and 10, including the appointment of a business manager at each office. This article is general information and does not make the final judgment on whether a given sign needs a permit or meets the standards. ## Which signs need an outdoor-advertisement permit?

An outdoor advertisement is something displayed outdoors to the public, continuously or for a certain period, such as a signboard, standing sign, poster or placard, and an advertising tower, advertising board, or one mounted on or displayed on a building or other structure (Article 2(1) of the Outdoor Advertisement Act). A shop's wall sign, projecting sign, free-standing sign, banner, or standing sign at the storefront can all be outdoor advertisements. Whether a permit is needed depends on the location (which area) and the size/height, and the specifics follow the ordinance that governs the place.

| Example of a sign | Is it an outdoor advertisement? | Rough guide to the permit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wall sign / projecting sign | Yes | A permit is often needed depending on location and size |
| Free-standing sign / advertising tower | Yes | The larger the height/area, the more likely a permit is needed |
| Banner / storefront standing sign | Yes | The ordinance may treat it as a simple advertisement with its own rules |
| A display facing inside the shop | Often not, as it is not displayed outdoors to the public | Check the ordinance |

Even a "self-use advertisement" showing your own shop's name or business is not automatically outside the regulation. Some areas set an exemption up to a certain area by ordinance, but the standards differ by area. It is a separate system from other shop-opening filings (for a restaurant, for example, [the restaurant business permit and facility standards](https://luck428.com/legal/column/inshokuten-eigyo-kyoka-hokenjo-setsubi-kijun-shokuhin-eisei)), so a sign must be checked as a sign. ## What are the prohibited/permit areas and the size and height standards?

The Act frames by ordinance a division of areas and places into "prohibited" and "permit (restricted)". Article 3 lets a prefecture set by ordinance the areas and places where display/installation is prohibited for good landscape/scenic preservation (paragraph 1), the prohibition of display on objects such as bridges, street trees, statues and monuments (paragraph 2), and prohibition where necessary to prevent public danger (paragraph 3). Article 4 lets it require the governor's permit and impose other restrictions for areas other than prohibited ones, and Article 5 lets it set, by ordinance, standards for the shape, area, colour, design and other display methods and for the shape and other installation methods of mounting objects.

| Article of the Act | What the ordinance may set |
| --- | --- |
| Article 3 | Prohibited areas/places, prohibited objects (bridges, street trees, etc.), prohibition to prevent danger |
| Article 4 | Areas requiring a permit, the governor's permit and other restrictions |
| Article 5 | Standards for size (area), height, shape, colour, design and other display methods |
| Article 7 | Orders to stop display/installation of violating advertisements, removal and other necessary measures |

So specific numerical standards such as "up to how many square metres" or "up to how many metres above ground", and which use-zones fall under prohibition or permit, are not uniform nationwide; you must check the outdoor-advertisement ordinance governing the installation site directly. In landscape districts under the Landscape Act, and around cultural properties or in natural parks, the treatment can be stricter. Because judging conformity to the standards involves reading the ordinance, an administrative scrivener assists within the scope of application support. ## Where do you apply, and what about validity and renewal?

The application goes to the prefecture, ordinance-designated city, core city, or landscape-administration body that governs the installation site, and the window, forms, attachments (location map, drawings, design drawings, documents on structure or strength, etc.), validity period and renewal treatment are set by ordinance. A permit has a validity period, and to keep displaying after it expires a renewal permit is generally required, but the length differs by ordinance.

| Item to check | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Where to apply | The prefecture / ordinance-designated city / core city / landscape-administration body governing the site |
| Attachments | Location map, vicinity map, design drawing, dimensions, documents on structure or strength, etc. (as specified by ordinance) |
| Validity | The period set by ordinance (differs by area); continuing after expiry often needs a renewal permit |
| Change / removal | Filings and procedures for changing content or removing are also set by ordinance |

Because the specific number of years of validity and the fee amount differ by ordinance, this article does not give a uniform figure. It is surest to check the ordinance and the latest practice for the planned site just before writing (our policy is not to write unverified figures). ## What is the "registration of the outdoor advertising business" needed by the installer?

The outdoor advertising business is the business of displaying outdoor advertisements or installing the objects on which they are mounted (Article 2(2) of the Outdoor Advertisement Act). A prefecture may, by ordinance, set up a registration system requiring a person who intends to run an outdoor advertising business to be registered with the governor (Article 9). That ordinance is to set the validity period of the registration, the registration requirements, the appointment of a business manager, and revocation of registration or business suspension (Article 10(1)), with standards such as a validity period of five years and the appointment of a business manager at each office (Article 10(2)). A sign-installation contractor who makes and installs signs as a business is subject to this registration.

| Position | Procedure that may be needed |
| --- | --- |
| The shop/business putting up a sign | Permit to display the outdoor advertisement (ordinance under Article 4) |
| The advertising business making/installing the sign | Registration of the outdoor advertising business (ordinance under Articles 9 and 10) |
| Each office of the registered business | Appointment of a business manager (from course-completers, exam-passers, etc.) |

The shop side's "display permit" and the installer side's "registration of the outdoor advertising business" are separate procedures. Even when outsourcing the sign, the display permit generally involves the person who displays the advertisement (the shop side), so organising who needs which procedure before proceeding reduces mix-ups. An administrative scrivener also supports the registration application and matters relating to the business manager. ## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a permit even for a sign that just shows my shop's name?**
A. It depends on the location and size. Even a self-use advertisement showing your shop's name or business needs a permit if it is in a permit area under an ordinance based on Article 4 of the Outdoor Advertisement Act and exceeds the size set by ordinance. Some areas exempt up to a certain area, but the standards differ by area, so check the outdoor-advertisement ordinance governing the site.

**Q. Are banners and storefront standing signs also regulated?**
A. Banners and standing signs can be outdoor advertisements (Article 2(1) of the Outdoor Advertisement Act). Posters, placards and standing signs may be restricted by ordinance as simple advertisements as to method and place, and violating items can be removed (Article 7). Where placed on a pavement or road, separate rules such as road-occupancy apply, so check the site's ordinance and the manager's treatment.

**Q. If I ask a contractor to install the sign, will the contractor also handle the permit?**
A. Not necessarily. The "display permit" borne by the shop side (ordinance under Article 4) and the "registration of the outdoor advertising business" borne by the installer (ordinance under Articles 9 and 10) are separate procedures. The display permit involves the person who displays the advertisement, so organising who handles which procedure before the contract is safe. Support for drafting and submitting the application is done by an administrative scrivener as an independent business.

**Q. What happens if I put up a sign without a permit?**
A. For an advertisement violating an ordinance, the prefectural governor and others may order the display/installation to stop, or set a reasonable period and order removal or other necessary measures (Article 7 of the Outdoor Advertisement Act). Simple violating items may be subject to immediate removal. Penalties such as a non-penal fine may be set by ordinance, so we recommend checking whether a permit is needed before installing.
