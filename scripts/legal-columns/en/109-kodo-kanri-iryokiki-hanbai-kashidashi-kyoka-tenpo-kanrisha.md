**In short:** to sell or lease contact lenses and other highly controlled medical devices, you need a permit from the prefectural governor for each business office (Article 39(1) of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act). The permit requires premises that meet the standards and a qualified business-office manager. Controlled medical devices such as hearing aids need only a notification.

**Information checked: 11 October 2026.** This article organises the sales and lease permit for highly controlled medical devices, covering the premises (business office) requirements and how to appoint a business-office manager, from the stage of choosing a property. The basis is the Act on Securing Quality, Efficacy and Safety of Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, etc. (Act No. 145 of 1960) and its enforcement regulation, with provisions checked on e-Gov.

## What permit is needed to sell or lease highly controlled medical devices?

Article 39(1) provides that anyone who sells, delivers or leases highly controlled medical devices (including specially controlled maintenance devices) as a business must obtain a permit for each business office. Under Article 39(2), the permit is granted by the prefectural governor for the office's location (in cities and special wards with a public health centre, the mayor or ward head is the point of contact).

Medical devices are classified by risk, and the procedure differs by the class handled.

| Category | Approximate class | Example | Sale/lease procedure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Highly controlled medical device | Class III/IV | Contact lenses | Permit | Article 39 |
| Controlled medical device | Class II | Hearing aids | Notification | Article 39-3 |
| General medical device | Class I | Steel instruments | Generally none | — |

Non-corrective colour contact lenses are also treated as highly controlled medical devices. Under Article 39(6), the permit must be renewed every six years or it lapses. Confirm which class your products fall into with the prefectural pharmaceutical-affairs section before applying.

## Who can serve as the business-office manager? (qualifications and training)

Article 39-2(1) requires a permit holder to appoint, for each business office, a business-office manager who meets standards set by ordinance. The qualification standard is in Article 162 of the enforcement regulation; paragraph (1), item (i) lists a person who, after three or more years of experience in sales-related work, has completed a basic course run by a party registered with the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (there are also provisions for those recognised as equivalent, such as doctors and pharmacists).

Article 168 of the enforcement regulation further requires the seller or lessor to have the office manager take annual continuing training run by a party that has notified the Minister. Securing the manager's experience and course records, and planning the yearly training, from the hiring and placement stage makes the application smoother.

| Item to confirm | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Sales work history (three years or more as a guide) | Whether the Article 162 standard is met |
| Basic-course completion certificate | Proof of the qualification |
| Schedule for yearly continuing training | The Article 168 training obligation |

Qualification categories differ by the products handled (highly controlled, specially controlled, etc.). Confirm the current standards and eligible courses in the prefectural review criteria.

## What is required of the premises and storage?

One permit standard is the premises. The Article 39 permit standard provides that a permit may be withheld where the office's premises do not meet the ordinance standard (Article 4 of the Regulation on Structure and Equipment of Pharmacies, etc.). In general, the premises must store devices hygienically and safely and keep them separate from other goods. For a leased property, the key points are area, partitioning and storage space, and whether you can prepare the site location sketch and floor plan to attach to the application.

Detailed numeric standards and attachment forms differ by municipality. At the viewing stage, confirm that you can secure a storage area and that the use does not conflict with zoning or building rules, and do preliminary checks at the pharmaceutical-affairs desk before signing. We assist with property selection and preliminary viewing checks, but the final judgement on whether the premises meet the standard is made by the authority receiving the application.

## How does notification for controlled medical devices differ?

The sale or lease of controlled medical devices (excluding specially controlled maintenance devices) requires only a notification to the prefectural governor for the office's location, under Article 39-3(1). Rather than a prior review like a permit, it is a procedure of notifying the prescribed items. Hearing aids are a cited example. General medical devices (Class I) generally require no special procedure for sale or lease.

That said, even for controlled medical devices, other obligations may apply by category, such as appointing an office manager (e.g. a manager for specially controlled devices) or a best-effort duty for continuing training. "Notification means nothing else is needed" is not correct. Correctly determining the category of the products handled is the starting point.

## How long does the permit take, and what does it cost?

The permit application fee is set by prefectural ordinance and regulation, and the amount differs by municipality. The standard processing period for the review is also shown in each municipality's review criteria. In general the process includes preparing the application documents, the office floor plan, confirming the manager's qualification and an on-site check; deficient documents lengthen it.

Confirm the exact fee, standard processing period and list of required documents in the latest guidance of the prefecture (or public-health-centre city or special ward) with jurisdiction over the office, before applying. This article does not give fixed amounts or days (the variation between municipalities is large, and we have not verified them here, so they are treated as unverified).

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a permit if I only sell contact lenses?**
A. Yes. Both corrective and non-corrective colour contact lenses are highly controlled medical devices, and selling or leasing them as a business needs an Article 39(1) permit for each business office.

**Q. Must I hire a business-office manager from outside?**
A. If the qualification standard is met (Article 162 of the enforcement regulation: three or more years of experience and completion of the basic course), the owner or an existing employee may serve. Annual continuing training (Article 168) is also required.

**Q. If I only sell online, do I still need premises?**
A. You need a business office that serves as the sale or lease base, and you obtain the permit for that office. Controlled medical devices, excluding specially controlled maintenance devices, require a notification. Mail-order display rules also apply, so confirm with the receiving authority.

**Q. How long does the permit take to be granted?**
A. The standard processing period is set in the municipality's review criteria, and the amount also varies by municipality. It depends on whether the documents and premises are ready. This article does not give fixed days or costs.
