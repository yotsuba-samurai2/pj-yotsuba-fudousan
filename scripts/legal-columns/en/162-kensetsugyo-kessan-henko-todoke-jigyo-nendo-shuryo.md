**In short:** a business that has obtained a construction business licence must file a "settlement change notification (business-year-end report)" each time a business year ends. The basis is Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Construction Business Act, and the deadline is within four months after the end of each business year. This is not a procedure you may skip for your own convenience; it is required even in a year with no work and while the business is dormant. If you forget it, the unfiled years must be filed together before the five-yearly renewal of the licence or the addition of a business type (such as an ippan/tokutei new application) will be accepted, and a false or long-unfiled report can become the object of a supervisory disposition or penalty. Note too that the deadlines differ by procedure: changes to the trade name, office, capital or officers are due within 30 days of the change (Article 11, paragraph 1), while a change of the managing officer or the full-time engineer is due within two weeks (paragraphs 4 and 5). ## What, and by when, do you file the settlement change notification (business-year-end report)?

The settlement change notification is a notification that a licensed business files, under Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Construction Business Act, within four months after the end of each business year. It is also called the "business-year-end report" or the "settlement report," but these point to the same thing. For a corporation with a March settlement the guide is the end of July, and for an individual with a December settlement the end of April of the following year; confirm with the filing authority how a due date falling on a closed day is handled.

The main documents show the work performed that business year and the content of the settlement. Because the content changes with the licensing authority (a Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism licence or a prefectural governor's licence) and with whether you are a corporation or an individual, confirming the latest form in the filing authority's guide is the premise.

| Main document | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Work history | The record of work undertaken that business year (contract amount, period, etc.) |
| Amount of work performed in each of the past three business years | Amount by business type and prime/sub contract |
| Financial statements (construction-use) | Balance sheet, profit-and-loss statement, etc., in the form set by the Construction Business Act Enforcement Regulations |
| Business report | Required for a stock company |
| Tax certificates, etc. | Business tax and others as set by the authority |

Even in a year with no work, you must file it as "no record," and while dormant the notification duty remains as long as you keep the licence. ## How does not filing it affect renewal and the addition of business types?

The settlement change notification is less a self-contained procedure than the foundation for the next application — the five-yearly renewal of the licence, or the addition of a business type or an ippan/tokutei new application. The authority that accepts a renewal or an addition presumes that the settlement change notifications of the past business years are all in. If a year is unfiled, you are first asked to file that part, and if they have piled up you end up creating them all at once at the time of renewal, raising the risk of missing the deadline.

| Situation | What happens if the settlement change notification is unfiled |
| --- | --- |
| Five-yearly licence renewal | The renewal application is not accepted unless the unfiled business years are filed |
| Addition of business type / ippan-tokutei new application | Likewise, the premise is that the immediately preceding notifications are in |
| Business-condition evaluation (bidding for public works) | Filing of the settlement change notification is a premise; unfiled, you cannot proceed to the evaluation |
| False entry / long non-filing | Can become the object of a supervisory disposition (instruction, business suspension, etc.) or penalty under the Construction Business Act |

A business that takes part in bidding for public works proceeds, on the foundation of each year's settlement change notification, to the business-condition evaluation. ## How do the Construction Business Act financial statements differ from the tax settlement?

The financial statements attached to the settlement change notification differ in both form and accounts from the settlement documents of the tax return filed with the tax office. The tax settlement is made under the rules of accounting and tax law, but the Construction Business Act financial statements follow the form set by the Act's Enforcement Regulations and must be reorganized into construction-specific accounts such as "completed-work revenue," "completed-work cost," "uncompleted-work expenditure" and "work accounts payable."

| Category | Tax settlement | Construction Business Act financial statements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Corporation Tax Act, Income Tax Act, etc. | Construction Business Act and the form of its Enforcement Regulations |
| Professional who prepares it | Tax accountant (as part of the tax return) | Administrative scrivener reorganizes it into the form (our office) |
| Accounts | General sales, purchases, etc. | Completed-work revenue, completed-work cost, uncompleted-work expenditure, etc. |
| Filed with | Tax office | The construction licensing authority |

## By when must you file a change notification when officers, trade name or office change?

Apart from the settlement change notification, when a matter entered in the licence application changes you need a "change notification." The deadline splits by the kind of change. Article 11, paragraph 1 of the Construction Business Act requires a notification within 30 days of the change when there is a change to the trade name, the name or location of the office, the capital amount, the names of officers, and so on (the matters corresponding to Article 5, items 1 to 4).

On the other hand, when the managing officer, or the full-time engineer at each office, is replaced or retires, a notification is required within two weeks (Article 11, paragraphs 4 and 5). The managing officer and the full-time engineer are the licence requirement itself, so a shorter deadline is set to avoid a gap.

| Content of change | Deadline for notification | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trade name, office name/location, capital amount, officers | Within 30 days of the change | Article 11, paragraph 1 |
| Replacement/retirement of the managing officer or full-time engineer | Within two weeks of the change | Article 11, paragraphs 4 and 5 |
| Settlement for each business year (settlement change notification) | Within four months after the end of each business year | Article 11, paragraph 2 |

Note that the change in the company's registration (officer change, trade-name change, head-office relocation, etc.) and the construction-licence change notification are separate procedures. Registration is handled by the judicial scrivener as an independent business, and the administrative scrivener handles the construction-licence change notification based on that registration. Either one alone is not enough.

## FAQ

**Q. Is the settlement change notification required even in a year with no work?**
A. Yes. The settlement change notification (business-year-end report) is a duty to file within four months after the end of each business year as long as you keep the licence (Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Construction Business Act); a year with no work is filed as "no record," and while dormant it is filed as long as you hold the licence. If you forget it, you end up creating it together at renewal or type addition, with a risk of missing the deadline. Confirm the individual need with the licensing authority's guide.

**Q. I have let several years of settlement change notifications pile up. Will my licence be revoked?**
A. An unfiled notification does not immediately lead to revocation, but a false entry or long non-filing can become the object of a supervisory disposition or penalty under the Construction Business Act. In practice you are asked to file the unfiled years together before renewal or type addition. How many years to put in order, and how, depends on the situation, so consult the licensing authority and the administrative scrivener who prepares the documents early.

**Q. If I ask a tax accountant for the settlement, will they also file the settlement change notification?**
A. They are separate procedures. The tax accountant prepares the settlement for the tax return, but the financial statements attached to the settlement change notification must be reorganized into accounts such as completed-work revenue in the form of the Construction Business Act Enforcement Regulations and filed with the licensing authority together with the work history. The administrative scrivener handles this part as an independent business, and the tax judgement is made by the tax accountant. You contract with each separately.

**Q. When an officer changes, by when and what do I notify?**
A. A change of officers is filed within 30 days of the change (Article 11, paragraph 1 of the Construction Business Act). In addition, if that officer was the managing officer, a notification on the successor must be made within two weeks (paragraphs 4 and 5). The company's officer-change registration is by the judicial scrivener and the construction-licence change notification by the administrative scrivener, and you proceed by contracting with each separately.
