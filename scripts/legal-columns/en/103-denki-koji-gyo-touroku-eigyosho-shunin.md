**In short:** to undertake and run an electrical construction business, you generally need registration under the Act on the Proper Operation of Electrical Construction Businesses (Act No. 96 of 1970). If you do electrical work on general electrical installations (general electrical work), you become a registered electrical contractor (Article 3 of the Act); if you do only work on self-use electrical installations, you become a notifying electrical contractor who notifies by 10 days before starting the business (Article 17-2). Where a construction operator with a construction-business licence under Article 3, paragraph 1 of the Construction Business Act runs an electrical construction business, the registration provisions do not apply and the operator is treated as a deemed registered electrical contractor (by notification) or a deemed notifying electrical contractor (by notification) (Article 34). An office that does general electrical work must place, as a chief electrician, a first-class electrician or a person who, after receiving a second-class electrician's licence, has three or more years of practical experience in electrical work (Article 19). This article is general information and does not judge or guarantee whether a given business falls under registration or notification, or whether it meets the qualification requirements. Support for the application (registration, deemed registration, notification) is handled by the administrative scrivener of the Yotsuba group as an independent business; commercial registration for company formation and officer changes is for a judicial scrivener; securing the office premises (office and storage for materials and equipment) is for a real-estate agent, a separate business; and tax is for a tax accountant. The judgment of a worker's qualification under the Electricians Act is within the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Industrial Safety and Inspection Department, each contracted separately.

## What is the difference between "registration", "deemed registration" and "notification" for an electrical construction business?

The starting point is "which electrical installations you handle". The Act on the Proper Operation of Electrical Construction Businesses (the "Electrical Construction Business Act") requires registration of a business doing general electrical work on general electrical installations such as those in ordinary homes and small shops (Article 3), and notification of a business handling only self-use electrical installations such as high-voltage factories and buildings (Article 17-2). For a construction operator with a construction-business licence, a special rule (deemed registration / deemed notification) replaces registration and notification (Article 34).

| Category | Installations handled | Procedure (basis) | Chief electrician |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Registered electrical contractor | Does general electrical work (self-use also allowed) | Registration; 5-year term (Article 3) | Required |
| Notifying electrical contractor | Only self-use electrical work | Notify by 10 days before starting (Article 17-2) | Not required |
| Deemed registered electrical contractor | Licensed construction operator doing general electrical work | Notification (Article 34, paragraph 4) | Required |
| Deemed notifying electrical contractor | Licensed construction operator doing only self-use electrical work | Notification (Article 34, paragraph 5) | Not required |

The destination for registration and notification is the governor of the prefecture where the office is located if you set up an office in only one prefecture, and the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry (the head of the Industrial Safety and Inspection Department) if you set up offices spanning two or more prefectures. Because the procedure splits by which installations you handle and where your offices are, confirming with the authority for the office location at an early stage is essential. Note that "registration" and "the qualification to do the work" differ: to work on site you need an Electricians Act qualification such as a first-class or second-class electrician.

## How do the procedures change if you hold a construction-business licence?

Where a construction operator with a licence under Article 3, paragraph 1 of the Construction Business Act runs an electrical construction business, the special rule in Article 34 of the Electrical Construction Business Act applies and the registration provisions (Chapter 2) do not apply. A licensed construction operator is treated as a registered electrical contractor if it does general electrical work (paragraph 2), and as a notifying electrical contractor if it does only self-use electrical work (paragraph 3). Being "treated as" is not enough on its own: a notification is required when the electrical construction business starts, changes or ends (paragraph 4) or, for self-use only, a notification under paragraph 5.

| Situation | Required procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Licensed operator starts an electrical business including general work | Notification as a deemed registered electrical contractor (Article 34, paragraph 4) |
| Licensed operator starts a self-use-only electrical business | Notification as a deemed notifying electrical contractor (Article 34, paragraph 5) |
| The licence number/date changes on construction-licence renewal | Notification of change |
| An existing registered contractor obtains a construction-business licence | The former registration loses effect (Article 34, paragraph 6) = switch to deemed status |

What to watch is that even under deemed registration, the duty to place a chief electrician applies to an office doing general electrical work. Also, the construction-business licence itself (needed, for example, where the contract price of a single electrical work is 5 million yen or more, tax included) is a separate system from registration under the Electrical Construction Business Act. This is work the same administrative scrivener handles, but it is a different issue, so please also see [the requirements for a construction-business licence (managing responsible person, full-time engineer, financial basis)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kensetsugyo-kyoka-yoken-keikan-sengi-zaisan). Where demolition is involved, [the difference between demolition-business registration and a construction-business licence](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kaitai-koji-gyo-touroku-kensetsu-kyoka-chigai) is also a separate procedure.

## What qualifications and experience does the chief electrician placed at each office need?

Article 19, paragraph 1 of the Electrical Construction Business Act requires a registered electrical contractor to place a chief electrician at each office (a specified office) that does general electrical work. A chief electrician must be a first-class electrician, or a person who, after receiving a second-class electrician's licence, has three or more years of practical experience in electrical work.

| Item | Content (basis) |
| --- | --- |
| Where placed | Each office doing general electrical work (Article 19, paragraph 1) |
| Qualification | First-class electrician / second-class electrician with 3+ years of practical experience after the licence |
| Exception when self-engaged | An office where the representative, etc. meets the requirements and mainly engages themselves is treated differently (Article 19, paragraph 2) |
| When vacant | Appoint a new one within two weeks of the vacancy (Article 19, paragraph 3) |

Because the chief electrician manages the general electrical work, concurrently serving at multiple offices or at another electrical contractor is, in principle, not allowed. Whether someone holds the qualification and how practical experience is counted are judgments under the Electricians Act; an administrative scrivener does not make that judgment on your behalf as an exclusive matter. Whether the licence and experience can be substantiated is a point to confirm before applying.

## How far are the measuring instruments and safety setup at an office required?

Article 24 of the Electrical Construction Business Act requires an electrical contractor to keep at each office an insulation-resistance meter and other instruments prescribed by Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry ordinance. The required instruments change with the installations handled.

| Type of office | Instruments to keep (METI ordinance) |
| --- | --- |
| Office doing general electrical work | Insulation-resistance meter, earth-resistance meter, circuit tester (able to measure resistance and AC voltage) |
| Office doing self-use electrical work | The above plus a low-voltage voltage detector, high-voltage voltage detector, relay test device and dielectric-strength test device |

In addition, there is a duty to display a sign stating the name and registration number at each office and work site (Article 25), and a duty to keep and retain a ledger at each office (Article 26). The specific items and forms of the instruments are set by METI ordinance and local guides, and the final conformity of the model number or quantity is judged by the authority. The fact that "it was like this in another prefecture" does not apply directly is a point often at issue in registration practice.

## What happens if you forget the registration's term, renewal or change notification?

The term of a registered electrical contractor's registration is five years (Article 3, paragraph 2 of the Electrical Construction Business Act). If you wish to continue the electrical construction business after the term expires, you must obtain a renewal registration (paragraph 3). If you forget to renew and the term lapses, the registration loses effect, and you cannot continue to operate without registration.

| Procedure | Deadline/content (basis) |
| --- | --- |
| Term of registration | Five years (Article 3, paragraph 2) |
| Renewal registration | Required to continue after the term (Article 3, paragraph 3) |
| Notification of change to registered matters | Within 30 days of the change (Article 10) |
| Notification of discontinuance | When the electrical construction business is discontinued |

When there is a change to registered matters such as the name, office or chief electrician, you must notify within 30 days of the change (Article 10). Operating without registration, operating while neglecting notification, and operating with a lapsed renewal are subject to penalties. The names, forms and deadlines of the procedures differ in detail by local government, so managing the expiry date and notifying promptly when a change arises is the key in practice.

## FAQ

**Q. Which should I obtain, the electrical-business registration or the construction-business licence?**
A. They serve different purposes. Registration (or notification/deemed status) under the Electrical Construction Business Act is the procedure needed to run an electrical construction business. The construction-business licence is a separate system needed, for example, where the contract price of a single electrical work is 5 million yen or more (tax included), and holding it does not remove the need for notification under the Electrical Construction Business Act. Judge by both the installations handled and the contract amount.

**Q. Can I register with only a second-class electrician?**
A. An office doing general electrical work needs a chief electrician, and for a second-class electrician this requires three or more years of practical experience in electrical work after the licence (Article 19 of the Electrical Construction Business Act). An office without someone who meets the requirement cannot place a chief electrician and lacks the premise for registration. How practical experience is counted is a judgment under the Electricians Act.

**Q. Do I need registration even if I only undertake electrical work for high-voltage buildings and factories?**
A. If you do only electrical work on self-use electrical installations, you become a notifying electrical contractor rather than registering, and you notify by 10 days before starting the business (Article 17-2 of the Electrical Construction Business Act). In that case there is no duty to place a chief electrician, but on-site work requires an Electricians Act qualification (such as a certified electrical worker or a special-type electrical worker).

**Q. What happens if I forget to renew and the term lapses?**
A. The registration's term is five years, and continuing requires renewal (Article 3 of the Electrical Construction Business Act). If you forget to renew, the registration lapses and you cannot continue to operate without registration. Managing the expiry date and renewing with time to spare is important. When registered matters change, a notification within 30 days is also required (Article 10).
