**In short:** a social welfare corporation is a corporation established under the Social Welfare Act for the purpose of conducting social welfare services (Article 22 of the Social Welfare Act). Unlike a stock company or a general incorporated association, establishment requires the approval of the articles of incorporation by the competent authority (the governor of the prefecture where the principal office is located; or the ward mayor/city mayor of a special ward or city where the business is completed within that area — Article 30 of the Social Welfare Act), under Article 31 of the Social Welfare Act, and the corporation must hold the assets necessary to conduct social welfare services (Article 25 of the Social Welfare Act). Councillors, a councillors' board, directors, a board of directors and auditors must be placed (Article 36 of the Social Welfare Act); there must be six or more directors and two or more auditors (Article 44, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act), and councillors exceeding the number of directors — that is, seven or more if there are six directors (Article 40, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act); a specified social welfare corporation above a certain scale must also place an accounting auditor (Article 37 of the Social Welfare Act). ## How does a social welfare corporation differ from a stock company, an NPO, or a general incorporated association?

A social welfare corporation is defined in Article 22 of the Social Welfare Act as "a corporation established, as provided in this Act, for the purpose of conducting social welfare services." It is a corporate form with strong public-interest and non-profit character, meant for conducting social welfare services — such as services for persons with disabilities, elderly care, childcare, and the operation of public-assistance facilities — as its principal business. This is the major difference from a stock company, which may pursue profit, and from an NPO or a general incorporated association, whose establishment procedures differ.

The biggest procedural difference is "how the corporate status comes into being." A social welfare corporation is formed through the approval of the competent authority (Article 31 of the Social Welfare Act) and the registration of establishment (Article 34 of the Social Welfare Act). By contrast, an NPO is a system in which you receive the "certification" of the competent authority and then register; its establishment flow is organized in [establishing an NPO — the flow from the certification application to registration and the competent authority](https://luck428.com/legal/column/npo-hojin-setsuritsu-ninsho-nagare-shokatsucho). A general incorporated association follows the principle of norms and can be established with only notarization of the articles and registration, requiring no approval or certification ([establishing a general incorporated association — non-profit-type articles and two members](https://luck428.com/legal/column/ippan-shadan-houjin-setsuritsu-hieiri-teikan)). The approval flow for a medical corporation that provides healthcare is treated in [the approval flow and required documents for establishing a medical corporation](https://luck428.com/legal/column/iryohojin-setsuritsu-ninka-nagare-shorui). This article focuses on the social welfare corporation, whose asset requirements and councillors' board are especially heavy.

| Corporate form | How the corporate status arises | Main governing law |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social welfare corporation | Establishment approval of the competent authority + registration | Social Welfare Act |
| Stock company | Notarization of articles + registration (norm principle; profit allowed) | Companies Act |
| General incorporated association | Notarization of articles + registration (norm principle) | General Incorporated Associations and Foundations Act |
| NPO (specified non-profit activity corporation) | Establishment certification of the competent authority + registration | Act on Promotion of Specified Non-profit Activities |

## How large must the basic property and asset requirements for establishment be?

A social welfare corporation must hold the assets necessary to conduct social welfare services (Article 25 of the Social Welfare Act). These "necessary assets" are set in the articles as basic property and are examined at the heart of the competent authority's approval review. The thinking splits according to whether the corporation operates facilities.

A corporation operating facilities (such as a support facility for persons with disabilities or a special nursing home) is, in principle, required to own the real estate (land and buildings) devoted to the facility and make it basic property. There is also an exceptional treatment of securing it by loan or use permission from the state or a local government, but in any case the premise is that the property devoted to the business is stably secured. By contrast, for a corporation that does not operate facilities — such as one providing only home-based services — there is a review standard requiring it to hold a certain amount or more of assets as basic property. However, the concrete amount required and the method of valuing the assets are set by the competent authority's review standard and manual, and also differ by the type of business. This article does not assert individual amounts that cannot be verified. When you concretely consider establishment, be sure to confirm the latest review standard and manual issued by the competent authority of the planned location.

| Type of corporation | Main thinking on assets (basic property) |
| --- | --- |
| Corporation operating facilities | In principle owns the real estate devoted to the facility (makes it basic property) |
| Corporation with home-based services only | Secures a certain amount or more of assets as basic property (confirm the concrete amount in the competent authority's review standard) |
| Common | Holds the assets necessary to conduct social welfare services stably (Article 25 of the Social Welfare Act) |

## How should the directors, auditors and councillors' board be composed?

A social welfare corporation must place councillors, a councillors' board, directors, a board of directors and auditors (Article 36 of the Social Welfare Act). The 2016 (Heisei 28) reform of the Social Welfare Act strengthened the governance of the corporation's management organization, and since the enforcement on 1 April 2017 (Heisei 29) the councillors' board has been positioned as the highest decision-making body that must be placed. The councillors' board resolves on important matters such as the appointment and dismissal of officers, amendment of the articles, and approval of the financial statements.

There are lower limits on numbers. There must be six or more directors and two or more auditors (Article 44, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act), and councillors must exceed the number of directors (Article 40, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act), so if there are six directors there must be seven or more councillors. It is a three-layer structure in which the board of directors decides the execution of business, the auditors audit the directors' execution of their duties, and the councillors' board supervises that. Further, a specified social welfare corporation whose business scale exceeds the standard set by Cabinet Order must place an accounting auditor (Article 37 of the Social Welfare Act). In the operation as of November 2026, corporations whose business-activity revenue of the previous year exceeds 3 billion yen or whose liabilities exceed 6 billion yen are the target, but because a staged expansion of the scope has been under consideration, confirm the latest Cabinet Order and notices. Details such as restrictions on concurrent posts of each body and limits on the number of specially related persons need to be confirmed in the competent authority's review standard.

| Body | Number / basis for placement | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directors | Six or more | Article 44, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act |
| Auditors | Two or more | Article 44, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act |
| Councillors | A number exceeding the directors (seven or more if six directors) | Article 40, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act |
| Councillors' board | The highest decision-making body that must be placed (from 1 April 2017) | Article 36 and Article 45-8 of the Social Welfare Act |
| Accounting auditor | Must be placed by a specified social welfare corporation | Article 37 of the Social Welfare Act |

## In what order does it proceed from the approval application to the registration of establishment?

The overall flow is: draw up the articles, receive the approval of the competent authority, and then register the establishment to form the corporation. First the founders prepare the articles and arrange the contribution of assets and the composition of the bodies (councillors and officers). Next, they apply to the competent authority of the location of the principal office (the prefectural governor, or the ward mayor/city mayor of a special ward or city where the business is completed within that area — Article 30 of the Social Welfare Act) for approval of the articles for establishment (Article 31 of the Social Welfare Act). The competent authority examines the assets, business plan, composition of bodies and the like.

Once approval is received, the establishment is registered at the location of the principal office. A social welfare corporation is formed by this registration of establishment (Article 34 of the Social Welfare Act). The registration must be done promptly after the arrival of the approval document, and the registration application itself is the business of a judicial scrivener. After formation, procedures continue, such as transferring the title of the contributed basic property to the corporation. Because a considerable preparation period is needed from the pre-application consultation to approval, it is important to confirm the schedule of the competent authority of the planned location early.

| Stage | Main content | Basis / who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ① Preparing articles and asset contribution | Articles, business plan, arrangement of assets and bodies | Founders / administrative scrivener (document preparation) |
| ② Application to the competent authority for establishment approval | Approval application and review of the articles | Articles 31 and 30 of the Social Welfare Act |
| ③ Registration of establishment | Register at the principal office and form the corporation | Article 34 of the Social Welfare Act / judicial scrivener |
| ④ Procedures after formation | Title transfer of basic property, various notifications | Judicial scrivener, tax accountant, labor and social security attorney, etc. |

## FAQ

**Q. Where do you apply to establish a social welfare corporation?**
A. The application destination for establishment approval (the competent authority) is, in principle, the governor of the prefecture where the principal office is located. However, if the business the corporation conducts is completed within the area of a single special ward or city, that ward mayor or city mayor is the competent authority (Article 30 of the Social Welfare Act). In certain cases, such as a wide-area business spanning several prefectures, the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare may be the competent authority. Confirm which competent authority the planned location falls under with the local government of the planned location.

**Q. What is the minimum number of directors, auditors and councillors?**
A. There must be six or more directors and two or more auditors (Article 44, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act), and councillors must exceed the number of directors (Article 40, paragraph 3 of the Social Welfare Act). If there are six directors, there must be seven or more councillors. Since 1 April 2017 (Heisei 29), the councillors' board has been the highest decision-making body that must be placed. Details of composition, such as limits on the number of specially related persons, need to be confirmed in the competent authority's review standard.

**Q. Can it be established with only home-based services and no facility?**
A. A corporation providing only home-based services can also be established, but for a corporation that does not operate facilities there is a review standard requiring it to hold a certain amount or more of assets as basic property. The concrete amount required and the method of valuing the assets are set by the competent authority's review standard and manual and also differ by business type. This article does not assert individual amounts; we recommend confirming with the latest standard of the competent authority of the planned location.

**Q. Can you start the business immediately after receiving establishment approval?**
A. Receiving approval alone does not form the corporation. The social welfare corporation is first formed when the establishment is registered at the location of the principal office (Article 34 of the Social Welfare Act). The registration is the business of a judicial scrivener. Also, to start each individual social welfare service (such as services for persons with disabilities or care), separate designation or permission for each business may be required. The final eligibility is by the examination of the competent authority.
