**In short:** when you inherit a building on leased land (a building with a land-lease right), the heir succeeds, from the time the inheritance opens, as a matter of course to all the rights and duties that belonged to the decedent's property (Civil Code Article 896). The land-lease right (a lease of land) is likewise succeeded by inheritance, and because this is not a "transfer" to a third party, the landowner's consent required for the assignment or sublease of a lease (Civil Code Article 612) is in principle unnecessary, and the "name-transfer fee" paid to the landowner is generally understood to carry no legal obligation as a matter of course. The landowner's consent is required in "transfer" situations — a bequest or lifetime gift to someone other than an heir, or a sale to a third party after inheritance — where Land and Building Lease Act Article 19 (permission for the assignment or sublease of a land lease = a borrowing-land non-contentious case) is relevant. A rebuild or extension/renovation after inheritance turns on whether the contract has a special clause restricting extension/renovation; where such a clause exists and consent is not obtained, you seek permission in substitution for consent by the Article 17 adjudication of the Land and Building Lease Act (change of lease conditions and permission for extension/renovation). If the ground rent is to be raised, you think through it under Article 11 (right to claim an increase or decrease of ground rent). ## When you inherit a land-lease right, do you really need the landowner's consent or a name-transfer fee?

The conclusion is that succession of a land-lease right by inheritance requires, in principle, no consent from the landowner (the lessor). This is because the heir succeeds, from the time the inheritance opens, to all the rights and duties that belonged to the decedent's property (Civil Code Article 896). A land-lease right is a lease of land (or a superficies), a right that belonged to the decedent's property, so it passes to the heir as a matter of course by inheritance. This differs in nature from an "assignment or sublease" in which the lessee moves the lease to a third party by their own will.

The assignment or sublease of a lease requires the lessor's consent, and doing so without consent can lead to rescission of the contract (Civil Code Article 612). Inheritance, however, is a comprehensive succession that arises by operation of law rather than by the person's own will, so it is generally understood not to fall under the "assignment" of Article 612. Accordingly, even if the landowner demands a "name-transfer fee (name-change fee)," it is thought that there is no legal obligation as a matter of course to pay it for a succession by inheritance. A name-transfer fee is not money fixed by law but has been paid by custom as consideration for consent to an assignment.

| Situation | Landowner's consent | Name-transfer / consent fee | Basis / reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Succession by inheritance | In principle unnecessary | No legal obligation of course | Civil Code Article 896 (comprehensive succession) |
| Sale to a third party (assignment) | Required | May be sought as consideration for consent | Civil Code Article 612; Land and Building Lease Act Article 19 |
| Bequest / lifetime gift to a non-heir | Required | May be sought | Civil Code Article 612 |

That said, even though consent is unnecessary, notifying the landowner of the inheritance and updating the lessee's name and contact details is useful for avoiding trouble. ## If the landowner demands a renewal fee or consent fee, how should you think about it legally?

A renewal fee or consent fee is not money whose payment the law obliges as a matter of course. The Land and Building Lease Act has no provision imposing an obligation to pay a renewal fee or consent fee either. They have been paid against the background of a special clause in the contract, the parties' agreement, or local custom. So what to confirm first when demanded is the contents of the contract: whether the lease has a special clause on a renewal fee or consent fee, and if so, on what conditions and amount.

Even when a "name-transfer fee" is demanded in a succession-by-inheritance situation, as above, inheritance is not an assignment, so it is generally understood that there is no legal obligation of course. On the other hand, paying a certain sum by agreement to continue the relationship amicably is also an option. Specific legal judgments — whether to pay, what amount is appropriate, how to evaluate the validity of a special clause — and negotiations, mediation, or litigation over amounts are a lawyer's domain. The administrative scrivener provides general legal information on whether payment is required and supports drafting an agreement or memorandum once an agreement is reached.

## After inheritance, how far is the landowner's consent needed to rebuild or extend/renovate (and whom do you consult if it is refused)?

Whether the landowner's consent is needed for a rebuild or extension/renovation turns first on whether the lease has a "special clause restricting extension/renovation (a ban on extension/renovation, etc.)." Without such a clause, an extension/renovation of the building can in principle be done within the scope of the lessee's rights. Where there is such a clause, obtaining the landowner's consent under it is the right course, but where the parties fail to agree on an extension/renovation that should be deemed reasonable in the ordinary use of the land, the court may, on the lessee's application, grant permission in substitution for the landowner's consent (Land and Building Lease Act Article 17(2)). This is a non-contentious proceeding called a "borrowing-land non-contentious case," in which the court judges considering the remaining term of the lease, the state of the land, the prior course of the lease, and all other circumstances (Article 17(4)).

Also, where there is a lease condition restricting the type, structure, scale, or use of the building, and owning a building different from that condition has become reasonable due to a change of circumstances but the parties fail to agree, the court can change the lease conditions (Article 17(1)). These are dispute-resolution proceedings requiring an application to the court, a lawyer's domain. The administrative scrivener supports drafting notices, agreements, and memoranda with the landowner about extension/renovation and rebuilding, and hands over to a lawyer at the stage of proceeding to a borrowing-land non-contentious case where consent is not obtained. Because proceeding with an extension/renovation without consent risks a claim of rescission as a breach of the special clause, it is important to follow the proper order.

| Situation | Need for consent | Procedure if not obtained | Basis / referral |
|---|---|---|---|
| No extension/renovation-restriction clause | In principle within one's rights | — | Prior notice is useful |
| Extension/renovation-restriction clause exists | Obtain consent under the clause | Adjudication of permission in substitution for consent | Land and Building Lease Act Article 17(2); lawyer |
| Lease condition on building type/use exists | A condition change may be needed | Adjudication of lease-condition change | Land and Building Lease Act Article 17(1); lawyer |

## What should the notice to, and agreement with, the landowner contain?

Even though the landowner's consent is in principle unnecessary in inheritance, leaving the landowner unaware that the lessee has changed invites inconveniences — the ground rent has no known addressee, and future renewal or rebuild discussions sour. So, practically, notifying the landowner that the inheritance has occurred and who the new lessee (heir) is, and ideally keeping a written record, is advisable. The notice states the fact of the decedent's death, the description of the building and land on the leased land, the heir's name and contact details, the fact of succeeding to the lessee's position, and how the ground rent will be paid going forward.

Where several heirs inherit jointly, clarifying who succeeds to the land-lease right (the result of estate division) and consolidating the single payment window for the rent avoids confusion. While estate division is unsettled, the land-lease right is co-owned (quasi-co-owned) by the heirs. Where you confirm and agree on conditions with the landowner, keep them as an agreement or memorandum. Drafting these notices, agreements, and memoranda is the administrative scrivener's document-preparation work. However, where the content of an agreement greatly affects future rights and duties, or where you are in conflict with the landowner, the negotiation itself is a lawyer's domain.

## If asked to raise the ground rent, how should an heir respond (and to whom should it be referred)?

The ground rent can be increased or decreased, going forward, by either party when it has become unreasonable due to an increase or decrease of taxes and other public charges on the land, a rise or fall in land prices or other changes in economic circumstances, or in comparison with the rent of nearby similar land (Land and Building Lease Act Article 11(1)). However, where there is a special clause not to increase the rent for a certain period, that clause governs. Even if the landowner seeks an increase, the heir is not obliged to comply at once. Where the parties fail to agree on an increase, until a judgment affirming the increase becomes final, it suffices to pay the ground rent in an amount the payer deems reasonable (Article 11(2)).

So, upon receiving a demand for an increase, first confirm the previous rent, the contract's special clause, the surrounding market, and the trend of public charges, and consider the reasonableness. If the discussion does not settle, you proceed to civil mediation (disputes over an increase or decrease of ground rent are, in some situations, subject to mediation first) or litigation, which are a lawyer's domain. The administrative scrivener supports organizing the facts underlying the discussion and drafting an agreement once one is reached. Where there are several heirs, it is also important for the heirs to sort out who bears the ground rent and how.

| Issue | Reasoning | Referral |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of an increase claim | Public charges, land prices, economic circumstances, rent of nearby similar land | Land and Building Lease Act Article 11(1) |
| When the parties fail to agree | It suffices to pay a reasonable amount until the judgment is final | Article 11(2) |
| Mediation / litigation | Resolving the dispute over increase or decrease | Lawyer |
| Drafting an agreement | Putting the agreed conditions in writing | Administrative scrivener |

## FAQ

**Q. I inherited a house on leased land. Do I have to pay the landowner a name-transfer fee?**
A. Inheritance is a comprehensive succession under Civil Code Article 896 and is not a "transfer" to a third party, so the consent required for assigning a lease (Civil Code Article 612) is in principle unnecessary, and a name-transfer fee is generally understood to carry no legal obligation of course. Still, it is useful to notify the landowner of the inheritance and the new lessee. Specific judgments on whether to pay and how to evaluate a special clause, and negotiations, are a lawyer's domain.

**Q. After inheriting, I want to rebuild the old house. Is the landowner's consent needed?**
A. First confirm whether the contract has a special clause restricting extension/renovation. Without one it can in principle be done within your rights, but where there is such a clause and the parties fail to agree, there is a borrowing-land non-contentious proceeding to seek the court's permission in substitution for the landowner's consent (Land and Building Lease Act Article 17(2)). Because proceeding without consent risks a claim of rescission, consult a lawyer where consent is not obtained.

**Q. The landowner said, "Now that you've inherited, pay a renewal fee." Am I obliged to pay?**
A. A renewal fee is not money the law obliges of course; it depends on a special clause, agreement, or custom. First confirm whether the contract has a renewal-fee clause and its conditions. The succession by inheritance itself carries no obligation of course to pay a name-transfer fee. Judging whether payment is required and the validity of a clause, and negotiating the amount, are a lawyer's domain; the administrative scrivener supports drafting an agreement once one is reached.

**Q. There are several heirs. Whose land-lease right does it become?**
A. Until estate division is complete, the land-lease right is co-owned (quasi-co-owned) by all heirs. Decide who succeeds to it in the estate-division discussion, and consolidating the ground-rent payment window avoids confusion. Drafting the deed of estate division is by an administrative scrivener, the inheritance registration by a judicial scrivener, and mediation/adjudication if it becomes a dispute by a lawyer, each as an independent business under a separate contract.
