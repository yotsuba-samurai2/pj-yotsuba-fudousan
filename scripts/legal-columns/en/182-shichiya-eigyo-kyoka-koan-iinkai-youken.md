**In short:** to open a pawnshop, you must obtain the licence of the Prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the location, for each business office (Pawnbroker Business Act (Act No. 158 of 1950) Article 2(1); accessed 2026-11-20). A pawnshop business is a business of lending money by taking goods in pawn and attaching a clause that, where repayment is not received by the pledge-forfeit deadline, the pawned article is applied to repayment (Article 1(1)). The licence cannot be obtained if any of the statutory grounds for disqualification (Article 3(1), items (i) to (xi)) apply. Examples are a person who has not passed three years after finishing serving a sentence of imprisonment or heavier, an undischarged bankrupt, or a person who does not meet the storage-facility standards (Article 7). It is a licence separate, in both its governing law and the content of the business, from the secondhand-dealer licence (Secondhand Articles Dealer Act) for buying, selling, or exchanging secondhand goods. This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a particular licence is possible and on whether a ground for disqualification applies rests with the Public Safety Commission, the licensing authority, and qualified professionals. ## How does a pawnshop licence differ from a secondhand-dealer licence?

Pawnbroker Business Act Article 1(1) defines a pawnshop business as "a business of lending money by taking goods in pawn and attaching a clause that, where repayment is not received by the pledge-forfeit deadline, the pawned article is applied to repayment." That is, taking an item in custody as security and, in return, lending money; where principal and interest are not repaid by the deadline (the pledge-forfeit deadline), applying the item to repayment (ownership passing to the pawnshop) is the pawnshop business. A pawnbroker who has obtained the licence is called a "pawnshop" under the Act (Article 1(2)).

By contrast, a secondhand dealer operates under a licence based on the Secondhand Articles Dealer Act, buying, selling, exchanging, or selling on consignment goods that have been used once or, though unused, have been traded (secondhand goods). Both are licensed by the Prefectural Public Safety Commission, but the governing law differs, and the content of the business differs. A pawnshop is a business that "takes goods in pawn and lends money," and a secondhand dealer a business that "buys, sells, or exchanges secondhand goods." The basic division is a secondhand-dealer licence for a business that buys used goods and sells them, and a pawnshop licence for a business that lends money against goods as security. Where both are carried on, each licence may be required. The requirements for a secondhand-dealer licence are set out in [Requirements and the business office for a secondhand-dealer licence](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kobutsusho-kyoka-eigyosho-yoken).

| Axis of comparison | Pawnshop business | Secondhand dealer |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | Pawnbroker Business Act | Secondhand Articles Dealer Act |
| Content of business | Takes goods in pawn and lends money | Buys, sells, or exchanges secondhand goods |
| Licensing authority | Prefectural Public Safety Commission | Prefectural Public Safety Commission |
| Effect of passing the deadline | Acquires ownership of the pawned article on passing the pledge-forfeit deadline (Article 18(1)) | — |

Although a pawnshop lends money, a pawnshop business—which takes security called the pawned article and lends at the business office—is governed by rules separate from the money-lending business that requires registration under the Money Lending Business Act. It differs in the applicable law from the [registration requirements, net assets, and chief of the money-lending business](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kashikin-gyo-touroku-yoken-shunin-junshisan), which lends widely without security and without a store. Which licence or registration is needed must be judged in line with the actual facts of the business.

## What grounds for disqualification prevent the licence?

The Public Safety Commission must not grant the licence where the applicant falls under any item of Article 3(1) (items (i) to (xi)) of the Pawnbroker Business Act. The representative ones are as follows: a person who has been sentenced to imprisonment or heavier and has not passed three years after finishing serving it or after ceasing to be subject to it (item (i)); a person whose residence is not fixed (item (iii)); a person who, because of a mental or physical disorder, cannot carry on the pawnshop business properly as prescribed by Cabinet Office ordinance (item (iv)); an undischarged bankrupt (item (vi)); and a person whose licence was revoked under Article 25(1) and who has not passed three years from the day of revocation (item (vii)).

For a corporation, the licence cannot be obtained where an officer who carries on its business falls under a ground for disqualification (item (x)). Also, a person who would place at the office a manager falling under a ground for disqualification (item (ix)), and a person who does not meet the storage-facility standards of Article 7(1) (item (xi)), cannot obtain the licence. Before refusing the licence, the Public Safety Commission must give the applicant the opportunity to state an opinion and submit evidence (Article 3(2)), and where it refuses, it notifies in writing with reasons attached (Article 3(3)).

| Ground for disqualification (main) | Basis |
|---|---|
| A person within three years after serving a sentence of imprisonment or heavier | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(i) |
| A person whose residence is not fixed | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(iii) |
| An undischarged bankrupt | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(vi) |
| A person within three years from revocation of the licence | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(vii) |
| A corporation with a disqualified officer carrying on its business | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(x) |
| A person who does not meet the storage-facility standards | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(xi) |

The presence of a ground for disqualification is checked for the applicant, the officers, and the manager, respectively. The individual judgement of whether you fall under a ground for disqualification is made by the Public Safety Commission on the basis of the submitted documents. As the Act and the Cabinet Office ordinance may be amended, confirm the latest provisions and the handbook of the Prefectural Police Headquarters and Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction before applying.

## What material requirements must the store and storage facilities meet?

Because a pawnshop takes customers' items in custody, storage facilities that protect the pawned articles from fire, theft, and the like are a requirement. The Public Safety Commission may set standards for the storage facilities necessary to prevent fire, theft, and other accidents for the custody of pawned articles (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 7(1)), and where standards are set, the pawnshop must provide storage facilities in accordance with those standards (Article 7(3)). Because the specific specifications of the storage facilities (the structure of the warehouse, fire- and theft-resistance performance, etc.) are set by each Prefectural Public Safety Commission, confirmation is needed for each jurisdiction.

As for the store (business office) itself, confirmation from the standpoint of real estate transactions—such as the lease terms, the right to use, and the relationship with the use zone and surroundings—is indispensable. The selection and lease brokerage of the premises is a real estate broker's work, not an administrative scrivener's work. The requirements for the store premises are set out, on the real estate side, in [Requirements for pawnshop store premises and the Public Safety Commission licence](https://luck428.com/en/column/shichiya-eigyo-bukken-youken-koan-iinkai-kyoka). Yotsuba Real Estate Co., Ltd. and Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office are separate businesses, and these are handled under separate contracts.

| Material requirement | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Storage facilities | Facilities meeting standards to prevent fire, theft, and other accidents | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 7 |
| Content of facility standards | Set by each Prefectural Public Safety Commission (confirm per jurisdiction) | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 7(1) |
| Store premises | Confirmation of the right to use, lease, and use is the real estate side's domain | Real Estate Brokerage Act |

Where the storage facilities do not meet the standards, it falls under the ground for disqualification in Article 3(1)(xi), and the licence cannot be obtained. Because the specifications differ by jurisdiction, confirming the required storage-facility standards with the Prefectural Police Headquarters and Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction before signing the premises contract is important to avoid backtracking.

## In the licence application, what documents do you submit and where?

A person who intends to become a pawnshop must, by the procedure prescribed by Cabinet Office ordinance, obtain the licence of the Prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the location, for each business office (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 2(1)). In practice, it is common for the application window to be via the police station with jurisdiction over the office. The application shows, in the prescribed forms and attached documents, that the applicant (and, for a corporation, the officers) and the manager do not fall under a ground for disqualification and that the storage facilities meet the standards. Where you set up an office you do not manage yourself, you must appoint a manager (Article 2(2)).

When the Public Safety Commission grants the licence, it issues a licence certificate (Article 8). Because the forms of the application, the attached documents, and the fees are set by Cabinet Office ordinance and by each prefecture, do not fill in amounts or forms by guesswork; confirm the handbook of the Prefectural Police Headquarters and Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction just before applying. Confirming at the window any figures or requirements you cannot substantiate is important to avoid remand.

## What continuing duties—records, manager, pledge forfeit—follow after opening?

Obtaining the licence is not the end. A pawnshop must keep records in the form prescribed by Cabinet Office ordinance and, at each pawn contract, return of the pawned article, and disposal of a forfeited article, enter the date of the contract, the item name, quantity, and features of the pawned article, the address, name, occupation, age, and features of the pledgor, the method of confirmation, the date of return or disposal, and the like (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 13). These records are kept for three years from the day of the last entry (Article 14(1)). Where the records are damaged, lost, or stolen, the pawnshop must immediately report to the police station chief with jurisdiction (Article 14(2)).

The rules on pledge forfeit are also important. A pawnshop must post, inside the business office, the interest rate, the method of calculating interest, the pledge-forfeit deadline, business hours, and the like (Article 16(1)), and the pledge-forfeit deadline must not be set at a period of less than three months from the day the pawn contract is formed (less than one month where the pledgor is a dealer in goods and pawns goods it deals in; Article 16(2)). A pawnshop acquires ownership of the pawned article when the pledge-forfeit deadline passes (Article 18(1)). The pledgor may, at any time before the pledge-forfeit deadline, repay principal and interest and redeem the pawned article (Article 17(1)). Where there is a breach of duty, the Public Safety Commission may revoke the licence or order suspension of business for up to one year (Article 25(1)).

| Main duty after opening | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Entry in records | Entry of prescribed matters at each contract, return, and disposal | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 13 |
| Keeping records | Kept for three years from the day of the last entry | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 14(1) |
| Posting | Post the interest rate, interest-calculation method, pledge-forfeit deadline, business hours, etc. | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 16(1) |
| Pledge-forfeit deadline | Must not be less than three months from formation of the pawn contract | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 16(2) |
| Acquisition of forfeited article | Acquires ownership of the pawned article on passing the deadline | Pawnbroker Business Act Article 18(1) |

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need both a pawnshop licence and a secondhand-dealer licence?**
A. It depends on the content of the business. A pawnshop business that takes goods in pawn and lends money needs a pawnshop licence (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 2(1)); a business that buys, sells, or exchanges secondhand goods needs a secondhand-dealer licence (Secondhand Articles Dealer Act). Where you carry on both—for example, selling articles acquired by pledge forfeit—each licence may be required. Which licence is needed is judged by the Public Safety Commission in line with the actual facts of the business.

**Q. If I have a past criminal penalty, can I not obtain a pawnshop licence?**
A. Not uniformly. A person who has been sentenced to imprisonment or heavier and has not passed three years after finishing serving it or after ceasing to be subject to it falls under a ground for disqualification (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 3(1)(i)). If three years have passed, this item does not apply. However, whether another item applies is judged individually, together with the rest, by the Public Safety Commission on the basis of the submitted documents.

**Q. Can I set the pledge-forfeit deadline as short as I like?**
A. No. The pledge-forfeit deadline must not be set at a period of less than three months from the day the pawn contract is formed (Pawnbroker Business Act Article 16(2)). Where the pledgor is a dealer in goods who pawns goods it deals in, it must not be less than one month. The interest rate, interest-calculation method, pledge-forfeit deadline, business hours, and the like must be posted inside the business office (Article 16(1)).

**Q. Can I ask an administrative scrivener to find store premises and arrange the lease?**
A. No. Selecting the store premises and brokering the lease is a real estate broker's work, not an administrative scrivener's work. What the administrative scrivener handles is support for preparing the licence application (documents submitted to a public agency) to the Public Safety Commission. The premises are handled by a real estate broker, accounting and tax by a tax accountant, and disputes by an attorney, each as an independent business under a separate contract.
