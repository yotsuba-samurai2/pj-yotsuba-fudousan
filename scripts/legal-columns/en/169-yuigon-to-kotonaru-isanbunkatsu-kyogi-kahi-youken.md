**In short:** even where there is a valid will, the general view is that an estate division differing from the will can be made if everyone concerned agrees. The premise is that the decedent did not prohibit division of the estate in the will (Civil Code Article 908(1)). Co-heirs may, except where division was prohibited by will, divide the estate by agreement at any time (Article 907(1)). Those who must join the agreement are all the heirs, plus, where there is a devisee other than an heir, that devisee (Civil Code Articles 964, 990), and, where there is an executor, that executor's consent (Articles 1012, 1013). A devisee may renounce the devise (Article 986), and the renounced portion vests in the heirs (Article 995). On tax, where all heirs agree by their protocol to divide differently from the will, it is viewed that the devisee-heir in effect renounced the devise and an estate division was carried out among the co-heirs, so in principle no gift tax arises (National Tax Agency Q&A example, "Division of an estate differing from the content of a will and gift tax"). This article is general information; the interpretation of a particular will, whether a division is possible, and the propriety of taxation rest with a qualified professional and the parties. ## With a will in place, can an estate-division agreement of different content be made at all?

In some cases it can. The Civil Code provides that co-heirs may, except where the decedent prohibited division by will (Article 908(1)) or where they contracted not to divide, divide the whole or part of the estate by their agreement at any time (Article 907(1)). Where the will designates a share of inheritance or designates a method of division (a so-called "inherit"-type will) and only heirs benefit, those heirs may freely dispose of the shares they once obtained by the will. So where all the heirs, knowing the content of the will, agree to "make it a different division," an estate-division agreement differing from the will is generally said to come into being.

Conversely, there are cases where it cannot, or where care is needed. Where the decedent clearly prohibited estate division by will (Article 908(1); a period not exceeding five years from the opening of inheritance), there can be no division by agreement during that period. Also, where there is a devise giving property to a third party other than an heir, the heirs cannot exclude that devisee and divide among themselves. Scenes where the validity of the will itself, or its revocation or conflict, is in issue are a separate topic, sorted out in [Revocation and conflict of wills, and the priority of multiple wills](https://luck428.com/legal/column/yuigon-tekkai-teishoku-fukusu-yuigon-yuretsu).

## Who must join the agreement (heirs, devisees, executor)?

To validly bring about a division differing from the will, the key is not to miss "whose consent is needed."

| Party | How they are involved | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Heirs | Agreement of all is needed | Civil Code Article 907(1) |
| Devisee other than an heir | That person's consent, or renunciation of the devise, is needed | Civil Code Articles 964, 986, 990 |
| Executor | If there is one, obtaining consent is the safe course | Civil Code Articles 1012, 1013 |
| Decedent's prohibition of division | No division by agreement during the prohibited period | Civil Code Article 908(1) |

Where there is a devisee other than an heir, if that devisee renounces the devise (Article 986), the renounced property vests in the heirs (Article 995; but if the will shows a different intention, that is followed), and the heirs can divide it by their agreement among all of them. Because a universal devisee has the same rights and duties as an heir (Article 990), they become a party to the division agreement itself. The scope of heirs is backed by the family register. Confirming that everyone is present presupposes the register collection and heir investigation covered in [Can you make an estate-division agreement yourself?](https://luck428.com/legal/column/isan-bunkatsu-kyougisho).

## Where there is an executor, how does their consent come into it?

Where there is an executor, care is needed. An executor has the rights and duties to do all acts necessary to realise the content of the will, such as managing the inherited property (Civil Code Article 1012(1)). And where there is an executor, the heirs cannot dispose of the inherited property or do other acts that would obstruct the execution of the will, and acts in breach are void (though they cannot be asserted against a good-faith third party; Article 1013(1),(2)). A division differing from the will may, depending on the view, amount to an "act obstructing the execution of the will." For that reason, where there is an executor, agreeing after obtaining that executor's consent is the safe course. The executor's role is summarised in [Who becomes the executor? Duties and appointment](https://luck428.com/legal/column/yuigon-shikkosha-shokumu-sennin-dare).

That said, how to treat the validity of a division agreement differing from the will made by all heirs without the executor's consent is one on which lower-court rulings are split, and it is not a situation where an established Supreme Court precedent can be asserted (this point is held over as unverified). That is precisely why, in practice, securing the executor's consent is the prudent course. Whether there is an executor, how to obtain that consent, and whether the validity is in doubt in a particular case are scenes involving legal judgement, and if there is a dispute they fall within the domain of an attorney and the court. An administrative scrivener supports preparing the agreement where the agreement of all heirs (and the necessary devisees and executor) is reached, but does not judge the propriety of validity nor act for negotiation.

## When you divide differently from the will, how are inheritance tax and registration treated?

The tax treatment must be understood separately from the civil-law possibility. Where all heirs divide the estate differently from the content of the will by their protocol, the National Tax Agency's Q&A example, "Division of an estate differing from the content of a will and gift tax," states that it may be viewed that the devisee-heir in effect renounced the devise and an estate division was carried out among the co-heirs, and that no gift tax arises among the heirs. Inheritance tax is calculated according to what each person actually acquired. The Tax Answer (No. 4176) is to the same effect, explaining that gift tax is not imposed as a gift from the devisee-heir to the other heirs.

Care is needed where, having once completed inheritance registration and the inheritance-tax filing per the will, the estate is then redivided afresh. This scene is treated as a "redo of the estate division (re-division)," and the redistribution may be deemed a gift or exchange and become subject to gift tax or capital-gains tax. If you are considering a division differing from the will, fixing the policy among all before the registration and filing is the safe course. The division of the taxation of a redo is covered in [Can an estate-division agreement be redone? Rescission by agreement, nullity and cancellation](https://luck428.com/legal/column/isan-bunkatsu-kyogi-yarinaoshi-goui-kaijo). Registration falls under the duty to apply for inheritance registration within three years of knowing of the acquisition (Real Property Registration Act Article 76-2) and is a judicial scrivener's work, and the judgement on inheritance and gift tax is a tax accountant's work. Our office does not give tax consultation or file registrations. For the individual tax amount and treatment, be sure to consult a tax accountant.

## FAQ

**Q. Even with a valid will, can all heirs make a different division?**
A. Generally, it is organised that an estate division differing from the will can be made if all heirs agree. The premise is that the decedent did not prohibit division in the will (Civil Code Article 908(1)). Heirs may, absent a prohibition, divide by agreement at any time (Article 907(1)). If there is a devisee other than an heir, obtaining that consent, and if there is an executor, that executor's consent, is the safe course.

**Q. What happens where there is a will devising to someone other than an heir?**
A. The heirs cannot exclude that devisee and divide among themselves. If the devisee renounces the devise (Civil Code Article 986), the renounced property vests in the heirs (Article 995), and the heirs can divide it by agreement among all. Because a universal devisee has the same rights and duties as an heir (Article 990), they are a party to the division agreement.

**Q. If there is an executor, can one not divide differently at will?**
A. Where there is an executor, the heirs cannot do acts obstructing the execution of the will, and acts in breach are void (Civil Code Article 1013(1),(2)). A division differing from the will may touch on this, so obtaining the executor's consent is the safe course. The validity of an agreement without consent is split among lower courts and is not an established precedent (unverified).

**Q. Does dividing differently from the will incur gift tax?**
A. Where all heirs agree to divide differently from the will, it is viewed that the devisee in effect renounced the devise and an estate division was carried out, so in principle no gift tax arises (National Tax Agency Q&A example). However, if you redivide after once completing registration and filing per the will, it may be deemed a gift as a "redo." Consult a tax accountant on the judgement.
