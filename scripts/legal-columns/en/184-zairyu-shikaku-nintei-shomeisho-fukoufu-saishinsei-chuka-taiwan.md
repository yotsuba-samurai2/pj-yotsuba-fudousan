**In short:** when a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is not issued, the basic practice is first to confirm the reason for non-issuance, judge whether the cause can be resolved, then reinforce the supporting materials and reapply. The Certificate of Eligibility is a document by which, under the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act (Cabinet Order No. 319 of 1951), the Minister of Justice examines in advance whether a foreign national intending to land meets the status-of-residence category and the landing-permission criteria, and issues it when recognizing conformity; its basis is Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act Article 7-2. ## When does a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) get a non-issuance?

The Certificate of Eligibility is a document in which, before entry, the Minister of Justice confirms that a foreign national intending to land in Japan falls under a status of residence—such as for work or bringing over family—and meets the landing-permission criteria. Its basis is Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act Article 7-2; the examination looks at whether the activity to be carried on in Japan is not false, whether it falls under one of the activities or statuses listed in Appended Tables I and II of the Act, and whether it meets the landing-permission criteria (Immigration Services Agency guidance; accessed 2026-11-21).

Non-issuance occurs when these requirements cannot be confirmed. For example, where the substance of the activity or the continuity of the accepting organization's business is not sufficiently shown; where the backing for income or assets is lacking; where there are inconsistencies among the submitted materials; or where there are circumstances relating to past residence status or grounds for denial of landing. Because the lacking point differs by requirement, identifying which requirement one stumbled on is the starting point for rebuilding. For the [Business Manager status of residence, the 2025 amendment revised requirements such as capital and full-time employees](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/keiei-kanri-zairyu-2025-kaisei-shihonkin-joukin-shokuin-chuka-taiwan), and for [the Highly Skilled Professional, the points calculation directly affects whether issuance is possible](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kodo-senmonshoku-1go-point-keisan-chuka-taiwan). What should be reinforced changes with the status of residence.

| Points prone to non-issuance | Requirement examined |
|---|---|
| Substance of the activity; continuity of the accepting organization | Status-of-residence category |
| Backing for income, assets, and support capacity | Conformity with landing-permission criteria |
| Consistency among submitted materials | That the application is not false |
| Past residence status; grounds for denial of landing | Conformity with requirements set by law |

## Are you told the reason for non-issuance? How do you confirm it?

On non-issuance, a Certificate of Eligibility non-issuance notice is delivered, in which the reason for non-issuance is generally stated briefly. However, the wording of the notice is often general, and it can be hard to read from it exactly which part of which requirement was lacking. A more specific reason can be confirmed at the window of a regional immigration office in some operations, but as there are indications that the opportunity to receive an explanation of the reason is limited, it is important to record accurately, on the spot, what you confirmed (accessed 2026-11-21; operation may differ by office and period).

Confirming the reason is the premise for deciding the direction of the reapplication. If you decide to "just resubmit the same content" from the general wording of the notice alone, you may again receive a non-issuance for the same reason. Match the reason you could confirm against the content of the application already submitted, and organize which material for which requirement must be supplemented to suffice. We do not fill in a reason we cannot substantiate by guesswork, nor assert circumstances we have not confirmed.

| Step of confirmation | Content |
|---|---|
| Statement in the non-issuance notice | Confirm the general reason for non-issuance (wording is often brief) |
| Confirming the reason at the window | Confirm and record the specific reason at a regional immigration office |
| Matching with the submitted content | Identify which material for which requirement was lacking |

## Can you reapply with the same content? What should you reinforce?

In principle, if the cause of non-issuance can be resolved, reapplication is possible. However, merely resubmitting the same materials as before tends to lead to the same conclusion. In a reapplication, it is important to prepare, in response to the reason for non-issuance, a statement of reasons or supplementary explanation and additional materials that supplement the proof that was lacking, and also to check consistency with the content of the previous application. This is because a discrepancy in the explanation between the earlier and later applications can work unfavorably on the consistency side.

On the other hand, where the reason for non-issuance is of a nature that cannot be easily resolved—such as relating to grounds for denial of landing or a false application—a careless reapplication can work unfavorably. Whether a reapplication has meaning, whether supplementation suffices, and how to build the timing and content are judged carefully in light of the reason you could confirm. Here, the stance of not asserting whether issuance is possible and setting the direction within the range of facts you could confirm is important to avoid repeated remand.

| Documents reinforced on reapplication | Aim |
|---|---|
| Statement of reasons / supplementary explanation | Add an explanation squarely addressing the non-issuance reason |
| Additional supporting materials | Back the lacking requirement (substance, continuity, means, etc.) |
| Consistency check with the previous application | Avoid disadvantage from a discrepancy in explanation |

A Certificate of Eligibility application carries no fee and can also be filed online. The standard processing period is said to be one to three months (Immigration Services Agency guidance; accessed 2026-11-21). As the period and handling may change, confirm the latest guidance just before applying.

## Is there a route of administrative appeal or litigation? Who can represent?

The Certificate of Eligibility has the character of a procedure that confirms in advance conformity with the landing conditions. The Immigration Services Agency's guidance on this application states "Method of appeal: none" (accessed 2026-11-21), so in practice the central method is to rebuild the proof through a reapplication, rather than pushing back with an administrative appeal under the Administrative Complaint Review Act. That said, how to view the disposability of the non-issuance, and whether there is room to contest it through an appeal suit such as a revocation suit, are legal points on which views can differ and that require a case-by-case judgement.

Judging whether this dispute is possible, and representing in an administrative appeal or litigation, is an attorney's work; an administrative scrivener cannot act as agent in such appeals or litigation. What an administrative scrivener can handle is confirming the reason for non-issuance, preparing the reapplication documents (documents submitted to a public agency), and agency filing within the scope of agency filing. Where the option of a dispute comes into view, it is important to consult an attorney at an early stage and organize whether to rebuild through reapplication or through a dispute.

| Act | Who |
|---|---|
| Confirming the non-issuance reason, preparing reapplication documents, agency filing | Agency-filing administrative scrivener |
| Judging and representing whether to contest the disposability of the non-issuance by suit, etc. | Attorney |
| Representing in an administrative appeal, etc. | Attorney (an administrative scrivener cannot) |

## FAQ

**Q. Can I prepare a reapplication from the reason written on the non-issuance notice alone?**
A. The statement in the notice is often general, and it alone may not fully identify the lacking points. We recommend confirming the specific reason at the window of a regional immigration office, recording it, and then matching it against the content already submitted to organize which material for which requirement to supplement. As the opportunity to confirm the reason can be limited, it is good to come prepared (accessed 2026-11-21).

**Q. Can I bring an administrative appeal against a non-issuance?**
A. The Immigration Services Agency's guidance on this application states "Method of appeal: none" (accessed 2026-11-21). In practice, reapplication is central. How to view the disposability of the non-issuance, and whether there is room to contest it by a revocation suit, are legal points on which views can differ; judging and representing that is an attorney's work. An administrative scrivener cannot act as agent in an appeal or litigation.

**Q. What can an agency-filing administrative scrivener do for me?**
A. An agency-filing administrative scrivener who has notified the director of the regional immigration bureau can file the Certificate of Eligibility application or reapplication documents without the person's appearance. Confirming the non-issuance reason, preparing the statement of reasons and supporting materials, and agency filing are within the scope of agency filing. Representation in litigation or an administrative appeal is not included; that is an attorney's work.

**Q. Can I proceed with bringing over someone from China or Taiwan even with scant Japanese-language materials?**
A. The key is to divide the materials the accepting organization can prepare from those to be prepared in the home country, and to design how to supplement the lacking proof. The accuracy of translation and the consistency of materials also affect the examination. Because whether issuance is possible is judged individually by the authority on the basis of the submitted documents, we build the proof within the range of confirmed facts and proceed without assertion.
