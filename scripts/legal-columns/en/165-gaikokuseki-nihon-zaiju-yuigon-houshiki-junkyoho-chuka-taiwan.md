## Can a foreign national make a will in Japan? Which country's law decides the "form" of a will?

Being a foreign national is no reason you cannot make a will in Japan. What should be considered separately here is the "form" of the will (how to make it so it is formally valid) and the "formation and effect" of the will (whether it stands as a declaration of intent and what effect it has).

The form of a will is governed by a selective connection under Article 2 of the Act on the Law Applicable to the Form of Wills (Act No. 100 of 1964), which makes it valid as to form if it conforms to any of the following.

| Item | Law under which the form is valid if it conforms (Article 2 of the Act on the Law Applicable to the Form of Wills) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The law of the place of the act (the place where the will was made; if made in Japan, Japanese law) |
| 2 | The law of the country of which the testator was a national at the time of making the will or at death (national law) |
| 3 | The law of the place where the testator had a domicile at the time of making the will or at death |
| 4 | The law of the place where the testator had a habitual residence at the time of making the will or at death |
| 5 | For a will concerning real estate, the law of the location of that real estate |

This Act lets several laws be chosen so as to uphold the form of a will as far as possible. If a Chinese or Taiwanese national living in Japan makes a will in Japan, it conforms to item 1 (law of the place of the act = Japanese law), so a will made in the Japanese form (a notarial will, a holographic will, etc.) can be valid as to form. On the other hand, the formation and effect of a will are governed by the national law (Article 37(1) of the Act on General Rules for Application of Laws). Even if the form is valid under Japanese law, the substance — such as "to whom and how much can be left" — must be examined separately as a matter of the national law or the law governing inheritance. The framework of the law governing inheritance itself is organised in [Is a foreign national's inheritance under Japanese law or the national law (Article 36)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/gaikokuseki-hisouzokunin-junkyoho-tsusokuho-36jo).

## When a non-native speaker makes a notarial will, how are interpreting and witnesses handled?

A notarial will is made by a notary in the presence of two or more witnesses (Article 969 of the Civil Code). The notary makes the notarial deed in Japanese (Article 27 of the Notary Act). Where the person requesting it does not understand Japanese, the notary must have an interpreter interpret (Article 29 of the Notary Act). Where an interpreter is involved, the notary has the interpreter interpret the purport of the deed and obtains approval (Article 40 of the Notary Act).

| Point | Content (confirm in advance with the notary's office) |
| --- | --- |
| Witnesses | Two or more witnesses are needed. Presumptive heirs, legatees and their spouses or lineal relatives cannot be witnesses (disqualification under Article 974 of the Civil Code) |
| Language/interpreting | The notarial deed is made in Japanese (Article 27). A person who does not understand Japanese uses an interpreter's interpretation (Articles 29 and 40) |
| Identity/documents | Identity documents and documents showing the relation of assets and heirs. For a foreign national, confirm the status documents with the notary's office in advance |

Arranging and the need for an interpreter, who the witnesses will be, and how the day proceeds may differ by notary's office. Before the date of making, confirm the required documents and the interpreter/witness arrangements with the notary's office. A general organisation of witnesses and fees for a notarial will is in [Witnesses and fees for a notarial will and the notary's involvement](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kosei-shosho-yuigon-koshonin-shonin-hiyo). ## Can a holographic will be written in a foreign language? Can the Legal Affairs Bureau's storage system be used?

A holographic will is made by the testator writing the text, date and name in their own hand and affixing a seal (Article 968(1) of the Civil Code). The schedule of property need not be in the testator's own hand and may be typed or attached as a copy of a passbook and the like, but each page requires a signature and seal (Article 968(2)). The Civil Code does not expressly restrict the language used for a holographic will, so writing it in a foreign language is not itself immediately void. However, meeting the form (writing the full text, date and name in one's own hand, affixing a seal) and the possibility that a Japanese translation may be needed for later interpretation or procedures must be considered separately.

Using the Legal Affairs Bureau's holographic-will storage system (the Act on Storage of Wills by the Legal Affairs Bureau), a will can be deposited with the Legal Affairs Bureau (a will storage office) and the family court's probate becomes unnecessary. However, this system has strict formatting — A4 size, prescribed margins, single-sided writing, page numbers and so on — and a will outside the format is not accepted. Whether a holographic will written in a foreign language can be deposited under this storage system (acceptance or practice) has not been officially confirmed and is unverified. If you make one in a foreign language and are considering the storage system, confirm directly with the Legal Affairs Bureau (will storage office) in advance. The flow of the storage procedure is in [The holographic-will storage system and procedures after death](https://luck428.com/legal/column/jihitsu-yuigon-hokan-seido-souzoku-tetsuzuki), and the difference between a holographic and a notarial will is in [The difference between a holographic will and a notarial will](https://luck428.com/legal/column/jihitsu-kosei-yuigon).

## Is that will effective in the home country (China/Taiwan)? What happens to home-country assets?

Whether a will made in a valid form in Japan passes as-is in the home country (China/Taiwan) is a matter of the home country's law and procedures. Japan's Act on the Law Applicable to the Form of Wills is a framework for judging the formal validity in Japanese courts and procedures; recognition and enforcement in the home country depend on the home country's private international law and procedural law.

| Subject | General idea |
| --- | --- |
| Assets located in Japan | The formal validity of a will made in Japan may be judged by conformity with the items of Article 2 of the Act on the Law Applicable to the Form of Wills |
| Assets located in the home country (China/Taiwan) | Effect and recognition in the home country depend on the home country's private international law and procedural law; separate procedures, translation or authentication may be needed there |
| Recognition in the home country | Whether a Japanese notarial or holographic will can be used as-is, or authentication (notarisation, consular authentication, apostille, etc.) is required, depends on the home country's practice |

Where there is real estate or deposits in the home country, a Japanese will alone may not complete matters, and it is safer to confirm with a lawyer in the home country whether a separate will or procedure is needed there. Which assets need which country's procedure varies with the location and type of asset and the home country's law, so it cannot be asserted as "always." Individual judgement on effect and local procedures in the home country is reserved to a qualified professional (a lawyer) there. This article is general information that organises the Japanese-side drafting practice only.

## Who makes the final judgement on the content (who gets what), and whom to consult in a dispute?

The content of the will itself — "to whom, what and how much to leave" — is closely tied to the reserved portion and the law governing inheritance. In the inheritance of a foreign national, because the law governing inheritance (Article 36 of the Act on General Rules for Application of Laws) and the application of the national law are involved, how the content of the will is evaluated in future cannot be stated flatly. The formation and effect of a will are governed by the national law (Article 37), so even if the form is valid, the substantive effect of the content must be examined separately.

Where the design of the content needs a judgement on legal merits, or where a dispute has arisen or may arise among heirs, consult a lawyer. Effect and local procedures in the home country are the field of a lawyer there, inheritance tax and international taxation of a tax accountant, and inheritance registration of a judicial scrivener. The administrative scrivener handles support for drafting along an agreed and fixed policy, and organising documents, and does not make specific legal judgements or represent in disputes. ## FAQ

**Q. I am a Chinese or Taiwanese national — can I make a notarial will in Japanese in Japan?**
A. You can. The form of a will can be valid as to form if it conforms to the law of the place of the act (Japanese law) (Article 2, item 1, of the Act on the Law Applicable to the Form of Wills). The notarial deed is made in Japanese (Article 27 of the Notary Act), and a person who does not understand Japanese uses an interpreter's interpretation (Article 29 of the Notary Act). However, the point that the formation and effect of the will are governed by the national law (Article 37) must be examined separately.

**Q. Is a holographic will valid if written in my mother tongue? Can it be deposited with the Legal Affairs Bureau?**
A. Because the Civil Code does not expressly restrict the language of a holographic will, writing it in a foreign language is not itself immediately void. However, it must meet the form, such as writing the full text, date and name in one's own hand and affixing a seal. Whether a foreign-language will can be deposited under the Legal Affairs Bureau's storage system (acceptance) has not been officially confirmed, so confirm with the Legal Affairs Bureau (will storage office) in advance.

**Q. Can a will made in Japan be used as-is in China or Taiwan?**
A. Because effect and recognition in the home country depend on the home country's private international law and procedural law, a Japanese will is not necessarily usable as-is. Where there are assets in the home country, it is safer to confirm with a lawyer there whether a separate procedure, translation or authentication is needed. The individual judgement is reserved to a qualified professional in the home country.

**Q. What can I ask an administrative scrivener to do?**
A. Supporting the drafting of the will, gathering and organising the required documents, coordinating the schedule and papers with the notary's office, and organising materials to pass to each professional. Making a notarial will is for a notary, inheritance registration for a judicial scrivener, tax for a tax accountant, home-country procedures for a lawyer there, and disputes for a lawyer; the administrative scrivener does not make specific legal judgements or represent in disputes.
