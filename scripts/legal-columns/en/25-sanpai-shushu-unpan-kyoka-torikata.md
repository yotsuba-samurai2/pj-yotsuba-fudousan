**In short:** to collect and transport "another party's industrial waste" as a business (repeatedly and continuously) — waste arising from construction, demolition, transport and the like — you need a permit from the prefectural governor under Article 14, paragraph 1 of the Waste Management Act (Act on Waste Management and Public Cleansing). To obtain it, the facilities such as vehicles and containers and the applicant's ability must meet the standards set by Ministry of the Environment ordinance (Article 14, paragraph 5, item 1), the applicant must not fall under the disqualifications (item 2 of the same paragraph, which applies Article 7, paragraph 5, item 4), and the operator or officers must have completed the course run by the Japan Waste Management Association (JW Center). This article is general information organising the requirements, disqualifications, the course, the treatment of depots and transfer-storage sites, and the per-prefecture permit and renewal; it does not judge the outcome of any individual permit.

## What kind of business needs an industrial waste collection and transport permit?

Industrial waste means waste arising from business activity that falls within categories set by law, such as cinders, sludge, waste oil, waste plastics and rubble. When you collect and transport such waste on entrustment from another party, as a business (for consideration, repeatedly and continuously), this permit is required.

The dividing line is "whose waste you transport."

| Situation | Whether a permit is required |
| --- | --- |
| Transporting industrial waste discharged by another company on entrustment | A collection and transport permit is required |
| Transporting your own company's industrial waste to a disposal site yourself (in-house transport) | In principle no permit is required (but the transport and entrustment standards must be observed) |
| Transporting household refuse (municipal waste) | The industrial waste permit is not enough; a separate municipal collection and transport permit is required |

When you expand into this field from construction, demolition or transport, waste arising at the sites of a prime contractor or other companies is "another party's industrial waste" and needs a permit. ## What exactly are the "disqualifications" that block a permit?

A disqualification is a ground that, if any one of them applies, prevents you from obtaining the permit. For industrial waste collection and transport, Article 14, paragraph 5, item 2 applies Article 7, paragraph 5, item 4, mainly setting out the following.

| Category | Content (summary) |
| --- | --- |
| Mental or physical disorder | A person who, due to a mental or physical disorder, cannot perform the work properly, as prescribed by Ministry of the Environment ordinance |
| Bankruptcy | A person who has received a decision to commence bankruptcy proceedings and has not been restored to rights |
| Imprisonment | A person who was sentenced to imprisonment or a heavier penalty and for whom five years have not passed since the sentence was served or ceased to apply |
| Fine | A person fined for violating certain environment-related laws such as the Waste Management Act, for whom five years have not passed |
| Organised-crime ties | A member of an organised crime group, or one for whom five years have not passed since ceasing to be a member; a person whose business is controlled by such members |
| Dishonesty | A person with reasonable grounds to be found likely to commit a wrongful or dishonest act in the business |

The 2022 revision of the Penal Code unified imprisonment with and without labour into "imprisonment (kokin-kei)," and the provision is worded as imprisonment or a heavier penalty. Disqualifications extend not only to the applicant but also to a company's officers and certain employees. If a disqualification arises after the permit is granted, the permit is revoked, and there is a duty to notify when one arises. If you have a past criminal record, it is important to check early whether a disqualification applies.

## When and who should complete the course?

The application requires a completion certificate for the course run by the Japan Waste Management Association (JW Center). You must complete it before applying.

| Item | New permit | Renewal permit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course | Industrial waste collection and transport course (new) | Industrial waste collection and transport course (renewal) |
| Approximate days | Two days of lectures plus a completion exam | About one day of lectures plus a completion exam |
| Validity of the certificate | In principle five years | In principle two years (some municipalities treat it as five years) |
| Who attends | For a sole proprietor, the proprietor; for a company, the representative or an officer conducting the business (including an employee prescribed by ordinance) | Same as left |

If you handle specially controlled industrial waste (waste acid, waste alkali, infectious waste, etc.), you must separately complete the specially controlled industrial waste collection and transport course. The validity of the certificate and the treatment of the renewal course differ by municipality's examination standards, so check the latest guidance of the prefecture to which you apply.

## How do the site requirements change if you carry out transfer and storage?

Collection and transport is divided into "without transfer-storage" and "with transfer-storage."

- **Without transfer-storage**: waste is transported straight from the discharge site to the disposal or recycling facility without being unloaded on the way. Many new permits start from this category.
- **With transfer-storage**: waste is reloaded onto another vehicle or stored temporarily on the way. In this case, the storage site must meet standards such as enclosures and signboards, sorting by type, storage upper limits (quantity), and prevention of scattering, leakage and odour, and the examination is stricter.

A depot (parking) for the transport vehicles must, separately from the transfer-storage site, be able to show title to use (ownership or a lease) in writing. ## In which prefecture do you obtain the permit, and when is renewal due?

Article 14, paragraph 1 provides that, where you conduct only transport as a business, the permitting authority is the prefectural governor with jurisdiction over the "area where the industrial waste is loaded and unloaded." That is, for collection and transport without transfer-storage, a permit is required in the prefectures of both the loading place and the unloading place, and no permit is required in a prefecture through which you merely pass. There are also areas where a city designated by cabinet order issues its own permits.

The validity period is every five years (Article 14, paragraph 2); because it lapses with the passage of the period, you complete the renewal course before the deadline and then apply for renewal. If you are certified as meeting the standards of the system for certifying superior industrial waste disposal operators, the validity period becomes seven years.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a permit even to transport my own company's waste with my own truck?**
A. Transporting industrial waste arising from your own business to a disposal site yourself ("in-house transport") in principle does not require a collection and transport permit. However, you must observe the statutory transport standards, such as preventing scattering and leakage in transit. If you transport another party's (another company's) industrial waste on entrustment, a permit is required as a business activity.

**Q. Can I transport anywhere in Japan if I have a permit?**
A. No. For collection and transport without transfer-storage, a permit is required in each prefecture with jurisdiction over the area where the industrial waste is loaded and unloaded. Permits are required in both the prefecture where you load and the prefecture where you unload, and no permit is required in a prefecture through which you merely pass. We check the permits required along the transport route.

**Q. Who should attend the course?**
A. For a sole proprietor, the proprietor; for a company, the representative or an officer conducting the business (including an employee prescribed by cabinet order) completes the JW Center collection and transport course. The new course has two days of lectures and a completion exam, and the completion certificate has a validity period. You must complete it before applying.

**Q. I have a past fine. Can I still obtain a permit?**
A. It depends on whether a disqualification applies. For fines for violating certain environment-related laws such as the Waste Management Act, and for imprisonment or a heavier penalty, you may fall under a disqualification unless a certain period (often five years) has passed. The prefecture judges whether a disqualification applies and makes the individual decision, so we recommend checking before applying.
