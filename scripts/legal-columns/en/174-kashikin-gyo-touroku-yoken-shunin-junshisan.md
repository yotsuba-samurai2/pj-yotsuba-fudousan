**In short:** to carry on a money-lending business (including loans and guarantees to businesses), registration is required. Where offices are set up in two or more prefectures, you must be registered by the Prime Minister (the power is delegated to the Director-General of the Finance Bureau); where offices are only within one prefecture, by that prefectural governor (Money Lending Business Act Article 3(1)). The registration lasts three years and lapses unless renewed (Article 3(2)). There are three main hurdles. First, as the financial basis, net assets of at least the amount set by Cabinet Order (50 million yen) (Money Lending Business Act Article 6(1)(xiv) and (3); Money Lending Business Act Enforcement Order Article 3-2; the method of calculating net assets is set by Article 6(4) and the Enforcement Regulation). Second, a Chief of Money Lending Operations stationed full-time at each office, in a number giving a ratio of at least 1 per 50 persons engaged in the money-lending operations (Money Lending Business Act Article 12-3). Third, not falling under any ground for refusal of registration (Article 6(1)). This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a particular registration is possible and on the valuation of net assets rests with the registering authority and qualified professionals. ## Where do you apply for a money-lending "registration," and what is examined?

A person who intends to carry on a money-lending business must be registered. The place of application depends on the location of offices. Where offices are set up in two or more prefectures, registration is by the Prime Minister (in practice the power is delegated to the Director-General of the Finance Bureau with jurisdiction); where set up only within one prefecture, by the prefectural governor with jurisdiction over that location (Money Lending Business Act Article 3(1)). The registration has a term of validity and loses effect by lapse of time unless renewed every three years (Article 3(2)).

A "money-lending business" means carrying on, as a business, lending money or acting as an intermediary for lending money (including by discounting bills, sale on security, or similar methods). Not only lending to individuals but also lending to businesses, or carrying on guarantee as a business, may fall within the regulation. Carrying on a money-lending business without registration is subject to penalties. The examination looks, besides the financial basis, personnel structure and grounds for refusal set out below, at whether a structure to carry on the money-lending business properly is in place.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Applied to (two or more prefectures) | Prime Minister (delegated to Director-General of Finance Bureau) | Money Lending Business Act Article 3(1) |
| Applied to (only one prefecture) | Prefectural governor of the location | Money Lending Business Act Article 3(1) |
| Term of validity | Three years; renewal required | Money Lending Business Act Article 3(2) |
| Pillars of examination | Financial basis, chief, grounds for refusal, operating structure | Money Lending Business Act Article 6, etc. |

Preparing the application documents (documents submitted to a public agency) is an administrative scrivener's work. However, the accounting underlying the attached financial statements and the computation of net assets is a tax accountant's domain, and taking on or representing collection of claims after registration is an attorney's domain. Where the support is document preparation and where it becomes another profession's work is set out in the second half of this article. For another example of a registration-type licence where a financial basis is examined, see [Construction Business Permit: financial basis and management of business operations](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kensetsugyo-kyoka-yoken-keikan-sengi-zaisan).

## How do you prove the financial basis of 50 million yen in net assets?

A money-lending registration requires a financial basis sufficient to carry on the business properly. Concretely, a person whose net assets fall short of the amount set by Cabinet Order will have registration refused (Money Lending Business Act Article 6(1)(xiv)). The Act provides that this Cabinet Order amount "shall not be lower than 50 million yen" (Article 6(3)), and accordingly the Money Lending Business Act Enforcement Order Article 3-2 sets the amount at 50 million yen. In other words, net assets of at least 50 million yen are the financial-basis requirement (accessed 2026-11-17).

What matters is that this is not a requirement only at the time of registration. Net assets must be maintained throughout the period of carrying on the money-lending business, and falling below after registration becomes a supervisory problem. The method of calculating net assets is set by the Enforcement Regulation under Article 6(4): for a corporation, the total of the net-asset section of the balance sheet (or a document in lieu) for the latest business year; for an individual, the total assets in the schedule of property minus the total liabilities (the specific classification and calculation of net assets follow the Enforcement Regulation; accessed 2026-11-17).

| Category | Basis of net assets |
|---|---|
| Corporation | Total of the net-asset section of the balance sheet, etc. for the latest business year |
| Individual | Total assets in the schedule of property minus total liabilities |
| Required amount | At least 50 million yen (Enforcement Order Article 3-2) |
| Maintenance | Not only at registration but not falling below throughout operation |

Computing net assets involves accounting and tax judgement. Preparing financial statements and valuing assets and liabilities is a tax accountant's work, and an administrative scrivener cannot act for tax filing or accounting. In the application, the division of roles is that the administrative scrivener arranges the application documents on the basis of the computations prepared and checked by a tax accountant. The final evaluation of whether a particular net-asset figure meets the requirement is made by the registering authority on the basis of the submitted documents.

## How many Chiefs of Money Lending Operations are needed per office?

A money lender must station a Chief of Money Lending Operations at each office (Money Lending Business Act Article 12-3). The number required is not fixed: it is a number giving a ratio of at least 1 per 50 of those engaged in the money-lending operations at that office. As staff increase, the number of chiefs required also increases. In addition, the chief must be full-time, and at least one must be stationed per office.

The Chief of Money Lending Operations is a national qualification: a person who has passed the examination and been registered (the examination and registration affairs are handled by the Japan Financial Services Association, designated by the Prime Minister). The chief's duty is to give the employees engaged in the money-lending operations the advice or guidance necessary to carry out the operations properly in compliance with laws. A "name-lending" arrangement, where a name is placed without involvement in practice, is not contemplated.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Unit of stationing | Each office |
| Number required | A number giving a ratio of at least 1 per 50 staff (minimum 1) |
| Working form | Full-time |
| Qualification | Chief of Money Lending Operations (national qualification; exam pass + registration) |
| Duty | Advice and guidance to employees on legal compliance |

Obtaining the qualification itself is a matter for the individual sitting the exam; an administrative scrivener cannot obtain it on someone's behalf. What an administrative scrivener can support is preparing the application documents premised on the chief's stationing. For another registration example where a responsible person or chief per office is examined, see [Electrical construction business registration: offices and chief electrician](https://luck428.com/legal/column/denki-koji-gyo-touroku-eigyosho-shunin).

## What grounds for refusal (disqualification) are there?

Even if the financial basis and the chief's stationing are met, registration is refused if a ground for refusal (disqualification) applies. Money Lending Business Act Article 6(1) lists, item by item, the cases in which registration must be refused. Representative ones are: a person who cannot properly carry on the business owing to a mental or physical disorder; a person who has received a decision commencing bankruptcy and has not been rehabilitated; a person for whom five years have not passed since a registration was revoked; a person for whom five years have not passed since completing a sentence of imprisonment or heavier; and an organised-crime-group member, etc. (including a person for whom five years have not passed since ceasing to be one).

Further, where the applicant is a corporation, registration is refused if an officer or certain employees fall under these grounds. A person whose net assets fall short of the standard (item (xiv)), a person lacking the office requirement, and a person for whom a structure necessary to carry on the money-lending business properly is not found to be in place are also subject to refusal. A person whose business activity is controlled by an organised-crime-group member, etc. is included.

| Type | Content (examples) |
|---|---|
| Status / condition | Mental or physical disorder; unrehabilitated bankrupt |
| Past dispositions / convictions | Under 5 years since revocation; under 5 years since completing imprisonment or heavier |
| Antisocial forces | Organised-crime-group members, etc.; persons under their control |
| Finance / structure | Shortfall in net assets; lack of office requirement; inadequate operating structure |
| Persons related to a corporation / individual | Where an officer or certain employee has a ground for disqualification |

Whether a ground for disqualification applies requires confirming facts such as the existence of convictions or past dispositions, and the final judgement on applicability is made by the registering authority. Checking before application that your own situation does not touch each item is important to avoid remand or refusal. An administrative scrivener can support this check and the arrangement of application documents, but a scene where the propriety or legal evaluation of a past disposition is disputed is an attorney's domain.

## From where does collection and debt recovery after registration become an attorney's domain?

Even after obtaining registration and starting to lend, regulation continues. Collection acts in particular are regulated: you must not intimidate the obligor, etc., or act so as to harm the quiet of their private life or business (the regulation of collection acts in Money Lending Business Act Article 21). Collecting your own claims yourself is within a money lender's business, but if the method breaches the regulation it becomes a supervisory and criminal problem.

On the other hand, taking on and acting for the collection of another person's claims, or carrying on collection representation, negotiation or litigation for reward, involves separate regulation. Handling another's legal affairs (negotiation, litigation representation, etc.) for reward as a business is prohibited to non-attorneys by Article 72 of the Attorney Act (unauthorised legal practice), and to carry on, as a business, the management and collection of another's specified monetary claims as a servicer requires separate permission of the Minister of Justice under the Act on Special Measures Concerning Claim Management and Collection Businesses (the Servicer Act). A money-lending registration does not of itself allow these.

| Act | Who / basis |
|---|---|
| Collection of one's own claims (proper method) | The money lender; but subject to Money Lending Business Act Article 21 |
| Taking on collection of another's claims | In principle requires permission under the Servicer Act |
| Negotiation or litigation representation for reward | An attorney (Attorney Act Article 72; unauthorised practice prohibited) |
| Preparing the registration application | An administrative scrivener |
| Financial statements, net assets, tax | A tax accountant |

The roles therefore divide as follows. Confirming the requirements for registration and supporting preparation of the application to a Finance Bureau or prefectural governor is handled by Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office (administrative scrivener). Preparing financial statements, computing net assets and tax is by a tax accountant; the part of post-registration debt recovery amounting to negotiation, litigation, or acting for the collection of another's claims is in the domain of an attorney or servicer; and incorporation registration is by a judicial scrivener, each as an independent business under a separate contract. ## FAQ

**Q. Even if I only lend money to businesses, do I need a money-lending registration?**
A. If you carry on lending money or intermediating it as a business, it may amount to a money-lending business even if the borrower is a business, and registration is needed (Money Lending Business Act Article 3(1)). If you set up offices in two or more prefectures it is by the Prime Minister (Finance Bureau); if only in one prefecture, by the prefectural governor. Unregistered operation is subject to penalties. The individual judgement of applicability must be made on the actual facts of the transactions.

**Q. Do I only have to meet the 50-million-yen net assets at the time of registration?**
A. No. A person whose net assets fall short of the Cabinet-Order amount (50 million yen) will have registration refused (Money Lending Business Act Article 6(1)(xiv), (3); Enforcement Order Article 3-2), and this financial basis must be maintained throughout operation. Falling below after registration becomes a supervisory problem. Because computing net assets involves accounting and tax judgement, consult a tax accountant on preparing financial statements and valuation.

**Q. Is one Chief of Money Lending Operations in the company enough?**
A. It may not be. A chief must be stationed at each office, in a number giving a ratio of at least 1 per 50 of those engaged in the money-lending operations at that office, full-time (Money Lending Business Act Article 12-3). An office with many staff, or multiple offices, needs more chiefs.

**Q. Once registered, can I freely collect and recover all the money I lend?**
A. No. Even collecting your own claims is subject to Money Lending Business Act Article 21, and intimidation or acts harming quiet are prohibited. Further, carrying on negotiation or litigation over another's debt recovery for reward requires an attorney (Attorney Act Article 72), and carrying on the management and collection of another's claims as a business needs separate permission under the Servicer Act. A money-lending registration does not of itself include these.
