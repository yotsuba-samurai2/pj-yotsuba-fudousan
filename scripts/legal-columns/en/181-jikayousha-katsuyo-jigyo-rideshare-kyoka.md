**In short:** so-called "Japan-style ride-share" is the Private-Car Utilisation Business under the Road Transport Act: in areas, seasons, and hours where taxis are short, ordinary drivers carry passengers for a fee in private cars, under the management of a taxi operator. The basis is Road Transport Act (Act No. 183 of 1951) Article 78, item (iii): although a private car (a car other than a business-use car) is in principle barred from being used for carriage for a fee, where it is unavoidable to secure the public welfare, it may be used for carriage, limited by area or period, with the licence of the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (accessed 2026-11-20). This differs from the Private Paid Passenger Transport carried on by municipalities, NPOs, and others (Article 78, item (ii); carried on under the registration of the Minister under Article 79) in its basis item, its procedure (licence vs. registration), and who carries it on. The Private-Car Utilisation Business was created as a scheme in March 2024 (Reiwa 6), and carriage began in April of the same year (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism materials; accessed 2026-11-20). This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a particular licence is possible and on the design of the business rests with the licensing authority and qualified professionals. ## What kind of mechanism is Japan-style ride-share (the Private-Car Utilisation Business)?

A private car (a car other than a business-use car) must not, in principle, be used for carriage for a fee (Road Transport Act Article 78, main clause). There are three exceptions: first, where urgency is required because of a disaster (item (i)); second, where municipalities, NPOs, and others prescribed by Ministry ordinance carry on Private Paid Passenger Transport under the registration of the next Article (item (ii)); and third, where, in a case unavoidable to secure the public welfare, a car is used for carriage, limited by area or period, with the licence of the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (item (iii)). The Private-Car Utilisation Business, called "Japan-style ride-share," is a licensed business of the Minister based on this Article 78, item (iii).

The core is that the taxi operator becomes the managing party. According to the Ministry's explanation, as a transport service that, under the management of a taxi operator, fills the shortfall with local private cars and ordinary drivers in areas, seasons, and hours where taxis are short, the scheme was created in March 2024 (Reiwa 6) and carriage began in April of the same year. A transport contract is concluded between the user and the taxi operator; the taxi operator bears the carriage responsibility; and it must arrange a structure for operation management, vehicle maintenance management, and training and education (Ministry materials; accessed 2026-11-20). The scheme is built so that it is not a form in which, without placing a party responsible for operation management or vehicle maintenance, only the driver bears the carriage responsibility.

|---|---|---|
| Scheme name | Private-Car Utilisation Business (commonly "Japan-style ride-share") | Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii) |
| Licensing authority | Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism | Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii) |
| Operating party | Taxi operator (the counterparty to the transport contract; bears carriage responsibility) | Ministry materials (accessed 2026-11-20) |

Because the taxi operator arranges the operation- and maintenance-management structure, appointing an operation manager and a maintenance manager and building a structure for roll calls and driver education are the core of practice. On the other hand, it is a separate scheme from existing taxis (general passenger-car transport business) and from the [licence for a general chartered passenger-car transport business (bus)](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/ippan-kashikiri-ryokaku-bus-kyoka-youken-nagare). Which scheme to use changes with the area, demand, and form of the party carrying it on.

## How do the Private-Car Utilisation Business and Private Paid Passenger Transport differ?

The names are similar, but they are separate schemes. Private Paid Passenger Transport is based on Road Transport Act Article 78, item (ii): municipalities, NPOs, and others prescribed by Ministry ordinance, under the registration of the Minister under Article 79, carry local residents or sightseeing passengers and others who visit the area. Typical examples are "public ride-share" in depopulated or transport-inconvenient areas, with the parties carrying it on limited to municipalities, NPOs, and the like. The procedure is "registration," not "licence," and the registration application states the name and address, the type of transport, the transport area and offices, the range of passengers to be carried, and the structure for carriage management carried on with the cooperation of a general passenger-car transport operator (Article 79-2).

By contrast, the Private-Car Utilisation Business is a licensed business of the Minister under Article 78, item (iii): the taxi operator becomes the party, and it uses the private cars of ordinary drivers to fill the taxi shortfall within its operating area. That is, the basis item (item (ii) or item (iii)), the procedure (registration or licence), the party carrying it on (municipality/NPO or taxi operator), and the aim (the everyday mobility of the area, or supplementing peak taxi demand) each differ.

| Axis of comparison | Private Paid Passenger Transport | Private-Car Utilisation Business |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Road Transport Act Article 78, item (ii) | Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii) |
| Procedure | Registration of the Minister (Article 79) | Licence of the Minister |
| Main party | Municipality / NPO, etc. | Taxi operator |
| Typical setting | Everyday mobility of depopulated / transport-inconvenient areas | Supplement in areas, seasons, and hours of taxi shortage |

Which to use divides by the area's demand and the party carrying it on. Choosing the scheme and supporting preparation of the application documents is an administrative scrivener's work, but the final judgement of which scheme fits the facts is made after consulting the District Transport Bureau. Where transport accompanies nursing care or welfare, as with the [licence and garage requirements for care taxis and welfare transport](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kaigo-taxi-fukushi-yuso-kyoka-shako-yoken), yet another framework may be involved.

## In the licence application, what do you submit and where?

Because the Private-Car Utilisation Business is a licensed business under Article 78, item (iii), you apply for the licence to the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (the practical window is the District Transport Bureau with jurisdiction). The applicant is, in principle, a general passenger-car transport operator (taxi operator). As a premise of the licence, the areas, seasons, and hours where taxis are short and the number of vehicles short are shown by the Ministry, and carriage is carried on within that frame. The application must set out the structure for operation management, vehicle maintenance management, and training and education; the management of the private cars and drivers used; the setting of fares and charges; the operating area, and so on.

The costs, attached documents, and examination period may change with scheme revisions and local operation. Do not fill in amounts or forms by guesswork; just before applying, always confirm the Ministry's latest published materials and the guidance of the District Transport Bureau with jurisdiction. Confirming at the window any figures you cannot substantiate is important to avoid remand.

## Who handles the drivers, operation management, and insurance (dividing the work)?

As for the driver's licence, the Private-Car Utilisation Business suffices with a Class 1 driving licence (excluding one during the novice-driver period); a Class 2 driving licence is not necessarily required as with taxis. In addition, conditions such as a certain period without accidents or disciplinary action are required in operation (the Chiba Prefecture guidance; accessed 2026-11-20; as operation may differ by area, confirm with the District Transport Bureau with jurisdiction). Still, safety management such as driver education, roll calls, and grasping health condition is borne by the taxi operator as a structure.

Whether the driver is employed or engaged on a contract greatly changes how labour is handled. Where employed, arranging working hours, wages, social insurance, and work rules is needed, and this is a labour and social security attorney's work. Yotsuba Labour and Social Security Attorney Office is an independent business from Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office, and these are handled under separate contracts. Because the working hours of taxi and hire drivers involve rules such as the Improvement Standards Notification, labour points such as those in [the Improvement Standards Notification and working hours of taxi drivers](https://luck428.com/en/labor/column/taxi-kakujitsu-kinmu-2024-kaizen-kijun-roumu) should be consulted with a labour and social security attorney. Negotiation and representation in damages on an accident are an attorney's domain, and the design and underwriting of voluntary insurance a non-life insurance company's domain, not an administrative scrivener's work.

## How are the restrictions on area, hours, and number of vehicles decided?

The Private-Car Utilisation Business cannot carry passengers freely anywhere. The Ministry shows the areas (operating area), seasons, and hours where taxis are short and the number of vehicles short, and carriage is carried on within that frame. The number of vehicles short is calculated by a method based on dispatch-app data and a simple method used in other areas; in the latter, where a municipality within the operating area reports the number of vehicles short for a particular day of the week and time zone to the District Transport Bureau, that content is deemed the number of vehicles short (Ministry materials and each municipality's guidance; accessed 2026-11-20).

The target areas, hours, and number of vehicles short change with later reviews. The areas shown at the start of the scheme and the current targets are not necessarily the same. Confirm the latest designation for the area where you consider carriage with the Ministry's published materials and the District Transport Bureau with jurisdiction, just before applying. We do not fill in a target range we cannot substantiate by guesswork.

| How it is decided | Content |
|---|---|
| Area, season, hours | Designated by the Ministry from the taxi-shortage situation |
| Number of vehicles short | Method based on dispatch-app data / simple method by the municipality's report |
| Review | Target areas and hours may be changed (confirm the latest materials) |

## FAQ

**Q. Are Japan-style ride-share and overseas ride-share the same thing?**
A. The build of the scheme differs. The Private-Car Utilisation Business (Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii)) presupposes that the taxi operator becomes the party to the transport contract and bears carriage responsibility, and arranges a structure for operation management and vehicle maintenance management. It differs from a form in which no party responsible for operation management or vehicle maintenance is placed and only the driver bears carriage responsibility. Confirm the details with the Ministry's latest materials.

**Q. Which should I choose, the Private-Car Utilisation Business or Private Paid Passenger Transport?**
A. It changes with the aim and the party carrying it on. Private Paid Passenger Transport (item (ii)) is a scheme in which municipalities, NPOs, and the like support the area's everyday mobility under the Minister's registration (Article 79); the Private-Car Utilisation Business (item (iii)) is a scheme in which a taxi operator fills the taxi shortfall under the Minister's licence. Which fits the facts is judged after consulting the District Transport Bureau.

**Q. Can the driver not drive without a Class 2 licence?**
A. For the Private-Car Utilisation Business, a Class 1 driving licence (excluding one during the novice-driver period) suffices; a Class 2 driving licence is not necessarily required as with taxis. Conditions such as no accidents or disciplinary action are required in operation (accessed 2026-11-20). As operation may differ by area, confirm with the District Transport Bureau with jurisdiction. Safety education and roll calls are borne by the taxi operator.

**Q. Can I ask an administrative scrivener about damages or insurance when an accident happens?**
A. No. Negotiation and representation in damages on an accident are an attorney's work, and the design and underwriting of voluntary insurance a non-life insurance company's work, not an administrative scrivener's work. What the administrative scrivener handles is support for preparing the licence/registration application (documents submitted to a public agency). Damages, insurance, and labour are handled, each as an independent business, under separate contracts.
