**In short:** To carry passengers for a fare by hiring out a bus for sightseeing or transfers to unspecified customers, you need the permit for a general charter passenger transport business under Article 4, paragraph 1 of the Road Transport Act. This is the business defined in Article 3, item 1, sub-item (ro) as "carrying passengers in a vehicle of at least the seating capacity set by ministerial ordinance, hired out as a whole under a single contract," and the seating capacity is set by ministerial ordinance at 11 persons or more. Merely transporting your own officers and employees, or your own customers, free of charge needs no permit; but if you take a fare and carry passengers, a permit is needed. The core of the permit is "people, assets, money, and safety": you assemble an operations manager (Article 23 of the Act; appointed from holders of an operations manager qualification certificate), a maintenance manager (Article 50 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act), an office, a garage, liability capacity (voluntary insurance), and the required funds, while specific criteria such as the number of vehicles are set in the examination standards published by the transport bureau (criteria may be revised, so confirm them in the latest publication of the bureau you apply to). The permit is subject to renewal every five years under Article 8 of the Act. ## Why does a charter bus business need a permit, and how does it differ from white-plate transport?

The permit for a general charter passenger transport business is needed, under Article 4, paragraph 1 of the Road Transport Act, when you hire out a bus under a single contract and carry passengers for a fare from unspecified customers. The vehicle concerned is one "of at least the seating capacity set by ministerial ordinance" under Article 3, item 1, sub-item (ro), and the seating capacity is set at 11 persons or more. Hiring out a vehicle with a capacity of 10 or fewer is in the category of general passenger transport by hire (the "hire" category, including jumbo taxis and the like), which is the subject of a different permit.

| Category | Typical example | Need for a permit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General charter passenger transport business | Carrying passengers for a fare in a hired-out bus of 11+ capacity for sightseeing or transfers | Permit under Article 4, paragraph 1 of the Road Transport Act required |
| Own transport (white plate) | Transferring your own officers and employees, or your own customers, without taking a fare | No permit (but carrying out transport as a business for value becomes subject to a permit) |
| Paid transport by a private vehicle | Carrying out paid transport with a private vehicle | In principle prohibited (Article 78 of the Act; exceptions are limited) |

The core of the difference is "paid transport for a fare" and "carrying unspecified customers." Free transfers of your own people need no permit, but once you take consideration beyond actual cost or carry outside customers, it can become an issue as unpermitted paid transport. Which it falls under is judged by the substance of the contract and the consideration, so organise it at the design stage of the transfers. ## What must you satisfy for the people/assets/money requirements (operations manager, maintenance manager, office, garage, funds)?

The requirements can be organised into "people, assets, money, and safety." Specific criteria such as the number of vehicles and the amounts are set in the examination standards published by the transport bureau you apply to, and may be revised. The main items are as follows (the figures may differ by the bureau's publication, so confirm them in the latest publication immediately before applying).

| Category | Main requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Number of vehicles | In principle 3 or more per operating area. For using large vehicles, 5 or more is required in many publications (3 or more but fewer than 5 generally carries a condition limiting to medium and small vehicles) |
| Operations manager | Appointed per office from holders of an operations manager qualification certificate (Article 23 of the Road Transport Act). The required number rises with the number of vehicles |
| Maintenance manager | Appointed per office (Article 50 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act). The requirement is met by a mechanic qualification, or a certain practical experience plus pre-appointment training |
| Office and garage | The garage is in principle attached to the office. If it cannot be, within roughly 2 km in a straight line from the office. The office, garage and rest/sleep facility require a right of use of 3 years or more in many publications |
| Liability capacity | A plan for all vehicles to join voluntary insurance (mutual aid) with unlimited bodily-injury cover per victim and property cover of 2 million yen or more per accident |
| Required funds | Estimating the funds needed to start the business and securing them with own funds; substantiated by a bank balance |

The operations manager, under Article 23-2 of the Road Transport Act, is issued the qualification certificate after passing the operations manager examination and so on. These are criteria to be maintained after the permit too: the number of vehicles, the insurance and the appointment of managers must continue to be met after transport begins. The final examination and judgment of whether the requirements are met is made by the transport bureau (the district transport bureau or transport branch office). The design of labor matters such as drivers' working hours and rest periods is organised in the labor and social security attorney's [The improvement-standards notice for charter bus drivers (2024 working hours and rest) and labor](https://luck428.com/labor/column/bus-untensha-kaizen-kijun-2024-kousoku-kyusoku-roumu) (the labor and social security attorney is a separate business).

## How does the flow proceed from application to permit, start of transport, and audit?

The procedure proceeds in order: fix the business plan and apply, go through the legal examination and review, obtain the permit, and begin transport. After the permit, there is renewal every five years (Article 8 of the Road Transport Act). The rough flow is as follows. The standard processing period and operation differ by bureau, so confirm them in the guidance of the bureau you apply to.

| Stage | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Drafting the business plan | Decide the operating area, office, garage, vehicles and operations manager, and plan the required funds and voluntary insurance |
| Application | Submit the permit application and the business plan to the transport branch office with jurisdiction over the office (Article 5 of the Road Transport Act) |
| Legal examination | The applicant (a full-time officer for a corporation) sits a legal examination on the Road Transport Act and the like |
| Review and correction | Review of conformity with the requirements; confirmation and correction of facilities, personnel and funds |
| Permit | Once the permit is granted, pay the registration and license tax |
| Post-permit procedures | Setting fares and charges (notification), the appointment notifications for the operations and maintenance managers, the safety-management notification, and vehicle registration (green plate), etc. |
| Start of transport | Begin transport and submit the start-of-transport notification (confirm the filing deadline in the bureau's guidance) |
| Renewal and audit | The permit is renewed every five years (Article 8 of the Road Transport Act). Subject to audit and patrol guidance by the bureau and the compliance body |

A permit is not the end once obtained; renewal every five years and continued compliance with operations management, maintenance and labor are the premise. Confirm the specific forms, numbers of copies and standard processing period in the latest guidance of the transport branch office you apply to. ## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a permit even just to transfer my own employees or hotel guests by bus?**
A. Merely transferring your own officers and employees, or your own customers, by bus without taking a fare in principle needs no permit for a general charter passenger transport business. On the other hand, if you take a fare (consideration beyond actual cost) and carry passengers, or carry outside, unspecified customers, it can become subject to the permit under Article 4, paragraph 1 of the Road Transport Act. Paid transport by a private vehicle without a permit is in principle prohibited (Article 78). Whether the transfers stay within free own transport is judged by the substance of the contract and the consideration, so organise it at the design stage.

**Q. How many vehicles does a charter bus permit require?**
A. The number of vehicles is set in the examination standards published by the transport bureau you apply to; many publications show, in principle, 3 or more per operating area, and 5 or more when using large vehicles (3 or more but fewer than 5 generally carries a condition limiting to medium and small vehicles). This figure differs by bureau and may be revised, so confirm it in the latest publication of the transport branch office you apply to immediately before applying. Besides vehicles, you must also meet the requirements for the operations manager, the maintenance manager, the office, the garage, voluntary insurance and the required funds.

**Q. Once I obtain the permit, can I continue without renewing it?**
A. The permit for a general charter passenger transport business, under Article 8 of the Road Transport Act, loses its effect on the passage of time unless renewed every five years. Besides renewal, the appointment of the operations and maintenance managers, joining voluntary insurance, and managing drivers' working hours must continue to be met after transport begins, and are subject to audit and patrol guidance by the bureau and the compliance body. Confirm the renewal timing and the criteria to be maintained in the guidance of the transport bureau you apply to.
