**In short:** even a non-profit rescue activity can require a filing where a keeping facility is set up and animals are handled above a certain number. A person who **sets up a keeping facility and runs an animal-handling business** (transfer, custody, lending, training, exhibition, etc.) **as a business** must, except for those who should obtain Type-1 registration and the like, file in advance with the **prefectural governor** (mayor in a designated city) (Act on Welfare and Management of Animals, Article 24-2-2(1)). This is the **Type-2 animal-handling business** filing. Running adoption events, foster care or a shelter without the aim of profit is the typical case, and repeating it to keep finding adopters or to continue rescue can amount to "as a business." The filing is required where, in a **keeping facility** separable from the living quarters, animals are kept at or above the number set by Ministry of the Environment ordinance; the Ministry's guide gives rough thresholds of three or more large animals (cattle, horses, etc.), ten or more medium (dogs, cats, etc.) and fifty or more small (mice, squirrels, etc.). A for-profit handling, by contrast, is a **Type-1 animal-handling business** registration (Article 10(1)) and falls outside Type 2. This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a filing is needed and on "as a business" and "for profit" rests with each authority's screening and a qualified professional. ## Keeping a pet versus rescue and adoption: where does the filing start?

Keeping dogs or cats for yourself or your family is not an animal-handling business, however many you have. What triggers a filing or registration is handling animals "as a business." "As a business" means doing it repeatedly and continuously to the degree of an undertaking in social terms, and whether it is for profit or not does not matter. Holding adoption events regularly, or keeping many animals in foster care or a shelter on an ongoing basis, can amount to "as a business" even when it is free of charge.

Here the system splits in two. A for-profit handling is a **Type-1 animal-handling business** registration (Act on Welfare and Management of Animals, Article 10(1)); a non-profit handling is a **Type-2 animal-handling business** filing (Article 24-2-2(1)). Most rescue and adoption activity is non-profit, so the Type-2 filing is the entry point.

| Category | Basis | Nature | Typical example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keeping as a pet | Out of scope | No filing or registration | Keeping for yourself or family |
| Type-2 animal-handling business | Art. 24-2-2 | Filing (non-profit) | A rescue group's shelter, adoption events, foster care |
| Type-1 animal-handling business | Art. 10 | Registration (for profit) | Sale, exhibition, lending for profit |

That said, Type 2 still has the requirements of "setting up a keeping facility" and "a certain number or more." Merely keeping a small number of animals in a private home is not within the scope of the filing, as municipal guidance also indicates. The next section looks at the number thresholds.

## What number and facility requirements trigger a Type-2 filing?

A filing is required where, in a **keeping facility** separable from the living quarters, animals are kept or held at or above the number set by Ministry of the Environment ordinance. The Ministry's rough thresholds are below; mammals, birds and reptiles are counted by dividing them into large, medium and small by the animal's standard body size.

| Category | Mammal examples | Number threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Large | Cattle, horses, etc., and specified animals | Three or more in total |
| Medium | Dogs, cats, rabbits, etc. | Ten or more in total |
| Small | Mice, squirrels, hamsters, etc. | Fifty or more in total |

The way of counting has fine points, such as aggregating across categories, and the concrete judgement rests with the standards of the authority receiving the filing. What is covered is handling—transfer, custody, lending, training, exhibition—done by setting up a keeping facility. Conversely, activity without a keeping facility, or where the number falls below the threshold, is outside the scope of the Type-2 filing. Confirm the size and category of the counting on the relevant authority's official page just before filing.

## When does a non-profit activity still become a Type-1 registration?

Type 2 is premised on handling "not for the purpose of profit." In other words, those who should obtain a Type-1 animal-handling business registration (Article 10(1)) are excluded from the Type-2 filing (parenthetical of Article 24-2-2(1)). The for-profit character here is judged by whether, as a whole, the activity has a mechanism to make a profit. Asking for the actual costs of rescue (medical, vaccine, spay-neuter costs) at the time of transfer is not necessarily for profit, but obtaining consideration beyond the going rate and repeatedly transferring, or building sale, paid lending or exhibition of animals into the undertaking, may lead to a judgement that Type-1 registration is needed.

| Form of activity | Likely category |
|---|---|
| Non-profit rescue and transfer (asking roughly actual costs) | Type 2 (filing) |
| For-profit sale, exhibition, lending, etc. | Type 1 (registration) |
| A rescue-cat café with admission/food—for-profit exhibition | Examine whether Type-1 registration is needed for the exhibition, plus a restaurant-business permit |

A rescue-cat café, where visitors are shown cats (exhibition) and revenue comes from admission or food, needs individual confirmation of whether the exhibition amounts to a for-profit Type-1 animal-handling business. If it does, it is a registration rather than a filing, with heavier requirements such as appointing an animal-handling manager and the keeping-facility standards. The final judgement on for-profit character and "as a business" is made by the authority based on the reality of the activity. The requirements for Type-1 registration itself are sorted out in [Type-1 animal-handling business registration requirements](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/dai-isshu-doubutsu-toriatsukai-touroku-youken).

## The filing window (public health centre), attachments and facility standards

The filing goes, for each place where a keeping facility is set up, to the **prefectural governor** for that location (the mayor in a designated city). The actual window is the public health centre or the animal-welfare centre, and in a core city or designated city it may be a window separate from the prefecture. The filing uses a Type-2 animal-handling business filing form and a document stating the method of carrying it out, and where dogs or cats are transferred, a ledger must be prepared and kept for five years (entries include breed, date of birth, etc.; an electromagnetic record is allowed). The attachments differ by the type of operation and the contents of the filing.

The keeping facility is required, as the technical standards set by Ministry of the Environment ordinance, to install the necessary equipment, prevent escape, keep a clean environment, and prevent effects on the living environment such as noise and odour. The details of the standards and the operation of screening differ by municipality, and there is a caution that even after filing, the building or barn may be unusable under the City Planning Act or the Building Standards Act. Running the business without filing, or failing to follow an improvement order, carries a fine of up to 300,000 yen. Confirm the forms, attachments and operation of screening directly on the relevant authority's official page just before filing.

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Filed with | Prefectural governor for the keeping facility's location (mayor in a designated city) |
| Actual window | Public health centre, animal-welfare centre, etc. |
| Main attachments/forms | Filing form, document stating the method, etc. (differ by municipality) |
| Transfer of dogs/cats | Prepare a ledger and keep it for five years |
| Facility standards | Equipment, escape prevention, clean environment, prevention of effects on the living environment (ordinance) |
| Failure to file | Fine of up to 300,000 yen |

## FAQ

**Q. Is a filing needed even for a free adoption event?**
A. Even free of charge, if you set up a keeping facility and repeatedly and continuously handle animals above a certain number, it can amount to "as a business" and require a Type-2 animal-handling business filing (Act on Welfare and Management of Animals, Article 24-2-2(1)). Whether it is for profit does not matter. The rough thresholds are three or more large, ten or more medium, and fifty or more small animals. Merely keeping a small number in a private home is out of scope.

**Q. Does a Type-2 filing have a term or renewal?**
A. The general view is that the Type-2 animal-handling business filing has no term or renewal mechanism like the Type-1 registration (Type 1 is a registration system with renewal). However, when a filed matter changes or the use of the facility is discontinued, a change or discontinuance filing is required (Article 24-3). As the operation differs by municipality, confirm the authority's guidance.

**Q. Is a filing enough to start a rescue-cat café?**
A. When "exhibiting" cats to visitors is done for profit, you need to confirm individually whether a Type-1 animal-handling business registration (Article 10(1)) is required rather than a Type-2 filing. If you also serve food, a restaurant-business permit from the public health centre is separately needed. The judgement on for-profit character and "as a business" is made by the authority based on the reality of the activity.

**Q. Which is heavier, Type 1 or Type 2?**
A. Generally Type 1 (registration) is heavier, with more requirements such as appointing an animal-handling manager and the keeping-facility standards. Even Type 2 (filing) requires compliance with the keeping facility's technical standards (equipment, escape prevention, a clean environment, prevention of effects on the living environment). Which your activity falls under turns on for-profit character and "as a business."
