## How does a liquor manufacturing licence differ from a "liquor retail licence," and how do licences split by type?

Alcohol-related licences broadly split into "manufacturing" and "sales." The maker receives a liquor manufacturing licence (Article 7 of the Liquor Tax Act), and the seller of the finished liquor receives a liquor sales business licence (Article 9). Opening a brewery and brewing your own beer requires a manufacturing licence; retailing it in bottles or cans may separately require a "liquor retail business licence." If you brew and sell, you consider the sales licence separately from the manufacturing licence. The requirements and the way to think about premises for a retail licence are organised in [Liquor retail business licence and store/property requirements](https://luck428.com/legal/column/shurui-kouri-menkyo-tenpo-bukken-yoken).

The manufacturing licence is granted "by product category and per manufacturing site." That is, a beer licence to brew beer, a happoshu licence to brew happoshu — the licence splits by the product category you brew and is obtained for each manufacturing site (plant/brewery). The product categories are classified in Article 3 of the Liquor Tax Act into sake, beer, fruit liquor, happoshu, other brewed liquors, liqueur, spirits and so on, and even the same "craft beer" may count as beer, happoshu or other brewed liquor depending on the malt ratio and adjuncts. Which category of licence you apply for is confirmed with the competent tax office based on your ingredients and recipe. The final classification is the tax office's judgement.

## How much is the "minimum production volume standard" for beer, happoshu and other brewed liquors?

A liquor manufacturing licence cannot be received if the volume you expect to manufacture in one year at one site after being licensed does not reach the amount set for each product category (Article 7(2) of the Liquor Tax Act). This is the minimum production volume standard. The main categories are as follows.

| Product category | Minimum production volume standard (per year, per site) |
|---|---|
| Sake, synthetic sake, continuous-still shochu, beer | 60 kilolitres |
| Single-still shochu, mirin | 10 kilolitres |
| Fruit liquor, sweet fruit liquor, whisky, brandy | 6 kilolitres |
| Happoshu, other brewed liquors, alcohol for raw material | 6 kilolitres |
| Spirits, liqueur, powdered liquor, miscellaneous liquor | 6 kilolitres |

A frequent point for craft-beer start-ups is the gap between beer (60 kilolitres) and happoshu / other brewed liquors (6 kilolitres each). Depending on the malt ratio and the use of adjuncts other than hops, a product may be classified under the Liquor Tax Act not as "beer" but as "happoshu" or "other brewed liquor," and a licence may then be considered on the 6-kilolitre standard even at a small scale. However, classification is decided by ingredients and method, not by deciding a recipe in order to lower the standard. Which category applies requires the tax office's confirmation. The beer standard was lowered from 2,000 to 60 kilolitres in 1994, but the current amounts are as in the table above, confirmed against the latest National Tax Agency guidance just before writing.

## What requirements apply to the site and facilities? (application unit and manufacturing equipment)

Because the licence is per manufacturing site, the site and facilities are subject to review. Article 10 of the Liquor Tax Act sets grounds on which the tax office may refuse a licence where any item applies; the main viewpoints are as follows.

| Viewpoint | Relevant item of Article 10 of the Liquor Tax Act (outline) |
|---|---|
| Personal requirements | Persons within a set number of years of a licence revocation, delinquent disposition for national/local tax, years since a fine under the Liquor Tax Act and so on (items 1 to 8) |
| Location requirements | Setting up a manufacturing site in a place unsuitable for control without just cause (item 9) |
| Management-base requirements | Where the base of management is found to be weak (item 10) |
| Supply-demand adjustment | Where it is found inappropriate because of the need to maintain supply-demand balance for the preservation of liquor tax (item 11) |
| Technical/facility requirements | Where the manufacturing technical ability is lacking or the site's facilities are found insufficient (item 12) |

Manufacturing equipment (brewing, fermentation, storage, filtration, filling, measurement, etc.) and a system that can accurately manage production volume are looked at as "technical/facility requirements." Water supply and drainage, wastewater treatment, and the building's use also involve other laws (the Building Standards Act, water-quality regulation, food hygiene), so confirm with an architect/contractor and the competent window before construction or a lease. Building confirmation and use of manufacturing facilities are an architect's field; an administrative scrivener does not guarantee design or legal conformity.

## When are the standards eased in a structural reform special zone (dobroku zone, wine zone, etc.)?

The Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform includes special provisions on liquor tax that ease the minimum production volume standard within an authorised special zone. The representative ones are the so-called "dobroku zone" and "wine zone."

- Dobroku zone (Article 25 "Special provision on the Liquor Tax Act" of the Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform): where a specified farmer running a farm guesthouse and the like manufactures doboroku (unfiltered other brewed liquor) or fruit liquor from rice or fruit they produce themselves, to serve at their own place of business, Article 7(2) of the Liquor Tax Act (minimum production volume standard) does not apply.
- Wine zone / special-product liquor provision (Article 26): where an operator under an authorised plan makes fruit liquor from special-product fruit and the like, the fruit-liquor standard is read down from "6 kilolitres" to "2 kilolitres" and liqueur to "1 kilolitre" (single-still shochu and alcohol for raw material are exempt).

Both presuppose that the area has received the Prime Minister's authorisation of a special-zone plan for structural reform, and that the requirements for eligible persons, products and ingredients are met. If you make the same product outside the special zone, the ordinary minimum production volume standard applies. Whether your plan falls within the special zone is confirmed against the content of the special-zone plan and the guidance of the competent tax office and local government.

## Where and with what documents do you apply, and how long does review take?

The application goes to the tax office with jurisdiction over the manufacturing site (the competent tax office). In addition to the application, you are asked for attachments showing the business/production plan, manufacturing method, equipment, funding plan, the site's situation and so on. Confirm the specific forms and attachments against the National Tax Agency's "Guide to Applying for a Liquor Manufacturing Licence" and the competent tax office's guidance.

| Point | Content (confirm with the tax office/guide before applying) |
|---|---|
| Where to apply | The tax office with jurisdiction over the manufacturing site |
| Licence unit | By product category and per manufacturing site |
| Main attachments | Business/production plan, method statement, outline of equipment, site drawings/contract relations, revenue-and-expense forecast, the applicant's background, etc. |
| Review viewpoints | Personal, location, management-base, supply-demand and technical/facility requirements (Article 10), and the prospect of reaching the minimum production volume standard (Article 7(2)) |
| Standard processing period | Confirm against the standard processing period the tax office publishes (it varies with the application) |

The time review takes varies with the application and any corrections. The standard processing period is not a fixed statutory deadline, so confirm the schedule with the competent tax office. The final decision on whether to grant a licence is made by the competent tax office. ## FAQ

**Q. I want to brew craft beer on a small scale — must I always make 60 kilolitres a year to get a licence?**
A. If you take the licence under the beer category, you need the prospect of reaching the 60-kilolitre minimum production volume standard (Article 7(2)) at one site per year. However, if the malt ratio or adjuncts place it under the Liquor Tax Act as "happoshu" or "other brewed liquor," a licence may be considered on the 6-kilolitre standard. Which category applies is decided by ingredients and method, and the final classification is the competent tax office's judgement.

**Q. Do I need both a manufacturing licence and a liquor retail business licence?**
A. The act of brewing may require a manufacturing licence (Article 7), and retailing the finished liquor may separately require a liquor retail business licence (Article 9). If you brew and sell yourself, consider the need for both separately. Confirm details with the competent tax office.

**Q. Can I brew on a small scale in a dobroku zone or wine zone?**
A. Only where, within an authorised special zone, you meet the requirements for eligible persons, products and ingredients, there are provisions that disapply or lower the minimum production volume standard (Articles 25 and 26 of the Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform). Outside the special zone the ordinary standard applies. Whether your plan is eligible is confirmed against the special-zone plan and with the competent tax office and local government.

**Q. How long does review take?**
A. It varies with the application and any corrections. Use the standard processing period the competent tax office publishes as a guide. The standard processing period is not a fixed statutory deadline, and the final decision on whether to grant a licence is made by the competent tax office.
