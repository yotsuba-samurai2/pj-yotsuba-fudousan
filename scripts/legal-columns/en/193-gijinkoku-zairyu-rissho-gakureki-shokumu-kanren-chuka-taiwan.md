**In short:** Japan’s Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services status applies to work under a contract with a public or private organisation in Japan that requires expertise in natural sciences or humanities, or thinking or sensitivity grounded in another culture. The actual duties determine what evidence of education or experience applies. A university degree can exempt a translator, interpreter or language instructor from the three-year International Services experience rule. Separately, work involving direct interaction that chiefly draws on language ability requires CEFR B2-equivalent proficiency in the language used at work. Required documents depend on the organisation’s category and other factors. The Immigration Services Agency decides individual cases.

## For what kinds of work is Gijinkoku granted?

Gijinkoku is the residence status set in Appended Table I-2. The provision covers activities, under a contract with a Japanese public or private organization, in "work requiring technology or knowledge belonging to the field of physical science, engineering or other natural sciences, or of jurisprudence, economics, sociology or other human sciences," or in "work requiring thought or sensibility grounded in a foreign culture" (excluding activities falling under other statuses such as Professor, Artist, Journalist, Business Manager, Instructor, Intra-company Transferee and Entertainer).

In practice it is understood in three broad categories: (1) "Engineer" = science and engineering technicians (system engineers, mechanical design, and so on); (2) "Specialist in Humanities" = office work requiring knowledge of law, economics, management and the like (accounting, legal affairs, planning); and (3) "International Services" = translation, interpretation, language instruction, overseas trade, design and other work grounded in a foreign culture. The category you apply under changes the education and work-history requirements below.

| Category | Typical duties | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer | System engineer, mechanical/electrical design | Table I-2 (natural sciences field) |
| Specialist in Humanities | Accounting, legal, HR, planning, marketing | Table I-2 (human sciences field) |
| International Services | Translation/interpretation, language instruction, overseas trade, design | Table I-2 (work grounded in a foreign culture) |

When hiring a university-educated white-collar worker from China or Taiwan, the starting point is to settle at the outset which category applies and which requirement it meets.

## How far is a link between education and duties required?

For the natural/human-sciences technology-and-knowledge categories (1 and 2), the standard is having acquired "the technology or knowledge necessary for the work to be engaged in." As ways to show that, the Landing Permission Criteria Ordinance lists: (a) "having graduated from a university majoring in subjects related to the technology or knowledge, or having received education equivalent or higher"; (b) "having completed an advanced course or specialized course at a Japanese vocational school majoring in subjects related to the technology or knowledge (limited to cases meeting the requirements set by the Minister of Justice by notice)"; or (c) "at least 10 years of practical experience."

For the education route under Engineering or Humanities, show what the applicant studied and how it relates to the actual duties, not just the name of the major. The Immigration Services Agency applies the relevance test relatively flexibly to university graduates, including overseas university graduates, and also to graduates of designated Japanese vocational programmes accredited under the Ministry of Education’s career development scheme for international students. The decision is made on the evidence of the duties and the applicant’s studies.

| Way to show | Content | Note |
|---|---|---|
| (a) University degree | Graduated majoring in related subjects (or equivalent-or-higher education) | Overseas universities count; the major-duties link is central |
| (b) Vocational advanced course | Completed a Japanese vocational-school advanced/specialized course | Conditioned on meeting the Minister's notice requirements |
| (c) Practical experience | At least 10 years (including time spent majoring in related subjects) | International Services below has a different term |

## In which category can practical experience (work history) substitute?

Besides education, practical experience can satisfy the requirement in some cases. For the natural/human-sciences categories (1 and 2), route (c) — "at least 10 years of practical experience (including time majoring in related subjects at a university, college of technology, high school, the later course of a secondary school, or a vocational-school advanced/specialized course)" — is the substitute path.

International Services (3) has a different term. The Landing Permission Criteria Ordinance requires engaging in translation, interpretation, language instruction, public relations, advertising, overseas trade, clothing or interior design, product development or similar work, plus "at least 3 years of practical experience in work related to the work to be engaged in." **However, where a university graduate engages in translation, interpretation or language instruction, this 3-year requirement does not apply.** In every category, a common standard is receiving remuneration at least equal to what a Japanese national would receive for the same work (for information-processing work, there is an exception whereby passing a Minister-designated IT exam or holding a designated IT qualification removes the education/experience requirement).

**The experience exception and language proficiency are separate.** Work relying primarily on language ability in dealing with people, such as interpreting or working at a hotel front desk, requires CEFR B2-equivalent proficiency in the language used for the job. For applications from 15 April 2026, category 3 and 4 organisations must submit a declaration concerning their representative and, for relevant work, proof of language proficiency. Immigration can also request evidence in category 1 or 2 cases. For the relevant application types and evidence, see the [Immigration Services Agency’s requirements](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/gijinkoku.html) and [Annex 4, updated 14 October 2026](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan69.html). If someone is continuing the same type of duties, proof of language proficiency is generally not required with the renewal application, but may be requested during the review. For category 3 or 4 sponsors, it is required if a change of duties or employer makes the covered work the employee’s new primary duty.

| Category | Experience requirement | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer / Specialist in Humanities | At least 10 years (including time majoring in related subjects) | Replaceable by a designated IT exam/qualification |
| International Services | At least 3 years in related work | Not required for a university graduate in translation/interpretation/language instruction |

When proving by work history, you show, with employment certificates and the like, "which work for how many years." The counting of years and the assessment of the link are matters for Immigration's review.

## What kinds of cases tend to be refused or denied?

A weak showing of the link tends to lead to refusal or denial. Typical cases: (1) the university major does not connect to the job duties (for example, the description of the duties centers on "training" or "on-site work," so it cannot be read as work requiring technology or knowledge); (2) the duties look in substance like unskilled labor; or (3) the company's business and scale do not match the number hired or the duties. In International Services, even for a graduate, work other than translation/interpretation/language instruction (overseas trade, design, etc.) requires at least 3 years of experience, and applications sometimes fall short here.

If a Certificate of Eligibility is refused, find out why and consider reapplying with a clearer description of duties, employment contract, or education and work-history records. Reapplying is different from challenging the decision in court. Consult a lawyer about a court challenge. See [checking why a COE was refused and preparing a new application](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/zairyu-shikaku-nintei-shomeisho-fukoufu-saishinsei-chuka-taiwan).

## Will the employment contract and wages withstand the review?

In the Gijinkoku review, the content of the employment contract and the working-conditions notice themselves are examined. The points are whether the duties are written concretely as work requiring technology or knowledge, whether the position and duties match the remuneration, and whether the standard of "at least equal to what a Japanese national would receive for the same work" (Landing Permission Criteria Ordinance) is met. A contract where wages are below those of comparable Japanese nationals, or the duties are vague, is a weakness in the review.

For a hire coming to Japan from abroad, consider the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) process before entry. Under Article 7-2, the foreign national may apply, or a qualifying representative such as a member of staff of the receiving organisation may apply on their behalf. Filing can also be facilitated by a lawyer or administrative scrivener who has notified Immigration as an application intermediary. Include Japanese translations of documents prepared in another language. A labor and social security attorney can advise on the post-hire labour and social insurance arrangements. See also [the Highly Skilled Professional points calculation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kodo-senmonshoku-1go-point-keisan-chuka-taiwan).

## FAQ

**Q. If I graduated from a university in China or Taiwan, is the link with the Japanese duties not examined?**
A. If you rely on a university degree for the engineering or humanities branch, the Immigration Services Agency assesses how the coursework relates to the actual duties, applying a relatively flexible approach to degrees, including those from overseas. Under the separate international-services branch, university graduates doing translation, interpretation or language instruction are exempt from the three-year experience requirement. This is not the same degree-to-duties test. Check applicable requirements separately, such as B2-equivalent ability in the working language.

**Q. I have a humanities background and will do translation and interpretation. Is practical experience required?**
A. International Services generally requires three years of related practical experience, but a university graduate doing translation, interpreting or language instruction is exempt from this period. There is a separate CEFR B2-equivalent requirement in the working language for work that chiefly uses language skills in dealing with people. For relevant applications, a category 3 or 4 organisation must provide proof.

**Q. The Certificate of Eligibility was refused. Can I no longer hire the person?**
A. A refusal does not automatically mean you cannot hire the person. Find out why, then consider reapplying with fuller evidence of the duties, education or work history, or employment terms. Consult a lawyer if you intend to challenge the refusal in court.
