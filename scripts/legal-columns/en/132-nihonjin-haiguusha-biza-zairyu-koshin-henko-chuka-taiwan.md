**In short:** "Spouse of a Japanese National" is a status for the spouse or special adoptee of a Japanese national, or a person born as the child of a Japanese national (Appended Table II of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act). Certification, change and renewal look at the substance and continuity of the marriage, and if you remain without carrying on activities as a spouse for six months or more, you fall within the grounds for revocation of status (Article 22-4 of the Act), absent a justifiable reason.

## Who is covered by "Spouse of a Japanese National"?

According to the Immigration Services Agency's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026), those covered by this status are "a spouse or special adoptee of a Japanese national, or a person born as the child of a Japanese national." The Japanese national's husband or wife, their biological child, and a special adoptee all fall within it. The period of stay is one of five years, three years, one year, or six months.

| Category | Who is covered |
| --- | --- |
| Spouse of a Japanese national | A person in a legally valid marriage with a Japanese national |
| Special adoptee of a Japanese national | A child by special adoption under the Civil Code |
| Person born as a child of a Japanese national | A biological child whose father or mother was Japanese at birth |

There are three procedures for the status. To bring in a spouse living abroad, you apply for a certificate of eligibility; to switch from another status, you apply for a change of status; to extend the same status, you apply for an extension of the period of stay. The application is made by the person or by a representative etc. permitted by the regional immigration bureau.

## How is the substance of the marriage checked at certification and renewal?

Because you reside as a "spouse," it is checked that the marriage is genuine — not merely formal — and continuing. The Immigration Services Agency's list of documents to submit (referenced 24 October 2026) calls for materials showing the substance of the marriage and the state of your life. The specific forms and copies differ by the type of application and the bureau, so confirm them in the latest list.

| What is examined | Example materials (per the Agency's guidance) |
| --- | --- |
| Formation of the marriage | Family register, marriage certificate from the home country, etc. |
| Cohabitation and the substance of life | A copy of the resident record listing everyone in the household |
| Substance and interaction of the marriage | A questionnaire, photographs of the couple, records of contact, etc. |
| Livelihood and stability | A letter of guarantee, residence-tax taxation and payment certificates, a certificate of employment, etc. |

These materials help examine comprehensively whether the marriage is genuine and continuing. Even if the documents are formally complete, the examination is careful where the substance cannot be confirmed. How to prepare which documents depends on individual circumstances, and the final grant or refusal is decided by the bureau (this article makes no individual judgement on grant or refusal).

## Will renewal be refused when income or cohabitation is unstable?

Income and the state of cohabitation are among the factors considered in a renewal examination. But it is not decided mechanically by a single figure; the substance and continuity of the marriage and the stability of life are viewed comprehensively. Where income is low, you have just changed jobs, or you are temporarily living apart for a work posting or hospitalisation, the common practical approach is to attach a statement of reasons and supporting materials to show that the substance continues.

Whether a refusal is likely, and which document can supplement what, differs case by case. This article stays at general information; individual judgement rests on confirmation with the regional immigration bureau and consultation with a qualified professional.

## What happens to the status on living apart, divorce or bereavement (Article 22-4)?

If a person residing under "Spouse of a Japanese National" remains without carrying on activities as a spouse for six months or more, they fall within the grounds for revocation of status, absent a justifiable reason (Article 22-4 of the Act). The Immigration Services Agency's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) treats the target of this ground as the spouse residing under "Spouse of a Japanese National" or "Spouse of a Permanent Resident," and excludes a child or special adoptee of a Japanese national.

| Situation | Relationship to the status (a general outline) |
| --- | --- |
| Temporary separation (work posting, hospitalisation, etc.) | If the marriage continues, revocation does not follow at once; explaining the circumstances helps |
| Divorce or bereavement | Activities as a spouse become impossible; consider a change of status around a six-month marker |
| Living apart to escape DV etc. | May amount to a "justifiable reason" (the Ministry of Justice published examples in July 2012) |

Before revocation, an immigration inspector holds a hearing, where the person may state opinions, submit evidence and request to inspect materials. This ground (item vii) is a type where a period for departure (up to 30 days) is designated after revocation; it does not lead to immediate deportation (the Agency's guidance, referenced 24 October 2026). If you wish to continue residing in Japan after divorce or bereavement, the basic practice is to consider early a change to a work status, Long-Term Resident, or the like. What amounts to a "justifiable reason" or a possibility of change for a given person differs by circumstances, and the final judgement is made by the bureau.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I reside under "Spouse of a Japanese National" as soon as I register the marriage?**
A. The formation of the marriage alone is not enough; it is checked that the marriage is genuine and continuing. A spouse abroad applies for a certificate of eligibility, and someone switching from another status in Japan applies for a change of status, showing the substance with a questionnaire, resident record, materials of interaction and so on (Immigration Services Agency, referenced 24 October 2026). Grant or refusal is decided by the bureau.

**Q. Does my status disappear immediately on divorce?**
A. Divorce does not extinguish the status at once, but because activities as a spouse become impossible, if that state continues for six months or more without a justifiable reason, it can fall within the grounds for revocation (Article 22-4 of the Act). The common practice is to consider early, around a six-month marker, a change to a work status, Long-Term Resident, or the like.

**Q. What happens if I am living apart having fled DV?**
A. Living apart to escape DV etc. may amount to a "justifiable reason," and the Ministry of Justice published in July 2012 examples where revocation is not carried out. Consultation records, protection orders and medical certificates can serve as materials showing the circumstances. Whether it applies is judged by individual circumstances, so consult a lawyer or the regional immigration bureau early.

**Q. Will renewal always be refused if my income is low?**
A. It is not always refused. Income is one factor; the substance and continuity of the marriage and the stability of life are viewed comprehensively. Circumstances such as a recent job change or a temporary drop in income are commonly explained with a statement of reasons and supporting materials. The outlook differs case by case, so it rests on confirmation with the bureau and consultation with a professional.
