**In short:** when a spouse dies, the surviving spouse who was living, free of charge, in that building at the time inheritance began can keep living there free of charge for a set period until the estate division ends. This is the spousal short-term right of residence (Article 1037 of the Civil Code). The period is: where the division of the estate including that building is to be made, until the later of the day the building's attribution is fixed by the division or the day six months have passed from the start of inheritance (Article 1037(1)(i)); and where the building is not subject to the division, until the day six months have passed from the day the acquirer of the building makes the demand for its extinguishment (Article 1037(1)(ii) and (3)). This right is separate from the registrable (long-term) spousal right of residence (Article 1028 of the Civil Code): it arises automatically once the requirements are met, and cannot be registered. The system took effect on 1 April 2020 (Reiwa 2) and applies to inheritances that begin on or after that day. This article is general information and does not make the final judgment on whether a given residence can continue. ## When and in what case does the spousal short-term right of residence arise?

The spousal short-term right of residence is recognised where the spouse was living, free of charge, in a building that belonged to the decedent's property at the time inheritance began, as a right to use that building free of charge against the person who acquires it (the acquirer of the building) (Article 1037(1) of the Civil Code). It is a statutory right that arises automatically once the requirements are met, without being specially provided for in a will or division agreement; in nature it is described as a statutory claim similar to a loan for use. The system exists to prevent the surviving spouse from losing the home right after inheritance begins.

| Requirement | Content |
| --- | --- |
| The target building | A building that belonged to the decedent's property |
| State of residence | The spouse was living there free of charge at the start of inheritance |
| Content of the right | A right to use it free of charge against the acquirer of the building (short-term right of residence) |
| How it arises | Automatically once the requirements are met (no setting up by will/agreement) |

The "spouse" here means a legal spouse; a common-law relationship is not included. It is a right to "use" free of charge, and does not extend to "profit", such as earning rent income, which is where it differs from the long-term spousal right of residence. ## Until when can you stay? (the later of the division-fixing date and six months from the start of inheritance)

The period depends on whether the building is subject to the estate division. Where the division of that building is to be made among the co-heirs including the spouse, it lasts until the later of the day the building's attribution is fixed by the division or the day six months have passed from the start of inheritance (Article 1037(1)(i) of the Civil Code). Where it is not subject to the division, such as when the acquirer of the building is already decided by a will, the acquirer may make a demand for extinguishment (Article 1037(3)), and it lasts until the day six months have passed from that demand (Article 1037(1)(ii)).

| Situation | End point of being able to stay | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where that building is divided | The later of the day attribution is fixed by the division or six months from the start of inheritance | Civil Code Article 1037(1)(i) |
| Otherwise (acquirer fixed by bequest, etc.) | Six months from the day the acquirer makes the demand for extinguishment | Civil Code Article 1037(1)(ii) and (3) |

In other words, however the division proceeds, the home is secured for at least six months from the start of inheritance. The spouse must use the building according to the former method of use and with the care of a good manager, and cannot let a third party use it without the acquirer's consent (Article 1038(1) and (2) of the Civil Code). If this is breached, the acquirer may extinguish the short-term right of residence by a manifestation of intention (Article 1038(3)). ## How does it differ from the (long-term) spousal right of residence? Is registration needed?

The similarly named "spousal right of residence" (long-term) is a different right. It is a right the spouse acquires over a building in which they were living at the start of inheritance, when it is to be acquired by the division of the estate or is made the object of a bequest, to use and profit from the whole building free of charge (Article 1028(1) of the Civil Code). This is a registrable right, and the spouse is granted a right to claim registration (Article 1031(1) of the Civil Code). The spousal short-term right of residence, by contrast, is a short-term right that arises automatically once the requirements are met, and cannot be registered. When the spouse acquires the (long-term) spousal right of residence over the same building, the short-term right of residence is extinguished (Article 1039 of the Civil Code).

| Aspect | Spousal short-term right of residence | Spousal right of residence (long-term) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Civil Code Articles 1037 to 1041 | Civil Code Articles 1028 to 1036 |
| How it arises | Automatically once requirements are met | Acquired by division, bequest or gift on death |
| Content of the right | Use free of charge | Use and profit free of charge |
| Duration | In principle short-term, at least six months from the start of inheritance | In principle for life (a different term may be set) |
| Registration | Cannot be registered | Can be registered (right to claim registration, Article 1031) |

## When is the short-term right of residence not recognised?

The spousal short-term right of residence is not recognised in the following cases. Where the spouse had acquired the (long-term) spousal right of residence over the building at the start of inheritance, or where the spouse falls under disqualification of an heir (Article 891 of the Civil Code) or lost the right of inheritance by disinheritance, the short-term right does not arise (the proviso to Article 1037(1)). Also, because the premises are that the building "belonged to the decedent's property" and that the spouse was living there "free of charge" at the start of inheritance, where the spouse had been renting it for rent from before, you consider whether they can keep living there under the ordinary rules of lease rather than this system.

| Case where it is not recognised / a premise is lacking | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Had acquired the (long-term) right of residence at the start of inheritance | The long-term right makes the short-term one unnecessary (proviso to Art. 1037(1)) |
| Lost the right of inheritance by disqualification/disinheritance | Excluded by the proviso (Article 891, etc.) |
| Was not living there free of charge at the start of inheritance | The premise of "living free of charge" is lacking |

When the spousal short-term right of residence is extinguished, the spouse must in principle return the building (Article 1040 of the Civil Code). Certain provisions on loans for use and the like are applied mutatis mutandis (Article 1041 of the Civil Code). ## FAQ

**Q. Even if the will says the house goes to someone else, can the spouse keep living there?**
A. They can for a set period. Even where the acquirer of the building is fixed by a will, a spouse who was living there free of charge at the start of inheritance is granted the spousal short-term right of residence and may use it free of charge until six months have passed from the day the acquirer makes the demand for extinguishment (Article 1037(1)(ii) and (3) of the Civil Code). Disputes over residence after that are a field for a lawyer.

**Q. Can the spousal short-term right of residence be registered?**
A. It cannot. What can be registered is the long-term spousal right of residence (Article 1028 of the Civil Code; the right to claim registration is Article 1031(1)); the short-term right arises automatically once the requirements are met and is not an object of registration. When the spouse acquires the long-term spousal right of residence over the same building, the short-term right is extinguished (Article 1039 of the Civil Code).

**Q. While living there, who bears the upkeep and property tax?**
A. The spouse bears the duty to use the building according to the former method of use and with the care of a good manager (Article 1038(1) of the Civil Code), and in principle bears the ordinary necessary expenses (Article 1034, etc., applied mutatis mutandis). The specific bearing and settlement of costs such as property tax differ by the individual circumstances, so this article gives no uniform conclusion. Please confirm the judgment with a qualified professional.

**Q. Is the spousal short-term right of residence recognised for a common-law wife (husband)?**
A. The "spouse" of the spousal short-term right of residence means a legal spouse, and a common-law (de facto) partner is not included. Whether a common-law partner can keep living there is examined individually under a different legal construction such as a loan for use or lease, and if it becomes a dispute it is a field for a lawyer.
