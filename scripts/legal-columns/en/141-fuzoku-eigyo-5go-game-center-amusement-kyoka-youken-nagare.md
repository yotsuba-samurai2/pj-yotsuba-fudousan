**In short:** to run a game arcade or amusement facility that provides crane games and gaming machines, the business falls under type 5 (Article 2, paragraph 1, item 5 of the Act on Control and Improvement of Amusement Business, the "Entertainment Business Act"), and under Article 3, paragraph 1 you must obtain, for each premises, the licence of the prefectural public safety commission with jurisdiction over its location. The licence requires satisfying all of: (1) the personal requirement (the grounds for disqualification in Article 4, paragraph 1), (2) the location requirement (Article 4, paragraph 2, item 2 and the distance from protected facilities set by cabinet order), and (3) the technical standards for structure and equipment (Article 4, paragraph 2, item 1 and Article 7 of the Enforcement Regulation, such as illumination and partitions); you file the application and drawings through the police station with jurisdiction and can operate only after examination and an on-site inspection. This article is general information organising the type-5 requirements, the application flow and the operating rules; it does not judge the outcome of any individual licence.

## Why is a game arcade a type-5 business under the Act, and which shops need a licence?

The Act defines amusement businesses by type in each item of Article 2, paragraph 1. A game arcade or amusement facility falls under item 5. The provision reads as follows.

- Article 2, paragraph 1, item 5: "a business that, in a shop or similar partitioned facility equipped with slot machines, video game machines or other gaming equipment that can be used for games risking inciting a speculative spirit in a use other than its original use (limited to those set by National Public Safety Commission rules), lets customers play games with that equipment" (excluding a business falling under item 4, a mahjong or pachinko parlour).

What is regulated here is not every machine in the shop, but gaming equipment "that can be used for games risking inciting a speculative spirit in a use other than its original use," limited to those set by National Public Safety Commission rules (the Enforcement Regulation). Which equipment falls within scope, and how it is judged by the scale of the shop and the proportion of equipment, depends on the Enforcement Regulation's standards, so among shops that "have games," some are type-5 businesses and some are not. Whether your shop falls under type 5 (whether a licence is needed) is best confirmed, before deciding on a property, with the police station (community safety division) with jurisdiction, showing the specific type and layout of the equipment you will install.

| Type | Typical example | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type 4 | Pachinko parlour, mahjong parlour | Act Art. 2(1)(iv) |
| Type 5 | Game arcade, amusement facility | Act Art. 2(1)(v) |

A shop falling under type 5 requires the licence of the prefectural public safety commission for each premises under Article 3, paragraph 1, and cannot start operating until the licence is granted. ## What must the structural standards (sight lines, illumination, gaming machines) meet?

The structural standard is the technical standard under Article 4, paragraph 2, item 1 of the Act, made concrete by National Public Safety Commission rules (Article 7 of the Enforcement Regulation) and prefectural ordinances. The typical standards examined for a type-5 business are as follows.

| Item | Approximate standard (type 5) |
| --- | --- |
| Illumination | Structure and equipment that keep the illumination in the premises from falling to 10 lux or below (maintenance during operation is Article 14 of the Act) |
| Sight lines | No equipment inside the guest room that blocks the line of sight |
| Public morals | No photographs, decorations or other equipment that risk harming good public morals, a clean environment, or the sound upbringing of minors |
| Exits | No locking device on the guest-room exit (except an exit leading directly outside the premises) |
| Gaming equipment | No gaming equipment with a device that accepts banknotes for game charges or that provides cash or securities |

A type-5 business is prohibited from mechanisms that provide cash or securities according to the result of play. The handling of prizes (which items may be provided and within what price range) also follows the National Police Agency's interpretive standards, and the handling of crane-game prizes needs to be checked shop by shop. Because being flagged in the on-site inspection follows when the drawings submitted with the application (area-calculation drawing, floor plan, lighting-equipment drawing) and the actual state differ, it is safer to build the illumination, sight lines and equipment layout into the interior design stage. As the specific figures and forms differ by prefecture, check the latest content in the guidance of the police station with jurisdiction.

## Where are the land-use zone and the distance from protected facilities decided?

For type 5 too, the "location requirement" is directly tied to property choice. Article 4, paragraph 2, item 2 of the Act provides that a licence will not be granted where the premises are in an area designated by prefectural ordinance, in accordance with standards set by cabinet order as needing special restriction to preserve a good public-morals environment. That cabinet order is Article 6 of the Enforcement Order, which sets a framework allowing ordinances to designate as restricted areas: (1) areas where many dwellings are concentrated, and (2) the areas within a certain distance around "protected facilities" such as schools, libraries, child welfare facilities, hospitals and clinics. For the surroundings of protected facilities, the Enforcement Order sets a limit of roughly a 100-metre zone around the site.

The actual distances differ by prefectural ordinance. Even if the property is in a commercial zone, a nearby school or hospital can make the licence unobtainable due to the distance restriction. Land-use zones are fixed by city planning under the Building Standards Act and change block by block even along the same street. Before signing the property lease, it is essential to check, under the ordinance for the property's location, both the land-use zone and the distance from protected facilities.

| Item to check | How to check |
| --- | --- |
| Whether it is a restricted area | The prefecture's ordinance under the Entertainment Business Act for the property's location |
| Distance from protected facilities | The positions of nearby schools, libraries, child welfare facilities, hospitals and clinics, and the ordinance's distances |
| Land-use zone | The municipality's city-planning information (Building Standards Act) |

## What do you file with the public safety commission, and how long does it take?

The application is made, through the police station (community safety division) with jurisdiction over the premises' location, to the prefectural public safety commission. The main documents are as follows (forms, numbers of copies and attachments differ by prefecture).

| Category | Main documents |
| --- | --- |
| Common | Licence application (prescribed form), a document stating the method of business, a document establishing the right to use the premises (such as the lease), the floor plan, area-calculation drawing and lighting drawing of the premises, and an outline map of the surroundings (showing the positions of protected facilities within roughly 100 metres) |
| Individual applicant | The applicant's certificate of residence, certificate of status, and a written pledge |
| Corporate applicant | Articles of incorporation, certificate of registered matters, and the certificate of residence, certificate of status and written pledge of all officers |
| Manager | The premises manager's certificate of residence, written pledge, photograph, etc. |

After filing, in addition to the documentary examination, the police carry out an on-site inspection (checking the drawings against the actual state). The standard processing period is said to be roughly 55 days depending on the prefecture, and some commissions count business days excluding weekends and holidays. You cannot operate until the licence is granted. The fee (licence application fee) is set by each prefecture. Some commissions do not accept applications by post or fax, and changing the premises' structure and equipment requires a separate change approval or notification, so always check the form, period and fee in the official guidance of the police station (prefectural public safety commission) with jurisdiction just before applying. Confirming facts about a criminal record or history relating to the grounds for disqualification (Article 4, paragraph 1) is within the jurisdiction of the applicant and the police; our office cannot certify on the applicant's behalf whether there is a criminal record.

## What operating rules apply, such as admitting minors under 16 and under 18?

Even after the licence is granted, there are rules to observe during operation. The handling of minors' entry differs by age.

| Target | Approximate restriction | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 18 | Not admitted as a customer between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day | Act Art. 22(1)(v) |
| Under 16 | Not admitted as a customer after sunset (such as after 6 p.m.); ordinances set an exception for accompaniment by a guardian | Prefectural ordinance |

That a person under 18 may not be admitted as a customer between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day is set by Article 22, paragraph 1, item 5 of the Act. The entry of a person under 16 after sunset is restricted by many prefectural ordinances, which set an exception where a guardian accompanies the minor. The amendment in force on 23 June 2016 relaxed the handling of the time an accompanied minor may stay, but the specific times and exceptions differ by prefecture. Check the application of ages and hours, and the restriction on operating hours themselves (set by prefectural ordinance), in the guidance of the police station with jurisdiction. Besides this, there is an obligation to maintain the structural standards during operation too, such as keeping the illumination in the premises from falling to 10 lux or below (Article 14).

## FAQ

**Q. Does any shop with games need a type-5 licence?**
A. No. A type-5 business is a shop equipped with gaming equipment that can be used for games risking inciting a speculative spirit in a use other than its original use (limited to those set by National Public Safety Commission rules), that lets customers play games with that equipment (Article 2, paragraph 1, item 5). Depending on the type of equipment and the scale of the shop, it may not fall under type 5. Whether your shop falls under type 5 (whether a licence is needed) should be confirmed with the police station with jurisdiction, showing the equipment and its layout, before deciding on a property.

**Q. Isn't a notification enough?**
A. No. Type 5 requires a "licence." A late-night alcohol-serving restaurant business (without entertainment) needs only the "notification" under Article 33, paragraph 1, but type 5, which lets customers play games with gaming equipment, is subject to a "licence" that examines the personal, location and structural requirements. You cannot start operating until the licence is granted.

**Q. If the property is in a commercial zone, is the distance restriction irrelevant?**
A. No. Even in a commercial zone, a nearby protected facility such as a school, library, child welfare facility, hospital or clinic can make the licence unobtainable under the prefectural ordinance's distance restriction. The distance differs by ordinance, and Article 6 of the Enforcement Order sets a limit of roughly 100 metres around a protected facility. Check the ordinance for the location before signing the lease.

**Q. Until what time may children enter?**
A. A person under 18 may not be admitted as a customer between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. the next day (Article 22, paragraph 1, item 5). The entry of a person under 16 after sunset is restricted by many prefectural ordinances, which set an exception for accompaniment by a guardian. Because the specific times and exceptions differ by prefecture, check the guidance of the police station with jurisdiction.
