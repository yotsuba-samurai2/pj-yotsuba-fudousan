**In short:** to start a recruitment-agency business (fee-charging employment placement), you need the licence of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Employment Security Act Article 30(1)). The criteria for the licence are three: (1) a financial basis sufficient to carry on the business soundly; (2) measures to manage personal information properly and keep the secrets of job offerers, job seekers, and others; and (3) the ability to carry on the business properly (Article 31(1)). In practice, as the financial basis, net assets (total assets minus total liabilities) of at least 5 million yen per office and cash and deposits in your own name of at least 1.5 million yen are required (the licensing criteria of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare "Guidelines on the Operation of Employment Placement Businesses"; accessed 2026-11-17), and an Employment Placement Manager must be appointed at each office (Employment Security Act Article 32-14). The licence lasts three years when new and five years on renewal (Article 32-6). Occupations in port transport work and construction work cannot be handled through fee-charging placement (Article 32-11). This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a particular licence is possible and on conformity with the criteria rests with the licensing authority and qualified professionals. Preparing the licence application (documents submitted to a public agency) is handled by Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office (administrative scrivener); where worker dispatch is also carried on, the dispatching-undertaking responsible person and labour management, and social insurance and work rules for the employment after placement, by a labour and social security attorney; negotiation and representation in labour disputes such as unpaid wages by an attorney; incorporation registration by a judicial scrivener; and accounts and tax by a tax accountant, each as an independent business under a separate contract. Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office and Yotsuba Real Estate Co., Ltd. are separate businesses, and our office receives no referral fee.

## On what law is a fee-charging employment placement licence based?

A person who intends to carry on a fee-charging employment placement business must obtain the licence of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare (Employment Security Act Article 30(1)). "Employment placement" means receiving offers of jobs and of work and mediating the formation of an employment relationship between the offerer and the seeker; a licence is needed where this is done for a fee (receiving commissions or remuneration) as a business. Free placement has its own rules, but this article focuses on fee-charging placement. Carrying on fee-charging placement without a licence is subject to penalties.

The licensing criteria are set by law. The Minister must grant the licence when the application is found to meet the following criteria (Employment Security Act Article 31(1)). First, the applicant has a financial basis sufficient to carry on the business soundly. Second, measures necessary to manage personal information properly and keep the secrets of job offerers, job seekers, and others are in place. Third, besides these, the applicant has the ability to carry on the business properly. Specific amounts and facility standards are shown not in the Act itself but in the licensing criteria such as the Ministry's "Guidelines on the Operation of Employment Placement Businesses."

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Licensing authority | Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare | Employment Security Act Article 30(1) |
| Criterion (1) | Financial basis | Employment Security Act Article 31(1)(i) |
| Criterion (2) | Proper management of personal information and confidentiality | Employment Security Act Article 31(1)(ii) |
| Criterion (3) | Ability to carry on the business properly | Employment Security Act Article 31(1)(iii) |
| Term of validity | Three years when new; five on renewal | Employment Security Act Article 32-6 |

Preparing the application (documents submitted to a public agency) is an administrative scrivener's work. On the other hand, preparing the attached financial statements and balance sheet and valuing net assets is a tax accountant's domain; social insurance procedures and work rules for workers after hiring are a labour and social security attorney's domain; and negotiation and representation in disputes such as unpaid wages are an attorney's domain. Where the support is document preparation and where it becomes another profession's work is set out in the second half of this article. ## How are the asset requirements (net assets of 5 million yen; cash and deposits of 1.5 million yen) checked?

The financial basis has amount guides in the Ministry's licensing criteria. There are two representative standards. First, net assets (total assets minus total liabilities, valued excluding deferred assets and goodwill) must be at least 5 million yen multiplied by the number of offices. Second, cash and deposits in your own name must be at least 1.5 million yen plus 600,000 yen for each office beyond one (1.5 million yen for one office; 2.1 million yen for two) (the licensing criteria of the "Guidelines on the Operation of Employment Placement Businesses"; accessed 2026-11-17).

For both corporations and individuals, these are checked in principle against the balance sheet for the latest business year, or, for an individual, the state of assets and liabilities. A corporation in its first year that has not yet closed its accounts is judged on the balance sheet at establishment. Because the valuation involves accounting and tax judgement, a tax accountant prepares the financial statements and values the assets and liabilities, and the administrative scrivener arranges the application on the basis of those computations. The final evaluation of whether particular assets meet the standard is made by the licensing authority on the basis of the submitted documents.

| Requirement | How it is calculated (for one office) | For multiple offices |
|---|---|---|
| Net assets | Total assets minus total liabilities, at least 5 million yen | 5 million yen x number of offices |
| Cash and deposits | At least 1.5 million yen in your own name | 1.5 million yen + 600,000 yen x (offices - 1) |
| Basis of valuation | Balance sheet, etc. for the latest business year | Same |

Figures and formulas may change as the criteria are revised. Before applying, always confirm the latest "Guidelines on the Operation of Employment Placement Businesses" and the handbook of the Prefectural Labour Bureau with jurisdiction. The amounts here are as of the access date. Confirming at the window any figures you cannot substantiate, rather than filling them in by guesswork, is important to avoid remand.

## What are the requirements on office size and the Employment Placement Manager?

As for the office, a floor-area guide of "roughly 20 square metres or more" was once applied, but at present the core requirements are securing privacy and protecting personal information. It must have a structure that can manage the personal information of job offerers and seekers properly and protect the privacy of job seekers (division by private rooms or partitions, lockable document storage, etc.), independence as an office, and documentary proof of the right to use it. The floor-area guide is applied in a relaxed manner as an "interim handling," and treatment differs for online-only formats without in-person contact (the Ministry's licensing criteria; accessed 2026-11-17; as operation may change, confirm with the Labour Bureau with jurisdiction).

As a requirement on people, an Employment Placement Manager must be appointed at each office (Employment Security Act Article 32-14). The Employment Placement Manager supervises the handling of complaints, the management of job offerers' information and job seekers' personal information, the receipt of offers and advice and guidance, liaison with employment security bodies, and so on. In practice, at least one is appointed per office for roughly every 50 staff engaged in placement, and the appointee must have completed an Employment Placement Manager training course within five years before the date the application is accepted (the Ministry's licensing criteria; accessed 2026-11-17). A minor cannot be an Employment Placement Manager.

| Category | Core requirement |
|---|---|
| Office | Structure that secures privacy and manages personal information properly; documentary proof of the right to use it |
| Employment Placement Manager | Appointed per office; at least one per roughly 50 staff |
| Training | Completion of the Employment Placement Manager course within five years before acceptance of the application |
| Disqualification | A minor cannot serve; must not fall under the statutory grounds for disqualification |

## In what order does the application proceed, and what occupations (port transport, construction) cannot be handled?

A fee-charging placement operator cannot place every occupation. Occupations in port transport work, and occupations in construction work (work relating to the construction, alteration, preservation, repair, change, destruction, or dismantling of earthworks, buildings, or other structures, or the preparatory work for these), cannot be handled through fee-charging placement (Employment Security Act Article 32-11(1)). These fields are left to separate schemes (the Port Labour Act and the rules of construction labour) from the standpoint of worker protection. Mistaking the range of occupations handled can amount to unlawful placement beyond the scope of the licence.

The order of application generally proceeds as follows. First, the person to become the Employment Placement Manager takes the Employment Placement Manager training course. Second, you secure an office and set up the structure for securing privacy and managing personal information. Third, you confirm the financial basis (net assets and cash/deposits) against the latest financial statements. Fourth, you prepare the application including a business plan and apply through the Prefectural Labour Bureau with jurisdiction. After examination, a licence certificate is issued. The costs are a registration and licence tax of 90,000 yen (a fixed amount per licence) and a licence fee (revenue stamps) of 50,000 yen, with 18,000 yen added for each office beyond one where there are two or more (the Ministry's guidance; accessed 2026-11-17).

## FAQ

**Q. Can I start fee-charging placement and worker dispatch with the same licence?**
A. No. Fee-charging placement is the licence of the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare under Employment Security Act Article 30(1); worker dispatch is a separate licence under the Worker Dispatch Act, with a different basis and different requirements. To do both, you must obtain each licence. Where dispatch is also carried on, appointing the dispatching-undertaking responsible person and labour management are a labour and social security attorney's domain, handled as an independent business under a separate contract.

**Q. If my assets fall short of 5 million yen, can I not get the licence?**
A. The financial basis is one of the criteria: net assets of at least 5 million yen per office and cash/deposits of at least 1.5 million yen are required (increased for multiple offices; accessed 2026-11-17). Not meeting this makes the licence difficult. Because the valuation involves accounting and tax judgement, consult a tax accountant on preparing financial statements and valuing assets. The individual judgement is made by the licensing authority on the submitted documents.

**Q. Is one Employment Placement Manager in the company enough?**
A. It may not be. An Employment Placement Manager must be appointed at each office, with roughly one per 50 staff engaged in placement as a guide (Employment Security Act Article 32-14; the Ministry's licensing criteria). The appointee must have completed the Employment Placement Manager course within five years before the application is accepted. Where there are multiple offices, one must be placed at each.

**Q. Can I place, for a fee, people who work at construction sites?**
A. No. Occupations in construction work, together with occupations in port transport work, are prohibited from fee-charging placement (Employment Security Act Article 32-11(1)). These fields are left to separate schemes and are outside the scope of a fee-charging placement licence. The individual judgement of applicability must be made on the actual facts of the work.
