**In short:** manufacturing alcoholic beverages requires a licence for each category, and you must expect to meet a per-category minimum annual production volume (60 kilolitres for beer and sake, 6 kilolitres for happoshu and other brewed drinks). A doburoku special zone (Article 25 of the Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform) is an exception that removes this standard. Apply at the tax office for the manufacturing site and confirm requirements with the National Tax Agency guide.

Updated: 2026-10-10

## What differs between manufacturing licences by category?

Under Article 7(1) of the Liquor Tax Act (酒税法), you must obtain a licence from the head of the tax office for the manufacturing site, for each category of beverage you intend to make. Categories include refined sake, synthetic sake, continuous-still and single-still shochu, mirin, beer, fruit wine, sweet fruit wine, whisky, brandy, happoshu, other brewed beverages, spirits, liqueurs, raw-material alcohol, powdered liquor and miscellaneous liquor. You may not make a category outside the licence granted. Craft beer is generally classified as "beer", and doburoku as an "other brewed beverage" made from rice and not filtered.

Whether a licence is granted is judged against the refusal grounds listed in Article 10 of the Act (requirements for a manufacturing licence). The main grounds appear below; the tax office assesses each item individually.

| Main refusal grounds, Article 10 | Content (per the guide) |
|---|---|
| Item 6 | Subject to a delinquency disposition for national or local tax within the two years before applying |
| Item 8 | Less than three years since completion of a sentence of imprisonment or heavier |
| Item 10 | A weak management base, such as an undischarged bankrupt |
| Item 11 | Inappropriate for maintaining the supply-demand balance of liquor, for liquor tax preservation |
| Item 12 | Lacks the technical ability to manufacture, or the site's equipment is insufficient |

Which category to apply under is confirmed with the tax office in advance, based on ingredients, process and expected volume.

## How does the minimum-production standard differ between craft beer and special-zone doburoku?

Article 7(2) of the Liquor Tax Act provides that a licence cannot be granted if the volume you expect to make at one site in the year after licensing does not reach the volume set for the category. This is the minimum-production standard. The main categories are as follows.

| Category | Minimum production standard (per year, per site) |
|---|---|
| Refined sake, synthetic sake, continuous-still shochu, beer | 60 kilolitres |
| Single-still shochu, mirin | 10 kilolitres |
| Fruit wine, sweet fruit wine, whisky, brandy, happoshu, other brewed beverages, spirits, liqueurs, raw-material alcohol, miscellaneous liquor | 6 kilolitres |

A craft brewery applies under "beer", so an expected annual volume of 60 kilolitres is the benchmark. Note that the 2018 reform (effective 1 April 2018) broadened the definition of beer: some products formerly made as happoshu were reclassified as beer, and the benchmark shifted from 6 to 60 kilolitres in such cases. Because the applicable category changes the standard, show your ingredients and recipe to the tax office. Special-zone doburoku is treated differently (next section).

## What structural and equipment requirements must the premises meet?

The site's equipment must not fall under Article 10, Item 12 of the Act ("the site's equipment is insufficient"); it must have manufacturing and storage equipment suited to the category. Specific standards follow the National Tax Agency guide and the tax office's review. It is safer to consult the tax office before leasing or constructing, together with the use and lease terms.

| Point to check | Content (varies by category and scale) |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing equipment | Mashing, fermentation and storage equipment and capacity for the category |
| Measurement and records | Measuring equipment and bookkeeping to track production volume |
| Separation and hygiene | Separation of the manufacturing area, water/drainage, cleanable structure |
| Right to use the premises | Whether the lease permits use as a manufacturing site |

Liquor manufacturing itself is handled under the Liquor Tax Act, but making other products such as soft drinks on the same site, or certain sales forms, may separately require a permit or notification under the Food Sanitation Act; confirm with the local public health centre. An architect advises on any change-of-use or confirmation under the Building Standards Act, and fire equipment is discussed with the fire department. ## How far are requirements relaxed in a doburoku special zone?

Article 25 of the Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform (manufacturing of specified liquor by specified farmers) provides that, within an approved special zone, a farmer who also runs a business "serving liquor for drinking at their own premises" — such as a farm guesthouse or farm restaurant — and who makes doburoku and similar from rice they grew at their own site, is not subject to Article 7(2) (the minimum-production standard) or Article 12, Item 4 of the Liquor Tax Act. This is the doburoku special zone.

| Comparison | Ordinary manufacturing licence | Doburoku special zone (Article 25) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum-production standard | 6 kilolitres for other brewed beverages | Not applied |
| Who qualifies | Applicants meeting the requirements generally | Farmers in the zone who also serve liquor for drinking |
| What can be made | The licensed category | Doburoku etc. (as approved in the zone plan) |
| Manufacturing licence | Required | Required (not waived) |
| Bookkeeping and liquor tax | Required | Required (not waived) |

What is relaxed is part of the requirements, such as the minimum-production standard; the manufacturing licence itself is still required. Unlicensed manufacturing violates the Liquor Tax Act. Bookkeeping and liquor tax payment are not waived. Because zone approval and practice vary by municipality and tax office, confirm before applying whether you fall within the zone plan and the current treatment.

## FAQ

**Q. How much must I expect to make to start a craft brewery?**
A. If you apply under "beer", the minimum-production standard in Article 7(2) of the Liquor Tax Act is 60 kilolitres a year. A licence is not granted if the volume you expect to make at one site in the first year does not reach it. Because the standard changes with whether your product is beer or happoshu, show your ingredients and recipe to the tax office.

**Q. Can I make a small amount in a doburoku special zone?**
A. Under Article 25 of the Act on Special Zones for Structural Reform, a qualifying farmer in the zone who serves liquor at their own premises is not subject to the minimum-production standard in Article 7(2) when making doburoku. A manufacturing licence is still required, and bookkeeping and liquor tax are not waived. Confirm with the municipality and tax office that you fall within the zone plan.

**Q. Does a manufacturing licence let me sell as well?**
A. A manufacturing licence covers manufacturing. Selling liquor may require a separate liquor sales licence. Because the need varies with the sales form, see also [retail liquor licences and shop premises](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/shurui-kouri-menkyo-tenpo-bukken-yoken) and confirm with the tax office.

**Q. Can an administrative scrivener handle tax after the licence?**
A. Liquor and corporate tax returns and bookkeeping are the work of a tax accountant. An administrative scrivener prepares and submits the licence documents. You contract separately with each professional, and our office receives no referral fee.
