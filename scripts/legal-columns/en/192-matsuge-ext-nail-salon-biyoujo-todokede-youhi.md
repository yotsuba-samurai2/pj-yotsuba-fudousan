**In short:** Eyelash extension work is treated as "biyo" (beauty) under the Beauty Culturists Act, so a licensed beautician performs it at a beauty shop (biyojo) that has been notified in advance to the public health center (the beauty-shop notification under Article 11 of the Act, per Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare notices). A nail-only salon does not clearly fall within the Act's definition of "biyo" (Article 2(1): permanent waving, hairdressing, make-up and the like), so a beautician's license and a beauty-shop opening notification are, in principle, read as not required — but the public health center and the prefecture make the final call. Artmake, which injects pigment into the skin with a needle, is the field of a physician: no one but a doctor may do it as a business (Medical Practitioners Act, Article 17).

## Do eyelash extensions require a "beauty shop" notification?

Eyelash extension work is treated by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) as "biyo" under the Beauty Culturists Act. Article 2(1) of the Act defines "biyo" as "making a person's appearance beautiful by methods such as permanent waving, hairdressing and make-up"; paragraph (2) defines a "beautician" as a person licensed by the Minister who practices biyo as a business, and paragraph (3) defines a "beauty shop" (biyojo) as a facility set up to carry on the business of biyo.

The MHLW, starting with its notice of 7 March 2008 "On thoroughly preventing harm from eyelash extensions" and in later notices of 18 February 2010 and 28 November 2012, has positioned eyelash extensions as a beauty act under the Act. On that footing, a shop that performs eyelash extensions as a business has a licensed beautician work at a beauty shop that has been notified in advance to the prefectural governor (in practice, the public health center). The opening notification is set out in Article 11(1) of the Act: a person who intends to open a beauty shop must notify the prefectural governor **in advance** of the location, the structure and equipment, the names of the managing beautician and other staff, and other necessary matters (a prior notification).

| Business type | Beautician's license | Beauty-shop opening notification | Main basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eyelash extensions | Required (practitioner) | Required (prior notification) | Act Art. 11; MHLW notices |
| Nail only | Generally read as not required | Generally read as outside scope | Definition of "biyo", Art. 2(1) |
| Artmake (needle pigment injection) | Not a beautician matter | — | Medical Practitioners Act Art. 17 (medical act) |

The intent of the notices and their application to a given case vary by municipality and may be revised, so show your service menu and confirm with the public health center with jurisdiction right before you proceed.

## Does a nail-only salon really need no notification?

A nail-only salon is generally read as not falling clearly within the Act's "biyo." The Article 2(1) definition is "permanent waving, hairdressing, make-up and the like," wording that does not plainly cover work on the nails of the hands and feet. On that reading, a nail-only shop is, in principle, not required to hold a beautician's license or file a beauty-shop opening notification. This is not a settled administrative ruling, however; the public health center and the prefecture decide on the facts of the actual service.

In support, the hygiene of nail salons is handled not under the Beauty Culturists Act but under a separate guideline. The MHLW Health Bureau Director-General's notice "On the guideline for hygiene management at nail salons" (15 September 2010, Ken-hatsu 0915 No. 4) treats barber and beauty shops as **outside the guideline's scope** — because the Barbers Act and the Beauty Culturists Act already require them to secure a hygiene standard — and handles nail salons separately. In other words, nail salons are placed under hygiene management distinct from the beauty-shop framework of the Beauty Culturists Act. That guideline is technical advice under Article 245-4(1) of the Local Autonomy Act, serving as a benchmark for the public health center's guidance and advice.

If one shop offers both nail work and eyelash extensions, the eyelash portion may require a beautician's license and a beauty-shop opening notification. Because the answer turns on the combination of services, the more you run multiple business types in one shop, the more worthwhile it is to confirm with the public health center in advance.

## Which acts require a beautician's license?

Article 6 of the Beauty Culturists Act provides that "a person who is not a beautician must not practice biyo as a business," prohibiting unlicensed beauty business. Article 7 further provides that a beautician must not practice biyo anywhere other than a beauty shop (except where there is a special circumstance set by Cabinet Order). So a treatment amounting to biyo (such as eyelash extensions) is, as a basic rule, (1) performed by a licensed beautician (2) at a notified beauty shop.

The measures to be taken when practicing biyo are also set by law. Article 8 requires keeping clean the cloths and instruments that touch the skin, replacing the skin-contact cloths for each customer, and disinfecting the instruments for each customer. On management, a beauty shop with two or more beautician staff at all times must appoint a managing beautician — a person who has engaged in biyo for at least three years after licensing and has completed a training course designated by the prefectural governor — to manage the shop hygienically (Article 12-3).

| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| Art. 6 | No one but a beautician may practice biyo as a business (ban on unlicensed business) |
| Art. 7 | A beautician must not practice biyo outside a beauty shop |
| Art. 8 | Keep instruments and cloths clean; replace and disinfect per customer |
| Art. 12-3 | A shop with two or more beauticians at all times must appoint a managing beautician |

Which treatment amounts to "biyo," and how far an act requires a license, is ultimately decided by qualified persons, the public health center and the prefecture. This article is general information only.

## What must the facility meet when you notify a beauty shop?

As to structure and equipment, the person opening a beauty shop must not use it until the prefectural governor has inspected the structure and equipment and confirmed that they are suitable for taking the measures in Article 13 (Article 12). The order is thus: notification → inspection and confirmation of structure and equipment → start of use.

The measures to be taken are set in Article 13: (1) keep the shop clean at all times; (2) provide disinfection equipment; (3) provide adequate daylight, lighting and ventilation; and (4) other hygiene measures prescribed by prefectural ordinance. Note that **the concrete numerical standards — floor area, partitioning, number of wash basins — are set not by a nationwide ordinance of the ministry but by the ordinances and guidance of the prefecture (including public-health-center cities and special wards)**. Unlike acupuncture facilities or laundries, the floor area is not fixed by national ministerial ordinance, so you must check the ordinance of the location where you open.

The prefectural governor may, when it finds it necessary, have officials enter and inspect a beauty shop (Article 14), and where the opener violates Article 12-3 or Article 13, or lets a non-beautician practice biyo there, may order the shop closed for a fixed period (Article 15). Bring a floor plan and consult the public health center of the location in advance. For the whole picture of the beauty-shop opening notification and facility standards, see [the beauty-shop opening notification and facility standards](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/biyoushojo-kaisetsu-todokede-kozo-setsubi-kijun).

## Where is the line with medical practice (artmake, etc.) that you must not cross?

Artmake — injecting pigment into the eyebrows or eyeline with a needle (so-called medical or cosmetic artmake) — is an act of piercing the skin with a needle to insert pigment, and the MHLW treats it as a medical act. Article 17 of the Medical Practitioners Act provides that "no one but a physician may practice medicine," so only a physician (or a nurse and the like acting under a physician's instruction) may do this as a business. It is not a field a beautician's license or a beauty-shop notification can substitute for, and it is a line that unqualified persons — including an administrative scrivener or a real estate agent — must not cross.

Eyelash extensions are "biyo" and artmake is a "medical act": the governing law and the person who may perform them differ. Listing the two together on a menu as if they were the same can raise a Medical Practitioners Act problem. Whether a specific treatment amounts to a medical act is judged case by case on the method and degree, so if you are considering a menu item near the boundary, be sure to confirm with a physician and the public health center with jurisdiction. This article does not enter judgments under the Medical Practitioners Act.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I open an eyelash-extension salon in a room of my home?**
A. Eyelash extensions are treated as biyo under the Beauty Culturists Act, performed by a licensed beautician at a notified beauty shop (Article 11; MHLW notices). The structure and equipment are inspected and confirmed under Articles 12 and 13, and the concrete floor area and partitioning depend on the prefecture's ordinance and guidance. Whether a room of your home qualifies turns on the layout and the local standard, so bring a floor plan and consult the public health center in advance.

**Q. For nails only, is truly no notification needed?**
A. Nail-only work does not clearly fall within the Act's "biyo" (Article 2(1)), so a beautician's license and a beauty-shop opening notification are, in principle, read as outside scope. This is not a settled administrative ruling, however; the public health center and the prefecture decide. For hygiene, the separate "guideline for hygiene management at nail salons" serves as a benchmark. Confirm with the public health center before opening by showing your services.

**Q. Can lash lifts or eyebrow artmake also be done on a beautician's license?**
A. Treatments amounting to "biyo," such as a lash lift, are a matter of the beautician's license and the beauty-shop framework. Artmake, which injects pigment with a needle, is treated by the MHLW as a medical act, so only a physician may do it as a business (Medical Practitioners Act, Article 17). Because beauty and medical practice are governed by different laws, confirm menu items near the boundary with a physician and the public health center.
