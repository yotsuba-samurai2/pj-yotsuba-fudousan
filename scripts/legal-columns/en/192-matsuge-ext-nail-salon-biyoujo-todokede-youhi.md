**In short:** In Japan, eyelash extensions must be applied by a licensed beautician at premises notified and inspected before use (Beauty Culturists Act, Articles 6, 7, 11 and 12). A salon offering only ordinary nail care and decoration generally does not need that licence or notification. If you plan to offer both, ask the local public health centre about the layout before signing a lease. Permanent makeup on areas such as the eyebrows or eyelids, applied with a needle and pigment, is a medical act and cannot be offered by a beauty salon.

## Do eyelash extensions require a "beauty shop" notification?

Eyelash extension work is treated by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) as "biyo" under the Beauty Culturists Act. Article 2(1) of the Act defines "biyo" as "making a person's appearance beautiful by methods such as permanent waving, hairdressing and make-up"; paragraph (2) defines a "beautician" as a person licensed by the Minister who practices biyo as a business, and paragraph (3) defines a "beauty shop" (biyojo) as a facility set up to carry on the business of biyo.

MHLW notices, starting on 7 March 2008, treat eyelash extensions as beauty work under the Act. The practitioner must be licensed, and the operator notifies the local public health centre in advance of the premises, facilities and names of staff, among other details (Arts. 6, 7 and 11(1)). The competent authority is the prefectural governor, or the mayor or ward head in a city with a public health centre or a special ward (Art. 20). Filing is not enough to begin operating: the premises must be inspected and confirmed before use (Art. 12).

| Business type | Beautician's license | Beauty-shop opening notification | Main basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eyelash extensions | Required for the practitioner | Required; the premises must also be inspected and confirmed before use | Beauty Culturists Act, Arts. 6, 7, 11 and 12; MHLW notices |
| Nail only | Generally read as not required | Generally read as outside scope | Definition of "biyo", Art. 2(1) |
| Artmake (needle pigment injection) | Not a beautician matter | — | Medical Practitioners Act Art. 17 (medical act) |

Before signing a lease, give the public health centre your service list and floor plan to check the applicable layout and facility standards.

## Does a nail-only salon really need no notification?

A salon offering only ordinary nail care, shaping, reinforcement or decoration generally does not need a Japanese beautician's licence or a beauty-shop opening notification. If you also offer other services, or intend to offer something beyond ordinary nail care and decoration, show the full menu to the local public health centre.

In support, the hygiene of nail salons is handled not under the Beauty Culturists Act but under a separate guideline. The MHLW Health Bureau Director-General's notice "On the guideline for hygiene management at nail salons" (15 September 2010, Ken-hatsu 0915 No. 4) treats barber and beauty shops as **outside the guideline's scope** — because the Barbers Act and the Beauty Culturists Act already require them to secure a hygiene standard — and handles nail salons separately. In other words, nail salons are placed under hygiene management distinct from the beauty-shop framework of the Beauty Culturists Act. That guideline is technical advice under Article 245-4(1) of the Local Autonomy Act, serving as a benchmark for the public health center's guidance and advice.

If one shop offers both nail work and eyelash extensions, a licensed beautician must still apply the extensions at premises that have been notified, inspected and confirmed before use. Ask the local public health centre how to separate the spaces and which facilities may be shared, before signing a lease or starting work.

## Which acts require a beautician's license?

Article 6 of the Beauty Culturists Act provides that "a person who is not a beautician must not practice biyo as a business," prohibiting unlicensed beauty business. Article 7 further provides that a beautician must not practice biyo anywhere other than a beauty shop (except where there is a special circumstance set by Cabinet Order). So a treatment amounting to biyo (such as eyelash extensions) is, as a basic rule, (1) performed by a licensed beautician (2) at a notified beauty shop.

The measures to be taken when practicing biyo are also set by law. Article 8 requires keeping clean the cloths and instruments that touch the skin, replacing the skin-contact cloths for each customer, and disinfecting the instruments for each customer. On management, a beauty shop that regularly employs two or more licensed beauticians must appoint a managing beautician — a person who has engaged in biyo for at least three years after licensing and has completed a training course designated by the prefectural governor — to manage the shop hygienically (Article 12-3).

| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| Art. 6 | No one but a beautician may practice biyo as a business (ban on unlicensed business) |
| Art. 7 | A beautician must not practice biyo outside a beauty shop |
| Art. 8 | Keep instruments and cloths clean; replace and disinfect per customer |
| Art. 12-3 | Appoint a managing beautician if the shop regularly has two or more staff members who are licensed beauticians |

Which treatment amounts to "biyo," and how far an act requires a license, is ultimately decided by qualified persons, the public health center and the prefecture. This article is general information only.

## What must the facility meet when you notify a beauty shop?

As to structure and equipment, the person opening a beauty shop must not use it until the prefectural governor has inspected the structure and equipment and confirmed that they are suitable for taking the measures in Article 13 (Article 12). The order is thus: notification → inspection and confirmation of structure and equipment → start of use.

Article 13 requires cleanliness, disinfection equipment, sufficient light and ventilation, and any further sanitary measures set by local ordinance. Check the ordinance and public health centre guidance for your location about minimum workroom size, partitions and other facility details.

The prefectural governor may, when it finds it necessary, have officials enter and inspect a beauty shop (Article 14), and where the opener violates Article 12-3 or Article 13, or lets a non-beautician practice biyo there, may order the shop closed for a fixed period (Article 15). Bring a floor plan and consult the public health center of the location in advance. For the whole picture of the beauty-shop opening notification and facility standards, see [the beauty-shop opening notification and facility standards](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/biyoushojo-kaisetsu-todokede-kozo-setsubi-kijun).

## Where is the line with medical practice (artmake, etc.) that you must not cross?

Permanent makeup as defined by MHLW, such as eyebrow or eyeliner pigmentation with a needle, is a medical act. A beautician's licence or beauty-shop notification does not allow a salon to offer it. In a medical institution the procedure can be performed by a physician or, where appropriate, by a nurse assisting in care under a physician's examination and direction. A doctor's instructions alone do not authorise a beauty salon to offer the procedure (Medical Practitioners Act, Art. 17; MHLW notices).

Eyelash extensions and permanent makeup are governed by different laws and must be provided in different types of premises. For a different procedure whose classification is unclear, explain the method to the local public health centre or other competent authority.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I open an eyelash-extension salon in a room of my home?**
A. Eyelash extensions are treated as biyo under the Beauty Culturists Act, performed by a licensed beautician at a notified beauty shop (Article 11; MHLW notices). The structure and equipment are inspected and confirmed under Articles 12 and 13, and the concrete floor area and partitioning depend on the prefecture's ordinance and guidance. Whether a room of your home qualifies turns on the layout and the local standard, so bring a floor plan and consult the public health center in advance.

**Q. For nails only, is truly no notification needed?**
A. A shop offering ordinary nail care and decoration alone generally does not need a beautician's licence or a beauty-shop opening notification. MHLW publishes separate nail salon hygiene guidance. If the salon also offers regulated beauty work such as eyelash extensions, the associated beauty premises must be notified, inspected and confirmed before use.

**Q. Can lash lifts or eyebrow artmake also be done on a beautician's license?**
A. Lash lifts fall within beauty work and must be performed by a licensed beautician at premises notified and inspected before use. Needle-applied permanent makeup such as eyebrow pigmentation is a medical act and cannot be offered under a beautician's licence. See MHLW's guidance on aesthetic medicine for the medical setting.
