**In short:** to run a "commercial warehouse" that stores another party's goods for a fee as a business, you need registration by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism under Article 3 of the Warehouse Business Act (Act No. 121 of 1956; hereafter the "Warehouse Business Act"). Whether you can register turns on whether the target property meets the facility standards set for each type of warehouse (Class 1, 2 and 3, open-yard, storage-tank, dangerous-goods, refrigerated, water-surface and trunk-room), according to the goods you store. These standards rest on Article 6, paragraph 1, item 4 of the Warehouse Business Act, with the details placed in Article 3-4 of the Regulation for Enforcement of the Warehouse Business Act. You must also appoint a warehouse management supervisor for each warehouse (Article 11). This article is general information organising whether registration is required, the facility standards, the supervisor and the division of roles; it does not judge whether any individual property meets the registration standards.

## When does storage require warehouse-business registration?

A warehouse business means the business of storing, in a warehouse, goods received on deposit, and to run it you must obtain registration from the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (in practice, through each District Transport Bureau) under Article 3 of the Warehouse Business Act. Operating without registration carries penalties. The dividing line is "whether you receive another party's goods for a fee and bear responsibility for their safekeeping."

| Category | Registration | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial warehouse (storage on deposit) | Required | Receiving and storing goods for a fee from a shipper |
| Own-use warehouse (storing your own goods) | Not required | Storing your own products or materials in your own warehouse |
| Leasing space (rental warehouse, rental container, etc.) | Often not a warehouse business | Merely lending space without bearing safekeeping responsibility for deposited goods |

Whether you re-register an existing warehouse as a commercial warehouse or start a new one, the framework of the procedure is the same. When converting an existing warehouse into a commercial warehouse, the key is to confirm before contract or construction whether the building meets the facility standards below and whether it can be used as a commercial warehouse in terms of zoning and the Building Standards Act and Fire Service Act. ## How do the facility standards change by type of warehouse?

The facility standards for a commercial warehouse rest on Article 6, paragraph 1, item 4 of the Warehouse Business Act (registration is refused if a property does not meet them), with the details set in Article 3-4 of the Regulation for Enforcement of the Warehouse Business Act. Which goods may be stored in which warehouse is organised in Articles 3-3 to 3-11 and an appended table of the same regulation, and the standards required change with the type of warehouse.

| Type of warehouse | Example of goods stored | Tendency of the standards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class 1 warehouse | A wide range of general cargo | The broadest set of items: right of use, conformity with related laws, fixity to the land, strength of the frame, outer walls and floor, waterproofing, moisture-proofing, heat-shielding, fire resistance or fire prevention, fire extinguishers, crime prevention, rodent prevention, lighting, etc. |
| Class 2 and 3 warehouses | Less-flammable or lower-risk goods | Part of the Class 1 items (such as fire resistance/prevention and moisture-proofing) is relaxed |
| Open-yard warehouse | Minerals, timber, vehicles and other goods that withstand outdoor storage | Surrounding walls or fences, lighting, drainage |
| Storage-tank warehouse (silo, tank) | Bulk grain, liquids, etc. | Structure and airtightness of the tank |
| Dangerous-goods warehouse | Hazardous materials and high-pressure gas under the Fire Service Act, etc. | Standards on top of conformity with separate regulations such as the Fire Service Act and Building Standards Act |
| Refrigerated warehouse | Perishables and frozen goods stored at 10 degrees or below | Structure and equipment that keep a set temperature |

Thus, even in the same building, the type and standards required change with "what you store." Because meeting the standards concerns the building's structure and its fire, water and other performance, design and the building-confirmation application are the domain of an architect and a designated confirmation and inspection body, and fire-fighting equipment and the handling of dangerous goods are the domain of the fire department and fire-equipment engineers. ## Who can be appointed as the warehouse management supervisor?

Under Article 11 of the Warehouse Business Act, a warehouse operator must appoint a warehouse management supervisor for each warehouse and have the warehouse management conducted properly. Who can be appointed is set by the requirements in Article 9 of the Regulation for Enforcement of the Warehouse Business Act.

| Eligible person (any one) | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Practical experience (supervisory) | A person with two or more years of supervisory practical experience in warehouse management |
| Practical experience | A person with three or more years of practical experience in warehouse management |
| Completion of a course | A person who has completed the course on warehouse management designated by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism |

The practical experience here means experience in a commercial warehouse; experience in an own-use warehouse is not counted. A warehouse management supervisor is in principle appointed for each warehouse, but for warehouses on the same site that are regarded as functionally integrated, or for several warehouses directly managed and supervised by the same business office or other establishment, the same person may be appointed. If there is no one in the company who meets the requirements, there is a way to meet them by completing the course. Check the latest guidance of the running body for the course schedule and its handling.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need registration even for a warehouse that only stores my own products?**
A. In principle, no. Warehouse-business registration is required for a commercial warehouse that receives and stores another party's goods on deposit (Article 3). An own-use warehouse, where you store your own products or materials in your own warehouse, is not a warehouse business. However, if you come to receive goods from another company for a fee, it may amount to a commercial warehouse, so it is judged by the substance of the business.

**Q. Is registration required for a business that lends rental warehouses or rental space?**
A. A lease that merely lends space without bearing safekeeping responsibility for deposited goods is often not a warehouse business. On the other hand, if there is substance of receiving goods and bearing responsibility for their safekeeping, it may amount to a commercial warehouse. Because it is judged by the contract and the substance of safekeeping rather than the name, confirm with the District Transport Bureau in advance where the line is subtle.

**Q. Can I register an existing building as a commercial warehouse?**
A. You can register it if that building meets the facility standards for the type of warehouse matching the goods you store (Article 6, paragraph 1, item 4, and Article 3-4 of the Enforcement Regulation). The key is to confirm, before contract or renovation, its fire, water, moisture and fire-resistance performance and its relation to zoning, the Building Standards Act and the Fire Service Act. Whether it conforms is a matter for the examination.

**Q. Can the warehouse management supervisor be an outside person?**
A. Anyone who meets the requirements of Article 9 of the Enforcement Regulation (two or more years of supervisory practical experience, three or more years of practical experience, or completion of the course designated by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism) can be appointed. If no one in the company meets the requirements, there is a way to meet them by completing the course. Practical experience in a commercial warehouse is what counts; experience in an own-use warehouse is not included.
