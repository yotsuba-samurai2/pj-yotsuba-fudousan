**In short:** to sell cosmetics under your own brand, you first need a cosmetics marketing-authorisation licence (Article 12 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act, "the Act") — the licence that ships to market and bears final responsibility. A separate cosmetics manufacturing licence (Article 13) is needed at the site that makes, packs, labels or stores (before shipping decision) the product, and importing to sell under your own name generally needs both.

**Sources checked: 24 October 2026.** This article organises the difference between the two licences, the three officers and GQP/GVP for businesses that want to sell cosmetics under their own brand through OEM or import. The law's name is the Act on Securing Quality, Efficacy and Safety of Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices and Other Products ("the Act"), and the provisions were reconciled by law name on [e-Gov](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/335AC0000000145). Each ministerial ordinance's number and final amendment date, the cabinet-order article setting the licence's validity period, and each municipality's fees, standard processing periods and credit requirements remain unverified. The competent prefecture (pharmaceutical-affairs section) and qualified professionals assess individual licensing.

## What is the difference between a cosmetics "marketing-authorisation licence" and a "manufacturing licence"?

They are two separate licences with different purposes; both must be in place before you can put a self-branded cosmetic on the market. Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) describes the marketing-authorisation licence as "a licence to ship products to market; this licence does not allow manufacturing," and the manufacturing licence as "a licence to manufacture (including packing, labelling and storage before the shipping decision); this licence does not allow shipping to market." The marketing-authorisation holder bears final responsibility for the product.

| Type of licence | Basis | What it allows | What it does not allow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetics marketing-authorisation licence | Article 12 of the Act | Shipping to market; final responsibility | Manufacturing itself |
| Cosmetics manufacturing licence | Article 13 of the Act | Manufacturing, packing, labelling, pre-shipping storage | Shipping to market |

The marketing-authorisation licence is, in principle, granted by the prefectural governor for the location of the office with the main function. The manufacturing licence is granted, for each manufacturing site, by the prefectural governor for that site. The licence has a validity period (generally stated as five years) and lapses if not renewed before expiry. The renewal timing and the cabinet-order article setting the period must be confirmed in municipal guidance and the cabinet order; this article gives no fixed value (unverified).

## How is a cosmetics manufacturing licence divided into categories?

The manufacturing licence is divided by the processes it allows. Aichi Prefecture's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) treats "general" as the category that can perform integrated manufacture, and "packing, labelling and storage" as the category that cannot perform bulk manufacture or filling and may only pack into containers, label, store and inspect. Filling into containers and other primary packaging needs the "general" category.

| Manufacturing-licence category | Processes allowed |
| --- | --- |
| General | Integrated manufacture of cosmetics (including filling and other primary packaging) |
| Packing, labelling, storage | Secondary packaging, label application, storage and inspection only (no bulk manufacture or filling) |

If you only plan and make shipping decisions in-house and outsource the manufacture, you hold the marketing-authorisation licence and leave the manufacture to a contractor that holds a manufacturing licence. Which category you need depends on which processes you keep in-house.

## Do you need a marketing-authorisation licence even just to import and sell?

Yes. Even for cosmetics made abroad, it is the domestic marketing-authorisation holder who ships to the domestic market and bears final responsibility. Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) states that "where you import and ship to market as your own product," both the marketing-authorisation licence and a manufacturing licence (category: packing, labelling, storage) are needed, and notes that "even when importing a product that already carries Japanese labelling, you must store it once at a domestic manufacturing site and conduct the necessary testing and inspection."

The licences you need change with the form of trade. If you outsource the import itself to another company holding a manufacturing licence and have it ship as its product, your own marketing-authorisation licence may suffice. Where you handle only the import and storage and ship under another's name, a packing-labelling-storage manufacturing licence is said to be required. Confirm which case applies with the competent pharmaceutical-affairs section (this article makes no judgement on an individual trade pattern).

Note that cosmetics must follow a pre-marketing notification (marketing notification) for each sales name, and restrictions on ingredients that may be blended (prohibited and restricted ingredients, rules on preservatives, UV absorbers, tar colours, and so on). This article does not give a definitive view on individual ingredients or labelling (the details and latest operation are unverified and need confirmation from the competent authority and qualified professionals).

## Who may be the three officers, including the marketing-authorisation supervisor?

At the centre of the marketing-authorisation structure are the three officers known in practice as the "three officers": the marketing-authorisation supervisor under Article 17 of the Act, the quality-assurance officer under the GQP ordinance, and the safety-management officer under the GVP ordinance. On the manufacturing side, a responsible engineer is placed at each manufacturing site.

| Role | Position | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing-authorisation supervisor | Oversees quality management and post-marketing safety management | Article 17 of the Act |
| Quality-assurance officer | Handles quality-management operations | GQP ordinance |
| Safety-management officer | Handles post-marketing safety management | GVP ordinance |
| Responsible engineer | Manufacturing and quality control at the site | Manufacturing (per site) |

The qualification for a cosmetics marketing-authorisation supervisor rests on Article 17 of the Act and Article 85 of its enforcement regulation. Kyoto Prefecture's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) lists, as cosmetics categories, a pharmacist; a person who completed a specialist course in pharmacy or chemistry at a high school or an equivalent or higher school; and a person who, after acquiring subjects in pharmacy or chemistry at such a school, worked for three years or more in quality-management or safety-management operations for pharmaceuticals, quasi-drugs or cosmetics. The range of qualification is broader than for pharmaceuticals, and a non-pharmacist may meet the requirement — a feature of cosmetics. The treatment of required credits etc. differs by municipality (Tokyo's guidance states 12 credits or more). Confirm the definitive requirement in the competent authority's latest guide.

## What structure do GQP and GVP require?

A marketing-authorisation licence presupposes not only placing people but being able to document and operate the systems that manage quality and safety — that is GQP (the quality-management standard) and GVP (the post-marketing safety-management standard). Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance (referenced 24 October 2026) gives the GQP ordinance as the "Ministerial Ordinance on Standards for Quality Management of Pharmaceuticals, Quasi-drugs, Cosmetics and Regenerative Medicine Products" and the GVP ordinance as the "Ministerial Ordinance on Standards for Post-Marketing Safety Management of Pharmaceuticals, Quasi-drugs, Cosmetics, Medical Devices and Regenerative Medicine Products."

- GQP: prepare procedure documents to secure product quality, and set up a structure for managing manufacturing sites, deciding whether to ship, and handling recalls.
- GVP: set up a structure to collect and examine quality and safety information after marketing and connect it to necessary measures (recalls, information provision, and so on).

The application attaches an organisation chart, procedure documents and documents certifying the three officers' qualifications. The ordinances' formal numbers, final amendment dates and attachment forms must be confirmed in the ordinance texts and municipal guidance; this article gives no fixed value (unverified).

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a marketing-authorisation licence even if I only do OEM (outsourced manufacture) and don't make anything myself?**
A. Even if you outsource the manufacture, a cosmetics marketing-authorisation licence is required as long as you ship to market under your own brand (Article 12 of the Act). The manufacture itself is done by a contractor holding a manufacturing licence, while you make the shipping decision and bear final responsibility. The need for a licence changes with the form of trade, so confirm with the competent pharmaceutical-affairs section.

**Q. Do I also need a manufacturing licence to sell imported cosmetics?**
A. If you import and ship under your own name, a manufacturing licence (packing-labelling-storage category) is said to be required in addition to the marketing-authorisation licence. Even with Japanese labelling already applied, you must store the product at a domestic site and conduct testing and inspection (Kanagawa, referenced 24 October 2026). Depending on the outsourcing arrangement, the marketing-authorisation licence alone may suffice.

**Q. Must one be a pharmacist to serve as one of the three officers?**
A. A cosmetics marketing-authorisation supervisor is not limited to pharmacists. A person who completed a pharmacy or chemistry course at a high school or above, or who acquired pharmacy or chemistry subjects and then worked three years or more in quality- or safety-management operations, may also qualify (Article 17 of the Act, Article 85 of the enforcement regulation; Kyoto, referenced 24 October 2026). The treatment of required credits differs by municipality, so confirm in the competent authority's guide.

**Q. How long does licensing take, and what does it cost?**
A. Fees are set by prefectural ordinance, and the standard processing period is shown in each municipality's review criteria. It varies with how ready the three officers, the GQP/GVP procedure documents and the manufacturing or storage facilities are. This article gives no fixed amounts or days (the variation is large and we have not verified them, so they remain unverified). Confirm the latest guide with the competent pharmaceutical-affairs section.
