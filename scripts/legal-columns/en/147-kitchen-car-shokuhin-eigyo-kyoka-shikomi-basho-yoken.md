**In short:** to serve food from a kitchen car (mobile sales), you must obtain a food business permit for the vehicle (automobile) itself from the health center, and in principle a separate permit, matching the use, is also required for the "prep site" where you prepare ingredients (Article 55 of the Food Sanitation Act; the facility standards are set by each prefecture's ordinance under Article 54 of the same Act). This article is general information within the scope of preparing documents submitted to a public office; the judgement of whether the facility meets the standards and the on-site inspection are done by the health center, and our office (administrative scrivener) does not make specific hygiene judgements.

## How does a kitchen car's (mobile sales') permit differ from a store's permit?

Under the Food Sanitation Act as amended and fully enforced on 1 June 2021, food businesses were organised into categories requiring a permit, requiring notification, or requiring neither. A person who runs a business requiring a permit must obtain the permit of the prefectural governor (including the mayor of a city with a health center or the head of a special ward) (Article 55, paragraph 1 of the Food Sanitation Act). A kitchen car obtains this permit with an "automobile" as its facility, and in many municipalities it is called "mobile food business." The framework is the same as a fixed store, but the facility is a vehicle and the cooking processes that can be done inside the vehicle are limited.

Another major difference is hygiene management in line with HACCP. Under the amended Act, in principle every business operator bears the duty to carry out hygiene management in accordance with standards set by Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare ordinance (Article 51, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the Food Sanitation Act). A kitchen car is no exception: it is required to prepare a hygiene management plan and keep records. In addition, a food hygiene manager must be designated for each business.

| Item | Fixed store | Kitchen car (automobile) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Article 55 of the Food Sanitation Act (business permit) | Same (the facility is an automobile; called "mobile food business" in many municipalities) |
| Facility standards | Ordinance under Article 54 of the Food Sanitation Act | Same (standards for the vehicle: water supply/drainage, hand-washing, etc.) |
| Hygiene management | Hygiene management in line with HACCP (Article 51) | Same |
| Cooking process | Can be completed inside the store | Processes in the vehicle are limited; prep is in principle done at a separate facility |

## Where do you look for the facility standards for a food business permit for the vehicle (automobile)?

The standards for a business facility are set by the prefecture by ordinance, for businesses that have a marked effect on public health (Article 54 of the Food Sanitation Act). Each prefecture's ordinance is built on the nationwide minimum line of the Enforcement Regulation of the Food Sanitation Act (Article 66-7 of the Enforcement Regulation and its appended table). For a kitchen car, roughly the following points are checked (the Enforcement Regulation and the guidance of each municipality, accessed 2026-11-01).

| Point checked | Main content |
| --- | --- |
| Water supply / drainage | A storage tank and a drainage tank. In some municipalities the range of food you may serve changes with tank capacity (e.g., Ibaraki Prefecture divides the range of cooking you may do by classes of roughly 40 L, 80 L, and 200 L, accessed 2026-11-01) |
| Hand-washing facility | A facility where the cook can wash hands, connected to the water supply |
| Refrigeration / storage | Equipment that can control food temperature |
| Structure / partition | A structure and partition that avoid dust and exhaust and allow food to be handled hygienically |

The tank-capacity classes differ by municipality and are not uniform nationwide. Before you buy or build the vehicle, it is safer to consult the health center with jurisdiction in advance, bringing the facility design drawings. If the facility is found not to meet the standards after completion, modifications may be required.

## Why does the "prep site" need a separate business permit?

Because a kitchen car has limited equipment and water, many municipalities set limits on the cooking processes that can be done inside the vehicle (e.g., treating the cooking of raw food and full cooking involving pre-processing as not allowed; the guidance of each municipality, accessed 2026-11-01). Therefore, the pre-processing and prep of ingredients are, in principle, done at a fixed cooking facility (the prep site) that is separate from the vehicle and has obtained a business permit.

Because the prep site itself is also a facility handling food, a separate business permit matching the use (such as a restaurant business permit) is required for it. Depending on the municipality, you are asked to submit a copy of the prep site's permit when you apply for the kitchen car's permit. A home kitchen is in most cases not recognised as a prep site, because it uses household equipment and often lacks sufficient partitioning. One method is to use a licensed rental kitchen (shared kitchen) as the prep site.

| Place | Permit needed | Typical role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The vehicle (kitchen car) | Food business permit for the automobile | Finishing, heating, plating, and other cooking within a hygienically unproblematic range |
| The prep site (fixed facility) | Business permit matching the use (such as a restaurant business) | Pre-processing, prep, and storage of ingredients |

How much is done in the vehicle and from where the prep site takes over varies with the food you serve and the municipality's handling. Confirm the specific application with the health center.

## How does the permit work when you operate across multiple health centers?

A food business permit has effect within the jurisdiction of the permitting authority (the prefectural governor, the mayor of a city with a health center, or the head of a special ward). After the 2021 amendment, the practice spread that within the same prefecture you may operate on one permit even across the areas of multiple health centers (e.g., in Ibaraki Prefecture, a permit obtained on or after January 2024 is valid throughout the prefecture, including Mito City, accessed 2026-11-01). On the other hand, if you operate in a different prefecture, you obtain a permit in each prefecture as a rule.

The range in which it is valid, and whether additional procedures or notifications are required at the municipality of an event you attend, differ by municipality. Once you decide the area, confirm the latest handling just before you write, both with the health center with jurisdiction over the vehicle's base and with the municipality with jurisdiction over the place you attend.

| Range of operation | Principle |
| --- | --- |
| Multiple areas within the same prefecture | The practice of validity on one permit is spreading (depends on the municipality's handling) |
| Across different prefectures | As a rule, obtain a permit in each prefecture |

## FAQ

**Q. Can a kitchen car operate on the vehicle's permit alone?**
A. In principle, no. In addition to the food business permit for the vehicle (automobile) (Article 55 of the Food Sanitation Act), a separate permit matching the use is generally needed for the prep site where ingredients are pre-processed, because many municipalities limit the cooking processes inside the vehicle. How much can be done inside the vehicle varies with the food you serve and the municipality's handling, so confirm with the health center with jurisdiction.

**Q. Can I make my home kitchen the prep site?**
A. In most cases it is not recognised, because it uses household equipment and does not meet the partitioning, hand-washing, and equipment standards required for business use (the guidance of each municipality, accessed 2026-11-01). One method is to use a licensed rental kitchen (shared kitchen) as the prep site. Whether you can remodel part of your home for business use and obtain a permit should be discussed individually with the health center.

**Q. Can I operate in a neighbouring city or prefecture on the same permit?**
A. Within the same prefecture, the practice is spreading that you may operate on one permit even across the areas of multiple health centers (e.g., in Ibaraki Prefecture a permit obtained on or after January 2024 is valid throughout the prefecture, accessed 2026-11-01). If you operate in a different prefecture, you obtain a permit in each prefecture as a rule. Because the handling of the area you attend differs by municipality, confirm in advance.

**Q. Does an administrative scrivener also judge the vehicle equipment and hygiene?**
A. We do not. The judgement of whether the facility meets the standards and the on-site inspection are done by the health center, and the vehicle structure and hygiene equipment are handled by the manufacturer or modifier. Our office (administrative scrivener) assists within the scope of preparing and submitting the documents to apply for the food business permit to the health center. The property is contracted separately with Yotsuba Real Estate Co., Ltd., and disputes with a lawyer, each as an independent business. Our office receives no referral fee.
