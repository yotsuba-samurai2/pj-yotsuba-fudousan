**In short:** a detective business is not a licensing system but a notification system. Under Article 4 of the Act on Proper Control of Detective Business (the Detective Business Act), before you begin operating you submit, per office, a detective-business commencement notification to the public safety commission with jurisdiction over the location of that office (via the crime-prevention section of the police station with jurisdiction). There is no notification fee. The detective-business notification certificate formerly issued was abolished by the revision in force on 1 April 2024 (Reiwa 6) and replaced by displaying at the office a sign showing that notification has been made (Article 12). The disqualifications that bar a notification are set in Article 3: a person who has been declared bankrupt and has not been restored, a person who has not passed five years since finishing a sentence of imprisonment or above, an organized-crime-group member, and, for a corporation, where an officer falls under these. Lending your own name so that another operates a detective business is prohibited by Article 5, and operating without notification or name-lending carries imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to 300,000 yen. ## Is a detective business a licensing or a notification system, and what must you submit before starting?

A detective business is not a "licensing system" where you start after obtaining a licence, but a "notification system" where you notify before you begin. Article 4 of the Act on Proper Control of Detective Business (the Detective Business Act; Act No. 60 of 2006, in force 1 June 2007 (Reiwa, i.e. Heisei 19)) provides that a person who intends to operate a detective business must, before the day operating begins, submit a notification per office to the public safety commission with jurisdiction over the location of that office. In practice it is submitted via the crime-prevention section of the police station with jurisdiction over the office.

| Item | Content (Detective Business Act) |
| --- | --- |
| System | Notification system (not licensing) |
| Where | The public safety commission with jurisdiction over the office (via the crime-prevention section of the police station) |
| When | Before the day operating begins (Article 4(1)) |
| Unit | Per office |
| Fee | None |
| Name-lending | Prohibited (Article 5) |

"Detective business affairs" here means affairs where, on request of another, with the aim of gathering information on the whereabouts or conduct of a specified person, you carry out an on-site investigation by interviews, tailing, stakeouts and similar methods and report the result to the requester (Article 2(1)). Work done solely on request of a news organization for its reporting is excluded from a detective business (Article 2(2)). ## What are the "disqualifications" that bar a detective-business notification?

Article 3 of the Detective Business Act sets disqualifications, providing that a person falling under any of the following may not operate a detective business. Before notification, you check that you (or, for a corporation, an officer) do not fall under them.

| Item | Disqualification (Article 3) |
| --- | --- |
| Item 1 | A person who has been declared bankrupt and has not been restored |
| Item 2 | A person who has not passed five years since finishing a sentence of imprisonment or above, or a fine for violating this Act |
| Item 3 | A person who, within the last five years, violated a disposition under Article 15 (suspension/abolition order) |
| Item 4 | An organized-crime-group member, or one not five years past ceasing to be one |
| Item 5 | A person who, due to a mental or physical disorder, cannot properly perform detective-business affairs, as prescribed by Cabinet Office Order |
| Item 6 | A minor without the same capacity as an adult regarding the business, whose legal representative falls under Items 1–5 or Item 7 |
| Item 7 | A corporation an officer of which falls under any of Items 1–5 |

By the 2022 (Reiwa 4) revision of the Penal Code and related acts, imprisonment with and without work was unified into "imprisonment" (koukinkei), in force from 1 June 2025 (Reiwa 7), so the text reads "a sentence of imprisonment or above." The final assessment of whether a disqualification applies is a field for the public safety commission; an administrative scrivener does not guarantee acceptance on your behalf.

## What requirements and display duties apply to an office or home-based start-up?

The Detective Business Act has no building structure or facility standard like that for restaurant business, but it requires notification per office and the display of a sign showing that notification has been made. The former notification certificate was abolished by the revision in force on 1 April 2024 (Reiwa 6) (review of the notification procedure) and changed to a method where the operator itself prepares and displays a sign in a conspicuous place at the office (Article 12).

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Office | Notification is required per office; a home may be the office (jurisdiction follows the office location) |
| Sign | Display at a conspicuous place a sign showing notification was made (the old certificate is abolished) |
| Employee roster | Keep a roster per office (Article 12) |
| Education | Education so employees perform detective-business affairs properly (Article 11) |

The sign states the name of the public safety commission that received the notification, the acceptance number of the notification, the date of notification, the trade name/name, and the name and location of the office. Because the format and instructions are shown by each prefectural police, you confirm the latest content on the jurisdiction's official page before preparing it. ## What are detective-business operators required to do in the important-matters statement and contract documents?

In contracting with a requester, a detective-business operator is required both to deliver a document before the contract and to deliver a document after the contract (Article 8). In addition, it must receive from the requester a document stating that the result of the investigation will not be used for an unlawful purpose such as a crime or discrimination (Article 7).

| Stage | Basis | Main items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the contract (explaining important matters) | Article 8(1) | Trade name/address, the public safety commission notified, matters on confidentiality, content of affairs, whether there is subcontracting, approximate consideration and timing of payment, rescission, disposal of materials |
| After the contract (clarifying contents) | Article 8(2) | Name of the person in charge and contract date, content/period/method of investigation, method/deadline of the report, amount/timing/method of consideration, provisions on rescission and disposal of materials |
| Document from the requester | Article 7 | Confirmation that the result will not be used for an unlawful purpose |

In performing detective-business affairs, the operator must not infringe individual rights and interests, such as by harming the peace of people's lives, and this Act does not make lawful acts that are prohibited or restricted by other laws (Article 6). Also, subcontracting the whole or part of detective-business affairs to a person who is not a detective-business operator is prohibited, and the operator must not perform the affairs once it knows the result will be used for an unlawful act (Article 9). ## What penalties and risks come with failing to notify or lending your name?

Operating a detective business without notifying (operating without notification) and lending your own name so that another operates a detective business (name-lending) carry penalties. Name-lending is expressly prohibited by Article 5, and both operating without notification and name-lending are subject to imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to 300,000 yen (Article 18).

| Act | Basis | Penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating a detective business without notification | Violation of Article 4(1) | Imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to 300,000 yen (Article 18) |
| Name-lending | Violation of Article 5 | Imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to 300,000 yen (Article 18) |
| Breach of operating rules, false notification, etc. | Each provision | May be subject to a suspension/abolition order (Article 15) or penalties |

If you notify without checking the disqualifications, you may later become subject to a disposition. Where you run other businesses such as an antiques dealer in parallel, because the governing law and supervising authority differ, you check them separately. The antiques dealer is organised in [Office requirements for an antiques dealer permit](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/kobutsusho-kyoka-eigyosho-yoken) and the security business in [Requirements, disqualifications and training for security-business accreditation](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/keibigyo-nintei-yoken-kekkaku-kenshu). A major difference is that a security business is an "accreditation," whereas a detective business is a "notification."

## FAQ

**Q. Do you start a detective business after obtaining a licence?**
A. No. A detective business is not a licensing system but a notification system. Under Article 4 of the Detective Business Act, before the day operating begins you submit a notification, per office, to the public safety commission with jurisdiction over the location (via the crime-prevention section of the police station). There is no notification fee. It is not a system of waiting for a licence screening, but not falling under the disqualifications and performing duties such as document delivery are preconditions.

**Q. Can I start up using my home as the office?**
A. You must notify per office, but using your home as the office is itself possible. Jurisdiction is decided by the office location. You must display a sign showing notification was made in a conspicuous place at the office and keep an employee roster (Article 12). The former detective-business notification certificate was abolished by the revision in force on 1 April 2024 (Reiwa 6) and changed to displaying a sign.

**Q. What happens if I start a detective business without notifying?**
A. A person who operates a detective business without notification is subject to imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to 300,000 yen (Article 18). Name-lending, where you let another operate under your name, is also prohibited by Article 5 and subject to the same penalty. The notification must be completed before the day operating begins.

**Q. How far can an administrative scrivener handle?**
A. An administrative scrivener handles, as an independent business, support for preparing the detective-business commencement notification and submitting it to the public safety commission, and organising the check of disqualifications. Response to Anti-Stalking Act violation, defamation or invasion of privacy where tailing or stakeouts go too far is a field for a lawyer, and the office property and lease are a field for a real-estate agent, a separate business, each contracted separately.
