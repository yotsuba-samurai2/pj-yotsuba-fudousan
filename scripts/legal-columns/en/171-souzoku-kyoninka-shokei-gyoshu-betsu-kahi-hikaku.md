**In short:** a business licence is not taken over automatically on inheritance the way deposits or real estate are; **the treatment of succession differs by industry.** A construction licence can be taken over if the heir applies to the licensing authority for **approval** within 30 days of the owner's death, and during the review the owner's licence is deemed made to the heir so the business can continue (Construction Business Act Article 17-3). A restaurant's business permit passes to the heir, who succeeds to the position of licensed operator by operation of law and needs only to make a prompt **notification** (Food Sanitation Act Article 56). A hotel business is taken over by the heir applying for the governor's **approval** within 60 days of the death (Inns Act Article 3-4), and an adult-entertainment business by the heir obtaining the public-safety commission's **approval** within 60 days of the death (Act on Control and Improvement of Amusement Business, etc., Article 7). On the other hand, the industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal business licence has no provision for succession on inheritance, so to continue the business the heir must as a rule obtain a **fresh licence** (Waste Management Act Article 14). In short, "take over by approval, by notification, by consent-approval, or re-obtain" splits by industry. This article is general information; the individual possibility of succession and the within-deadline procedure rest with each licensing authority's screening and a qualified professional. ## Is a licence taken over automatically on inheritance?

No. A licence is an administrative disposition granted with attention to the "person" who is the operator, and it does not pass to heirs automatically like property. In the past, when a person running a licensed business died, the heir in principle had to obtain a fresh licence from scratch to continue, and the business could not operate in the meantime, leaving a gap.

To avoid this inconvenience, some industries have a mechanism in law by which the heir can take over the position directly (a succession system). But the method varies by industry and falls broadly into four types.

| Way of taking over | Nature | Representative example |
|---|---|---|
| Take over by approval | Apply after the event; reviewed; deemed continuation during review | Construction |
| Take over by notification | Succeed by operation of law, notify after; no review | Restaurant (Food Sanitation Act) |
| Take over by consent-approval | Apply within a deadline; reviewed; deemed continuation during review | Hotels, adult-entertainment |
| Re-obtain | No succession provision; a fresh licence needed | Industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal |

Mistaking which industry is which can mean missing a deadline and losing the chance to take over, or finding that a licence you thought could be succeeded actually has to be re-obtained. The following looks at this by industry. The general picture of succession while alive (transfer, merger, split) is sorted out in [Does a lifetime business succession require re-obtaining the licence](https://luck428.com/legal/column/souzoku-jigyo-shokei-kyoninka-shoukei-saishutoku). This article focuses on "succession on death."

## Can a construction licence be taken over by inheritance succession (approval)?

It can. For construction licences, a succession-on-inheritance system was created by the 2019 amendment (in force 1 October 2020). When an heir intends to continue the deceased's construction business (the whole of it), they apply to the licensing authority for **approval** within 30 days of the owner's death (Construction Business Act Article 17-3(1)). On receiving approval, the heir succeeds to the position of construction-business operator.

What matters is that, so long as the application is made, no gap arises. When the heir has applied for approval, from the day of the owner's death until the day approval is received (or notice of non-approval), the construction licence made to the deceased is deemed made to the heir (same Article). However, what can be succeeded is the "whole" of the construction business; succeeding to only part is not contemplated. If the 30-day deadline passes, the inheritance approval cannot be used and a fresh licence must be re-obtained.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Approval (apply after, reviewed) | Construction Business Act Article 17-3(1) |
| Deadline | Within 30 days of the owner's death | Same paragraph |
| During review | Deemed continuation from death to approval/non-approval notice | Same Article |
| Scope | The whole of the construction business | Same Article |

The licence requirements themselves, such as the manager of business administration and the full-time qualified engineer, must continue to be met after succession. The full picture of the requirements is in [Construction-business licence requirements, management experience and the full-time qualified engineer](https://luck428.com/legal/column/kensetsugyo-kyoka-yoken-keikan-sengi-zaisan).

## Restaurants and hotels: can they be succeeded, or is a fresh licence needed?

Both can be succeeded, but the method differs. For a **restaurant business** (the Food Sanitation Act business permit), under the system in force from 1 June 2021, a transferee of the business, an heir, a surviving company after a merger, or a company that succeeded to the business on a split, **succeeds by operation of law** to the position of licensed operator (Food Sanitation Act Article 56(1)). No governor's licence or approval is needed; the successor need only, without delay, make a **notification** to the prefectural governor with a document proving the fact of succession (same Article, paragraph 2). Where there are several heirs, they can designate the succeeding heir by the consent of all. Of the four methods this is the lightest, succeeding without a review.

A **hotel business** is taken over when the heir, intending to continue the deceased's hotel business, applies to the prefectural governor within 60 days of the death and obtains its **approval** (Inns Act Article 3-4(1)). On applying, from the day of death until the day of approval (or notice of non-approval), the licence to the deceased is deemed made to the heir (same Article, paragraph 2). A lifetime transfer is under Article 3-2 of that Act, and a company merger or split under Article 3-3, each treated separately.

| Industry | Method | Deadline | During review | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant business | Succeed by law + notify (no review) | Notify without delay | — (succeeds by law) | Food Sanitation Act Article 56 |
| Hotel business (inheritance) | Approval (reviewed) | Within 60 days of death | Deemed continuation | Inns Act Article 3-4 |

The equipment standards for a restaurant permit itself are covered in [Restaurant-business permit equipment standards](https://luck428.com/legal/column/inshokuten-eigyo-kyoka-hokenjo-setsubi-kijun-shokuhin-eisei).

## Why is an adult-entertainment business a "consent-approval" with a deadline?

An adult-entertainment business is licensed on the premise that the operator does not fall under a ground for disqualification, for the sake of public safety and good morals. So on inheritance too, the system first confirms that the heir is free of grounds for disqualification before allowing succession, which makes it a **consent-approval**. When an heir intends to continue the deceased's adult-entertainment business, they apply to the prefectural public-safety commission within 60 days of the death and obtain its approval (Act on Control and Improvement of Amusement Business, etc., Article 7(1)).

On applying, from the day of death until the day of approval (or notice of non-approval), the licence to the deceased is deemed made to the heir, and the business can continue (same Article, paragraph 2 = deemed operation). The approval is conditioned on the succeeding heir not falling under a ground for disqualification in Article 4(1) of that Act (Article 7(3) applies Article 4(1) mutatis mutandis). Where there are several heirs, they designate the succeeding heir by agreement. A lifetime merger is under Article 7-2, and a split under Article 7-3. The details of adult-entertainment inheritance succession are in [Inheritance succession of an adult-entertainment licence (deemed operation)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/souzoku-fuzoku-eigyo-kyoka-shoukei-minashi-todokede).

On the other hand, the **industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal business** (the Article-14 licence of the Waste Management Act) has no provision for succession of position on inheritance, transfer, merger or split. So if a sole proprietor holding a licence dies, to continue the business the heir must as a rule obtain a **fresh licence**. As there is no deemed-continuation mechanism either, they cannot carry out collection, transport or disposal as a business until the licence is obtained. Note that the presence or absence of a succession provision differs greatly by industry. How to obtain an industrial-waste collection-transport licence is covered in [How to obtain an industrial-waste collection-and-transport licence](https://luck428.com/legal/column/sanpai-shushu-unpan-kyoka-torikata).

| Industry | Treatment on inheritance | Deadline | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult-entertainment business | Approval (disqualification review, deemed operation) | Within 60 days of death | Entertainment Business Act Article 7 |
| Industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal | No succession provision → a fresh licence needed | — | Waste Management Act Article 14 |

## FAQ

**Q. If I inherit my parent's licence, can I use it as-is automatically?**
A. It depends on the industry. A restaurant business (Food Sanitation Act Article 56) succeeds by operation of law and needs only a notification, but construction (Construction Business Act Article 17-3) needs an approval application within 30 days of death, and hotels (Inns Act Article 3-4) and adult-entertainment (Entertainment Business Act Article 7) need a consent-approval application within 60 days. The industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal business (Waste Management Act Article 14) has no succession provision and a fresh licence is re-obtained.

**Q. What happens if I miss the succession deadline?**
A. Construction has a 30-day deadline and hotels and adult-entertainment a 60-day one; missing it means the succession approval cannot be used and, in principle, a fresh licence must be re-obtained. As the basis for deemed continuation is also lost, there is a risk of being treated as operating without a licence. The deadline runs in principle from the owner's death, but confirm it for each industry.

**Q. Why is the industrial-waste collection-transport business a fresh acquisition?**
A. Because the Waste Management Act places no provision for succession of position on inheritance, transfer, merger or split for the industrial-waste collection-transport and disposal business licence (Article 14). So to continue, the heir must in principle obtain a fresh licence. Note that the business cannot be carried out until the licence is obtained.

**Q. Can I ask for the succession application, inheritance registration and inheritance tax together?**
