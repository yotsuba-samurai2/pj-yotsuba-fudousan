**In short:** to sell manufactured tobacco at retail as a business, you need a licence from the Minister of Finance for each place of business (Article 22, paragraph 1 of the Tobacco Business Act). The review centres on the location of the place of business (the distance from the nearest existing shop = the distance standard) and the expected volume of manufactured tobacco handled (the expected-volume standard) (Article 23 of the same Act). The application is submitted to the branch of Japan Tobacco Inc. (JT) nearest the planned place of business, and the standard processing period is within two months from the last day of the month in which the application is accepted. This article is general information organising the requirements and the flow; it does not judge whether a specific licence will be granted.

## Who grants the retail tobacco sales licence, and what is reviewed?

Anyone who intends to sell manufactured tobacco (such as cigarettes and heated tobacco) at retail as a business must obtain a licence from the Minister of Finance for each place of business where the tobacco is sold at retail (Article 22, paragraph 1 of the Tobacco Business Act). The counter for consultation is the Local Finance Bureau (or branch) with jurisdiction over the planned place of business, and the application is submitted to the branch of Japan Tobacco Inc. (JT) nearest the planned place of business (guidance of the Ministry of Finance, accessed 2026-10-30).

The review takes the form of checking whether the case falls under the "grounds on which the licence may be refused" set out in Article 23 of the Tobacco Business Act. The main points are as follows.

| Point | Organisation in the provisions |
| --- | --- |
| Location (distance standard) | Whether the location does not fall under a case prescribed by Ministry of Finance ordinance as unsuitable for conducting retail sales as a business (Article 23, item 3 of the Tobacco Business Act; Article 20 of the Enforcement Regulation) |
| Expected volume (volume standard) | Whether the expected volume of manufactured tobacco handled reaches the standard prescribed by Ministry of Finance ordinance (Article 23 of the Tobacco Business Act; Article 21 of the Enforcement Regulation) |
| Grounds of personal disqualification | Such as having been sentenced to a fine or heavier for violating the Tobacco Business Act without a set period having passed, or having had a licence revoked in the past without a set period having passed (Article 23 of the same Act) |

Conditions or a time limit may be attached to the licence (Article 24 of the same Act), and even after the licence, certain violations may lead to revocation of the licence or suspension of business (Article 31 of the same Act). The final application of which standard fits is judged by the Local Finance Bureau with jurisdiction after its review.

## What exactly do the "distance standard" and the "expected-volume standard" look at?

The distance standard looks at the distance between the planned place of business and the place of business of the nearest existing retail seller. Article 20 of the Enforcement Regulation of the Tobacco Business Act sets, as one of the cases in which the location is unsuitable, the case where that distance does not reach "the distance determined by the Minister of Finance within the range of 25 metres to 300 metres." The actual standard distance for each category is in a notification of the Minister of Finance (an appended table), and it is determined by a combination of the area category (designated city, city-system area, town-and-village-system area, and the like) and the environment category (downtown, urban area, residential area, and the like); the distance is measured along the road on which people and vehicles ordinarily pass. Because categories are applied and exceptions exist, confirm the specific standard distance with the Local Finance Bureau and JT branch with jurisdiction.

The expected-volume standard looks at the expected volume of manufactured tobacco handled at that place of business. Article 21 of the Enforcement Regulation sets its standard at "40,000 units per month" (except where the Minister of Finance determines otherwise). The expected volume is calculated from such factors as the number of households in the area where supply is expected or the number of users of a facility, and if it is not expected to reach the standard it may become a ground for refusal.

| Standard | Basis | What is looked at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance standard | Article 23, item 3 of the Tobacco Business Act; Article 20 of the Enforcement Regulation | Distance from the nearest existing place of business (whether it reaches the distance determined by the Minister of Finance within the range of 25 to 300 metres) |
| Expected-volume standard | Article 23 of the Tobacco Business Act; Article 21 of the Enforcement Regulation | The expected volume handled (the standard is 40,000 units per month) |

Both the distance and the expected volume can change the conclusion through the application of categories and exceptions. This article stays with information on the framework of the system and does not apply any specific figure.

## How does the licence change for vending machines or a convenience-store combination?

When you sell tobacco over the counter of a convenience store or an individual shop, a licence is still needed for each place of business. Because opening a new shop near an existing shop readily hits the distance standard, confirming this before deciding the location is the key.

When you place a vending machine, the principle is to install it at the licensed place of business, and the application requires a drawing that clearly shows the positional relationship between the vending machine and the shop. When a vending machine is installed at a place operated or managed by someone other than the licence applicant, a written pledge concerning the prevention of smoking by persons under the age of twenty is required (guidance of the Ministry of Finance, accessed 2026-10-30). When you conduct mail-order sales via the internet or the like, a separate attachment of licence conditions is required.

The category of selling within a facility such as a theatre, inn, restaurant, or large store to the users of that facility (specific retail sales business) is treated differently from general retail sales as to the application of distance under Article 20 of the Enforcement Regulation. Because the required documents and the viewpoint of the review change depending on which category applies, it is safer to confirm with the Local Finance Bureau and JT branch with jurisdiction once the form of the shop is decided.

## How long does it take for the licence to be granted, and what documents are needed?

The standard processing period is within two months from the last day of the month in which the application is accepted (guidance of the Ministry of Finance, accessed 2026-10-30). If there is correction of documents or the like, it may take further days. When the licence is granted, payment of a registration and licence tax (15,000 yen) is required.

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Submission destination | The branch of Japan Tobacco Inc. (JT) nearest the planned place of business |
| Consultation counter | The Local Finance Bureau (or branch) with jurisdiction over the planned place of business |
| Standard processing period | Within two months from the last day of the month in which the application is accepted |
| Cost after the licence | Registration and licence tax of 15,000 yen |

The required documents are the licence application, plus a location map and floor plan of the place of business, and, where a vending machine is placed, a drawing showing its position. Because additional documents are required depending on the category and the form of the shop, the Ministry of Finance advises that you first consult the JT branch nearest the planned shop about how to fill in the application. Confirm the latest required documents and the standard distance for each category with the Local Finance Bureau and JT branch with jurisdiction immediately before you act.

## FAQ

**Q. Whose licence do I need to sell tobacco at retail?**
A. To sell manufactured tobacco at retail as a business, you need a licence from the Minister of Finance for each place of business (Article 22, paragraph 1 of the Tobacco Business Act). The application is submitted to the branch of Japan Tobacco Inc. (JT) nearest the planned place of business, and consultation on the licence is received at the Local Finance Bureau (or branch) with jurisdiction over the planned place of business. A licence per place of business is needed whether it is a convenience-store combination or an individual shop.

**Q. Will the licence be refused if there is another tobacco shop nearby?**
A. There is a distance standard: if the distance between the planned place of business and the nearest existing place of business does not reach the distance determined by the Minister of Finance within the range of 25 metres to 300 metres, the location may become a ground for refusal as unsuitable (Article 23, item 3 of the Tobacco Business Act; Article 20 of the Enforcement Regulation). Because the standard distance for each category and exceptions exist, confirm with the Local Finance Bureau and JT branch with jurisdiction before deciding the location.

**Q. How many units is the "standard" of the expected-volume standard?**
A. Article 21 of the Enforcement Regulation of the Tobacco Business Act sets the standard of the expected volume handled at 40,000 units per month (except where the Minister of Finance determines otherwise). The expected volume is calculated from such factors as the number of households in the area where supply is expected or the number of users of a facility, and if it is not expected to reach the standard it may become a ground for refusal. The specific calculation is confirmed with the Local Finance Bureau and JT branch with jurisdiction.

**Q. How long does it take for the licence to come down?**
A. The standard processing period is within two months from the last day of the month in which the application is accepted (guidance of the Ministry of Finance, accessed 2026-10-30). If there is correction of documents or the like, it may take further days. When the licence is granted, payment of a registration and licence tax of 15,000 yen is required.
