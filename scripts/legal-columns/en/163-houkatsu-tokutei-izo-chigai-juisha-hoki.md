**In short:** a bequest comes in two kinds, a "comprehensive bequest" and a "specific bequest." A comprehensive bequest gives by a share, such as the whole estate or one-half; a specific bequest gives a designated asset, such as "this land" or "this deposit" (Article 964 of the Civil Code). The position of the legatee differs greatly between the two. Because a comprehensive legatee "has the same rights and duties as an heir" under Article 990 of the Civil Code, they succeed not only to positive assets but also to debts in proportion to their share, and they join the estate-division agreement. The method and deadline for renunciation also differ. A specific bequest can be renounced at any time by a declaration to the bequest obligor (Article 986 of the Civil Code), but the renunciation of a comprehensive bequest is treated like a renunciation of inheritance and must be filed with the family court within three months of the day on which one knew that an inheritance had commenced for oneself (Article 915 and following of the Civil Code). ## Where do a comprehensive bequest and a specific bequest differ in law?

Article 964 of the Civil Code provides that "a testator may dispose of the whole or a part of their property by comprehensive or specific title." The "comprehensive" title here is a comprehensive bequest, and the "specific" title is a specific bequest. The difference lies in what is given, and how it is designated.

A comprehensive bequest gives by a share or by the whole, such as "all property" or "one-third of the property." Because individual assets are not specified, the legatee succeeds to positive assets and to negative assets (debts) in proportion to their share. A specific bequest designates the object, such as "A land" or "the deposit at ○○ Bank," and the legatee in principle receives only that specific asset and, absent a designation, does not succeed to the debts.

| Item | Comprehensive bequest | Specific bequest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manner of giving | By the whole or a share (e.g., all property, one-half) | A designated asset (e.g., this land) |
| Basis | Articles 964 and 990 of the Civil Code | Articles 964 and 986 of the Civil Code |
| Succession to debts | Succeeds in proportion to the share | In principle does not succeed (except by special provision) |
| Estate-division agreement | Participates like an heir | In principle does not participate |

## When is a comprehensive legatee treated the same as an heir?

Article 990 of the Civil Code provides that "a comprehensive legatee has the same rights and duties as an heir." By this, a comprehensive legatee — even a third party other than an heir — stands in the same position as an heir in the following respects.

First, they join the estate-division agreement as a party. A comprehensive legatee given a share decides, in consultation with the heirs, which assets they will concretely acquire. Second, they succeed to debts in proportion to their share; they cannot pick out and receive only the positive assets. Third, renunciation and acceptance follow the provisions on acceptance and renunciation of inheritance.

However, "the same as an heir" has limits. Because a comprehensive legatee is not an heir as such, the provisions on the legally secured portion and on substitute succession do not apply, and the comprehensive legatee themselves has no legally secured portion. What Article 990 gives is "the same rights and duties as an heir," not the status of an heir itself.

| Respect | Treatment of the comprehensive legatee |
| --- | --- |
| Estate-division agreement | Participates as a party |
| Debts | Succeeds in proportion to the share |
| Renunciation/acceptance | Follows the provisions on acceptance and renunciation of inheritance (Article 915 and following of the Civil Code) |
| Legally secured portion | The comprehensive legatee themselves has none |

The thinking on the legally secured portion itself is covered in [the basics of the legally secured portion (to whom, how much)](https://luck428.com/legal/column/souzoku-iryubun-kiso).

## When you want to renounce a bequest, how do the method and deadline differ by kind?

If you do not want to receive a bequest, you may renounce it, but the method and the deadline split by kind. Get this wrong and a supposed renunciation is treated as acceptance, or conversely you rush the procedure.

For a specific bequest, Article 986 of the Civil Code provides that "a legatee may renounce a bequest at any time after the death of the testator," and the renunciation takes effect retroactively to the time of the testator's death. The declaration of renunciation is addressed to the bequest obligor (the person bound to perform the bequest; usually an heir or an executor). There is no set deadline, but under Article 987 of the Civil Code the bequest obligor or other interested persons may fix a reasonable period and demand acceptance or renunciation; if the legatee does not express their intent to the bequest obligor within that period, the bequest is deemed accepted.

For a comprehensive bequest, because under Article 990 of the Civil Code they have the same rights and duties as an heir, renunciation too follows the same provisions as a renunciation of inheritance. That is, it must be filed with the family court within three months of the day on which one knew that an inheritance had commenced for oneself (Article 915 of the Civil Code and the provisions on acceptance and renunciation of inheritance). If the procedure is not taken within this period, simple acceptance is deemed, and you succeed to the debts as well. Note that once made, the acceptance or renunciation of a bequest cannot be withdrawn (Article 989 of the Civil Code; the room for rescission for mistake, fraud, etc. is a separate matter).

| Item | Renunciation of a specific bequest | Renunciation of a comprehensive bequest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Article 986 of the Civil Code | Article 990 → Article 915 and following |
| Deadline | At any time (but subject to the demand under Article 987) | Within three months of when one knew |
| Method/addressee | Declaration to the bequest obligor | Filing with the family court |
| If not done | No response to the demand is deemed acceptance | Simple acceptance is deemed and debts are succeeded |

## What are the points on the debts the legatee bears and on a bequest with a burden?

Because a comprehensive legatee succeeds to debts in proportion to their share, they may suffer a disadvantage if the debts exceed the assets. It is precisely here that the three-month renunciation or qualified acceptance deserves consideration. Even with a specific bequest, a bequest with a burden — such as "giving real estate on condition of taking over a loan" — carries a duty for the legatee.

On a bequest with a burden, Article 1002, paragraph 1 of the Civil Code provides that "a person who receives a bequest with a burden is liable to perform the duty of the burden only to the extent that does not exceed the value of the object of the bequest." That is, the liability to perform the burden is capped at the value of the property received, and one does not bear the burden beyond that. Paragraph 2 provides that when the legatee renounces the bequest, the person who is to benefit from the burden may themselves become the legatee (unless the testator has expressed a different intent in the will, in which case that intent prevails).

| Situation | Point | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comprehensive bequest with excess debts | Consider renunciation/qualified acceptance within three months | Article 915 and following of the Civil Code |
| Bequest with a burden | Liability to perform is capped at the value of the bequest | Article 1002, paragraph 1 of the Civil Code |
| Renunciation of a bequest with a burden | The person benefiting from the burden may become the legatee | Article 1002, paragraph 2 of the Civil Code |

Where there is a dispute among heirs and legatees over the existence or amount of debts, or over the performance of the burden, the legal assertion, negotiation and litigation are the field of the attorney. ## FAQ

**Q. Is a will saying "I give one-half of all my property" a comprehensive bequest?**
A. Yes; a gift by a share is a comprehensive bequest (Article 964 of the Civil Code). Because a comprehensive legatee has the same rights and duties as an heir under Article 990, they succeed, at the one-half share, to positive assets and to negative assets (debts), and they join the estate-division agreement. Which assets they concretely acquire is decided in consultation with the heirs. Please consult a qualified professional on the interpretation of an individual will.

**Q. When I want to decline a bequest of only a specific deposit, what should I do?**
A. A specific bequest can be renounced at any time after the death of the testator under Article 986 of the Civil Code, and the renunciation takes effect retroactively to the time of death. The declaration of renunciation is addressed to the bequest obligor (usually an heir or an executor). However, if the obligor fixes a reasonable period and demands, and you do not express your intent within that period, acceptance is deemed (Article 987 of the Civil Code), so convey your intent early if you decline.

**Q. Is there a deadline for renouncing a comprehensive bequest?**
A. Yes. Because a comprehensive bequest is treated the same as an heir under Article 990 of the Civil Code, renunciation, like a renunciation of inheritance, must be filed with the family court within three months of the day on which one knew that an inheritance had commenced for oneself (Article 915 and following of the Civil Code). After this period, simple acceptance is deemed and you succeed to the debts as well. Please confirm the individual judgement on deadlines and procedure with a qualified professional.

**Q. Can I ask an administrative scrivener for the name change and inheritance tax of real estate received by bequest?**
A. What an administrative scrivener can handle is the document side — supporting will drafting and organizing inheritance relationships and assets.
