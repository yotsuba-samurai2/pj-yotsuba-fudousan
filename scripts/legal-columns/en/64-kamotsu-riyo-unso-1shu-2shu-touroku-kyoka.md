**Bottom line first:** If you arrange transport by other carriers without owning your own trucks (forwarding), you need a procedure under Japan's Freight Forwarding Business Act. Truck-based forwarding, and sea/air/rail forwarding that does not include pickup and delivery, is covered by a "first-class freight forwarding business" registration with the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Art. 3(1)). By contrast, if you undertake sea, air, or rail transport together with the pickup and delivery (truck collection/delivery) before and after it as a single through service, you need a "second-class freight forwarding business" license (Art. 20). This article is general information organizing the split between first- and second-class, the financial base (net assets of 3 million yen or more), and how this differs from general trucking. Whether a registration or license is granted depends on the transport bureau's review.

## What is the difference between first-class registration and second-class licensing?

A freight forwarding business arranges transport for shippers by using the transport of the carriers who actually move the goods (actual carriers), without owning the vehicles, ships, aircraft, or railcars itself. The Freight Forwarding Business Act (Act No. 82 of 1989) divides this into "first-class" and "second-class."

| Category | Procedure | Business covered (summary) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-class | Registration with the Minister (Art. 3(1)) | Forwarding other than second-class. Truck forwarding, and sea/air/rail forwarding that does not include pickup and delivery |
| Second-class | License from the Minister (Art. 20) | Using sea, air, or rail carriers' transport and undertaking the pickup and delivery (truck collection/delivery) before and after it as a single through service |

The dividing line is whether you undertake sea/air/rail transport as a through service together with pickup and delivery (Art. 2(7)(8) definitions). Arranging port-to-port container transport alone is first-class; collecting from the shipper's warehouse, shipping by sea, and delivering by truck at the destination as one service is second-class. ## What financial base and offices does first-class registration require?

First-class registration is granted if none of the grounds for refusal in Art. 6 apply. In practice, the financial base (Art. 6(1)(vii)) and facilities (Art. 6(1)(vi)) matter most.

| Item | Content (summary) |
| --- | --- |
| Financial base | Under Art. 8 of the Enforcement Regulations, net assets of 3 million yen or more is the benchmark. A newly formed company that has not yet closed its first fiscal year is treated as meeting this with capital of 3 million yen or more |
| Office | You must have the right to use it, and it must comply with the City Planning Act, Building Standards Act, and related laws |
| Warehouse | If you store goods, you must secure a facility suited to storage; not required if you do not store |
| Disqualification | You must not fall under any ground in Art. 6(1) |

After registration, a registration and license tax applies (Registration and License Tax Act, Appended Table 1; confirm the amount at the time of filing). Because offices and warehouses require zoning and use-right checks first, confirm the requirements before signing a lease. For the property-side requirements, also see the [requirements for transport-business offices and vehicle depots](https://luck428.com/column/unsou-eigyosho-shako-bukken-yoken) compiled by Yotsuba Real Estate, a separate entity.

## What do you need for international transport or pickup/delivery under a second-class license?

A second-class license (Art. 20) is required when you combine sea, air, or rail transport with truck pickup and delivery as one through service. Many international sea and air freight forwarders obtain this license.

| Item | Content (summary) |
| --- | --- |
| Financial base | As with first-class, net assets of 3 million yen or more is the benchmark |
| Pickup/delivery system | Plan the pickup/delivery area and the transport that carries it out (own trucking or entrustment to other carriers) so that transport safety is ensured |
| Office/warehouse | Secure the facilities needed to run the business |
| License / tax | A registration and license tax applies after the license (Appended Table 1; confirm the amount at the time of filing) |

For international transport, additional points arise, such as how the use of foreign actual carriers is handled and the notification of international tariffs. Because the distinction from first-class is that you undertake pickup and delivery as one service, if you do the pickup and delivery with your own trucks, you must also design whether a general trucking license is needed.

## How do you distinguish general trucking (green plates) from forwarding?

The required procedure changes fundamentally depending on whether you "carry with your own trucks" or "arrange other carriers' transport."

| Form of business | Required procedure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carry for hire with your own trucks (green plates) | License for general motor truck transportation | Motor Truck Transportation Business Act, Art. 3 |
| Carry for hire with light trucks (black plates) | Notification for light motor truck transportation | Motor Truck Transportation Business Act |
| Arrange other carriers' transport without vehicles (truck/domestic) | First-class freight forwarding registration | Freight Forwarding Business Act, Art. 3(1) |
| Undertake sea/air/rail plus pickup and delivery as one service | Second-class freight forwarding license | Freight Forwarding Business Act, Art. 20 |

The requirements for general trucking with your own vehicles (offices, depots, minimum number of vehicles) are covered in [the license for general motor truck transportation](https://luck428.com/legal/column/ippan-kamotsu-unso-kyoka-eigyosho-shako-yoken), and the notification for light trucking (black plates) in [the notification for light motor truck transportation](https://luck428.com/legal/column/keikamotsu-kei-jidosha-unso-todokede). Whether forwarding alone suffices, or a trucking license is also needed, depends on whether you carry the goods yourself.

## What is the flow from application to opening?

The flow is similar for first-class (registration) and second-class (license), but the weight of the review differs.

| Stage | Content / benchmark |
| --- | --- |
| Preliminary check | Confirm first- or second-class, whether the financial base (net assets of 3 million yen or more) is met, and whether an office and warehouse can be secured |
| Filing | File with the competent regional transport bureau (some cases involving foreign vessels are handled at the ministry) |
| Standard processing period | Published by the bureau. Roughly 2–3 months for first-class and 3–4 months for second-class as a benchmark (absent deficiencies; confirm with the filing office) |
| Registration / license | Pay the registration and license tax |
| Opening | Complete opening procedures such as setting and notifying fares and charges, and start operations |

## FAQ

**Q. Can I start the business without owning a single truck?**
A. Yes. A freight forwarding business arranges transport for shippers using actual carriers' transport, without owning transport means. Truck forwarding without pickup and delivery can start with a first-class registration (Freight Forwarding Business Act, Art. 3(1)). If you carry with your own vehicles, you separately need a license such as general motor truck transportation.

**Q. Should I choose first-class or second-class?**
A. If you undertake sea, air, or rail transport together with pickup and delivery (collection/delivery) as one through service, second-class licensing is the basis; otherwise, first-class registration. Truck-only domestic forwarding is first-class. Because it depends on the form of your business, we recommend checking against your business plan.

**Q. How much capital do I need?**
A. For both first- and second-class, the financial base benchmark is net assets of 3 million yen or more (Enforcement Regulations, Art. 8). A company that has not yet closed its first fiscal year is treated as meeting this with capital of 3 million yen or more. Confirm the calculation method and documents with the filing office.

**Q. What if I want to be an international freight forwarder?**
A. If you combine international sea or air transport with pickup and delivery (truck collection/delivery) as one service, a second-class license is typical. If you only arrange port-to-port or airport-to-airport without pickup and delivery, it may be first-class. Because points such as international tariff notification differ from domestic-only cases, individual confirmation is needed.
