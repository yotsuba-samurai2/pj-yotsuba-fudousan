## In what situation does re-transferred inheritance arise?

Re-transferred inheritance arises where an heir dies partway through the deliberation period, having neither accepted nor renounced the inheritance. For example, if a grandfather (A) dies and his child, the father (B), dies before deciding whether to accept or renounce, the grandchild (C) takes on the father's inheritance (the second inheritance) together with the "position to accept or renounce the grandfather's inheritance" that the father held. C is the re-transferred heir.

It is easily confused with successive inheritance, but the two are different situations. Successive inheritance refers to a case where the next inheritance overlaps after the earlier heir has accepted the inheritance, whereas re-transferred inheritance is a case where the heir died before acceptance or renunciation. In re-transferred inheritance, the distinctive feature is that C may choose acceptance or renunciation for each of the two inheritances.

| Situation | State of B (the intermediate heir) | C's position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Re-transferred inheritance | Died without accepting or renouncing A's inheritance | May choose acceptance/renunciation for both A and B |
| Successive inheritance | Died after accepting A's inheritance | Joins the division of A's estate through B |

## When does the deliberation period start under Civil Code Article 916?

Civil Code Article 915(1) provides that an heir must, within three months (the deliberation period) from when they learn the inheritance has begun for them, make a simple acceptance, a qualified acceptance or a renunciation. In re-transferred inheritance, Civil Code Article 916 places a special rule on the deliberation period for the first inheritance (A's).

Civil Code Article 916 provides that "where an heir dies without accepting or renouncing the inheritance, the period under paragraph (1) of the preceding Article runs from the time that person's own heir learns the inheritance has begun for them." On how to read this "learns," the Supreme Court judgment of 9 August 2019 held that it means the time when the re-transferred heir (C) learns that, through inheriting from the intermediate heir (B), they have succeeded to the position of heir in the inheritance (from A) that B had neither accepted nor renounced. In other words, the clock starts when C learns that "the position to accept or renounce A's inheritance has come to me."

| Inheritance concerned | When the clock starts | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First inheritance (A's) | When C learns they succeeded to the position to accept or renounce A's inheritance | Civil Code Article 916; Supreme Court judgment of 9 August 2019 |
| Second inheritance (B's) | When C learns the inheritance has begun for them regarding B | Civil Code Article 915(1) |

Where C is a minor or an adult ward, the clock starts when the legal representative learns of it (Civil Code Article 917). If they neither make a qualified acceptance nor renounce within the period, they are deemed to have made a simple acceptance (statutory simple acceptance; Civil Code Article 921(ii)).

## How can acceptance and renunciation be combined across the first and second inheritances?

A re-transferred heir may choose acceptance or renunciation for each of the two inheritances, but the combination is subject to an ordering constraint. The key is that the position to accept or renounce A's inheritance passes to C through B's inheritance.

- If C renounces B's inheritance (the second), C is no longer B's heir and so does not succeed to the position to accept or renounce A's inheritance (the first). In that case, C can neither accept nor renounce A's inheritance.
- If C accepts B's inheritance (or leaves it un-renounced), C may choose acceptance or renunciation for A's inheritance. C may accept B's inheritance while renouncing only A's.
- Even if C first renounces A's inheritance and then renounces B's inheritance, the effect of the earlier renunciation of A is not lost (Supreme Court judgment of 21 June 1988).

| C's choice | What C can do about A (the first inheritance) |
| --- | --- |
| Renounce B's inheritance | Cannot accept or renounce A (does not succeed to the position) |
| Accept B's inheritance | May choose either acceptance or renunciation of A |

Which order and combination you choose greatly changes the assets and debts you succeed to. Because some points are matters on which views differ, consult a judicial scrivener or an attorney for an individual judgement in light of the presence of debts and the content of the estate. Note that once a renunciation is made, it cannot be withdrawn even within the deliberation period (Civil Code Article 919(1)).

## Who may prepare the renunciation filing?

A renunciation is made by filing a declaration with the family court (Civil Code Article 938). Once the filing is accepted, the person is deemed never to have been an heir with respect to that inheritance (Civil Code Article 939). In re-transferred inheritance, the declaration must make clear which of the first or second inheritance is being renounced.

The point to note here is the difference in who may act. Preparing documents submitted to the family court — the declaration of renunciation and the petition — is outside an administrative scrivener's scope. These are handled by a judicial scrivener or an attorney. An administrative scrivener handles investigating the estate and debts, assisting with the estate inventory, and preparing the estate-division agreement.

| Document / procedure | Who prepares / represents |
| --- | --- |
| Declaration of renunciation | Judicial scrivener / attorney |
| Petition to extend the deliberation period | Judicial scrivener / attorney |
| Negotiation with creditors; inheritance disputes | Attorney |
| Investigating the estate and debts; assisting with the estate inventory and the estate-division agreement | Administrative scrivener |

## When the deadline is close, what do you do first?

In re-transferred inheritance, two deliberation periods run in parallel, so the time left is hard to read. If you are unlikely to make it in time, you may petition the family court within the deliberation period to extend it (proviso to Civil Code Article 915(1)). Because investigation and filing proceed at the same time, the basic approach is to split the roles and engage early.

## FAQ

**Q. How does re-transferred inheritance differ from successive inheritance?**
A. Re-transferred inheritance is where an heir dies without accepting or renouncing and that person's heir (the re-transferred heir) takes on two inheritances. Because it is before acceptance or renunciation, the re-transferred heir may choose acceptance or renunciation for each of the two inheritances. It differs from successive inheritance, where the next inheritance overlaps after acceptance.

**Q. When does the deliberation period for the first inheritance start?**
A. It runs from the time the re-transferred heir learns that, through inheriting from the intermediate heir, they have succeeded to the position to accept or renounce the first inheritance (Civil Code Article 916; Supreme Court judgment of 9 August 2019). It is not necessarily the date the decedent died or the time you merely learned of it.

**Q. If I renounce the second inheritance, is the first automatically renounced too?**
A. It is not automatically renounced; rather, you no longer succeed to the position to accept or renounce the first inheritance. So if you renounce the second inheritance, you can neither accept nor renounce the first. Because the result changes with the order and combination, consult a judicial scrivener or an attorney for an individual judgement.

**Q. Can an administrative scrivener prepare the renunciation declaration?**
A. Preparing the declaration and petition submitted to the family court is outside an administrative scrivener's scope and is handled by a judicial scrivener or an attorney. An administrative scrivener investigates the estate and debts and assists with the estate inventory, the estate-division agreement and collecting family registers.
