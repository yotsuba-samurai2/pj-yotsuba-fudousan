**In short:** to start a care taxi (transporting people who need care to hospital and outings for a fee), you need a permit from the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (in practice, the head of the District Transport Bureau) under Article 4 of the Road Transport Act. In most cases this is a permit for general passenger motor vehicle transport limited to "welfare transport," and if you use a welfare vehicle equipped for wheelchairs or stretchers you can apply with a single vehicle. The driver needs a Class 2 driving licence (Article 86 of the Road Traffic Act) to carry passengers for a fee, and if care transport is done in an ordinary sedan-type vehicle, the driver must also hold a qualification such as certified care worker or home helper. This article is general information organising the permit types, the office and garage (property) requirements, the Class 2 licence and care qualifications, the cost and time guide, and who to hand the property, licence and labour matters to. The outcome of any individual permit is a matter for the Transport Branch Office and the District Transport Bureau.

## What permit do you need to start a care taxi (welfare transport)?

A care taxi is a business that transports people who need care for a fee, in response to their demand, by chartering a passenger vehicle. To start it, Article 4 of the Road Transport Act requires a permit for general passenger motor vehicle transport business.

Article 3 of the Road Transport Act classifies passenger motor vehicle transport by type, and a care taxi falls under "general passenger motor vehicle transport" (the business of transporting passengers by chartering, under a single contract, a vehicle with a seating capacity of fewer than eleven). In practice, the central form is a permit "limited to welfare transport," which limits the persons transported to those who need care.

The permit standards are set out in Article 6 of the Road Transport Act; in summary:

| Category | Content (summary) |
| --- | --- |
| Transport safety (Article 6) | The business plan is appropriate for ensuring transport safety |
| Appropriate business plan (Article 6) | The plan for the office, garage, vehicles and so on does not impair transport safety or the public interest |
| Ability to conduct (Article 6) | You have the ability (funds and the like) to conduct the business properly and continuously by yourself |

In addition, you must not fall under the disqualifications in Article 7 of the Road Transport Act (such as a person for whom a certain period has not passed since a permit was revoked). ## How does general passenger transport differ from "welfare transport limited"?

Even within general passenger motor vehicle transport, the ordinary corporate taxi and the welfare-transport-limited form differ in who is transported and the minimum number of vehicles.

| Item | Ordinary general passenger (corporate taxi) | Welfare transport limited (care taxi) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Persons transported | Unspecified general passengers | Limited to those certified as needing care or support, those issued a physical disability certificate, and others who find independent movement or use of public transport difficult due to physical or internal disability |
| Minimum vehicles | The minimum set for each operating area (five or more in many areas) | Can apply with a single vehicle |
| Main vehicles used | Ordinary passenger cars | Welfare vehicles (equipped with a lift or ramp for wheelchairs/stretchers) or ordinary vehicles (sedan type and the like) |

Because welfare transport limited can start with one vehicle, it suits individual or small-scale opening. On the other hand, the big difference from an ordinary taxi is that those you may carry are limited to people who need care. Because the permit type changes with whom you transport, it is important to organise the target at the business-plan stage.

## What are the requirements for the office and garage (property) and the vehicles?

The office, garage, rest-and-nap facility and vehicles each have requirements.

| Facility / vehicle | Main requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Office | Title to use (ownership or a lease). A place that does not conflict with related laws such as the Building Standards Act, the City Planning Act and the Agricultural Land Act. In principle it cannot be located in an urbanisation control area |
| Garage | In principle attached to the office. If it cannot be attached, within a set distance from the office (2 km in some areas, varying by District Transport Bureau). It can hold all the vehicles used, and the frontage road complies with the Vehicle Restriction Order |
| Rest-and-nap facility | Attached to the office or garage, where drivers can rest |
| Vehicles | Welfare vehicles (equipped with a lift or ramp for wheelchairs/stretchers, a rotating seat, and so on) or ordinary vehicles (sedan type). Title to use is required |

If you sign for a property first and then find it unusable because of zoning or land category, you have to search again. ## What about the driver's Class 2 licence, care qualifications and labour?

Carrying passengers for a fee requires, separately from the Road Transport Act, a Class 2 driving licence under Article 86 of the Road Traffic Act. The categories of driving licence are set out in Article 84 of the Road Traffic Act, and to drive a passenger vehicle for the transport of passengers you must hold a Class 2 licence.

Further, when care transport is done in an ordinary vehicle such as a sedan type, rather than a welfare vehicle, the driver is required to hold one of the following qualifications.

| Vehicle used | What the driver needs |
| --- | --- |
| Welfare vehicle (with lift/ramp, etc.) | A Class 2 driving licence appropriate for the vehicle used |
| Ordinary vehicle (sedan type, etc.) | A Class 2 licence plus one of the qualifications such as certified care worker, home helper, home-care worker, or completion of the care-transport-service worker training |

Labour matters such as the driver's working hours, social insurance and work rules are the field of a certified social insurance labour consultant. If you hire drivers to start, you need to arrange the labour design separately.

## What is the guide to the flow, cost and time up to the permit?

The flow from application to the start of transport and the cost guide are as follows.

| Stage | Content / guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Filing the application | Filed with the District Transport Bureau via the Transport Branch Office with jurisdiction |
| Required funds | You must secure your own capital based on an estimate of labour, vehicle, fuel and insurance costs |
| Standard processing period | The examination is generally about two months (varies by District Transport Bureau). A guideline where there are no deficiencies or corrections |
| Permit and registration and licence tax | After the permit, you pay the registration and licence tax (30,000 yen) |
| Start of transport | Register the business vehicles (green number plates), arrange the operation- and maintenance-management system, and notify the start of transport |

Where you apply as a corporation for a taxi business, there is a law examination for officers, while the welfare-transport-limited form may be treated differently. Because the details of the requirements are treated differently by each District Transport Bureau and Transport Branch Office, check the latest guidance of the authority to which you apply. Even after the permit, you register the business vehicles (welfare-limited green number plates) and go through the pre-start confirmation before you can actually begin transporting.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I start a care taxi with just one vehicle?**
A. General passenger motor vehicle transport limited to welfare transport can be applied for with a single vehicle. An ordinary corporate taxi needs the minimum number of vehicles set for each operating area (five or more in many areas), but the welfare-transport-limited form relaxes this requirement. Which one applies depends on whom you transport and the business plan.

**Q. Can I run a care taxi with an ordinary sedan car?**
A. It may be possible, but to do care transport in an ordinary vehicle such as a sedan type, the driver must hold, in addition to a Class 2 driving licence, a qualification such as certified care worker or home helper. If you use a welfare vehicle equipped for wheelchairs/stretchers, a Class 2 licence appropriate for the vehicle is enough.

**Q. Can I make my home the office or garage?**
A. It may be possible if you have title to use and it does not conflict with the Building Standards Act, the City Planning Act, the Agricultural Land Act and so on. However, in principle it cannot be located in an urbanisation control area. The garage is in principle attached to the office; if it cannot be attached, it must be within a set distance from the office (2 km in some areas, varying by District Transport Bureau). We recommend checking against the requirements before deciding on a property.

**Q. How long after applying does the permit come?**
A. The standard processing period for the examination is generally about two months (varies by District Transport Bureau). This is a guideline where there are no deficiencies or corrections, assuming the securing of required funds and the resolution of corrections are completed within the period. The individual examination period is a matter for the Transport Branch Office and the District Transport Bureau.
