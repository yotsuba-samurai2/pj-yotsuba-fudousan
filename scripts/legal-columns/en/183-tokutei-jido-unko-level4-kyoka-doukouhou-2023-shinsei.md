**In short:** to run driverless, Level-4-class operation (specified automated operation), you must obtain the permit of the prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the place where it is carried on. The basis is the Road Traffic Act (Act No. 105 of 1960); the 2022 amendment (the Act Partially Amending the Road Traffic Act, Act No. 32 of 2022) added Chapter 4-2 (Specified Automated Operation), and under the relevant Cabinet Order it entered into force on 1 April 2023 (National Police Agency and Cabinet Office materials; accessed 2026-11-21). The core of the permit is Road Traffic Act Article 75-12, paragraph 1: a person who intends to carry on specified automated operation submits to the Public Safety Commission an application containing a specified automated operation plan and obtains the permit. The permit standards are set in Road Traffic Act Article 75-13 and the disqualifications in Road Traffic Act Article 75-14. This is a separate scheme from the crewed Japan-style ride-share (Private-Car Utilisation Business; Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii)) and from the chauffeur service business (Act on Proper Operation of Chauffeur Service Business). This article is general information; the final judgement on whether a particular permit is possible and on the design of the plan rests with the licensing authority (the Public Safety Commission) and qualified professionals. ## What is specified automated operation, and how does it differ from ride-share and chauffeur service?

Specified automated operation is a scheme that allows driverless, Level-4-class operation—in which the automated driving device, rather than a driver in the vehicle, becomes the operating subject—to be carried on under a permit prescribed by law. The 2022 amendment (Act No. 32 of 2022) added Chapter 4-2 (Specified Automated Operation) to the Road Traffic Act, and under the relevant Cabinet Order it entered into force on 1 April 2023. In the scheme, specified automated operation is excluded from the definition of "driving" under the Road Traffic Act and is positioned as operation that does not presuppose the presence of a driver (National Police Agency materials; accessed 2026-11-21).

By contrast, the so-called Japan-style ride-share, the [Private-Car Utilisation Business is a licensed business under Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii)](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/jikayousha-katsuyo-jigyo-rideshare-kyoka), presupposing crewed operation in which an ordinary driver drives a private car. The [certification of the chauffeur service business](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/unten-daiko-nintei-eigyosho-zuihan-jidosha-yoken) (Act on Proper Operation of Chauffeur Service Business), in which a substitute driver drives on behalf of an intoxicated passenger, is also a crewed arrangement. Specified automated operation differs in its basis act (Road Traffic Act or Road Transport Act, etc.), the presence of a driver (driverless or crewed), and the permitting authority (Public Safety Commission or the Minister, etc.).

| Axis of comparison | Specified automated operation | Japan-style ride-share | Chauffeur service business |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis act | Road Traffic Act Article 75-12 | Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii) | Chauffeur Service Proper Operation Act |
| Driver | None (Level-4-class) | Ordinary driver drives | Substitute driver drives |
| Permitting/certifying authority | Prefectural Public Safety Commission | Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism | Prefectural Public Safety Commission |
| Assumed setting | Mobility in depopulated areas, on-site conveyance, etc. | Supplementing taxi shortage | Taking home intoxicated passengers, etc. |

Which scheme to use changes with the form of operation and the party carrying it on. Choosing the scheme and supporting preparation of the application documents is an administrative scrivener's work, but the final judgement of which scheme fits the facts is made after consulting the Public Safety Commission and the relevant authorities.

## Where and with what documents do you apply, and what does the Public Safety Commission examine?

Under Road Traffic Act Article 75-12, paragraph 1, a person who intends to carry on specified automated operation must obtain the permit of the prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the place where the operation is to be carried on. Under paragraph 2 of the same Article, the applicant submits to the Public Safety Commission an application containing a specified automated operation plan that states the applicant's information and the vehicles used, the route, the dates and times of operation, the subjects of carriage, and the like. Where the route spans the jurisdiction of several Public Safety Commissions, consultation between the Commissions is handled (prefectural Public Safety Commission examination standards; accessed 2026-11-21).

The permit standards are set in Road Traffic Act Article 75-13, under which it is examined whether operation along the plan meets the usage conditions of the automated driving device, whether there is a prospect of smoothly and reliably carrying out the obligations under the Road Traffic Act, whether there is no risk of markedly impeding other traffic, and—where the aim is to carry persons or goods—whether it contributes to the convenience and welfare of local residents. The disqualifications are set in Road Traffic Act Article 75-14 and include a person for whom five years have not passed since the permit was revoked, and a corporation of which such a person is an officer. The Public Safety Commission must grant the permit where the plan meets the standards and the applicant does not fall under a disqualification.

| Item | Content | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Permitting authority | Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the place | Road Traffic Act Article 75-12, paragraph 1 |
| Content of the application | Includes a specified automated operation plan (route, dates/times, subjects, etc.) | Road Traffic Act Article 75-12, paragraph 2 |
| Permit standards | Meeting usage conditions, reliable performance of obligations, no impediment to traffic, etc. | Road Traffic Act Article 75-13 |
| Disqualifications | A person within five years of revocation, a corporation whose officer is such a person, etc. | Road Traffic Act Article 75-14 |

The fee is set by prefectural ordinance. For example, the Kanagawa Prefecture guidance states a specified automated operation permit application fee of 79,200 yen and a plan-change permit application of 78,500 yen (accessed 2026-11-21). Amounts may be revised, so always confirm the latest guidance of the prefectural Public Safety Commission and Police Headquarters with jurisdiction just before applying. We do not fill in figures we cannot substantiate by guesswork.

## What requirements apply to the specified automated operation chief and the operation plan?

A permitted specified automated operation implementer bears the obligation to carry on operation along the specified automated operation plan after placing a specified automated operation chief. The chief may be placed in the vehicle, or at a location outside the vehicle equipped with a remote monitoring device, and bears the role of taking necessary measures when an accident or breakdown occurs. Where remote monitoring is used, the remote monitoring device is required to be able to receive, at all times and immediately, the surrounding road and traffic conditions, the in-vehicle video and audio, and the vehicle position (National Police Agency, "Entry Guidelines for Specified Automated Operation Permit Applications," June 2024; accessed 2026-11-21).

The implementer must designate, besides the specified automated operation chief, an on-site measure operation performer to act for the chief's obligations, and is required to provide the chief with the education prescribed in Road Traffic Act Article 75-19. The operation plan is required to state, besides the route and the dates and times of operation, the equipment, personnel, and procedures for promptly moving the vehicle off the main carriageway where specified automated operation ends on a main carriageway and the chief cannot drive it (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism and National Police Agency materials; accessed 2026-11-21). As these specific requirements may be revised, confirm the latest published materials before applying.

| Point | Content |
|---|---|
| Specified automated operation chief | Placed in the vehicle, or outside it at a location with a remote monitoring device |
| Remote monitoring device | Receives surrounding/in-vehicle video and audio and vehicle position at all times and immediately |
| On-site measure operation performer | Designated as a person to act for the chief's obligations |
| Operation plan | Route, dates/times, subjects; the structure for measures on an accident or on ending operation |

The requirements for the chief and the content of the education may change in operation. Preparing the plan is supported by an administrative scrivener, but judging whether the vehicle and the remote monitoring device meet technical standards is the domain of manufacturers, engineers, and the relevant authorities.

## Are a road-use permit or vehicle registration separately required?

Separately from the specified automated operation permit, where the use of the road involves construction, work, or passage by a special method, a road-use permit under Road Traffic Act Article 77 may also be required. The road-use permit is a separate permit whose application destination (the competent police station chief) and requirements differ from the specified automated operation permit, and whether it is required changes with the form of operation. Preparing these application documents submitted to the Public Safety Commission and police, and the agency filing of the application, is an administrative scrivener's work.

Also, where the vehicle used for specified automated operation is equipped with an automated driving device, vehicle-side procedures such as registration under the Road Transport Vehicle Act and conformity with safety standards are involved. Here, dividing in advance who handles which of the permit, registration, and technical standards is important to avoid remand and duplicated rework.

| Act | Who / basis |
|---|---|
| Preparing the specified automated operation permit application (submitted to the Public Safety Commission) | Administrative scrivener |
| Preparing / agency filing of the road-use permit (Article 77) application | Administrative scrivener |
| Vehicle registration / safety-standard conformity procedures | Administrative scrivener (registration), engineer |
| Judging technical-standard conformity of the remote monitoring device, etc. | Manufacturer, engineer |
| Negotiation, representation, and litigation in damages on an accident | Attorney |

For all these procedures, support for preparing the permit application submitted to the Public Safety Commission is handled by Yotsuba Administrative Scrivener Office (administrative scrivener). Judging technical-standard conformity, underwriting insurance, and representing in disputes divide among different parties.

## Who should you consult about liability when an accident happens?

Even with driverless operation, it is not that damages on an accident or disputes between the parties never arise. Negotiation, representation, and litigation in damages are an attorney's work, not an administrative scrivener's work. The design and underwriting of voluntary insurance is a non-life insurance company's work. What the administrative scrivener handles is support for preparing the specified automated operation permit application submitted to the Public Safety Commission (documents submitted to a public agency); specific legal judgements such as whether damages are owed and the apportionment of fault are made by an attorney or the court.

## FAQ

**Q. Is the specified automated operation permit filed with the national government or the prefecture?**
A. With the prefectural Public Safety Commission. Under Road Traffic Act Article 75-12, paragraph 1, you obtain the permit of the prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction over the place where specified automated operation is carried on. Where the route spans the jurisdiction of several Public Safety Commissions, consultation between the Commissions is handled (accessed 2026-11-21). The permitting authority differs from the Japan-style ride-share (Road Transport Act Article 78, item (iii)), which is a licence of the Minister.

**Q. Is a chief required even when no driver is on board?**
A. The specified automated operation implementer bears the obligation to carry on operation after placing a specified automated operation chief. The chief may be placed in the vehicle, or at a location outside the vehicle equipped with a remote monitoring device, and takes necessary measures on an accident or breakdown. As the specific requirements may be revised, confirm the latest guidance of the National Police Agency and the Public Safety Commission before applying.

**Q. Can an administrative scrivener also handle the road-use permit and vehicle registration?**
A. Yes. Preparing and agency-filing the specified automated operation permit application and the road-use permit (Road Traffic Act Article 77) application submitted to the Public Safety Commission and police, and the vehicle registration procedures, are an administrative scrivener's work. However, judging whether the vehicle and the remote monitoring device meet technical standards is the domain of manufacturers, engineers, and the relevant authorities, not something an administrative scrivener judges.

**Q. Is an administrative scrivener fine for consulting about damages or insurance on an accident?**
A. No. Negotiation, representation, and litigation in damages on an accident are an attorney's work, and the design and underwriting of voluntary insurance a non-life insurance company's work, not an administrative scrivener's work. What the administrative scrivener handles is support for preparing the permit application. Damages, insurance, and disputes are handled, each as an independent business, under separate contracts.
