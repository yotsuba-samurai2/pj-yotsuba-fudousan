**In short:** To run a qualifying Level 4 service on a road in Japan, obtain permission from the Public Safety Commission for each prefecture on the route. The application is about more than the vehicle: it must show how people will respond when the vehicle stops or an incident occurs.

## Does an empty driver's seat mean this permit applies?

No. The question is whether anyone is responsible for driving the vehicle in response to road and traffic conditions. Article 2(1), item 17-2 of the Road Traffic Act (道路交通法) defines *specified automated operation* (特定自動運行) on a road as operation by a qualifying automated driving system within its permitted conditions of use. If someone is assigned to steer or brake from outside the vehicle in response to traffic, that is driving, not merely monitoring. An on-board supervisor, by contrast, need not be a driver.

Japan's permit system took effect on 1 April 2023. It is set out in Chapter IV-3 of the Act. It is distinct from [Japan's ride-sharing scheme](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/jikayousha-katsuyo-jigyo-rideshare-kyoka), in which people drive private cars, and from the [designated-driver service](https://luck428.com/en/legal/column/unten-daiko-nintei-eigyosho-zuihan-jidosha-yoken), in which a driver takes the wheel of the customer's car.

## Where do we apply and what should we prepare?

Use the route, not the operator's head-office address, to identify the competent commission (Article 75-12(1)). A route running through both Tokyo and Kanagawa needs permission from both commissions. Permission from one does not extend into the other's territory.

| Bring to the planning meeting | Questions the application must answer |
|---|---|
| Route map, days and hours of operation | What people or goods will be carried, when and where? Are the route and expected weather within the system's operating conditions? |
| Vehicle records | Identify the model and chassis number, vehicle inspection records and documented conditions of use for the automated driving system. |
| Staffing and response plan | Identify the supervisor, training arrangements and the people responsible for communication and on-site action after a stop or accident. |

Article 75-12(2) covers the application and operating plan; paragraph 3 covers supporting documents, including vehicle inspection information. The Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department asks applicants to consult it in advance. Its listed fees are ¥79,200 for an initial permit application and ¥78,500 for permission to change a plan. Check each jurisdiction's own instructions rather than assuming Tokyo's fees apply elsewhere.

## How is the plan assessed?

Article 75-13(1), items 1–5, addresses five questions: Can this vehicle perform the operation? Does the plan stay within the system's conditions of use? Can the operator and staff carry out the measures the law requires? Is serious disruption to other traffic unlikely, including when automated operation ends? Finally, is the operation intended to carry people or goods, and does that transport improve convenience or welfare for local residents? The last point is a criterion, not an optional explanation only for applicants who happen to carry passengers.

Under paragraph 2, the commission seeks the views of the competent transport authorities on the vehicle and its conditions of use, and of the heads of municipalities along the route (including Tokyo's special wards) on the local benefit criterion. These consultations do not replace the operator's permit application. Article 75-14 also specifies grounds for disqualification, including certain permit revocations within the past five years.

## How does remote supervision change the staffing plan?

The operator must designate a supervisor (特定自動運行主任者) and train its operating staff. If the supervisor is stationed at a remote monitoring location with the prescribed equipment, the operator must also designate a person to take the required action at an accident scene (現場措置業務実施者). When the supervisor rides in the vehicle instead, a separate on-site responder need not be designated under this provision (Articles 75-19(1)–(3) and 75-20(1)).

Test the plan against a stop at a specific point on the route: who detects it, who is contacted, and who goes to the scene? A diagram can reveal assignments the written plan has missed. Assessing whether the vehicle and equipment meet technical requirements is a matter for the operator, manufacturer, technical staff and relevant authorities.

## FAQ

**Q. Can we start operating as soon as we obtain a Level 4 vehicle?**
A. No. Vehicle compliance and operational permission are separate. The commission reviews the proposed route, times, operating conditions and staffing arrangements.

**Q. For a route through two prefectures, is applying where the company is based enough?**
A. No. Each commission with jurisdiction over part of the route must grant permission. Check each prefectural police department's filing arrangements.

**Q. Is a separate road-use permit always necessary?**
A. No automatic requirement follows from the Level 4 permit alone. If the proposal also involves works or another activity covered by Article 77(1), ask the local police station about permission for that activity.
